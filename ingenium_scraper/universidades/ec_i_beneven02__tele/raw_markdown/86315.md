## Contents

The theoretical part includes the study of statistic methods for the analysis of experimental data. Units and references. Analogue and digital electronic instrumentation (voltmeters, ammeters, multimeters, counters). Measurement methods (Wheatstone bridge, a.c. bridges). Hints on automatic measurement systems.

## Learning objectives

The general objective of the course is to provide students with fundamental theoretical knowledge and practical skills in electronic measurements, with particular emphasis on experimental data processing, the use of digital measurement instrumentation, and signal processing. The course combines theoretical and experimental activities to develop the skills required to address measurement problems in engineering applications. D1. Knowledge and Understanding: Upon completion of the course, students will be able to understand the fundamental principles of electronic measurements, experimental data acquisition and processing methodologies, the characteristics of electronic measurement instruments, and the main signal processing techniques. Students will also understand the concepts of measurement uncertainty and the main sources of error in measurement processes. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding: Students will be able to apply the acquired knowledge to perform measurements using digital electronic instrumentation, acquire and process signals, analyze experimental data, and evaluate the uncertainty associated with measurement results. They will also be able to use appropriate methods and tools to address basic measurement problems in engineering applications. D3. Making Judgements: Students will be able to critically evaluate measurement results, interpret experimental data, identify the main sources of error, and assess the reliability of the obtained results. They will also be able to select the most appropriate measurement methods and instruments according to the specific problem under consideration. D4. Communication Skills: Students will be able to clearly and rigorously communicate the adopted methodologies, experimental results, and related analyses using appropriate technical and scientific terminology. They will also be able to present and discuss measurement results through graphs, tables, and technical reports. D5. Learning Skills: Students will develop the ability to independently expand their knowledge in the field of electronic measurements, understand the operation of new measurement instruments and techniques, and continuously update their skills in response to technological developments in data acquisition and signal processing.

## Assessment methods

The final exam consists of an oral interview designed to assess knowledge of the topics covered in the program and, more generally, achievement of the course objectives, as well as the knowledge developed in the laboratory activities. The following are assessed: (i) the relevance of the answers to the questions asked; (ii) the quality of the content; (iii) the mastery of technical language and overall expressive ability; (iv) the ability to connect the topics covered in the program; (v) the ability to provide examples. In the learning assessment, the student’s answers contribute to the final grade according to the following percentage indicators: Laboratory activities = 33% of the overall grade 2 questions related to the course syllabus: 66% of the overall grade. Students with valid certifications for DSA, disabilities, or other educational needs are invited to contact the teacher or the degree program/department disability coordinator before the learning assessment, to agree on any assessment methods that, while respecting the objectives of the course, take into account individual learning needs.
