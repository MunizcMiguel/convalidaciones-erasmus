## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the fundamental concepts for the analysis and understanding of elementary electronic circuits, both analog and digital. To this end, the operational characteristics of fundamental semiconductor devices, such as diodes and MOSFET transistors, are introduced, studying their applications in digital logic, amplification, and data acquisition systems. The course is not divided into separate modules or partitions. Knowledge and understanding (D1). Upon successful completion of the course, students will gain a deep understanding of the operating principles of basic electronic devices (diodes, MOSFETs, operational amplifiers) as well as the structure and operation of fundamental logic gates and integrated circuit families, both analog and digital. Applying knowledge and understanding (D2). Students will be able to analyze and size basic analog and digital electronic circuits, evaluate the frequency response of amplifiers, design basic block diagrams, and configure microcontroller- and sensor-based data acquisition systems. Making judgements (D3). Students will be able to critically evaluate the performance of an electronic circuit, select the most suitable circuit topology based on design specifications, and independently interpret experimental data measured during laboratory sessions. Communication skills (D4). Students will be able to present theoretical concepts of electronics clearly, rigorously, and with appropriate technical language, as well as write clear and concise technical reports regarding the experimental activities carried out in the laboratory. Learning skills (D5). Students will develop the capability to independently consult and interpret technical manuals (datasheets) of electronic devices and components, which is essential for the continuous updating of their practical skills.

## Teaching methods

In coherence with the total course workload (9 ECTS, 72 hours), teaching activities are structured by combining theoretical lectures, classroom numerical exercises, and laboratory hands-on sessions. Lectures cover approximately 2/3 of the course (flexible range between 65% and 70% of total hours), dedicated to theoretical foundations of analog and digital electronics. Classroom exercises account for approximately 1/6 of the course (flexible range between 10% and 15% of total hours), focusing on circuit analysis, numerical calculations, and problem solving related to diodes, voltage regulators, amplifiers, and various other types of analog and digital circuits. Laboratory activities represent approximately 1/6 of the course (flexible range between 15% and 20% of total hours) and are carried out by small student groups (three to four students) in the Multipurpose Laboratory. These hands-on activities are dedicated to designing, sizing, assembling on breadboards, and testing analog and digital circuits. Key topics covered during these sessions include an introduction to laboratory instrumentation, the study of operational amplifiers in inverting and non-inverting configurations, single-phase half-wave rectifiers with filtering, and the usage of the Arduino platform to read sensor signals and drive basic actuators. Attendance is not mandatory, but participation in lectures and laboratory sessions is strongly recommended. On the HANDY e-learning platform, learning materials, laboratory worksheets, and study guidelines are available for working and non-attending students. Students with certified disabilities or learning support requirements are invited to contact the professor and the Department disability coordinator at the beginning of the term to agree on suitable teaching arrangements while respecting the learning outcomes.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the objectives achievement set will take place through an oral interview, aimed at assessing the of topics knowledge degree, the ability to apply them, the critical spirit with which the student faces the analysis of electronic circuits and clarity of exposure. In evaluating the oral exam will take into account: a) the maturity reached on the course contents, b) the oral exposure quality, c) the critical spirit with which the topics are addressed, d) judgment autonomy, e) the mastery of scientific language. The oral examination may include in the discussion aspects related to the classroom exercises and to the projects that the student has carried out during the laboratory exercises that the student will present during the examination to the commission. In this case the oral test is based on the quality of the report and on the student's knowledge of the subject taught during the course and the critical spirit with which he will present the report: the final grade, expressed out of thirty, will be composed on the basis of the degree of achievement of previous requisites of the oral exam. The praise can be had in the case of an oral test that fully satisfies the evaluation criteria.
