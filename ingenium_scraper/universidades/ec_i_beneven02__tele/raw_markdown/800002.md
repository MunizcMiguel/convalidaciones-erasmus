## Contents

The course consists of 120 hours of lectures. The first half covers the following topics: Real functions. Inverse function; elementary functions and their inverse. Sequences, limits. Bounded sequences; Monotone sequences; Infinite and infinitesimal. Related theorems. Numerical series. Geometric, Mengoli&#39;s, harmonic series. Convergence&#39;s Criteria. Alternate series. Absolute convergence. Functions&#39; limits. Continuity. Discontinuity and related theorems. Composition and limits. Infinite and infinitesimal. Differentiation and geometrical meaning. Derivatives of inverse function, of the composition of functions, of elementary functions. Relative maximum and minimum. Fermat&#39;s theorem. Rolle, Lagrange theorems and applications. Increasing and decreasing functions. Monotonicity. Convexity. Hospital&#39;s theorems. Graph of a function. Taylor&#39;s formula. Peano&#39;s and Lagrange&#39;s rests. Applications. Taylor&#39;s series. Integration; continuity and integrability. Mean&#39;s theorems. Integration on an interval. Primitives of a function. Fundamental theorem of integral calculus. Fundamental formula of integral calculus. Main integration&#39;s methods: decomposition in sum, by parts, of rational functions. The second half of the course is dedicated to the following topics: Functions depending on several variables. Limits and continuity. Weierstrass&#39; theorem. Partial derivatives. Schwartz theorem. Gradient. Tangent plane. Directional derivative. Differentiability theorem. Homogeneous functions. Euler&#39;s theorem. Quadratic forms definite and indefinite. Eigenvalues. relative maximum and minimum. Necessary and sufficient conditions. Convexity. Ordinary differential equations. Metric spaces. Fixed point theorem. Cauchy&#39;s problem and theorem. Separated variables. General integral. Linear equations. Bernoulli&#39;s and other types of equations. Regular curves. Integral on a curve. Differential forms exact or closed. Primitives. Integrability&#39;s theorem. Normal domains; multiple integrals. Reduction formulas. Gauss-Green formulas. Divergence&#39;s theorem. Polar coordinates. Spherical and cilindrical coordinates.

## Learning objectives

The general aim is to provide students with the main tools of Mathematical Analysis, as well as knowledge of infinitesimal calculus, differentiation, integration, and their applications, regarding both functions of a single variable and functions of several variables. Upon completion of the course, students will have acquired the autonomy to use this knowledge as tools for evaluation and interpretation in various fields. They will also acquire communication skills characterized by rigor and clarity.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of: 1) Two partial written tests, covering the topics of the first and second half of the course syllabus, respectively. These partial written tests are not restricted to attending students only; 2) An oral exam. The first partial written test is graded out of thirty and remains valid up to and including the December exam session. The second partial written test is evaluated concurrently with the oral exam. The latter includes some theoretical questions and a discussion of the exercises of the second written test. The final grade is based on a weighted average of the aforementioned evaluations. Students with certified SLD, disabilities, or special educational needs should contact the professor and department coordinator early in the course to arrange suitable exam accommodations.
