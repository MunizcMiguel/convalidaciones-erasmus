## Contents

The course covers the following topics: Fundamentals of Algorithms and Complexity Analysis: Definition and properties of an algorithm; computational problems, problem instances, and input size; computational models and the RAM model; algorithm correctness and loop invariants; time and space complexity; best-case, average-case, and worst-case analysis; asymptotic notation: O, Ω, and Θ; analysis of iterative and recursive algorithms; recurrence relations and the main methods for solving them; amortized analysis and amortized cost of operations. Abstract Data Types and Fundamental Data Structures: Abstract data types, interfaces, and implementations; arrays and dynamic arrays; bags, stacks, and queues; singly and doubly linked lists; iterators and data structure traversal; sets and dictionaries; Union-Find data structures; comparison of data structures. Sorting and Selection: Properties of sorting algorithms: stability, in-place sorting, adaptivity, and computational complexity; elementary sorting algorithms: Selection Sort, Insertion Sort, and Shell Sort; Merge Sort; Quick Sort; Heap Sort; comparison of sorting algorithms; selection problems and the Quickselect algorithm. Priority Queues and Heaps: The priority queue abstract data type; binary heaps; insertion, extraction, and priority-update operations; implementation and applications of priority queues. Searching and Symbol Tables: The symbol table or dictionary abstract data type and its implementations; binary search trees and their operations; balanced search trees: AVL trees and Red-Black trees; hash tables and their implementations. Graphs and Their Representations: Basic definitions and terminology; directed and undirected graphs; weighted and unweighted graphs; adjacency-list and adjacency-matrix representations; depth-first search (DFS); breadth-first search (BFS); reachability and path finding; connected components and strongly connected components; cycle detection; topological sorting; weighted undirected graphs; Kruskal’s algorithm; shortest paths in graphs; Dijkstra’s algorithm.

## Learning objectives

The overall objective of the course is to provide students with the fundamental knowledge and methodological tools required to design, implement, and evaluate efficient algorithms and data structures. The course covers the main algorithm design techniques, computational complexity analysis, and the criteria for selecting the most appropriate data structures. It also highlights the application of algorithms to programming problems and selected application domains, including search, optimization, and Artificial Intelligence. D1. Knowledge and Understanding Upon completion of the course, students will be able to: - understand the main data structures and fundamental algorithm design techniques; - understand the principles of computational complexity analysis in terms of time and space requirements; - understand the operation and characteristics of fundamental algorithms for searching, sorting, graph traversal, and other classical computational problems; - understand the role of algorithms in solving computational problems and in selected Artificial Intelligence applications. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding Students will be able to: - design and implement algorithms using appropriate algorithmic design techniques; - select the most suitable data structures according to the characteristics of the problem; - analyze and compare the efficiency of different algorithmic solutions; - apply algorithms and data structures to solve programming problems of moderate complexity. D3. Making Judgements Students will be able to: - evaluate the correctness and efficiency of algorithms and data structures; - compare alternative algorithmic solutions and identify the most appropriate one for a given problem; - justify the design choices adopted in the development of algorithmic solutions. D4. Communication Skills Students will be able to: - describe algorithms, data structures, and their properties using appropriate technical terminology; - explain the design choices and complexity analysis of the developed solutions, both orally and in writing. D5. Learning Skills Students will develop the ability to: - independently deepen their knowledge of algorithms, data structures, and algorithm design techniques; - apply the acquired methodologies to new computational problems; - build upon the acquired competencies in advanced Computer Engineering courses, including Artificial Intelligence, Software Engineering, Databases, and Distributed Systems.

## Assessment methods

The final examination consists of a practical laboratory test and an oral examination designed to assess the student's knowledge of the topics covered in the course contents and, more generally, the achievement of the intended learning outcomes. In particular: - in the practical laboratory test, the students will be required to design and implement an original solution to a given problem (50% of the evaluation). - in the interview, the students will have to demonstrate to have acquired theoretical and methodological knowledge presented during the course (50% of the evaluation). During the interview the following aspects are evaluated: (i) relevance of the answers to the questions asked; (ii) quality of the content presented; (iii) appropriate use of technical terminology and overall clarity of expression; (iv) ability to establish connections among the topics covered in the course; and (v) ability to provide appropriate examples. Students with valid certifications for Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), disabilities, or other special educational needs are encouraged to contact the course teacher and the Disability Coordinator of the Degree Programme/Department at the beginning of the course to discuss appropriate assessment arrangements. Any agreed accommodations will be designed to meet individual learning needs while ensuring that the intended learning outcomes and objectives of the course are fully respected.
