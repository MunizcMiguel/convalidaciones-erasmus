## Contents

Introduction to computer networks - 1 CFU. Network types and access networks. Packet switching networks. Network model. Networking software. Layered architectures. Concepts of protocol, service and interface. OSI reference model and TCP/IP network architecture. Application layer - 2 CFU. Client/Server model. Socket interface for network programming and synchronous I/O multiplexing. Concurrent programming.The Web and its infrastructure. HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2, and introduction to QUIC and HTTP/3. Transport layer - 1 CFU. Addressing. Multiplexing and demultiplexing. Principles of reliable transmission. ARQ protocols. UDP and TCP protocols. Flow control. Congestion control principles. Congestion control in TCP. Network layer - 1 CFU. Addressing. Routing. IP protocol. Datagram fragmentation. Internet Routing. Subnetting, supernetting, CIDR, NAT e NAPT. Introduction to SDN: separation between data-plane and control-plane. Data link layer - 0,5 CFU. Multiple access to the channel. IEEE 802.3 and 802.11. ARP protocol. Web applications programming - 2,5 CFU. REST paradigm and RESTful services. JAX-RS. Introduction to CSS, DOM, Javascript and technologies for developing Single Page Applications. Server-side frameworks. IoT applications programming - 1 CFU. MQTT Broker. CoAP and MQTT client and server programming. Flow-based programming.

## Learning objectives

The general objective of the course is to provide students with the theoretical and methodological knowledge required to understand the operation of computer networks and to develop distributed applications based on the main network protocols and services. The course explores the software architecture of computer networks, with particular emphasis on the application layer of the Internet, message-passing programming interfaces, and the technologies used for Web application development. Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to understand the operating principles of network-based distributed systems, select appropriate tools for developing network applications, and implement software solutions based on Internet protocols and services. D1. Knowledge and Understanding Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to: describe the architecture of computer networks and the role of the main Internet protocols; explain the operating principles of distributed applications and, in particular, client-server systems; identify the main programming interfaces for message-based communication; understand the architectures, protocols, and technologies underlying Web applications. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding Students will be able to: design and develop simple distributed applications based on network protocols and services; implement client-server applications using the main communication APIs; develop Web applications using the principal technologies covered in the course; integrate software components that communicate through standard network protocols. D3. Making Judgements Students will be able to: evaluate the characteristics of different architectural solutions for network applications; select appropriate protocols, tools, and technologies according to application requirements; critically analyze the performance of client-server applications. D4. Communication Skills Students will be able to: clearly describe the architectures and protocols of computer networks using appropriate technical terminology; document and present the design choices adopted in the development of network applications; discuss technical solutions and design issues using the terminology of software engineering and computer networking. D5. Learning Skills Students will develop the ability to: independently explore new technologies and frameworks for network application development; consult and interpret technical documentation, standards, and Internet protocol specifications; adapt their knowledge and skills to the evolution of software technologies and network architectures.

## Prerequisites

For an adequate and effective understanding of the course contents, students are expected to have previously acquired knowledge of the fundamental principles of I/O management, concurrent execution, and system calls, as well as procedural and object-oriented programming, with particular emphasis on I/O programming. Therefore, students are required to have successfully completed the Operating Systems and Programming 2 courses before taking the final examination of this course.

## Teaching methods

The total workload of the course is approximately distributed as follows: Lectures (70%): concepts and theoretical topics of the course. Exercises (30%): programming exercises focused on the development of network applications and on the analysis of network protocols. Attendance is not mandatory; however, participation in the course activities is strongly recommended. Teaching materials and guidance for independent study are available on the University's Handy e-learning platform and are accessible to working students as well as to those who do not regularly attend classes. ## Assessment methods

The exam is oral and composed of an exercise, a discussion of some exercises developed during the course and questions about the theoretical topics addressed during the course. The assessment is based on the following criteria: (i) the relevance of the answers to the questions asked; (ii) the quality of the content presented; (iii) the appropriate use of technical terminology and overall clarity of expression; (iv) the ability to establish connections among the topics covered in the course; and (v) the ability to provide appropriate examples. Students with valid certifications for Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), disabilities, or other special educational needs are encouraged to contact the course teacher and the Disability Coordinator of the Degree Programme/Department at the beginning of the course to discuss appropriate assessment arrangements. Any agreed accommodations will be designed to meet individual learning needs while ensuring that the intended learning outcomes and objectives of the course are fully respected.
