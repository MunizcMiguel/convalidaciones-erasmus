### Contents

The course syllabus combines general B2 English content and English for Special Purposes (ESP), namely technical language for IT Engineering. The course is meant as an active lab during which students can develop language and communication skills by practicing vocabulary and grammar, pronunciation and project presentation skills. Grammar and syntax of B2 CEFRL levels: nouns and articles; possessives, pronouns and quantifiers; prepositions; adjectives and adverbs; present tenses; past tenses; future forms; duration forms; modal verbs; conditionals; word order and sentence patterns; verbs with -ing forms and infinitives; reported speech; relative, participle and other clauses; linking words; passive forms; phrasal verbs; expressing habits: “be used to”, “used to”, “get used to”; word combinations; word formation; formal and written English vs spoken English. Communicative functions of B2 CEFR Levels: talking about oneself and other people; describing actions (habits and ongoing actions), places, people and professions; giving instructions; making comparisons; talking about past events; talking about future events; writing an email and an accurate CV; taking part effectively in job interviews; describing a project; describing processes; making Maths operations. Topics and specific lexis: Italian, British and American education systems; a university in an English speaking country; phonetic transcription and pronunciation in keeping with the International Phonetic Alphabet; homophones and homographs; main varieties of English: British and American Englishes; life skills; CV European format; specific lexicon related to IT Engineering within the topics dealt with during the course; presentation of a student’s work connected to a professional area. * The final programme published on the teacher’s personal webpage at the platform handy.unisannio.it will list, in detail, all the topics introduced during the course.

### Learning objectives

The core aim of the course is to train students of Engineering to reach a language competence in English corresponding to level B2 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), in relation to the four basic language skills (listening, speaking, reading, writing), with special emphasis on some genres of specialised and professional communication, such as project descriptions, project proposals for design competitions and reports. In particular, in keeping with the Dublin descriptors, this educational objective aims at developing: - Knowledge and understanding: understanding globally and selectively both oral and written texts on topics belonging to the personal and social sphere and to English for IT Engineering. - Communication skills: taking part in conversations and interact in discussions in a way appropriate to the context in different social and professional communicative situations; being able to write simple texts of personal and professional content; - Applying knowledge and understanding: producing linear and cohesive oral and written texts to refer facts and describe situations connected to personal experiences and to the specialised field of IT Engineering; analysing, rephrasing and summarising simple texts on professional topics; - Making judgements: ability to select, analyze, apply critically one’s own judgment to the materials, the situations and the areas of study and work; reflecting on the linguistic system (phonology, morphology, syntax, lexicon) and on language uses (functions, variety of registers and texts, etc.), also in a comparative perspective, in order to identify analogies and differences with the Italian language. - Lifelong learning skills: acquiring skills and competences useful to obtain success in the world of work and in society (life skills e soft skills); reinforcing foreign language learning strategies in order to develop study autonomy; enhancing professional lexis and topics in the English language, by using texts and materials drawn from various sources.

### Prerequisites

In order to successfully study and attend the course, a competence in English corresponding at least to B1 level of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFRL) represents an excellent prerequisite. Attendance is highly recommended as it allows the student: ~ an active participation through the interaction with the teacher and the other students in the triangular relation: teacher-student, student-student and student-teacher; ~ the acquisition of both oral and written language competences also through activities in class and autonomously at home; ~ the exposition to correct morpho-syntactic, sound and intonation models; ~ the acquisition of life and soft skills (self-confidence, giving a public presentation, etc.).

### Teaching methods

The approach to the teaching-learning method is an integrated communicative-humanistic-cooperative one and learner-centered: the focus is on the student, who is the main protagonist of his own learning process. Specifically, the course includes: a) frontal lessons to fix the basic linguistic contents (12 hours); b) class discussions to stimulate interest and involve students; c) written and oral exercises to allow students to practice new structures and constantly reinforce those already acquired (10 hours); d) active participation of students through guided tasks and oral presentations connected to their professional area; e) exam simulations on the e-learning platform UNISANNIO Handy to enable students to evaluate the level reached and identify possible areas for improvement (total: 2 hours; one hour for the mid-course mock test and an additional hour for the end-of-course mock test). The students will actively participate in the interactive frontal lectures and in the activities; they will be encouraged to acquire academic and learning skills useful to autonomously study in English in their future academic and professional experiences.

### Other information

Attendance is not compulsory, but highly recommended as it is functional to acquiring the competences needed to develop language skills and a professional communication. During the lectures students will be able to practice the structures and lexis introduced during the course and improve their oral and written productive skills also receiving feedbacks on possible areas to improve. Additionally, during the course, students will develop their language and communication skills through activities focused on grammar, syntax, and comprehension of oral/written texts.

### Assessment methods

The levels of competence reached in the four language skills are assessed through the final exam, consisting in a written test and an oral interview, both in English. Each test makes up 50% of the final mark. To sit the exam, an online registration through ESSE3 portal is obligatory within 5 days before the exam date. The written test lasts one hour and can be paper or computer based (via handy.unisannio.it platform). The use of any dictionary or electronic device is prohibited. The test includes: a grammar section and a short composition or a listening comprehension activity. To pass the written test the student needs to get a mark of at least 18/30. Passing the written test is a precondition to access the oral test. The oral test is about: topics related to the student’s personal and professional areas; the topics introduced during the course; the presentation of a professional topic of the student’s choice; the discussion of the written test. The oral test will assess the student’s grammar accuracy, linguistic variety, pronunciation and intonation, the ability to interact and the language fluency, the appropriateness of the micro-language.
