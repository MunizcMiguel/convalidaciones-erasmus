## Contents

The course programme, presented in a schematic and chronological form, covers the following topics: 1. Feedback control: linearization; Lyapunov asymptotic stability; hovering control; trajectory stabilization; linear quadratic regulator (LQR). 2. Motion planning in environments with obstacles: discrete search techniques (breadth-first search and depth-first search); discrete optimal planning (Dijkstra and A* algorithms); randomized techniques (Rapid Random Tree, Rapid Random Graph, and RRT*); continuous trajectory planning; differential flatness. 3. State estimation, localization, mapping, and SLAM: nondeterministic filters and the Bayesian filter; Kalman filter, extended Kalman filter, and particle filter; localization with a known map; mapping with known robot pose; SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping). Radio Frequency Localization Systems 4. Vision and learning for robotics: introduction to vision; optical flow for reconstructing object velocity in the image plane; principles of deep learning for image processing (stochastic gradient descent, multilayer perceptron, backpropagation, overfitting and regularization, convolutional neural networks); introductory notions of reinforcement learning for robot control. 5. Overview of the ethical, social, and economic impacts of robotics. Legal issues and challenges arising from the deployment of robotic systems. The sequence of topics may be adapted by the instructor according to course progress, while maintaining consistency with the learning objectives.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide a systematic introduction to robotics, with particular focus on mobile robotics. Starting from an overview of application scenarios, the course focuses on quadrotors, addressing their modelling and control; it then introduces the main pillars of mobile robotics: feedback control, motion planning, state estimation, localization, mapping, vision, and learning. The course is not divided into modules/partitions. D1. Knowledge and understanding: by the end of the course, students will know and understand the models and control principles of mobile robotic systems, with reference to quadrotors, as well as the main methodologies for motion planning, state estimation, localization, mapping, vision, and learning. D2. Applying knowledge and understanding: students will be able to formulate basic models, set up control and stabilization problems, select planning and filtering algorithms, and interpret the use of the studied techniques in mobile robotics scenarios. D3. Making judgements: students will be able to critically evaluate the suitability of models, control algorithms, planning algorithms, and estimation methods with respect to the requirements of an application, recognizing assumptions, limitations, and main trade-offs. D4. Communication skills: students will be able to describe problems, models, and solutions for mobile robots using appropriate technical language and presenting their reasoning clearly. D5. Learning skills: students will develop autonomous learning skills that allow them to further study advanced mobile robotics techniques and to use specialized teaching and bibliographic material.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of dynamical systems and automatic control is recommended (in the latter case, the Automatic Control exam is a prerequisite). Familiarity with linear algebra, differential calculus, and probability is also useful, as these are helpful for understanding the models, estimation algorithms, and planning techniques covered in the course.

The language of instruction is Italian, and the teaching materials provided by the instructor are also in Italian. ## Assessment methods

The final examination consists of a written test followed by an oral discussion. The written test comprises open-ended questions. The examination is designed to assess the student's ability to apply the concepts, models, and algorithms covered in the course. The written test and the final grade (which takes into account both the written test and the oral discussion) are each graded on a 30-point scale. Assessment criteria include: the relevance of the answers to the questions asked; technical accuracy; completeness and quality of the content; the ability to establish connections among the topics covered in the course; clarity of presentation; and the appropriate use of technical terminology. No midterm or continuous assessment tests are scheduled. Students who do not attend the course are required to take the examination based on the same syllabus. Incoming international exchange students (e.g. from Erasmus program) may arrange with the instructor to take the examination in English. Students with valid certification for specific learning disorders (SLD), disabilities, or other special educational needs are encouraged to contact the instructor before the examination to discuss any appropriate assessment arrangements. Such arrangements will be made in accordance with the learning objectives of the course while taking individual needs into account.
