## Partition: FONDAMENTI DI INFORMATICA (Cognomi A-L)

### Language

Italian

### Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide the fundamental theoretical and practical knowledge required for C programming:  
  
- Knowledge of the low-level representation of information, the basic architecture of processors and the compilation process.  
- Knowledge of the syntax and semantics of the C language for the development of structured programs.  
- Knowledge of basic data structures and iterative and recursive programming paradigms.  
- Knowledge of the main elementary search and sorting algorithms and of methods for assessing computational complexity.  
  
By the end of the course, students will have acquired a grounding in the fundamentals of computer architecture, programming techniques in the C language, and elementary search and sorting algorithms, and will be able to understand and apply these methods to solve real-world problems by writing correct and functional programs.  
  
With reference to the Dublin Descriptors:  
  
**Knowledge and Understanding**  
cc7 - Knowledge of the fundamentals of computer science and the algorithmic approach to problem solving.  
cc8 - Knowledge of the architecture of a computer and of an operating system.  
cc9 - Knowledge of methodologies and tools for procedural and object-oriented programming.  
  
**Ability to Apply Knowledge and Understanding**  
ca7 - Ability to translate informational or numerical problems into simple computer code.  
  
**Autonomy of Judgment**  
ag1 - Develop the ability to independently analyze the field under study, applying the methods and knowledge acquired during the training program.  
ag4 - Critically evaluate the results obtained, applying autonomy of judgment in the preparation of written work and in passing examinations.  
  
**Communication Skills**  
ac1 - Develop the ability to communicate the results of one’s activities clearly and in a structured way, both in written and oral form, with a high level of technical rigor.  
  
**Learning Skills**  
ap3 - Learn in a guided way in preparing for oral exams, in drafting analytical or design reports, and in the reports of activities carried out.

### Contents

Numerical coding of data types; low level coding of symbolic instructions and numeric coding; architecture of Risc processor; compiling and assembling. Definition of the C programming language; syntax, grammar, syntactic tree, BNF, semantics; data types, variables and constants, operators and expressions, pointers, arrays, instructions, functions, structured data.  
Lists: implementations and operations; iterations and recursions.  
Searching and sorting algorithms. Execution costs and complexities.

### Prerequisites

Interest, capacity in logical reasoning, deductive knowledge, capacity in attention and application to real problems.

### Teaching methods

Class teaching with some programming assignment in laboratory.

### Assessment methods

The exam comprises a written and oral examination.  
The written test includes some programming exercises which requires to produce C functions to solve specific problems. Other exercises can comprise the grammar and syntax of the C language. To access to the oral examination, in addition to score a positive evaluation, the candidate must produce a running program of one of the exercise of the written test. This program must be self-contained and it must be possible to compile and run it. The written test is performed on paper (non PC use). After the conclusion, the assigned tests and their solutions are made available on the Moodle platform.  
The oral examination starts with the discussion of the program and of the written test, and continues with questions that covers all the course content.  
  
During the course there will also take place two ongoing tests which, if passed, replace the written test: the first takes place in mid/late November, the second at the beginning of January.  
  
The examination is designed to assess the theoretical and practical knowledge acquired on the course topics:  
  
1. KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
1.1 Knowledge and understanding of the basic concepts of computer science and the major functionalities of a basic computer system. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.2 Knowledge and understanding of general methods of studying a programming language, the algorithmic approach to problem solving, and general methods of reasoning in the design of data structures and algorithms. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.3 Knowledge and understanding of the c language. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.4 Knowledge and understanding of the structure and operating principles of a computer, the relationship between the c language and its execution environment, and the process of compilation, assembly, and linking. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.5 Advanced ability in c language design and development of data structures and algorithms. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.6 Ability to formalize the problem of the complexity of an algorithm. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.7 Ability to formalize the concept of correctness of an algorithm. Verification through written and oral test.  
1.8 Ability to develop, debug, and apply basic algorithms to cases of engineering interest to solve information or numerical problems. Verification through written and oral test.  
  
2. ABILITY TO APPLY KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
2.1 Ability to apply acquired knowledge to solve c programming problems. Verification through written and oral test.  
2.2 Ability to apply acquired knowledge to solve elementary questions on low-level representation. Verification through written and oral test.  
  
3. AUTONOMY OF JUDGMENT  
3.1 Ability to verify own degree of learning and understanding of the concepts exposed during the course. Verification through written and oral test.  
3.2 Ability to evaluate solutions to c programming problems. Verification through written and oral test.  
  
4. COMMUNICATION SKILLS.  
4.1 Ability to express own knowledge correctly and logically. Verification through written and oral test.  
4.2 Ability to present own knowledge in appropriate technical language. Verification through written and oral test.  
  
5. LEARNING SKILLS  
5.1 Ability to independently study new programming languages. Verification through oral test.  
  
Assessment Criteria and Grading Rubric  
  
Fail (Insufficient)  
Negative evaluation due to serious conceptual errors, fragmentary or non-existent understanding of the subject matter, inability to apply knowledge, major gaps, and a lack of independence.  
  
18–22  
Sufficient or more than sufficient evaluation due to elementary knowledge of the topics, the presence of some errors, the ability to handle only simple or guided tasks, and a basic level of independence.  
  
23–25  
Satisfactory evaluation due to a satisfactory understanding of the topics, the ability to correctly apply knowledge to familiar contexts, and the ability to complete exercises independently with some inaccuracies; demonstrates a satisfactory level of independence and operational competence.  
  
26–28  
Good or very good evaluation due to a solid theoretical understanding of the discipline, the ability to connect concepts, solve problems, and provide critical analysis; ability to perform complex tasks independently, precisely, and effectively, with a good level of independence and operational competence.  
  
29–30 and Honors (Lode)Excellent or outstanding evaluation due to complete, in-depth, and well-structured knowledge; the ability to operate independently and flexibly even in complex or new contexts; appropriate, precise, and articulate use of specialized terminology; demonstrates critical thinking, originality, and rigor in both presentation and application.  
Honors (Laude) is awarded for work or performance distinguished by brilliance and originality, exceeding standard curricular expectations.

### Extended programme

PART I: LOW LEVEL REPRESENTATION  
Introduction to the C programming language; numerical coding of data type; low level coding of instructions in synbolic form (assembler MIPS) and numeric form (machine language MIPS); computer architecture of a Risc processor (Risc4000); assembling and compiling .  
  
PART II: C PROGRAMMING LANGUAGE  
Definition of a programming language: syntax, grammar, syntactic tree, BNF, semantics. C programming language: types, variables, and constants, operators and expressions, pointers and arrays, instructions, functions, structured data, standard library, system calls.  
  
PART III: ELEMENTARY DATA STRUCTURE  
List: sequential representation, representation with arrays and indexes, linked representation with pointers; iterative and recursive algorithms.  
  
PART IV: ELEMENTARY ALGORITHMS  
Execution cost and complexity; Search algorithms: sequential search, binary search; Sorting algorithms: sequential sort, bubble sort, merge sort, quicksort. Complexity of a problem: the case of sorting.
