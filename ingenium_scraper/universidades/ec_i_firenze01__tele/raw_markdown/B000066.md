## Learning objectives

The course aims at providing students with the basic knowledge of chemistry essential for understanding the relationships between microscopic structure of matter and macroscopic properties of materials which are essential for addressing the interpretation of chemical-physical phenomena typical of electronic engineering.  
  
With reference to the Dublin Descriptors system:  
  
1.KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
cc1: the mathematical principles and experimental sciences underlying engineering (e.g., knowledge and understanding of the fundamental models and laws of chemistry relevant to electronic engineering).  
cc4: the fundamental principles of chemistry, the chemical-structural characteristics of matter, and the basics of thermochemistry (e.g., the relationships between chemical composition, microscopic structure, and the macroscopic properties of materials, with particular reference to electrical properties; the transformations of matter).  
  
2.APPLYING KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
ca1: applying logical-mathematical analysis methodologies to the study of other scientific-technological disciplines (e.g., the ability to solve mono-disciplinary chemistry problems using the proposed models and laws).  
ca2: extrapolating application-oriented information from analytical results to be used in the study of engineering problems (e.g., the ability to predict the relationships between chemical composition/microscopic structure of matter and the macroscopic properties of materials (including the transformations they may undergo)).  
ca4: writing and balancing chemical equations, performing stoichiometric calculations.  
3. MAKING JUDGEMENTS  
ag1: identifying appropriate calculation methods, procedures for collecting and processing information (measurements, experimental results, laboratory activity outcomes, etc.), and interpreting the resulting data, also through statistical analysis.  
ag3: selecting relevant information in a specific context and formulating independent yet rigorous and technically sound judgements, including reflection on any social, scientific, or ethical issues related to them. (e.g., the ability to identify and critically and independently evaluate the chemical and physical transformations of matter, as well as its properties, linking them to its microscopic characteristics).  
4. LEARNING SKILLS  
ap2: appropriately preparing for subsequent levels of education (Master's degrees) (e.g., the ability to read, consult, and understand a scientific text or document with chemistry content. Utilizing the fundamental chemistry knowledge acquired to approach the study of subsequent disciplines requiring basic knowledge of chemistry).

## Contents

Atoms, ions and molecules.  
Electronic structure of atoms and chemical periodicity.  
Chemical bonding and molecular structure.  
Intermolecular forces and states of matter.  
Solid state. Classification of solids by type of attraction of units.  
Chemical equations and reaction stoichiometry.  
Electrochemistry. Voltaic cells and cell potential. Some commercial voltaic cells. Corrosion. Electrolytic cells. Stoichiometry of electrolysis.  
Electrons in solids.

## Extended programme

Atoms, ions and molecules: the atomic model of matter; subatomic particles; atomic weight, molecular weight, isotopes, mole.  
The electromagnetic radiation; the atomic spectra; the black body radiation and the photoelectric effect; the wave-particle duality and De Broglie's relationship; the Heisenberg uncertainty principle; the wave function; the Schrodinger equation; the particle in the box; the quantization of energy; energy levels; the atomic orbitals and the quantum numbers; the wave function in polar coordinates and its meaning.  
The polyelectronic atoms; the quantum number of spin; the Aufbau principle.  
The periodic table of the elements; periodic quantities.  
Introduction to chemical bonding. The covalent bond; the geometry of molecules (VSEPR theory); VB theory, resonance; pure and polar covalent bonds; dipole moment; polar and apolar molecules;  
Introduction to Molecular Orbitals and Band theories. Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors (valence bond vs band theory models).  
Conductive polymers (elements).  
The ionic bond.  
General characteristics of metals and metallic bond (gas electron theory).  
Intermolecular forces and states of aggregation of matter. The solid state: amorphous solids and crystalline solids. Crystalline lattices and elementary cells. Types of solids and related properties: metallic, ionic, covalent and molecular. Allotropy, polymorphism and isomorphism. Crystal defects (elements).  
Gaseous state and ideal gas law equation. Gas mixtures. Real gases.  
State diagrams.  
Chemical reactions. Reactions in solution (molarity and pH). Oxidation-reduction reactions; oxidation number, balancing redox reactions.  
Electrochemistry. Voltaic cells; the Nernst equation; spontaneity and displacement of redox reactions; oxidation-reduction reactions of water; electrolysis of molten salts and in aqueous solution; Faraday laws. Commercial Voltaic cells. The phenomenon of corrosion. Strategies for corrosion protection.
