Grading System
1st Call
The evaluation of learning in this call will be carried out taking into account the classroom and
non classroom training practices carried out in the laboratory, a final exam to be taken by all
students and the integrated project of the course.
The on-site laboratory training practices will have a weight of 10% of the final grade and their
evaluation will be pass if they have been carried out and fail if they have not been carried out.
The non face-to-face practices must be presented and defended before the professor in
schedules established for this purpose. The deadlines for the delivery of these practices will be
published together with the statements of the same with sufficient time in advance. These
practices will have a valuation between 0 and 10 but with a different weight between them
depending on the degree of difficulty. As a whole they will have a weight of 30% of the final
grade.
The evaluation of the exam will be between 0 and 10. Its weight will be 35% of the final grade.
In this part it will be obligatory to obtain a grade equal or superior to 4 out of 10 to pass the
course.
The Integrated Project will have a weight of 25% (project + oral defense) on the final grade.
Thus, the grade of the course will be the sum of the grades of the on-site and off-site training
practices, the final exam and the integrated project of the course.
In order to pass in this first call, the student must obtain a total grade in the final grade of 5
points out of 10.
2nd Call
In this call, the student may choose to keep the marks of the classroom and non-classroom
training practices (weight of 10%+30%). Likewise, the grade of the integrated project will be
kept if it has been carried out (weight of 25%).
In case of keeping the notes of the practices, a multiple-choice exam with a weight of 35% of
the final grade will be taken. The grade of this exam must be equal or higher than 4 out of 10 to
pass the course.
If the practical notes are not kept, in addition to the aforementioned multiple-choice exam, a
computer-based practical exam must be taken (weight 40%).
In case of not doing an integrated project, the exam has a weight of 50% and the practical
exam/practical exam the other 50% of the grade.
Regulation and levels of use of artificial intelligence (AI):
In the software development sector, tools based on Generative Artificial Intelligence are part of
the current professional ecosystem. However, in the academic context, their use must be
subject to the genuine acquisition of the intended competences and learning outcomes.
For each assessment instrument, activity, assignment or project, teaching staff may specify
which of the following three levels of use is permitted. If no level is specified, Level 2 will be
deemed to apply, except in the case of any in-person assessment, for which the permitted
level will be Level 1:
Level 1 - Use not permitted:
Description: the use of any AI tool is not permitted.
Typical context: assessment tests, in-person examinations, project defences, specific
activities, etc.
Consequences: the use of AI at this level will be considered plagiarism or academic
misconduct and may result in a penalty of up to a mark of 0 for the corresponding
assessment instrument. It may even result in failure of the course in both examination
periods.
Level 2 - Use permitted with simple citation:
Description: the use of AI is permitted solely as a learning aid, but students must
explicitly declare which tool has been used and for what purpose.
The lecturer will expressly establish which uses of AI are permitted in the assessment
activity, such as, for example:
Spelling and grammar correction, formatting, accessibility, translation, ideation,
outlining, organisation, generation of study questions, summaries for personal use and
explanation of concepts, coding, analysis/argumentation, etc.
How to cite: in the corresponding submission, whenever AI is used or any resource not
covered in the course content is included, students must add a section or note stating:
"For the completion of this activity or this specific section, [Name of the AI, e.g. ChatGPT
/ GitHub Copilot / ...] was used for the purpose of [detailed description of how it was
used]."
Citation example: OpenAI. (2026). ChatGPT (GPT-5.5 model) [Large language model].
https://chatgpt.com
Anthropic. (2026). Claude (Sonnet 5) [Large language model]. https://claude.ai
Consequences: undeclared or uncited use of AI at this level will be considered
plagiarism or academic misconduct and may result in a penalty of up to a mark of 0 for
the corresponding assessment instrument.
Level 3 - Use permitted with full traceability (prompt history):
Description: extensive use of AI is permitted in developing the solution. However, in
order to validate the learning process, students must demonstrate how they interacted
with the AI.
How to cite: in addition to citing the tool used, students must attach either a link to
the public history of the conversation with the AI or, alternatively, an accompanying text
document containing the complete history of the prompts (requests) used and the
responses obtained that influenced the final result.
Consequences: the use of AI without providing full traceability at this level will be
considered plagiarism or academic misconduct and may result in a penalty of up to a
mark of 0 for the corresponding assessment instrument.

The use of tools based on artificial intelligence can be useful as support for study and
academic production. However, it is essential to make ethical, critical, and responsible use of
them, bearing in mind that the works you produce must be original so that they contribute to
the personal development of knowledge.
To ensure transparency in their use, always make reference to them by citing all the sources
from which you have taken any type of information. The citation style, for example with
ChatGPT, is APA; for instance: “(OpenAI, 2023).”
Citing does not mean literally copying the texts provided by the AI, because using the texts
generated by the system verbatim, without analysis, reflection, and subsequent elaboration, is
an activity that does not contribute to your learning.
