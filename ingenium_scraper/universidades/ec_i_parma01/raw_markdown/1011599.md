# Experimentation and simulation of energy systems

- Discipline: Macchine a fluido (ING-IND/08)
- Hours: 48 hours of face-to-face activities
- Credits: 6 credits
- Language: course unit in ITALIAN
- Semester info: Second semester

## Learning objectives

At the end of the course the student will have acquired - knowledge relating to the design of the experimentation (even virtual) on Energy Systems and the interpretation of the obtained results; - skills concerning the numerical and experimental characterization of Energy Systems operations; - tools for the implementation, execution and analysis of models for the simulation of Energy Systems and for the interpretation of simulation results.

## Prerequisites

A basic knowledge of fundamentals of Mathematics, Physics Thermodynamics, Fluid Mechanics and Energy Systems is required.

## Course unit content

The course is a complement to the theoretical contents presented in the Energy Systems AB (I Module) course, which is held in the first semester. During the course, energy conversion systems (turbogas, steam plants and internal combustion engines) and their components will be analyzed with IT tools in order to determine their performance as the operating conditions vary. The main problems related to the experimental characterization of energy systems and fluid machines will also be analysed in this framework.

## Full programme

The course is divided into two parts. The first part analyzes the equipment required for experimentation, with a particular focus on the instruments and sensors typically used in test cells, the measurements acquired, and signal processing. Emphasis is placed on the procedures needed to perform test‑bench experiments under both steady‑state and transient conditions. The second part of the course focuses on the use of virtual environments for the simulation and modeling of energy systems. Specifically, several exercises will be proposed to numerically solve engineering problems through physical–mathematical modeling (calculation of thermodynamic transformations, analysis of energy systems). The models developed will then be used to investigate the effect of the main control parameters on system performance; in this way, students will be able to understand their impact through virtual experimentation of the system under study. These data will subsequently be used to generate reports that clearly and technically present the results of the analyses performed.

## Teaching methods

Frontal lessons. Classroom exercises (students are invited to participate with a computer).

## Assessment methods and criteria

The exam is oral and consists of the discussion of the report outlining the exercises proposed in the classroom and carried out independently by the individual student.
