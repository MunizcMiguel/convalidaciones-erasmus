# MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 1

- Discipline: Analisi matematica (MAT/05)
- Hours: 96 hours of face-to-face activities
- Credits: 12 credits
- Language: course unit in ITALIAN
- Semester info: First semester

## Learning objectives

At the end of the course, the student is expected to have acquired knowledge and skills in integral and differential calculus for functions of a real variable. They should also be able to understand how these tools can be applied to solve concrete problems and to handle them confidently. Specifically, the student should: 1) Be familiar with the concept of function/sequence and their limits; use these to understand the notion of derivative and, consequently, be able to locally approximate functions using polynomials. Have a solid understanding of Riemann integration theory, including possible generalizations, and be able to apply it to the computation of integrals. Know various convergence criteria for numerical series and understand their relation to improper integrals. (Knowledge and understanding) 2) Be able to construct and develop logical arguments with a clear identification of assumptions and conclusions. Apply the theoretical knowledge acquired to the solution of concrete problems, including those of intermediate complexity, through extensive classroom practice. Understand its applicability in applied courses. (Applying knowledge and understanding) 3) Evaluate the coherence and correctness of obtained results; analyze appropriate solution strategies for the proposed exercises, based on their own knowledge. (Judgment and decision-making skills) 4) Use formally correct language to communicate the course content in a clear, precise, and concise manner. Lectures and direct interaction with the instructor will support the acquisition of a specific and appropriate scientific vocabulary, enabling the student to communicate clearly and accurately even beyond purely computational contexts. (Communication skills) 5) Independently deepen their knowledge, starting from the foundational material provided in the course, in order to effectively manage the use of additional tools and concepts from mathematical analysis, both in subsequent courses and in individual study. (Learning skills) 6) Begin to develop a flexible mindset that enables them to adapt easily to new problems, work both independently and in groups, acquire new knowledge through self-study, and efficiently retrieve information from available literature and sources.

## Prerequisites

Preliminary requirements: elementary algebra; trigonometry; analytic geometry; rational powers; exponential and logarithm; elementary functions.

## Course unit content

Logic and set theory. Numerical sets. Combinatorial calculus and elementary probability. Real functions. Sequences. Continuous functions. Properties of differentiable functions. Indefinite and definite integral. Numerical series.

## Full programme

Preliminary requirements: elementary algebra; trigonometry; analytic geometry; rational powers; exponential and logarithm; elementary functions. Logic and set theory; equivalence and ordering. Numerical sets: natural numbers and induction principle; combinatorial calculus and elementary probability; integers and rationals; real numbers and supremum; complex numbers and their n-th roots. Real functions: maximum and supremum; monotone, odd and even functions; powers; irrational functions; absolute value; trigonometric, exponential and hyperbolic functions; graphs of the elementary functions and geometric transformations of the same. Sequences: topology; limits and related theorems; monotonic sequences; Bolzano-Weierstrass and Cauchy theorems; basic examples; the Neper number "e"; recursive sequences; complex sequences. Continuous functions: limits of functions; continuity and properties of continuous functions (including intermediate values, Weierstrass theorem); uniform continuity and Heine-Cantor theorem; Lipschitz continuity; infinitesimals. Properties of differentiable functions (including Rolle, Lagrange, Hopital theorems); Taylor expansion (with Peano and Lagrange remainder); graphing a function. Indefinite and definite integral: definition and computation (straightforward, by parts, by change of variables); integral mean and fundamental theorems; Torricelli theorem; generalised integrals: definition and comparison principles. Numerical series: definition, convergence criteria, Leibniz and integral criteria.

## Teaching methods

The course includes 8 hours of classroom teaching per week. Lectures, delivered in the traditional format at the blackboard, present the topics rigorously, always supported by practical examples.

## Assessment methods and criteria

Learning will be assessed through a written exam, preceded by a multiple-choice test to verify basic knowledge, and followed by an oral exam focused mainly on theoretical aspects. The written exam is passed with a grade of 18 or higher. The oral exam will evaluate understanding of the course content, the use of correct mathematical formalism, and appropriate language. The final grade will be a weighted average of the two. The assessment aims to measure achievement of the expected learning outcomes, particularly points 1 to 4.
