# INNOVATIVE METALLIC MATERIALS

- Discipline: Metallurgia (ING-IND/21)
- Hours: 48 hours of face-to-face activities
- Credits: 6 credits
- Language: course unit in ITALIAN
- Semester info: First semester

## Learning objectives

Knowledge and ability to understand: (I Dublin Descriptor) By the end of the course, the student will have developed and integrated his or her basic knowledge of metallic materials suitable for component fabrication using foundry and additive manufacturing technologies. He/she will have gained awareness of issues related to physical metallurgy, relationships between microstructure and mechanical properties of ferrous and non-ferrous alloys. In particular, by the end of the course the student will be able to select appropriate materials and heat treatments for engineering design. Skills: (II Descriptor: Ability to apply knowledge and understanding) Through lessons and in-class exercises related to all program topics, the student will learn how to apply the knowledge gained in a design context. Autonomy of judgment: (III, IV, V Dublin descriptor) The student should be able to critically understand the characteristics and behavior of matter by evaluating its properties and suitability in relation to the intended applications. He/she will also have to process the relevant data to verify the required performance. Communication skills: (III, IV, V Dublin descriptor) At the end of the course the student should have acquired sufficient language skills, at least as regards the specific technological terminology of the teachings.

## Full programme

Basic knowledge of physical metallurgy: Brief introduction to crystallography. Diffusion phenomena correlated to the main heat treatments: I° and II° Fick’s law. Defects within the metallic materials. Phase diagrams: basic characteristics, lever rule, cooling pathways at the thermodynamic equilibrium. Phase diagram of Fe-Fe3C and microstructures. Strengthening mechanisms of the metallic materials. Solidification in conventional and innovative processes. Mechanical characterisation: Tensile test, hardness test, and Charpy impact test. Innovative production process: Metal additive manufacturing: general description, applications and operation of the machine. Process parameters. Advantages and Disadvantages. Steel: Designation. The main microstructures obtained during the main industrial heat treatments. Heat treatments: quenching, annealing, tempering, normalizing. Nitriding and carburizing. Brief description of structural steels, special structural steels, tool steels, and stainless steels. Aluminium alloys: Designations. Main aluminium alloys: microstructure, mechanical properties and applications. Production of aluminium. Main heat treatments and effects induced by cold deformation. Additive Manufacturing. Analysis of application cases and selection criteria for metallic materials: During the course, various application cases will be presented, in which the selection of metallic materials will be carried out based on the required mechanical properties, the component’s function, and the choice of suitable treatments to meet specific operating conditions at room and high temperatures.

## Teaching methods

The knowledge and skills will be acquired by the students through frontal lessons using the projection of slides.

## Assessment methods and criteria

The evaluation of learning takes place through an assessment of knowledge structured by a written exam and a possible oral exam. The written exam (2h) is structured by 4 open questions. The exam will be passed if the total score is equal to or higher than 18/33. The vote “30 cum laude” will be given if the total score is higher than 30/33 and if the precise vocabulary is used. The student may choose to take an oral exam to improve (or worsen) the grade of the written exam (if the total score is higher than 18/33). If the student reaches a total score of 16/33 in the written exam, he/she will still be able to pass the exam by taking an oral exam.
