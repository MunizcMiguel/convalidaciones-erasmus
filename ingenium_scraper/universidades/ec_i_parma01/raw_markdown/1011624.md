# ENERGETIC SYSTEM AB (II MODULO)

- Discipline: Macchine a fluido (ING-IND/08)
- Hours: 48 hours of face-to-face activities
- Credits: 6 credits
- Language: course unit in ITALIAN
- Semester info: Second semester

## Learning objectives

This module aims to provide in-depth knowledge and skills (i.e., the ability to apply knowledge) necessary to assess the real-world operating characteristics and performance of energy systems, with particular emphasis on most commonly used the power plants (gas turbines, Internal Combustion Engines, and steam plants). To develop deeper skills in assessing the performance of any thermal power plant for energy generation and conversion, students are required to demonstrate the achievement of the following objectives by the end of the module. Knowledge and understanding: At the end of the course, students will be able to understand the real operating characteristics and performance of power generation systems and the main prime movers used today. They will also have acquired specific knowledge for assessing real thermodynamic transformations, for calculating thermodynamic parameters and heat and work exchanges with reference to real cases. They will therefore be familiar with the problems, limitations, advantages, and disadvantages of the considered power plants and their main components in real operating conditions. Applying knowledge and understanding: The student will be able to calculate the performance parameters of engines, with particular reference to efficiency and specific fuel consumption. They will also be able to define the behavior of real thermodynamic transformations occurring in power plants and their components in order to qualitatively and quantitatively evaluate the thermodynamic states and real transormations, as well as identify solutions to improve performance. Making judgments: The student will have tools and knowledge needed to critically evaluate and compare different solutions for implementing real energy conversion processes in power systems, taking into account thereal behavior of transformations and processes, while also considering possible alternatives. Communication skills: The student will gain the tools required to effectively present evaluations and comparisons of different solutions for implementing energy conversion processes in power plants, through both graphical and numerical representations of the real thermodynamic transformations and cycles, as well as the main performance parameters commonly used. Learning skills: Using the knowledge acquired in the course, students will be able to independently get information, characteristics, and technical data on Energy Systems that can be studied and/or available on the market, thus improving own skills on the solutions proposed and/or applied for the implementation and management of Energy Systems.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of fundamentals of Mathematics, Physics, Thermodynamics and Fluid Mechanics is required.

## Course unit content

Energy Systems and Fluid Machines: classification and components. Review of Thermodynamics. Fundamental equations: mass and energy conservation; momentum and moment of momentum equations. Application to fluid flow in fixed and moving channels. Ideal, Limit, and Real thermodynamic cycles. Overview of Energy costs and utilization factors. Compressible Fluid Machines. Compression and expansion processes on thermodynamic diagrams; compression and expansion efficiencies. Total and stagnation quantities. Fluid Flow in fixed channels: converging channels, converging-diverging channels. Velocity and pressure trends, sonic and choking conditions. Steam Engines. Real Cycles: thermodynamic transformations in real Rankine cycles with saturated and superheated steam; performance calculation for reheated and regenerated steam plants in real operation. Steam generators: types and schemes; efficiency. Superheaters and economizers. Air preheaters. Condenser and cooling towers. Reciprocating internal combustion engines (ICEs). Limit thermodynamic cycles: an overview of calculation methodology. Real thermodynamic cycles: trends of real transformations and main causes of deviation from the ideal case. Indicated cycle and calculation of effective power in internal combustion engines. Mean effective pressure. Volumetric efficiency. Overview of combustion processes. Air and fuel supply processes in ICEs. Mechanical supercharging and turbocharging. Characteristic curves. Gas turbines: thermodynamic processes in the Joule cycle in the limit and real cases. Real thermodynamic cycles on the (h, s) and (T, s) diagrams; effective specific work, effective power, and thermodynamic efficiency in limit and real Joule cycle. Effect of compression ratio and maximum cycle temperature. Turbine inlet temperature (TIT) and air-fuel ratio: effects on efficiency. Optimization of the real Joule cycle with reference to specific work and efficiency.

## Full programme

Energy Systems and Fluid Machines: characteristics and classification. Internal and External Combustion Plants. Components of Energy Systems. Thermodynamics reminders: closed and open systems, Heat Exchangers and Fluid Machinery. Fundamental equations: mass and energy conservation; momentum and moment of momentum equations. Application of conservation equations to fluid flow in fixed and moving channels. Thermodynamic cycles and pseudo-cycles: thermodynamic transformations, work and heat exchange calculation. Ideal, Limit, and Real Cycles. Overview on energy cost and utilization coefficient. Compressible Fluid Machines. Compression and expansion transformations on thermodynamic diagrams; real and ideal transformations. Compression and expansion efficiencies, recovery and counter-recovery phenomena. Intercooled and post-combustion compression. total and stagnation quantities. Fluid flow in fixed-wall ducts: converging ducts, converging-diverging ducts. Evaluation of velocity and pressure trends with a 1-D approach. Sonic and choking conditions. Valves and pressure reducers. Notes on fluid flow in moving channels. Steam power plants: overview of the most common system configurations. Real cycles for steam power plants: transformations in real Rankine cycles with saturated and superheated steam. Regeneration and reheating: real thermodynamic cycles on the (h, s) and (T, s) diagrams. Performance calculation of simple, reheated, and regenerated steam power plants in real situations. Steam generators: types and basic schemes. Steam generator efficiency: direct and indirect calculations. Superheaters and economizers. Air preheaters. The condenser and cooling towers. The deaerator. Reciprocating internal combustion engines (ICEs). Review of operating principles and fundamental components. Limit thermodynamic cycles: an overview of calculation methodology. Real thermodynamic cycles: evolution of real transformations and main causes of deviation from the ideal case. Time losses, heat exchange, pressure drops through intake and exhaust valves and throttle valve. Indicated cycle and calculation of effective power in ICE. Mean effective pressure. Volumetric efficiency. Overview of combustion processes in spark-ignition and Diesel engines. Air and fuel supply processes in ICEs: intake and exhaust systems and polar diagram of ideal and real valves timing. Mechanical supercharging and turbocharging. Characteristic curves of internal combustion engines. Gas turbines: an overview of the most common configurations. Real cycles for gas turbine power plants: thermodynamic transformations in the Joule cycle in the limit and real cases. Real thermodynamic cycles on the (h, s) and (T, s) diagrams. Calculation of effective specific work, effective power, and thermodynamic efficiency in limit and real Joule cycle. Effect of compression ratio and maximum cycle temperature. Calculation of turbine inlet temperature (TIT) and air-fuel ratio values: effects on efficiency and problems arising from material resistance. Introduction to blade cooling. Introduction to combustion chamber geometry. Optimization of the real Joule cycle with reference to specific work and efficiency.

## Teaching methods

Teaching activities will be developed through classroom lectures, during which the course topics will be addressed both theoretically, to promote understanding of the topics presented (connecting them where appropriate with the student's existing knowledge), and practically, through the presentation of quantitative examples of specific power plants and the calculation procedures that lead to the numerical evaluation of the plants real operating characteristics, performance parameters, and the real thermodynamic transformations. The practical applications presented will be aimed at enabling students to acquire the necessary familiarity with units of measurement, the quantitative calculation of real thermodynamic transformations, and the performance evaluation of the considered power plants. The activities described will be primarily developed in the classroom, with the aim of allowing students to actively follow the procedures and methodologies employed. The teacher is available for clarifications and explanations by appointment (to be arranged via email) both in person and online (through the Teams platform).

## Assessment methods and criteria

The assessment of learning is carried out through the final exam only, which ensures the acquisition of knowledge and skills (i.e. acquisition of learning outcomes) through an oral interview. The oral exam consists in two questions on the topics of the course and on the application of the theory to original problems: in particular, critical thinking skills, presentation skills, the ability to correlate the topics covered and to perform numerical calculations to quantitatively evaluate parameters and quantities of interest in specific problems will be evaluated. Each question is assessed on a scale from 0 to 30. To pass the exam a partial score of at least 18/30 IS REQUIRED FOR BOTH QUESTIONS. The final score is obtained by calculating the arithmetic mean of the scores of the two questions. The final score is communicated at the end of the exam.
