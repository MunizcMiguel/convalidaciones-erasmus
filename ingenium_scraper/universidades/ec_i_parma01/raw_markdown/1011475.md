# MACHINE DESIGN A

- Discipline: Progettazione meccanica e costruzione di macchine (ING-IND/14)
- Hours: 48 hours of face-to-face activities
- Credits: 6 credits
- Language: course unit in ITALIAN
- Semester info: First semester

## Learning objectives

Knowledge and understanding: At the end of this course the student should know the basics of machine design. He should gain understanding of the many factors affecting the design of mechanical parts with special regards to safety and reliability. He should be familiar with design schemes and checks required to analyze machine parts. Applying knowledge and understanding: He should be able to design a mechanical part on the basis of suitable material and process selection, load analysis and functional requirements. He should be able to select from catalogues the main mechanical components found in machines. He should be able to draw and dimension the part for production. Making judgments: By the end of the course the student should be able to critically analyze machines and mechanical components and to their functions and requisites. Communication skills: The student should be able to clearly present the functioning of mechanical devices and the design issues involved.

## Prerequisites

It will be very useful to recall the following concepts: - Physics 1: units of measurement, forces and moments in a plane, vector representation, graphical resolution of static equilibrium - Machine drawing: views, dimensioning, dimensional and geometric tolerances, bearing assembly - Mechanical technology: machining technologies, heat treatment of steel - Metallurgy: metal alloys and their applications, mechanical testing - Structural science: beam theory, mass geometry, de Saint-Venant theory - Rational mechanics: kinematics, rigid body dynamics, kinematic mechanisms, and force calculations

## Course unit content

The course covers the principles of mechanical design applied to machine components, starting with the strength characteristics of commonly used materials and the concept of safety. It then introduces methods and models for the schematization of acting loads and the calculation and sizing of components. A key aspect is the introduction to strength testing under fatigue loads.

## Full programme

Introduction to Construction Design, Safety, Factor of Safety with Statistical Reliability Concepts, Technical Standards, Mechanical Testing of Materials, Tensile, Hardness, Impact, Fatigue, Fracture Testing, Metallic Materials (Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, Magnesium, Titanium Alloys), Non-Metallic Materials (Polymers and Ceramics), Manufacturing Technologies, Selection Criteria. Load Analysis, Equilibrium, Application of the Beam Model in Mechanics, Normal Force, Bending, Shear, and Torsion. Stresses and Strains. Mohr Circles. Principal Stresses, Strength Criteria (Tresca, Von Mises, Rankine), Ductility and Brittleness. Compressive Buckling and Eulerian Loading. Stiffness. Highly Curvatured Beams. Notched Geometries, Nominal and Local Stresses, Stress Concentration Factor Kt. Conformal and Non-Conformal Contact, Specific Contact Pressure, Hertzian Theory, Stresses in Contact between Elementary Bodies. Thermal Expansion. Fatigue of Metallic Materials, Characteristics of the Phenomenon, Fatigue Testing, Stress Limit fatigue, S/N curves, fatigue parameter estimates, influence factors, mean stress effect, fatigue damage accumulation, multiaxial stress effect, notch sensitivity, Notch effect factor Kf. Contact fatigue, pitting, fretting.

## Teaching methods

Course topics are presented through lectures and numerical exercises carried out in the classroom. Problems are proposed to be carried out at home with subsequent discussion in the classroom. Groups of students are also stimulated to analyse and study a real mechanical device.

## Assessment methods and criteria

The examination consists of a written test, which must be passed with a minimum score of 18/30, followed by an oral test. In the written exam, students will be required to solve an exercise concerning the design of a simple mechanical system, such as a lifting device, a pressure vessel or a drive shaft, considering the main factors that determine the safety of the system, such as the choice of the most suitable materials, durability, or compliance with regulations. In order to pass the written exam, it is essential to correctly perform the load analysis by means of static resolution and the determination of internal actions. In the oral exam, two theoretical topics are usually given for discussion, and a request may be made for an analysis of the failure of a mechanical component such as a spring, a drive shaft or a gear, for which possible improvements must also be provided. The final mark will be the sum of the marks for the two tests; admission to the oral exam may be granted with a mark of 17 with an asterisk, which means that a good oral performance is expected given the extreme criticality of the written exam. This exam structure is reserved for students whose study plan includes only Module I (Machine Construction A, 6 credits), while for students who opt for Module II (Machine Construction AB, 12 credits), i.e. for the Design curriculum, learning will be assessed in a single final examination for Module II, for which it is (obviously) essential to be able to solve the exercises in Module I. IMPORTANT: For students under the previous teaching system (up to the academic year 24/25), in which only Machine Construction (9 CFU) appeared in the study plan, the examination methods and content remain unchanged (written test, passing grade 18/30, oral test).
