## Learning outcomes, curriculum content
LEARNING OUTCOMES
The aim of the course is to provide students with a comprehensive
### Learning Objective: understanding and practical skills in applying numerical
methods—primarily the finite element method (FEM), the finite
volume method (FVM), and computational fluid dynamics
(CFD)—to solve problems in structural mechanics, fluid
mechanics, and fluid-structure interaction (FSI). Students will
learn the theoretical foundations of FEM through analysis of bar
structures, perform linear and non-linear static, thermal, and
modal analyses, and acquire the ability to model and simulate
coupled mechanical and fluid systems. The course emphasizes the
use of modern computational tools for solving complex
engineering problems involving solid and fluid domains, as well
as their mutual interactions.
The course integrates theoretical foundations with applied
computational methods in mechanics, focusing on the finite
element method (FEM), computational fluid dynamics (CFD), and
fluid-structure interaction (FSI). The content of the course is
aligned with current scientific research and reflects state-of-the-art
developments in numerical simulations of mechanical systems.
Lectures:
Fundamentals of the finite element method (FEM): discretization
principles, shape functions, stiffness matrices, boundary
conditions, equilibrium and compatibility.
Types and properties of finite elements: 1D (bar, beam), 2D (plane
stress, plane strain), and 3D elements.
FEM applications in structural mechanics: trusses, beams, frames.
Formulation of global stiffness matrices and coordinate
transformations.
Modeling errors and sources of numerical inaccuracy in FEM.
Advanced FEM topics: linear vs. non-linear analysis, modal
analysis, stress-thermal coupling, and contact problems.
Introduction to fluid mechanics: conservation equations (mass,

Turbulence modeling and discretization techniques in CFD (FVM

Solving Navier–Stokes equations for incompressible flows, with

Fundamentals of fluid-structure interaction (FSI): dimensional
analysis, coupling regimes (weak and strong), slow vs. fast flow

Overview and comparison of commercial and open-source

CFD, OpenFOAM.

modal analyses.

mesh resolutions.
