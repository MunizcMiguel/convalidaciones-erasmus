## Learning outcomes, curriculum content
LEARNING OUTCOMES
To provide students with knowledge of the basic physical laws
governing the phenomena of nature and forming the basis for the
functioning of technical devices. To form in students the ability to
independently solve tasks involving the application of the laws of
physics and mathematical methods to describe selected
phenomena and mechanical, thermodynamic, optical and
### Learning Objective:
electrical systems.
To develop in students the ability to work in the laboratory, to
perform measurements of various physical quantities, to develop
and present measurement data, to perform calculations of
measurement uncertainty and to verify theoretical models on the
basis of experiment.
The entire physics course - lecture, classes and laboratory - is
related to basics of scientific research.
The laboratory and some of the examples solved during the lecture
and classes are related to the formation of practical skills.
Curricular content is in accordance with the learning outcomes
and takes into account the current knowledge and its application
within the disciplines related to the conducted course.
LECTURE [W1, W2, U1]:
Kinematics: classification of motions, use of vectors to describe
multidimensional motions. Dynamics: types of forces and
interactions in nature; laws of dynamics; work and energy;
dynamics of a system of point masses; laws of conservation of
energy and momentum. Gravity. Rotational motion of a rigid body.
Microscopic structure of matter. Vibrational and wave motion.
Elements of thermodynamics and kinetic theory of gases.
Electrostatics: charge and electric field; capacitors; electrical
properties of matter. Electric current: Ohm's law; direct current
circuits and Kirchhoff's laws; power sources. Magnetic field.
Electromagnetic induction. Electrical and magnetic properties of
matter. Alternating current. Maxwell's laws and electromagnetic
waves. Fundamentals of geometrical and wave optics. Elements of
modern physics: waves and particles, elements of quantum physics
and solid state and nuclear physics, elements of relativistic
### Curriculum Content: physics.
Total: 30 [h].
CLASSES [W1, W2, U1]:
Rectilinear motion (uniform, uniformly accelerated and
decelerated). Curvilinear motion. Fundamentals of the dynamics
of a material point and a system of point masses, the composition
of forces, friction, inertia forces. Momentum, work, energy,
power, laws of conservation, collisions. Kinematics and dynamics
of rotational motion of a rigid body, moment of inertia. Simple
harmonic motion and wave motion. Elements of thermodynamics:
perfect gas, heat balance, first law of thermodynamics, energy
transformations. Electrostatic field. Electrical capacitance.
Connecting capacitors. Energy of a capacitor. DC circuits -
Kirchhoff's laws. Electrical resistance. Ohm's law. Power of
current. Magnetic field of conductors with current. Interaction of
conductors with magnetic field. Electromagnetic induction.
Alternating current circuits. Calculus checks.
Total: 30 [h].
LABORATORY [W2, U1, U2, U3, K1]:
Introductory classes: division of students into teams, assignment
of exercises; discussion of measurement uncertainty calculus and
principles of measurement data processing and presentation
(including - using a computer), as well as health and safety rules.
Subsequent activities: students perform laboratory exercises in
teams (5 exercises selected from the following list):
* Study of the spring pendulum.
* Study of a physical pendulum.
* Archimedes' law and determination of density of bodies.
* Determination of the coefficient of dynamic viscosity of
liquids.
* Thermoelectric phenomenon and the phenomenon of melting.
* Current-voltage characteristics of two-wire elements.
* Determination of the thermal coefficient of resistance for a
conductor.
* Determination of capacitance of a capacitor and study of
charging and discharging processes of a capacitor.
* Determination of the value and distribution of magnetic
induction in the gap between the poles of an electromagnet using
a hallotron.
* Testing a serial RLC circuit using a two-channel oscilloscope.
* Determination of the speed of sound in air using the
oscilloscope method.
* Determination of the focal distance of thin lenses.
* Determination of the diffraction grating constant and study of
the optical spectrum of an electric discharge lamp.
Total: 30 [h].
