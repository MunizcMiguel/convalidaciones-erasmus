## Learning objectives

The course’s main objective is to provide an overview of the problems and solutions encountered in the use of sensors for robotic applications. It includes practical experiments with sensors and systems commonly found in the work of a robotics engineer.

The course will also introduce some estimators and the tools to evaluate their performance. Examples of applications of these techniques to real or realistic systems will be presented throughout the course.

By the end of the course, the student will be able to:

- Understand the operation, limitations, and applications of the main robotic sensors.

- Integrate sensors into a robotic system, both at the hardware and software level.

- Design sensor fusion and state estimation algorithms.

- Analyze sensory data and use them for navigation, perception, and control.

## Prerequisites

Basic concepts of control, programming, and electronics.

## Contents

The course aims to provide students with knowledge and skills related to the use of sensors and measurement systems in robotic contexts, both mobile and industrial. Topics will include sensors for environmental perception, state estimation, and physical interaction, along with methods for sensor fusion, calibration, filtering, and real-time use. Fundamental concepts regarding measurement and uncertainty evaluation will be introduced in relation to robotics. Knowledge will be consolidated through simulations and data acquisition from real robotic and sensing systems. Specifically, the course will cover: - Introduction to sensing systems for robotics: classification of sensors (active/passive, absolute/incremental), performance parameters (accuracy, resolution, precision), basic concepts of measurement and uncertainty evaluation, hardware and interfacing architectures. - Position, velocity, and orientation sensors: optical and magnetic encoders, IMUs (accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers), inertial measurements, calibration. - Environmental perception: distance sensors (ultrasonic, infrared, LiDAR, ToF), computer vision (RGB, stereo, and RGB-D cameras), introduction to computer vision for robotics, localization and mapping (maps, landmarks, loop closure). - Sensor fusion and state estimation: least squares, Kalman filters, EKF, localization and SLAM algorithms, multi-sensor integration. - Sensors for interaction and control. - Exercises and laboratory sessions: implementation of pipelines for real and simulated sensors, basic use of ROS/ROS2 for data acquisition and management, experiments and exercises with real sensors (IMU, LiDAR, cameras, encoders) and on a robotic platform.

## Teaching methods

Students are strongly encouraged to attend the frontal lectures (about 50 hours), delivered using prepared teaching material (slides, notes, exercises), made available before class on the Esse3 portal. Classroom exercises and activities (about 10 hours) will complement the presentation of topics with realistic examples.

During lectures, the instructor will encourage active student participation through discussions and open questions to stimulate engagement. Students will consolidate the concepts through personal study of the slides and suggested textbooks.

## Assessment methods

To successfully pass the exam, students must complete a project and an oral examination.

The project, to be carried out in groups of two or three, will involve practical activities with real sensors and robots, or simulated sensing systems implemented in Python. The project must be accompanied by a short 3–4 page report.

The oral exam will be an open discussion on one or two topics from the course.

The final grade will be based on a weighted evaluation of the project (40%) and the oral exam (60%).
