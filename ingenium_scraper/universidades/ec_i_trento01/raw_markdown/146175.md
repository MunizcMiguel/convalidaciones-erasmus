## Learning objectives

The course on Fundamentals of Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer primarily focuses on the analysis of the thermodynamic systems, both closed and open, with particular attention to the mass and energy balances.

The fundamental aim of the course is to familiarize with the engineering issues of the thermodynamics and heat transfer topics, to understand and describe the basic modelling approach, to develop a systematic "problem solving" technique with particular attention to the importance of the numerical calculation and to the significance of the figures. From an extended point of view the objective of this course is to pursue a general energy management approach to analyse and investigate the role of the energy analysis in the industrial systems, energy plants, devices and processes.

## Prerequisites

The student is required to have a solid background in basic disciplines such as mathematics and physics following the issues acquired attending the basic mathematics and physics courses. There is a need for the ability to process specific mathematical formulations characteristic of the engineering disciplines proposed during the course and to understand the physical meaning of the equations/formulations presented, which, in the context of this teaching, are related to the concepts of mass and energy balance of a system, the study of thermophysical properties of fluids, the formulation of appropriate thermodynamic transformations jointly with the solutions of issues related to the energy efficiency of processes involved in to industrial systems

## Contents

The teaching program includes two macro-areas of technical/scientific disciplines that, in the background of the engineering topics, can be traced back to the following themes:

- fundamental principles and applications of Thermodynamics;

- heat transfer process and problems.

Topics regarding the THERMODYNAMICS section.

Introduction and definitions. Thermodynamic Systems: closed and open systems. First Principle: First Principle for simple and cyclic transformations, specific heat, conservation of mass and energy. Thermodynamic processes. Thermodynamic diagrams. State conditions for liquid and gaseous systems, saturation conditions.

Ideal gases: generality and equation of state; transformations of ideal gases. Pure substances: generality and diagrams (p, v, T), (p, T) and (p, v) title of the vapor and liquid-vapor mixtures. Second Principle: statements; reversibility and not reversible conditions , Carnot cycle, thermodynamic of reversible cycles. The entropy quantity: its significance and properties as function of state.

Standard Air cycles: Otto, Diesel, Brayton-Joule and steam cycles. The Rankine cycle with saturated and super-heated steam. Inverse cycles (refrigeration cycles and heat pumps).

Topics regarding the HEAT TRANSFER section.

Heat transfer exchange mode. Introduction and definitions. Conduction. The Fourier's law. Thermal conductivity. The Fourier equation. Steady state conduction with and without heat generation.

Convection mode: generality, forced and natural convection. Elements of dimensional analysis and correlations for the heat transfer coefficient calculation including example applications. Transmittance of a wall and the global heat transfer coefficient. Heat exchangers and the method of mean effective temperatures. Fundamentals of radiation heat transfer.

## Teaching methods

Lessons will be held exclusively in presence. Lectures will be conducted with the aid of both a blackboard and a projector to integrate the material presented on the board. They will also be supported by electronic materials (PPT, PDF, etc.) provided by the instructor. The sections (chapters) to be consulted in the reference texts listed in the appropriate section will also be indicated. Two laboratory experiences are also scheduled, which will be presented by the Teacher at the appropriate time and organized with the POVO teaching laboratory professors. The dates for these experiences will also be announced during the course based on laboratory availability. The instructor will provide instructions for presenting and discussing the experiences, which are fundamental part of the final exam.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the course includes a written test that consists of both numerical exercises and oral questions (multiple-choice questions and/or demonstrations). The candidate who passes the written test will have access to the oral interview, which will be particularly focused on discussing and analyzing the laboratory experiences that must be submitted to the teacher in advance. During the interview, topics related to the second part of the course (heat transfer) may be asked. The discussion is conducted by each student (not in groups). The date of the discussion will be indicated in the communication simultaneously with the publication of the schedule for all tests. Any changes to the date/time of the oral exam will be communicated by the teacher in correspondence to the publication of the written test results. The oral exam is an integral part of the overall assessment of the course. The written test is worth 75% of the overall result, while the remaining 25% pertains to the evaluation of laboratory experiences. Students who have passed the written test must take the oral exam in the same session as the written test. Failure to register for the oral exam results in the cancellation of the written exam. The following exemption is provided: since the course takes place in the second semester, the student who has passed the written exam at the June session can postpone the oral interview to the July session (no later). This exemption expires with the autumn session. All students must strictly send the reports of their laboratory experiences to the teacher by the registration deadline for the July oral exam.
