## Learning objectives

This course will study the science and technology of glasses and binding materials. For each class of materials, the structure/composition, the most important engineering properties, and the production and processing technologies will be briefly reviewed.

For each class of materials, laboratory tests will be carried out aimed at the practical demonstration of the theories presented in class. The student will acquire basic skills in conducting experimental laboratory activities, data processing, and analytical techniques useful for characterizing inorganic materials.

By the end of the course, the student will have acquired knowledge of the processing methods and engineering properties of vitreous (glass) materials and binders.

## Prerequisites

Chemistry, Materials Science and Technology

## Contents

The course will consist primarily of experimental activities, complemented by a number of theoretical short lectures aimed at reviewing the concepts required to carry out the experiments. Students will be expected to actively and proactively contribute to the definition of the laboratory activities, the processing conditions of the materials, and their characterization. The activities will focus on one or more of the following topics: (i) production and characterization of low-melting-point and low-environmental-impact glasses; (ii) techniques for improving the chemical or mechanical resistance of glass components; (iii) low-environmental-impact binders (geopolymers).

## Teaching methods

Short classroom lectures and laboratory sessions.

## Assessment methods

Students will be required to prepare an oral presentation on the laboratory activities, which will be discussed during the exam.
