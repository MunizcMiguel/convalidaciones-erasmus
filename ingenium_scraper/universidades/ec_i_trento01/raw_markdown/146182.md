## Learning objectives

The educational objective of the course is to provide students with fundamental knowledge of heat transfer processes, which are increasingly playing a significant role in both traditional industrial applications and, in particular, advanced ones such as mechatronics and electronics applications in industrial systems. The expected outcomes include the acquisition of the ability to analyze inherent technical issues and apply the fundamental concepts of the disciplines indicated, and the development of a systematic problem-solving technique that includes the ability to assess the importance of numerical computations in the proposed problems.

## Prerequisites

Students must have acquired a good level of proficiency in basic disciplines such as mathematical analysis, physics, and chemistry. They must also have developed strong problem-solving skills and analytical abilities from a physical-mathematical and systemic perspective (system balances and their mathematical formulation, equation solving methodologies). With regard to the required prerequisites, each student is responsible for ensuring that they have acquired the required prerequisites.

## Contents

The course begins with an introduction to concepts related to units of measurement, thermodynamic systems (closed and open), and the first and second laws of thermodynamics. Some notable thermodynamic transformations and their corresponding thermodynamic diagrams will also be presented. Cycle analysis, with particular emphasis on inverse cycles, will be covered. Most of the course will be devoted to heat exchange processes. Heat exchange modes. General information and definitions. Conduction. Fourier's law. Thermal conductivity. The Fourier equation. Steady-state conduction with and without heat generation. Electrothermal analogy. Convection: general information, forced and natural convection. Dimensional analysis. Forced and natural convection: general information and specific application cases. Wall transmittance and overall heat transfer coefficient. Heat exchangers and sizing analysis using the mean effective temperature difference method. Heat exchange by radiation. Applications related to heat exchange processes in electronic and industrial equipment.

## Teaching methods

The course includes a total of 60 hours of in-person instruction. The course includes classroom presentations and explanations, including the use of teaching supports provided in electronic format (PPT, PDF, etc.), which will also include the reference texts currently listed in the appropriate section of this format. There are no group activities, except for laboratory activities, which will be conducted in groups of 3/4 people. The organization of the laboratory experiences will be presented by the instructor during the course.

## Assessment methods

The course assessment focuses on both theoretical and numerical assignments. There are no ongoing tests. The exam will be written only and will include both numerical questions (exercises) and oral questions (multiple choice and/or demonstrations). If laboratory experiments are performed during the course, candidates who pass the written exam will be admitted to an oral exam, which will be dedicated to analyzing the laboratory work submitted to the instructor. Written assignments are not valid for one year. Therefore, the laboratory work must be discussed on the interview date scheduled for the written exam.

The written assignment accounts for 80% of the overall exam; the remaining 20% concerns the evaluation of the laboratory work. As per the calendar, there are five exam sessions distributed across three sessions (summer, fall, and winter). The overall grade will be recorded online. Registration for the written/oral exam is mandatory; failure to do so will result in exclusion from the exam.
