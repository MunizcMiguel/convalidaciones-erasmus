## Learning objectives

PART 1: ANALOG ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS This part of the course introduces the scientific and engineering foundations of electronic systems for the acquisition and processing of analog signals. In particular, the main topics of the course include: an introduction to fundamental electronic devices, the basics of analog electronic circuits. Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to: - understand the operation of elementary electronic devices (diodes and MOSFETs); - analyze the operation of simple electronic circuits containing diodes, MOSFETs, and operational amplifiers; PART 2: DIGITAL ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS This part of the course covers the methodological and engineering fundamentals of both digital electronic systems and computing systems. Specifically, the course main topics are: logic networks, digital electronic systems, and basic computer architectures. After successfully completing the course, the student will be able to: - understand the working principles of a digital electronic circuit; - analyse the processing algorithm performed by a combinatorial network; - understand the processing algorithm performed by a sequential network; - understand the architecture of a microprocessor-based system. PART 3: ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS LABORATORY By successfully completing the laboratory exercises, the student will be able to independently use the materials and instrumentation available in the laboratory to assemble simple analog and digital electronic circuits and perform measurements on them.

## Prerequisites

For a successful learning of the course contents, knowledge of the following concepts is required:

- Math: basics of differential calculus

- Electrical circuits: basic electrical bipoles, Kirchhoff’s laws, electrical circuits analysis, use of operational amplifiers in linear networks

- Informatics: basics of programming.

## Contents

* The order of the presented topics may vary from what is shown below.

**PART 1**:**ANALOG ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS**

Diodes: characteristics and examples of circuit applications. Photodiodes, photovoltaic cells, and LEDs.

MOSFET transistors: characteristics and applications in analog and digital circuits.

Signal processing circuits with operational amplifiers.

Op Amp with positive feedback: waveform generators.

**PART 2**: **DIGITAL ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS**

Combinatorial networks. Half adder and full adder. Decoder. Multiplexer. Asynchronous sequential networks. SR latch. Synchronous sequential networks. Flip-flops. Performance of digital electric circuits. Digital electronic technology evolution. Microprocessor-based systems. Von Neuman architecture and Harvard architecture. CPU, memory and I/O unit main features. Functional units communication: handshaking. System bus performance. CPU microarchitecture. Instruction cycle. CPU registers and data path. Instruction set architecture. CISC and RISC instructions. Memory hierarchy. I/O activities: programmed, interrupt, DMA. Hardware accelerators for machine learning.

**PARTE 3: ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS LABORATORY**

Assembly and analysis of simple analog and digital circuits.

Use of basic electronic instrumentation: power supplies, function generators, multimeters, oscilloscopes.

## Teaching methods

To optimize the learning activity, students are recommended to attend the lectures, which are held through a combination of board/i-Pad explanations and slides. Exercises solved during the lectures are an integral part of the course content. Students are strongly encouraged to study the course material (mostly slides) available on the course’s Moodle page and the notes taken during the lectures.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the students’ knowledge and understanding of the presented contents takes place through a single written test composed of: - PART 1: two short exercises and one open question on the material covered in class, aimed at verifying the level of knowledge. Each short exercise is worth up to 4 points, and the open-ended question up to 6 points. The maximum score for Part 1 is therefore 14 points. - PART 2: two open questions about the course contents, aimed at checking the student’s level of knowledge. A maximum score of 7 points is assigned to each question. The time allotted to the written test is 2 hours. If the written test is scored at least 18/30, with at least 6 points in each part of the course, the exam is passed. If the score of the written test is below 18/30, the exam is not passed and must be repeated in a subsequent exam session. In the final exam, an additional score is awarded to students who attend at least 75% of the laboratory activities and submit a short report on the work carried out at the end of the sessions. The maximum score for this part is 3 points, which are added to those obtained in the written exam to determine the final grade. For students unable to attend the laboratory sessions, the corresponding teaching material will be made available, and an additional question on this material will be included in the written exam, with a maximum score of 3 points. At the student’s request, the assessment may be supplemented by a short oral exam, which normally contributes up to 2 points, positive or negative, to the final grade. Two mid-term written tests are scheduled, which, if passed, replace the written exam of the final test.
