## Learning objectives

The course provides students with fundamental knowledge of the main manufacturing processes used in mechanical engineering. In particular, the course introduces the principles, characteristics and applications of bulk and sheet metal forming processes, machining processes, non-conventional manufacturing processes, joining processes and surface treatments.

The aim of the course is to develop the students’ ability to understand how manufacturing processes work, identify their main control parameters, evaluate their advantages and application limits, and relate technological choices to material properties, product quality and component requirements. Through examples, exercises and applied case studies, the course also aims to provide students with the basic tools needed to address simple problems related to the selection and analysis of manufacturing processes.

At the end of the course, students will be able to:

**Knowledge and understanding**

- describe the operating principles of the main manufacturing processes;
- recognise the fundamental characteristics of forming processes, machining processes, non-conventional processes, joining processes and surface treatments;
- explain the role of the main process parameters and equipment used in different manufacturing technologies;
- understand the relationships between the manufacturing process, the material being processed, product quality and component performance.

**Applying knowledge and understanding**

- identify the most suitable manufacturing process for simple mechanical components, according to material, geometry, tolerances, required quality and production volume;
- analyse the effects of the main process parameters on product quality and process efficiency;
- solve simple applied problems related to manufacturing processes through numerical examples, exercises and case studies;
- compare different manufacturing solutions by highlighting their advantages, limitations and fields of application.

**Making judgements**

- critically evaluate the suitability of a manufacturing process with respect to the technical requirements of a component;
- recognise the main technological, production-related and material constraints that influence process selection;
- interpret the results of simple analyses or applied case studies in order to justify a basic technological choice.

**Communication skills**

- describe the main manufacturing processes and their applications clearly and using appropriate technical terminology;
- present and discuss simple solutions to mechanical technology problems, justifying the choices made.

**Learning skills**

- use teaching materials, applied examples and exercises to independently consolidate the knowledge acquired;
- connect the course contents with other subjects in the mechanical and industrial engineering area;
- develop the foundations needed to attend more advanced courses in manufacturing technologies and manufacturing systems.

## Contents

The course introduces the fundamentals of mechanical technologies and the main manufacturing processes, with particular attention to the relationship between material behaviour, process parameters, product quality and the selection of the most appropriate manufacturing technology.

The main topics covered are:

**Fundamentals of the mechanical and technological behaviour of materials:** Mechanical behaviour of materials in manufacturing processes. Tests and loading conditions in tension, compression and torsion. Yield criteria and deformation work. Structural and technological properties of metallic materials relevant to manufacturing processes. Surface characteristics and surface condition of components. Friction, wear and lubrication in manufacturing processes. Elements of geometrical and dimensional metrology. Quality control and verification of dimensional and surface characteristics of products.

**Bulk plastic deformation processes:** Principles and applications of the main bulk forming processes. Forging, rolling, extrusion and drawing. Analysis of process parameters, equipment, fields of application and main technological limitations.

**Sheet metal forming processes:** Technological characteristics of sheet metals and main forming processes. Shearing, bending, stretch forming and deep drawing. Effect of process parameters on product quality, defects and material formability.

**Machining and abrasive processes:** Principles of machining with cutting tools. Mechanics of chip formation. Tool wear and tool life. Surface finish and surface integrity. Machinability of materials. Cutting tool materials and cutting fluids. Principles of abrasive processes. Types of abrasives and grinding wheels. Mechanics of grinding and grinding wheel wear.

**Non-conventional machining processes:** Principles and applications of the main non-conventional processes. Ultrasonic machining, chemical and electrochemical machining, electrochemical grinding, electrical discharge machining, high-energy beam machining and waterjet machining. Analysis of fields of application, advantages, limitations and characteristics of the components obtained.

**Surface processing and surface treatment processes:** Main coating, surface modification and surface processing technologies. Effects of surface treatments on the functional properties of components, with reference to wear resistance, corrosion protection, surface quality and in-service performance.

**Joining processes:** Principles of the main joining processes used in manufacturing. Brazing and welding. Joint characteristics, fields of application, main process parameters and technological aspects related to joint quality.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of 60 hours of teaching activities and adopts a blended learning approach, combining interactive lectures, exercise sessions, online activities, self-assessment quizzes, discussion of applied case studies, and individual or group learning activities. Lectures are devoted to introducing the theoretical and applied principles of the main mechanical technologies and manufacturing processes. During lectures, discussion and active student participation will be encouraged, with particular attention to the relationship between material behaviour, process parameters, product quality and the selection of the appropriate manufacturing technology. Exercise sessions include the guided solution of examples and numerical exercises related to the main topics of the course. As part of the blended learning path, online activities, theoretical in-depth materials, teaching videos, self-assessment quizzes and interactive activities will also be provided. The self-assessment quizzes are intended to support continuous study, progressively check the understanding of the course contents and help students identify topics that may require further study. During the course, practical and applied case studies will be presented and discussed, with the aim of connecting theoretical contents to real manufacturing problems. An optional final poster is also planned, focused on the discussion of a real case study. Through this activity, students will be able to apply the knowledge acquired to the analysis of a technological problem, the description of the manufacturing process and the evaluation of the main technical choices. Copies of the slides, solved exercises and any additional teaching materials will be provided as study support. Videos and theoretical in-depth materials will also be made available to support independent preparation and consolidation of the course contents. Students are expected to participate actively and continuously in lectures, exercise sessions, classroom discussions and online activities. Particular emphasis will be placed on the ability to connect the interdisciplinary knowledge already acquired, especially in the fields of materials, mechanics and design, to typical problems in manufacturing technologies. Students who participate in all the optional interactive activities included in the blended learning path, including the final poster, and complete them by the end of the semester according to the deadlines indicated by the lecturer, may access a reduced format of the final examination. In this case, the final assessment will take into account the work carried out during the semester and the competencies demonstrated through the blended learning activities.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the knowledge and competencies acquired during the course is mainly carried out through a final written examination. The written examination aims to assess the students’ understanding of the theoretical and applied principles of mechanical technologies and their ability to apply this knowledge to the solution of problems related to manufacturing processes. The written examination consists of a combination of numerical exercises and open-ended questions on the topics covered during the course. The examination typically includes 6–8 questions, consisting of numerical exercises and open-ended questions. The maximum number of points awarded for each question is indicated in the examination paper. Numerical exercises are intended to assess the ability to apply models, relationships and calculation criteria to the main manufacturing processes, while open-ended questions assess the understanding of concepts, process parameters, fields of application, advantages and limitations of the different technologies. As part of the blended learning path, optional interactive activities are planned, including self-assessment quizzes, online activities, exercise sessions and the preparation of a final poster focused on the discussion of a real case study. Students who complete all the optional interactive activities included in the blended learning path, including the final poster, by the end of the semester and according to the deadlines indicated by the lecturer, may access a reduced version of the final written examination. In this case, the assessment will also take into account the evaluation of the final poster. An optional oral examination is also available. The oral examination may complement the mark obtained in the written examination and in any optional activities, through the discussion of course topics, solved exercises, the poster or specific aspects that emerged during the written examination. The oral examination is intended to assess the student’s ability to clearly explain the fundamental concepts, use technical terminology correctly and justify technological choices in relation to the manufacturing processes studied. The assessment will take into account the following criteria: * correctness and completeness of the answers provided; * ability to apply theoretical knowledge to the solution of numerical exercises and applied problems; * understanding of the operating principles of the main manufacturing processes; * ability to compare different manufacturing technologies, highlighting their advantages, limitations and fields of application; * ability to connect process parameters, material behaviour, product quality and the selection of the manufacturing technology; * clarity of exposition, synthesis and correct use of technical terminology; * rigour in carrying out exercises and in justifying answers.
