## Learning objectives

Acquisition of knowledge relating to the main technologies used in plastics processing, with particular regard to aspects linked to the environmental sustainability of raw materials, transformation processes and end-of-life management.

• Understanding the main technologies used in plastics transformation, and the correlations between the process parameters used and the resulting technological properties.

• Learn about the processing machines needed for plastics transformation, with relative process parameter settings.

• Acquire the basic tools for assessing the environmental impact of different types of plastics, related transformation technologies and end-of-life processes.

• Propose the most suitable production process for a plastic product, also in relation to the environmental balance of the process and end-of-life management.

• Carry out group laboratory activities related to process technologies and characterisation of plastics, with particular attention to innovative and sustainable materials and processes.

• Present the experimental results in a comprehensive manner through an oral presentation and technical report.

## Prerequisites

At the beginning of the course, students should have a good basic knowledge of General Chemistry.

## Contents

Classroom lessons (approximately 30 hours)

1. Polymeric materials: definition, classification, environmental impact, presentation of the main types of plastics
2. Thermoplastic materials transformation processes: melt compounding, extrusion, thermoforming, moulding
3. Thermosetting materials transformation processes: compression moulding, manual lamination, resin transfer moulding, resin infusion
4. Elastomers transformation processes: compounding, incorporation of additives, vulcanisation
5. Additive Manufacturing processes for plastics
6. End-of-life management of plastics: landfill, waste-to-energy, recycling, biodegradation/composting

Laboratory exercitations (approximately 30 hours)

During the laboratory exercitations, students will be divided into groups (depending on the number of participants in the course), and based on the classroom lessons, the following topics will be explored in depth:

1. Melt compounding/hot pressing
2. Extrusion/spinning
3. Injection moulding
4. Manual lamination/resin transfer moulding
5. Vulcanisation of elastomers
6. Fused Deposition Modelling (3D printing)
7. Reprocessing/recycling of plastics
8. Physical characterisation of the manufactured products (thermo-mechanical properties)

If possible, meetings will be held with plastics processing companies.

## Teaching methods

• The course will be organised into classroom lectures according to the programme indicated.

• At the end of the lectures, laboratory exercises will be carried out on the topics presented in class.

• Students are required to be available for group work from an experimental point of view in the preparation of technical reports.

• Students will also be required to prepare and present individual PowerPoint reports on specific topics related to laboratory activities.

NB. Copying and pasting from other reports, slides from other courses, books, scientific articles, websites, etc. is strictly prohibited. Plagiarism will be prosecuted in accordance with the law. Submitted reports will be checked using software available at the University of Trento.

## Assessment methods

The final exam consists of three parts:

1. Preparation of group reports
2. PowerPoint presentation on experimental results (individual)
3. Oral exam on the theoretical content of the course (individual)

Group reports and Power Point presentations must be prepared in strict accordance with the instructions provided by the lecturer.

Group reports will be corrected once by the lecturer, and the corrected reports must be submitted at least 5 days before the exam, and in any case within 60 days of the end of the course.

The presentation must be submitted in PDF format the day before the exam.

Each of the three parts is considered mandatory for passing the exam, and the assessment is composed as follows:

1. Group reports: max 8 points
2. Oral presentation: max 7 points
3. Oral exam: max 15 points

At the discretion of the teacher, Cum Laude may be attributed.

NB. The exam can only be considered sufficient only if the oral exam assessment will be greater than or equal to 9/15.
