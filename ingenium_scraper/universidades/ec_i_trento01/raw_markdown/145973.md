## Learning objectives

The course introduces basic concepts of mechanics and provides the tools for the analysis and modelling of kinematics, statics and dynamics of point and rigid bodies using Newtonian methods. In addition, the course provides the basics of statics with an in-depth study of structural elements such as beams and trusses.

At the end of the course, the student will be able to deal with problems of static equilibrium and dynamics of planar and three-dimensional rigid bodies, in the presence of common types of constraints, friction and forces. The student will also be able to recognize the physical meaning of the models adopted and the scope of applicability to derive equations of motion and stress characteristics.

## Prerequisites

A solid background in physics, calculus and geometry, as well as the ability to read mechanical drawings, is required.

## Contents

1. Fundamental concepts of mechanics: forces, work, energy, power, ideal constraints, principle of constraint reactions, constraints with Coulomb friction. Degrees of freedom of the point and of the rigid body in the plane. 2. Statics of the point, of systems of points, of the rigid body and of systems of rigid bodies, isostatic, hyperstatic and unrestrained conditions. 3. Geometry of masses and areas. Centres of mass, inertia tensor, diagonalisation (eigenvalue problem). Introduction to the use of symbolic calculus (Mathematica). 4. Point and rigid body kinematics (in space). Rigid motions, coordinate transformations. Poisson formulas. Angular velocity. Relative motions. Centres of instantaneous rotation. Actual motion. Polars of motion. 5. Point and rigid body dynamics (in the plane) and applications of friction. Motion-quiet transition, inclined plane. Rolling. 6. Models of structural elements: beam, slab, shell, 3D models. Stress characteristics in beams and related diagrams. Reticular structures, frames, machines and mechanisms.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of a theory part and classroom exercises, in which the main methods of static, kinematic, and dynamic analysis are introduced. The lectures and exercises will therefore be very interconnected and often indistinct. Two problems will be assigned for individual homework. The homework consists of solving problems of simple to medium complexity, applying the elements learned during the course. Homework constitutes a personal work and are corrected after submission, but the correction will only be shown when the grade is recorded so that it is always possible at that time to ask for clarification of their resolution. It is therefore not possible to show solutions and corrections before that time. The teaching material, the syllabus, the proposed exercises and the homework will be assigned through an online teaching system (e.g. moodle). Students, even if not attending, are required to inform themselves about the arrangements that may be made during the course.

## Assessment methods

The examination is designed to test the knowledge acquired (theory) and the ability to apply the knowledge (ability to solve problems).

The grade is awarded by means of an overall assessment of the written paper and the homework completed and handed in within the allotted deadlines.

The assessment of the written paper and the homework is done individually by appointment, at which time clarifications and explanations may be requested on the written paper, the homework and the related theory.

Indicatively, 20 points will be allocated to the written test and 10 points to the homework assignments.

The completion of the homework is not compulsory. In the absence of homework, the student can aspire to a maximum mark of 20/30. It is not possible to supplement missing homework.

Any gaps on parts of the syllabus cannot be made up by answering additional questions on other parts of the syllabus. If preparation with gaps is established, the examination cannot be passed.
