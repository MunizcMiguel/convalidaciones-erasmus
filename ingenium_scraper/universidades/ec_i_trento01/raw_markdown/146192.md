## Learning objectives

The course is part of the Bachelor’s Degree programme in Industrial Engineering and aims to provide students with a solid understanding of the main aspects of industrial logistics, together with technical and engineering skills for analysing and managing the material and information flows that characterise modern logistics systems.

In particular, students will be guided in analysing packaging choices in relation to logistical, economic, environmental and regulatory constraints. They will acquire the skills needed to assess and size both traditional and automated material handling systems, including AGVs, and to analyse the organisation of distribution flows. The course also provides the theoretical and practical foundations required to understand and apply the main models for materials requirements planning and inventory management, in support of the integrated design and control of production and logistics systems.

**Expected Learning Outcomes**

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Describe the principles of industrial and sustainable logistics and their role within the business system;
- Analyse and design simple packaging and unit load solutions, taking into account logistical, economic and environmental aspects;
- Assess and size internal material handling systems, including AGV systems;
- Apply the main models for materials requirements planning and inventory management;
- Analyse and compare transport configurations and modes within distribution logistics;
- Correctly apply quantitative methods and interpret their results in relation to the problem under examination.

## Prerequisites

No specific prerequisites are required. All lectures will be conducted in Italian. Therefore, a good understanding of the Italian language is necessary in order to successfully follow the course and make use of the provided learning materials. Some supplementary handouts may be in English.

## Contents

**INTRODUCTION TO INDUSTRIAL AND SUSTAINABLE LOGISTICS.** Definition, objectives and main logistics activities. Physical and information flows. Inbound, internal, distribution and reverse logistics. Decision-making levels and the role of logistics within the business system.

**PACKAGING AND UNIT LOADS.** The product–packaging system. Functions and classification of primary, secondary and tertiary packaging. Volumetric efficiency. Pallets and EUR-EPAL management. Palletised unit loads, modular coordination and compatibility with pallets, containers and road vehicles. Pooling systems. Packaging and packaging-waste management. Economic and environmental assessment of alternative packaging solutions.

**TRADITIONAL INTERNAL MATERIAL-HANDLING SYSTEMS.** Hand pallet trucks, counterbalanced forklifts, reach trucks, bilateral and trilateral trucks, and stacker cranes. Main operating parameters and selection criteria: load capacity, speed, overall dimensions, lifting height, turning radius and aisle width. Fixed conveyors, including roller, belt, slat, apron and overhead conveyor systems.

**AUTOMATED GUIDED VEHICLE SYSTEMS – AGVs.** Main characteristics, guidance technologies and control systems. Design criteria for AGV-based transport networks. Mission analysis, calculation of travel distances and service times, flow balancing and rebalancing, and static fleet sizing.

**PRODUCTION PLANNING PROCESS.** Management of information flows within production planning, execution and control processes. Aggregate planning and development of the Master Production Schedule.

**MATERIAL REQUIREMENTS MANAGEMENT.** Requirements-based and inventory-based systems. Material Requirements Planning: principles, bill of materials, inputs, calculation logic, lead times, lot-sizing policies, advantages, limitations and closed-loop systems. Availability to Promise. Just-in-Time principles and implementation requirements.

**INVENTORY MANAGEMENT.** Fixed-order-quantity and fixed-order-interval models. Economic order quantity and economic production quantity. Reorder point and safety stock. Service level and stockout risk. Economic make-or-buy evaluations.

**DISTRIBUTION LOGISTICS AND TRANSPORT.** Objectives, service level, distribution channels and main physical distribution activities. Distribution system design. Characteristics and comparison of the main transport modes. Relationships among transport costs, transit times, vehicle utilisation and inventory levels. Intermodal transport, logistics infrastructure and transport externalities. Technical and economic assessment of alternative distribution configurations.

## Teaching methods

The course is structured around lectures that cover the fundamental concepts of each topic in the syllabus. Theoretical presentations are followed by sessions focused on solving exercises and specific problems. These applied sessions emphasize the practical nature of the subject and aim to teach methodology—that is, the ability to translate real-world problems into mathematical language in order to find solutions that can then be applied in industrial contexts.

## Assessment methods

Learning is assessed through a written examination consisting of one or more theoretical questions and one or more applied exercises. The theoretical questions are intended to assess students’ knowledge and understanding of the course topics, as well as their ability to provide relevant, well-structured and technically accurate answers. The applied exercises assess the ability to formulate and solve problems using the models and methods presented during the course and to interpret the resulting outcomes correctly. The examination is graded on a scale of 30 and is passed with a mark of at least 18/30. To pass the examination, students must demonstrate at least a satisfactory level of achievement in each theoretical question and each applied exercise. Omissions, irrelevant answers or substantial shortcomings in any component of the examination may prevent a passing grade. The overall evaluation takes into account the accuracy, completeness and relevance of the content, the clarity and coherence of the presentation, the correctness of the methodological approach, the appropriate use of formulas, assumptions and units of measurement, and the ability to interpret and contextualise the results. Diagrams, graphs and connections among course topics are considered when relevant to the question. Providing only the final numerical result, without a clear and adequately justified solution process, does not allow for full assessment of the exercise. Higher marks are awarded to examinations showing greater completeness, independent reasoning, command of the methods and quality of technical communication.
