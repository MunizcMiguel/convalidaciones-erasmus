## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide participants with a solid understanding of the fundamental principles and parametric modeling techniques necessary for the creation, verification, and management of industrial products. Students will acquire knowledge related to digital product management using 3D geometric modeling software (Autodesk Inventor) and finite element analysis (Ansys Workbench). The objective is to provide participants with the skills necessary to work with these programs, create 3D models, and understand how to modify them parametrically to improve their mechanical behavior.

At the end of the course, students will be able to digitally model mechanical components and assemblies, verifying their mechanical performance through Ansys Workbench.

Students will be able to:

- **Parametrically** **model** mechanical components;
- **Analyze and verify** the mechanical performance of modeled components;
- **Communicate** the results obtained to various company stakeholders (e.g., R&D, Marketing) through the production of technical and product documentation (e.g., bill of materials, reports).

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of industrial design for the representation of mechanical components and knowledge of the main elements of machines are considered prerequisites. Notions of mechanical elements (forces, stresses). Basics of materials mechanics (elastic modulus, yield strength, Poisson's ratio).

## Contents

The course is divided into several phases covering the entire design process, from the creation of the 3D model to its subsequent evaluation using a finite element simulator. Students will be able to create models of mechanical parts, complex assemblies, and drawings using parametric modeling techniques. Thanks to these skills, participants will be able to create models that meet specific design requirements, improving their mechanical performance.

The course is organized as follows:

Module I: CAD modeling lab with Autodesk Inventor

- Introduction to CAD modeling
- Part modeling (sketching, feature-based design, etc.)
- Assembly creation (constraints and kinematics)
- Technical documentation (drawings, animations, rendering)

Module II: finite element analysis lab with Ansys WorkBench:

- Introduction to finite element analysis (theoretical overview)
- Introduction to Ansys Workbench: analysis workflow (e.g., modeling, element selection, mechanical properties, loads, constraints)
- Structural analysis of mechanical parts
- Technical documentation: interpreting results and generating reports.

## Teaching methods

There will be 60 hours of classroom exercises in the computer lab, accompanied by theoretical introductions to the topics covered in the exercises. Of these, 30 hours will be dedicated to CAD modeling, and the remaining 30 to finite element analysis. The professors reserve the right to rearrange the hours as necessary.

The main teaching approaches used are:

- project-based learning: students will develop guided and independent exercises throughout the course;
- face-to-face lectures.

The activities required include:

- active participation during the practical lessons;
- individual study;
- development and presentation of a group project.

The course has a marked “practical” orientation.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a project related to the two parts of the course, composed as follows:

- CAD modeling of a “mobile” mechanism and related assembly (15/30);
- Finite element analysis of one or more components of the modeled assembly (15/30);

The modeling part and the finite element analysis part contribute equally to the final grade. Students will be evaluated on the basis of the project report and related technical documentation.

It is possible to intergarte the project evaluation with an oral exam or to submit a new project involving a new mechanism and a new component modeled using finite element analysis.
