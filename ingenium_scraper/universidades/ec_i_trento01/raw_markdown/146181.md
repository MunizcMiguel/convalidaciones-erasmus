## Learning objectives

The course will provide a review of the operating principles of the main electrical machines used in industrial applications and will deepen the modeling and analysis of dc machines, permanent magnets synchronous machines, asynchronous machines, and reluctance machines, and their role in the progressive energy transition and electrification of the society.

The main control techniques for such machines will be also introduced, besides the description of the power electronic components/systems used for their control.

Practical examples and exercises will be proposed to support the theory.

After completing the course, the student shall be able to:

· Understand the operating principles and fields of operation of the main electrical machines and drives.

· Master terminology and parameters characterizing electric drives.

· Design simple current and speed controllers for dc and ac drives.

· Read and correctly interpret nameplate data of the considered electrical machines and drives.

· Understand the main applications of electric drives for the transition of our modern society towards more sustainable energy paradigms.

## Prerequisites

It is necessary for the students of the “Electric Drives for the Energy Transition” course to have gained knowledge on electrical circuits (basic electrical bipoles, Kirchhoff’s laws of voltage and current, analysis of dc and ac systems, three-phase systems, concepts of active power, reactive power and power factor) and basic concepts related to electrical machines (structure, operating principles and basic models of dc machines, permanent magnet synchronous machines and asynchronous machines), which are taught within the course "Electrical circuits and machines".

Moreover, to understand the course content at best, students should have good command of the following concepts from the very beginning:

- Math: basics of differential calculus and trigonometric formulas.

- Physics: basics of mechanics and electromagnetism.

- Basics of automatic control: concepts of transfer function and frequency response and drawing of Bode diagrams.

## Contents

Introduction to electric drives; supplementary analysis of motor-load dynamics; basics of automatic control.

Power electronic converters for electric drives: Power switching devices. Types of power converters. DC-DC converters operating in 1, 2 and 4 quadrants. Principle of Pulse Width Modulation (PWM). Voltage source three phase inverters and main modulation techniques. Rectifiers. Exercises on power electronic converters.

Dc motor: review of the dc motor: structure, operating principle and dynamical model.

Drives for dc machines (current and speed loops). Controllers' design criteria. Exercises on dc motors and drives.

Stationary and synchronous reference frames: space vectors: definition, properties, and examples.

Permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM): review of the PMSM. Dynamical model in a rotating reference frame synchronous with the rotor. Electromagnetic torque expressions. Operating regions and limits. Discussion and comparison of different types of controllers. Linear controllers’ design principles. Exercises and examples on permanent magnets motors and drives.

Asynchronous machine: review of the asynchronous motors. Dynamical model. Equivalent circuit. Overview of the main types of drives for asynchronous machines. Scalar control of the speed (V/Hz control). Basics of field oriented control. Exercises on asynchronous motors and drives.

Reluctance machines and stepper motors: types. Structure and applications. Torque generation principle. Characteristics. Exercises on stepper motors.

Electrification process of the modern society and examples of application of electric drives.

## Teaching methods

It is recommended that students attend the theoretical lectures that are a mix of lectures developed on the board/i-Pad or through slides. As an integral part of the course, exercises will be solved in class on the operation of the electrical machines and drives’ design.

Students are strongly encouraged to revisit the course material (mostly slides) available through the Moodle page of the course and revise autonomously the notes taken during the lectures.

## Assessment methods

To pass the exam, students must pass a final written test including questions to assess their understanding of the theoretical content as well as exercises to assess their problem-solving capability. Questions aimed to assess the understanding of the theoretical content can include both open questions and multiple-choice questions.

A maximum score of 31 points can be earned in the written test. If the student earns at least 18/30 points, the exam is passed. If the score of the written exam is lower than 18/30 the exam is not passed and must be repeated in a later exam session.
