# Laboratory of chemistry applied to materials, energy and environment (146183)

## Learning objectives

The course aims to illustrate the main analytical chemical techniques, with reference to industrial applications in quality control of the products, in sustainability of chemical processes, in environmental impact and materials degradation processes, in characterisation of devices for energy production and harvesting. At the end of the course the student should acquire the basic knowledge on the different analytical methods and be able to appropriately apply the analytical tools for identifying unknown compounds and quantifying mixture components, considering the errors associated to the measurements.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of Chemistry and Physics: in particular, structural and chemical properties of matter, chemical equilibria (acid-base, redox), electromagnetic radiation-matter interactions. Moreover, basic knowledge of statistics and experimental data management. For any details, please refer to Chemistry and basi organic chemistry (140110) course and to Data Analysis and Statistics (146172/2) module.

## Contents

Theoretical part (20-25 hours) Introduction: analytical techniques and sustainable chemical processes Sampling, basic principles in laboratory, health and safety in the laboratory, instrumental calibration Chemical methods Gravimetric and volumetric methods Density measurements Displacement and gas pyknometry Electrochemical methods Potenziometry, voltammetry and polarography, conductivity Cromatography methods Liquid cromatography: HPLC, ionic cromatography. Gas-cromatography Spectroscopic methods Atomic spectroscopy: atomic absorption and emission. Molecular spectroscopy: IR and UV-vis Reference: experimental data management and statistics Laboratory part (35-40 hours) (exercises performed by students shared by groups) Sudents may express their preference for one of these activity: Line 1: chemical analysis on materials (polymers, textiles fibers, paper, wood, concrete, ....) Line 2: chemical analysis for energy (photovoltaic cells, batteries, ...) Line 3: chemical analysis for environment (pollutants photocatalytic methods, materials degradation, emissions, ...) Line 4: chemical analysi for food industry (oil and beverages, adulteration and sophistication, ...) Each line is made by two starting exercises that are needed for the experience of students in laboratory activities, experimental data collection and their management/verification with reference models. Another main exercise is planned and it will be realized starting from a DOE project, performed with the design of the case-study, its realization in the laboratory with the experimental data collection and elaboration, the statistical evaluation of the results.

## Teaching methods

The course is based on lectures and laboratory activities; the individual learning pathway is demanding. The teaching material is available online on the Moodle platform (PP slides, video recordings, notes, exercises) for learning, as well as the descriptions of laboratory experiences. A selection of questions and problems to be solved for the purpose of self-assessment and exam preparation is also provided. Students are asked to review the notes taken in class at home, using the available material and consulting the recommended texts. The laboratory-assisted activities integrate the course; they are organized by groups of students (usually 3-4), during which each student must appropriately employ the instruments and materials of the chemical laboratory and cooperate within the group for qualitative and quantitative determination of chemical compounds, taking care of the critical analysis of the experimental results.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of student learning and skills (according to the described objectives of the course) is based on: a) an oral presentation, performed by the students of each group, based on the experimental methods and the critical evaluation of the experimental results obtained during the laboratory exercises; b) a written report, performed by each group, based on the DOE study-case with the connected statistical analysis and result discussion. The final mark of each student is given, in a thirtieth grade, by the weighted average of the result of the written group report (with weight 50%) and of the score assigned for the oral presentation (weighting 50%).
