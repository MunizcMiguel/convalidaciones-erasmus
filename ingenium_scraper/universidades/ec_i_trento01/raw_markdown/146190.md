## Learning objectives

The objective of the course is to provide students with the necessary competences for the design, installation and management of industrial plants in order to maximize their technical and economic performances using appropriate mathematical models. Starting from the analysis of the target market, the course analyzes the choices dealing with the product to be manufactured, its production process, the required production capacity of the plant, the economic evaluation of the entire initiative, the development of alternative layouts for the production system, up to the installation of the industrial plant.

## Prerequisites

A prior knowledge and understanding of Mathematics is suggested (but not required) to attend with profit this course.

## Contents

- Introduction to industrial plants. FEASIBILITY STUDY OF AN INDUSTRIAL PLANT - Analysis of market demand and quantitative methodologies for its forecasting. - Choice of the production technological cycle and definition of the manufacturing diagram. - Criteria for the executive design of the service plants necessary for the production. - Calculation of the optimal size and capacity of an industrial plant. - Determination of the optimal production mix from a technical-economic perspective according to the different production factors. - Analysis and classification of production costs. - Influence of time on fixed costs: depreciation. - Criteria for assessing the profitability of industrial investments: VAN, TIR, PAYBACK. - Location of an industrial plant: characteristic parameters and selection criteria. EXECUTIVE DESIGN OF AN INDUSTRIAL PLANT - Analysis of the final product and production quantities of an industrial plant. - Mathematical models for determining the number of machines and operators in production by department systems. - Mathematical models for the determination of the number of machines and operators in the production line system. - Calculation of the stage potential for the different processing phases. - Basic concepts industrial plant maintenance: reliability, availability, maintainability. - Technical-economic evaluation for the production switch from departments to the in-line one: product characteristic curve. - Product Flow Analysis to determine the product families. Group technology and cluster analysis. - Cellular manufacturing and Flexible Manufacturing Systems. - Introduction and main features of manual assembly systems. - Mathematical models for the design and balancing of assembly lines. - Determination of the space required for the production activities of an industrial plant. - Quantitative analysis of material flows between production departments and proximity relationships. - Diagram of material flows and relationship between production activities. - Mathematical models for the definition of layout alternatives for an industrial plant. - Choice of the optimal layout from a technical-economic perspective. - Detailed analysis and executive design of the identified layout. - Industrial plant scheduling. - Plant scheduling metrics and objectives. - Models for scheduling identical and different machines in series and in parallel. - Algorithms for efficient industrial plant scheduling.

## Teaching methods

Theoretical lectures include numerical cases, applications and exercises to ensure the student familiarity with the described design methods for production systems. Industrial case studies will be presented during the course to propose the practical effects of the lecture topics within the modern manufacturing companies.

## Assessment methods

The final exam aims to assess the achievement of the learning objectives: -to provide the general criteria and the corresponding mathematical methods for conducting plant decisions, which govern the choice, design and installation of industrial plants; -apply the operational models and tools illustrated in the course to numerical examples and case studies; The final examination of the course is defined by a written test at the end of the course consisting of a technical report on theoretical topics covered in the course (two theoretical questions) and in numerical applications (two numerical exercises).
