## Learning objectives

The Additive Manufacturing course aims to introduce students to the basics of additive manufacturing and its industrial applications. The main production processes used for 3D printing of different classes of materials, metals, ceramics and polymers, are presented with the aim of understanding the correlations between process parameters, microstructure and properties.

At the end of the course, the student must:

a) understand the main additive technologies used in industry;

b) understand the materials suitable for additive manufacturing and their properties;

c) be able to identify the possible applications of additive manufacturing;

d) recognise the distinctive features of additive manufacturing compared to other technologies;

## Contents

The course programme covers the following topics:

Additive manufacturing of metallic materials (40 hours)

Introduction to the main additive manufacturing technologies for metallic materials:

- Powder Bed Fusion
- Directed Energy Deposition
- Binder Jetting
- Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing.

Metallic materials for additive manufacturing: steels, aluminium alloys, titanium, copper. Metal powders and wire.

Design elements for additive manufacturing.

Surface finishing and heat treatment processes.

Microstructure and properties of metal alloys produced using additive techniques

Industrial fields of application for additive manufacturing

Additive Manufacturing of Polymeric Materials (10 hours)

Main additive manufacturing technologies for polymeric materials:

- Extrusion-based technologies: Fused Filament Fabrication/Fused Deposition Modeling (FFF/FDM)
- Powder Bed Fusion technologies: Selective Laser Sintering (SLS)
- Binder Jetting and Material Jetting technologies
- VAT Photopolymerization: Stereolithography (SL), Digital Light Processing (DLP)

Polymeric materials for additive manufacturing: PLA, ABS, PETG, PC, PA, PEEK, TPU, photopolymer and epoxy resins; composite and nanocomposite materials; feedstock state: filaments, powders, liquids

Process parameters, supports and print orientation; microstructure and anisotropy of printed parts; common processing defects and prevention strategies; post-processing treatments: support removal, sanding/polishing, heat treatments

Industrial applications: prototyping, tooling, end-use parts

Future trends: 4D printing, bioprinting

Additive manufacturing of ceramics and glass (10 hours)

Fundamentals of additive manufacturing for ceramics and glass

3D printing technologies for ceramics and glass, potential, resolution, anisotropy, costs, and critical aspects:

- From powders: selective laser sintering/melting, binder jetting 3D printing
- From powder-filled polymer blends: fused deposition modeling, laminated object manufacturing
- From suspensions or slurries: stereolithography, inkjet printing, robocasting, freeze extrusion fabrication

Methods for preparing the starting systems (powders, powder-filled polymer blends, pre-ceramic polymers, inks, suspensions, slurries)

Post-printing processes and material consolidation/sintering

Applications of 3D-printed ceramics and glasses: bioceramics, piezoelectrics, resonators and antennas, transparent materials, composites

## Assessment methods

For the examination, the student must prepare a report on a topic agreed upon with the lecturers or on an experimental laboratory activity. The report must be submitted two weeks before the examination date for correction. During the oral examination, the student must demonstrate the ability to describe the topics covered in class and connect them. The final mark will be based on the quality of the report and the mark obtained in the oral examination.
