## Learning objectives

The course aims to educate students on the main classes of metallic materials and the technologies that can contribute to sustainable mobility. The following materials will be covered: high-strength steels; aluminum, magnesium, and titanium alloys for structural lightweighting; permanent magnets and rare earths; lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese for batteries; and catalysts for hydrogen production and fuel cells. Additive manufacturing processes will also be covered.

The expected learning outcome is student knowledge of the characteristics of the materials covered, their production technologies, and their sustainability. Regarding additive technologies, students will be expected to understand their operating mechanisms and the advantages and disadvantages of their applications in product optimization.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of phisical metallurgy of plastic deformation and fracture and of the strengthening methods of metallic materials

## Contents

1. High-strength steels
2. Light alloys: aluminum, magnesium, and titanium
3. Permanent magnets and rare earths
4. Batteries and lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese
5. Hydrogen, electrolysis and catalysts, fuel cells
6. Additive Manufacturing

## Teaching methods

Lectures by the teacher and in-depth analysis of the topics covered through student seminars, constructed by summarizing articles selected by the instructorteacher from the international scientific literature.

## Assessment methods

An oral exam on the topics covered will assess the student's knowledge of the basic elements of the various topics and their ability to reason and connect the various arguments. The seminar presented by the student will also be graded. The final grade will be calculated by averaging the two grades.
