## Learning objectives

The course will introduce the basic concepts of mathematical modeling for solving managerial and industrial problems. It will focus largely on what is called "Operations Research," defined by the Merriam-Webster dictionary as: "The application of scientific and especially mathematical methods to the study and analysis of problems involving complex systems."

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Identify the main quantitative problems related to various industrial and production processes.
- Describe and model these problems from a mathematical perspective.
- Solve them using the methods covered during the course.

## Prerequisites

Calculus and linear algebra

## Contents

**Linear Optimization:**Introduction to linear programming with examples and geometric interpretations. Study of the linear programming model and its mathematical properties. The simplex algorithm and duality.

**Special Problems:**Analysis of minimum-cost network problems, minimum spanning tree, maximum flow, and shortest path problems. Integer and combinatorial optimization with practical applications. Techniques for linearizing optimization problems.

**Stochastic Processes:**Markov chains: Chapman-Kolmogorov equation, classification of state types, long-term properties, and absorbing states. Random walks and related applications. Decision-making problems based on Markov chains.

**Queueing Theory:**Examples and basic structures, Little’s formula, properties of the exponential distribution. Analysis of single- and multi-server queues with exponential service times. Decisions on the optimal number of servers to use.

**Inventory Management:**Determination of the optimal order quantity. Inventory theory under uncertainty, the “newsvendor problem,” and overbooking management. Optimal stopping problems

**Simulation:**Time permitting, an introduction to simulation methods for analyzing complex systems.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of lectures with a predominantly theoretical focus, complemented by practical exercises on real-world problems. Classroom exercises may involve modeling problems and solving them, including through the use of computers.

## Assessment methods

Written exam, with the possibility of a midterm exam. The exam will consist of theoretical and practical questions and will require the ability to interpret and formulate the models studied in class.
