## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide both theoretical and practical training in surface science and engineering, highlighting its importance in the design and functionality of industrial products. Students will acquire the skills to understand, modify, and monitor the surfaces of materials (metals, polymers, composites) in relation to their optical, chemical-physical, aesthetic, adhesive, and mechanical properties, such as abrasion resistance. The course combines laboratory activities with theoretical lessons to encourage direct observation of the interactions between surfaces and environments, allowing for the evaluation of wettability, corrosion phenomena, and material coupling strategies. At the end of the course, students will be able to select and design surface treatments and adhesion strategies based on the desired surface properties, and understand how these influence the overall performance of the system.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of Materials Science, Chemistry, and Physics. It is recommended to have completed coursework in Materials Science.

## Contents

The importance of surfaces in industrial products Introduction to surface science: surface tension, free energy, interfacial interactions Aesthetic and optical properties: color, gloss, finish, instrumental measurement Measurement of wettability and adhesion: contact angle, adhesion models, application examples, and adhesion testing Methods for modifying surface energy: flame treatment, corona discharge, plasma treatment Surface preparation and cleaning techniques: degreasing, pickling, conversion treatments Basic concepts of coating deposition Introduction to corrosion and surface aging: mechanisms, protection strategies, accelerated testing Application examples on metals, polymers, and hybrid surfaces in various fields, from biomedical applications to design and the transport industry Laboratory activities: development of a project including the design, fabrication, and characterization of a functional surface prototype Lectures may be complemented by seminars presenting engineering and industrial application perspectives

## Teaching methods

Lectures and group-based experimental activities in the laboratory. One or two guest seminars by industry experts, illustrating engineering challenges and industrial applications related to surface science. Application-focused work on real samples, with the preparation of individual or group technical reports. Each group will identify a product or topic where surface properties play a critical role and will conduct a literature review to identify the most relevant engineering solutions. The final report will be presented and discussed collectively during class sessions.

## Assessment methods

Technical laboratory report (including data analysis and presentation); Written exam complementing the submitted report; Student performance will be assessed based not only on their understanding of the course content (50% of the final note), but also on their ability to apply this knowledge to real-world situations (50% of the final note). The evaluation of the report and its discussion will particularly highlight this applied aspect.
