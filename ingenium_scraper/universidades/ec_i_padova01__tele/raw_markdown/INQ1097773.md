## Prerequisites

The course requires solid knowledge of:

- Mathematical Analysis,
- Linear Algebra and Geometry,

in particular with reference to the following topics: complex numbers, differential and integral calculus, vector spaces.

General programming skills are also useful.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- ILO1 – apply mathematical tools to represent and analyze signals of different nature (continuous or discrete time, periodic or aperiodic). Dublin Descriptor: Applying knowledge and understanding;
- ILO2 – identify and describe the properties of transformations and systems, in particular of linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. Dublin Descriptor: Applying knowledge and understanding;
- ILO3 – interpret signals and systems in the frequency domain and understand their spectral characteristics. Dublin Descriptor: Applying knowledge and understanding;
- ILO4 – analyze the effects of sampling continuous signals and design procedures for the reconstruction of continuous signals from discrete data. Dublin Descriptor: Applying knowledge and understanding;
- ILO5 – solve differential and difference equations and interpret their results in engineering contexts. Dublin Descriptor: Applying knowledge and understanding;
- ILO6 – explain theoretical concepts and problem-solving approaches using appropriate technical language and clear logical reasoning in written form. Dublin Descriptor: Communication skills.

The competencies and concepts covered in this course provide fundamental knowledge for all specializations of Information Engineering.

## Assessment methods

The assessment criteria used to evaluate knowledge and skills will be:

1. correctness, accuracy, clarity, and readability of the answers;
2. completeness of the acquired knowledge;
3. ability to accurately describe a transmission and reception system;
4. ability to design multi-carrier and multi-antenna systems, based on different channel models;
5. mastery of technical language;
6. critical thinking in interpreting results;
7. proficiency in the use of computational tools for the study of signals and systems.

## Contents

The topics covered in the course will be organized into the following modules.

- Module#0 – Review of linear algebra and complex analysis. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO1-ILO5.
- Module#1 – Types and properties of signals. Continuous and discrete-time signals, symmetry, periodicity, energy and power signals, standard test signals. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO1, ILO6.
- Module#2 – Systems in the time domain. System definitions, LTI systems, convolution, impulse response, causality, stability. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO2, ILO6.
- Module#3 – Differential and difference equations. Solutions of linear time-invariant differential and difference equations, natural and forced responses. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO5, ILO6.
- Module#4 – Frequency domain analysis. Fourier series, Fourier transform, frequency response, filtering, Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT). The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO3, ILO4, ILO6.
- Module#5 – Sampling and reconstruction. Time and frequency domain perspectives of sampling, interpolation, and the Sampling Theorem. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO4, ILO6.
- Module#6 – Laplace and Z-Transforms. Region of convergence. Examples for notable signals. Properties. Unilateral transforms and their use for solving differential and difference equations. The module contributes to the achievement of the following ILOs: ILO5, ILO6.
