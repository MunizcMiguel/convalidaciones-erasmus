## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of algebra and trigonometry; elements of mathematical analysis: derivation and integration of continuous functions in one real variable.

## Learning objectives

The students will acquire knowledge of the basic concepts and fundamental laws of classical mechanics and thermodynamics. The goal of the course is to acquire the following skills: • formalizing the laws governing the behavior of simple mechanical and thermodynamic systems into equations • solving proposed problems as a function of input parameters • critically analyzing results by checking their consistency using dimensional analysis and numerical accuracy. During the laboratory the students will: • carry out experiments to familiarize with the main problems inherent to the measurement of physical quantities • compare measurements with theoretical predictions, evaluating their consistency with respect to the experimental errors • use statistical methods to discern between random and potential systematic errors.

## Contents

Mechanics: physical quantities. The International System. Vectors and vector calculus. Scalar and vector kinematics. Material point dynamics: Newton's laws. General concepts on relative motions and reference systems. Linear momentum and impulse theorem,conservation of momentum. Fundamental interactions. Weight force. Constraints. Static and dynamic friction. Elastic force and harmonic motion. Tension. Work, kinetic energy, kinetic energy theorem, conservative forces, potential energy, conservation of mechanical energy, energy balance in presence of dissipative forces. Angular momentum, angular momentum theorem. Central forces. Law of universal gravitation. Systems of material points. Internal and external forces. Center of mass (CM) and its motion, angular momentum and energy. CM reference system. König's theorems. Work of internal and external forces. Rigid body: moment of inertia, Huygens-Steiner theorem, translational and rotational dynamics. Collisions: elastic and inelastic. Conservation laws. Fluids: pressure, elements of fluid statics and dynamics. Thermodynamics: thermodynamic systems and variables, equilibrium states, equation of state. Calorimetry. Temperature, thermometers and temperature scales. Work in thermodynamic systems. First Principle of thermodynamics. Ideal gases. Thermodynamic cycles, thermal and refrigeration machines. Second Principle of Thermodynamics. Entropy. Introduction to kinetic theory of ideal gases.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of 96 hours of classroom lectures and laboratory exercises. The lectures will cover the theoretical aspects of mechanics and thermodynamics so that the students will acquire the foundational knowledge necessary to study the topics. During classes, exercises related to simple physical systems are solved to allow the students to acquire the methodology to approach applied cases. In the laboratory sessions, the students carry out experiences that allow them to experimentally verify some of the physical laws described in class, and to apply the concepts learned related to physical measurements with an hands-on approach. In addition to contacting the course instructor, students with disabilities, Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), Special Educational Needs (SEN), and other health conditions can reach out to the Student Services Office - Inclusion Unit to receive more information about opportunities to access teaching with specific support and tools. In Italian SEN and SLD are indicated by the acronyms DSA and BES. The use of artificial intelligence (AI) software is permitted for studying course material. AI may be used to support understanding of the topics covered. The primary reference for study remains the recommended textbook, which presents information accurately, coherently, and systematically. AI can also be used to assist in solving problems discussed in class or provided as preparation for the written exam. However, students should note that AI tools may sometimes offer misleading guidance, especially when solving problems. AI should always be used critically—students are encouraged to assess whether the response contains ambiguities, whether the AI has correctly interpreted the problem, and whether the logical steps followed align with the expected reasoning. Obviously, AI software cannot be used during written tests.
