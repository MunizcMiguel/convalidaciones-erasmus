## Prerequisites

Students are expected to possess the knowledge and skills acquired in the Foundations of Computer Science course, including imperative programming, control structures, functions, basic abstract data types, and fundamental algorithmic concepts. Students should also be able to develop and understand simple programs, analyze their behavior, and apply basic principles of abstraction and modular design.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide a systematic introduction to the fundamental principles underlying the design, analysis, and evaluation of efficient algorithms and data structures. Students are introduced to the main algorithmic techniques and data structures used to solve computational problems, developing the ability to select appropriate solutions according to efficiency and correctness requirements. The course also discusses the implementation of algorithms and data structures within the object-oriented programming paradigm, with specific reference to the Java programming language.

The expected learning outcomes, structured according to the five Dublin Descriptors, are reported below.

**1) Knowledge and understanding**

At the end of the course, students will be able to:

- describe the main data structures and algorithmic techniques presented in the course;
- explain the theoretical principles underlying correctness and complexity analysis;
- describe and analyze fundamental algorithms and properties of trees, heaps, hash tables, search trees, and graphs;
- distinguish advantages, limitations, and application domains of different algorithmic solutions.

**2) Applying knowledge and understanding**

Students will be able to:

- design algorithms for solving computational problems;
- select appropriate data structures according to application requirements;
- implement algorithms and data structures using object-oriented programming in Java;
- analyze correctness and efficiency through asymptotic analysis techniques.

**3) Making judgements**

Students will be able to:

- critically compare alternative algorithmic solutions;
- evaluate design choices in terms of correctness, efficiency, and maintainability.

**4) Communication skills**

Students will be able to:

- describe algorithms and data structures using appropriate technical terminology;
- justify and discuss design decisions clearly and rigorously.

**5) Learning skills**

Students will be able to:

- independently study new data structures and algorithmic techniques;
- use the acquired knowledge as a foundation for advanced courses.

## Examination methods

Learning is assessed through formative activities carried out during the semester and a final examination.

**Optional quizzes**

Online quizzes aimed at self-assessment and monitoring learning progress.

**Optional individual programming project**

An activity designed to apply algorithms and data structures to practical problems.

**Mandatory written exam**

The written exam lasts 150 minutes and consists of two equally weighted parts:

- **Part A – Theory and analysis**: assessment of theoretical understanding, correctness and complexity analysis, and knowledge of data structures.
- **Part B – Algorithm design**: assessment of algorithm design skills, data structure selection, and critical evaluation of proposed solutions.

**Possible oral examination (at the instructor’s discretion)**

The instructor may require an additional oral examination whenever further clarification is needed regarding the written examination or to better assess the achievement of the intended learning outcomes. The oral exam might cover the entire course programme and must be taken in the same exam session as the written exam, on the date set by the instructor.

## Assessment methods

The written exam represents the primary form of summative assessment. It consists of:

- **Part A – Theory and analysis (16 points)**;
- **Part B – Algorithm design (16 points)**.

To pass the examination, both parts must be individually sufficient.

The quizzes and the individual programming project are the formative assessment activities. Their scores are added to the written exam score, provided that the written exam has received a passing mark, as follows:

- up to **1 bonus point** from quizzes;
- up to **2 bonus points** from the programming project.

Quizzes and the project must be completed before the first examination session of the academic year. Bonus points remain valid throughout the academic year.

**Honours (cum laude)** may be awarded when the overall score obtained from the written examination and bonus points exceeds **31 points** and the student's performance demonstrates outstanding mastery of the course contents and intended learning outcomes. The award of honours remains at the instructor's discretion.

## Contents

- Object-oriented programming in Java: classes, interfaces, inheritance, polymorphism, iterators.
- Algorithm specification: computational models, computational problems, algorithms.
- Algorithm analysis: combinatorial analysis, asymptotic analysis, induction, correctness and complexity analysis.
- Trees: definitions, properties, traversals, binary trees.
- Priority queues and heaps.
- Maps, dictionaries, and hash tables.
- Search trees: binary search trees and (2,4)-trees.
- Graphs: definitions, properties, data structures for their representation, BFS, DFS, shortest paths, DAGs, and topological sorting.
- Sorting algorithms and lower bounds for comparison-based sorting.

## Teaching methods

Lectures, problem-solving activities, guided exercises, online formative quizzes with immediate feedback, and an individual programming project. Teaching materials, communications, and support activities are provided through Moodle.

The course is delivered in a blended format, with 72 hours of guided learning, consisting of approximately 48 hours of in-person lectures and 24 hours of synchronous and asynchronous online activities. This approach aims to effectively integrate the classroom activities proposed by the instructor with those carried out online by students, while also offering greater flexibility and environmental sustainability.

The in-person classroom lectures follow a traditional format, whereas the synchronous online lectures may vary in format depending on the topic and the instructor.

The course promotes the critical and responsible use of Generative Artificial Intelligence tools as support for learning activities. Such tools may be used for brainstorming, exploring alternative solutions, understanding concepts, code review, and study support. Any use in assessed activities must be declared according to the instructor's guidelines. The use of GenAI does not replace theoretical understanding, algorithm design skills, or students' personal responsibility.

The University of Padua promotes an inclusive learning environment. Students with disabilities, Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), Special Educational Needs (SEN), and other health conditions can reach out to the Student Services Office - Inclusion Unit to receive more information about opportunities to access teaching with specific support and tools.
