## Prerequisites

- Know the theoretical foundations of Limit Calculus for functions of one real variable. Be able to compute limits for functions of one real variable.
- Know the theoretical foundations of Differential Calculus for functions of one real variable. Be able to compute derivatives, solve optimization problems in one real variable, and use Taylor and Maclaurin expansions.
- Know the theoretical foundations of Integral Calculus for functions of one real variable. Be able to compute antiderivatives, definite integrals, and improper integrals.
- Know the theoretical foundations of Linear Algebra. Be able to work with matrices, compute the determinant of a matrix, diagonalize a matrix, and compute the rank of a matrix.
- Know the basic elements of Differential Equations. Be able to solve first-order linear equations, first-order nonlinear separable equations, and second-order linear equations with constant coefficients.

## Learning objectives

General description

The course provides a solid foundation in the concepts, methods, and tools of Mathematical Analysis that are useful for addressing problems involving functions of several real variables, such as:

- computing limits;
- solving unconstrained and constrained optimization problems;
- determining potentials for vector fields;
- computing multiple integrals for plane areas, volumes of solid figures, and other applications;
- qualitatively studying the solutions of a differential equation;
- studying the differentiability of functions of a complex variable.

Knowledge and competencies to be acquired

1) Knowledge and understanding

- Know the definitions, concepts, statements, and fundamental methods of the topology of a multidimensional Euclidean space, differential and integral calculus for functions of several real variables, and the Theory of Ordinary Differential Equations.
- Understand the theoretical meaning of the main results presented and the relationships among the various topics of the course.
- Be able to analyse and understand a hypothetical-deductive proof.

2) Applying knowledge and understanding

- Be able to correctly use tools and methods of Mathematical Analysis to solve exercises and problems.
- Be able to identify the most appropriate techniques in relation to the different types of theoretical and applied problems addressed in the course.
- Be able to apply definitions, theorems, and computational procedures in new contexts that are nevertheless consistent with the topics covered.

3) Making judgements

- Develop the ability to critically analyse mathematical problems, assessing the adequacy of the methods used and the correctness of the results obtained.
- Be able to identify assumptions, conditions of applicability, and limitations of the theoretical tools introduced in the course.
- Acquire autonomy in devising solution strategies and in logically verifying the procedures adopted.

4) Communication skills

- Acquire a solid command of mathematical language, with the ability to present definitions, theorems, and solution procedures correctly, clearly, and effectively.
- Be able to present proofs with logical rigour and argumentative coherence.
- Be able to appropriately use the symbolism, notation, and terminology specific to Mathematical Analysis.

5) Learning skills

- Acquire logical, analytical, and methodological skills useful for further studies in mathematics and the sciences.
- Develop an autonomous and rigorous study method capable of fostering individual in-depth study of the topics covered.
- Be able to independently consult and use textbooks and supporting materials related to the topics of the course.

## Innovative teaching methods: Teaching and learning strategies

In addition to lectures and guided exercise sessions, the course may include formative self-assessment opportunities, discussion of common mistakes, analysis of examples and counterexamples, and the use of digital tools to visualise mathematical concepts and phenomena. Guided activities may also be proposed on the critical and responsible use of generative Artificial Intelligence tools, aimed at supporting individual study, checking the clarity of explanations and arguments, comparing different solution strategies, and identifying possible errors or gaps in a solution. ## Examination methods

The examination consists of a compulsory final written test and, where applicable, an additional assessment, to be taken in the same examination session.

The final written test lasts approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes and includes a section of exercises to be completed through calculations and written arguments, as well as a theoretical section. The theoretical section may require:

- the precise formulation of a definition and/or its illustration by means of an example;
- the statement of a theorem or proposition among those listed in the official course programme, possibly including its proof;
- the solution of a theoretical exercise.

The additional assessment, which takes place a few days after the written test and within the same examination session, is a second part of the examination and is:

- compulsory if the written test meets the minimum requirement, see the assessment criteria, but the mark obtained is not sufficient to pass the examination;
- compulsory if the Examination Board has doubts concerning the authenticity of the written test;
- optional if the written test has been passed, but the student wishes to attempt to improve their mark. The additional assessment may modify the final mark either positively or negatively.

The additional assessment consists of theoretical questions and/or exercises, as in the written test, and may be followed by a brief oral interview.

During the examination, communication with other candidates is not permitted. Subject to any exceptions indicated by the University’s Inclusion Office, the use of notes, books, and electronic devices of any kind is not permitted. In particular, the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools is not permitted during examination tests. Any breach of these rules entails a negative assessment of the examination test and the referral of the persons responsible for such breach to the University’s disciplinary committee.

## Assessment methods

The assessment criteria are:

- ability to formulate and solve exercises correctly and with methodological rigour;
- clarity and completeness of the arguments provided;
- proper and precise use of logical-deductive language;
- ability to adapt known techniques to unfamiliar situations.

In the assessment of the written test:

- 2/3 of the score is assigned to the exercise section;
- 1/3 of the score is assigned to the theoretical section.

The examination is passed if both parts of the written test are completed correctly for at least 50% each, minimum requirement, and the overall mark is at least 18/30.

The additional assessment may result in an increase, confirmation, or decrease of the mark obtained in the written test. If the final overall mark is below 18/30, the entire examination must be repeated in a subsequent examination session.

## Contents

Summary programme: * Topology of Euclidean Spaces, limit calculus and continuity for functions of vector variables. * Differential Calculus for functions of vector variables, with applications to unconstrained and constrained optimization. * Vector fields, computation of potentials. * Integral Calculus for functions of vector variables. * Differential Equations: existence and uniqueness, qualitative study of solutions. * Differentiability of functions of a complex variable.
