## Contents

Introduction and summary of Applied Thermodynamics (6 hours). Energy sources and energy production. Classification of fluid machines and energy conversion systems. First Law of Thermodynamics, thermodynamic properties of fluids, thermodynamic diagrams and cycles. Performance of fluid machinery (10 hours). Classification of fluid machines. The compression and expansion processes. Real work, adiabatic and polytropic processes. Adiabatic, isothermal and polytropic efficiency. Fluid machines (6 hours). The stage of a turbomachine: the stator and the rotor. The Euler equation and the velocity triangles. Nozzles and diffusers. The shape of nozzles and diffusers. The flow in rotating machines. The shape of the blades. Operating machines (12 hours). Pumps, compressors and fans. Performance and main construction features. Choice of the operating machine in relation to the circuit. Operating machines in series and in parallel. Pump cavitation. Outline of reciprocating machines. Macchine motrici (8 ore). Turbines (8 hours). Classification and performance of turbines. Impulse and reaction turbines. Steam and gas turbines. Wind turbines: energy balance, power coefficient and power curve. Hydraulic turbines. Pelton, Francis e Kaplan machines. Power curve. Power generation plants (6 hours). Overall efficiency and specific fuel consumption of a power plant. Capacity factor and electricity production cost. Electricity market, incentives to power generation from renewable sources, CO2 emission factors. Steam-power plants (12 hours). Rankine cycle, energy balance and cycle efficiency. Influence of operating parameters on the performance of a steam cycle. Superheating and thermal regeneration. Plant configuration. The main system components: steam generator, condenser, pumps, turbine and heat exchangers. Outline of polluting emissions. Organic Rankine Cycles. Steam power plants fueled by biomass and integrated with concentrated solar collectors. Gas turbines (10 hours). Brayton cycle, energy balance and cycle efficiency. Maximum useful work and maximum efficiency. Adoption of regeneration in gas turbines. Current technologies of micro gas turbines, aeroderivative and industrial gas turbines. Outline of polluting emissions. Combined cycle power plants (4 hours). The basic scheme of a combined-cycle gas / steam power plant. The energy balance of the heat recovery steam generator. Overall efficiency. Combined heat and power plants (4 hours). The combined production of electricity and heat. System configurations and performance (efficiency and primary energy saving). Hydro Power Plants (4 hours). Basic scheme. Energy balance e conversion efficiency. Evaluation of energy production. Hydro Pumping plants. Wind and Photovoltaic systems (8 hours). Wind frequency distribution. Power curve of a wind turbine. Evaluation of the energy production. Photovoltaic effect. Cell and module efficiency. Evaluation of the energy production. Energy storage systems.

## Learning objectives

According to the educational objectives of the Bachelor's Degree, the specific educational objectives of the course are to give the basic knowledge in the field of fluid machines and sustainable power generation systems. According to the 5 Dublin descriptors, the main learning outcomes of the course are as follows: Knowledge and understanding • Achieve the basic knowledge about the operation of fluid machines. • Achieve the basic knowledge about the configuration and the operation of steam power plants, gas turbines, combined cycles and combined heat and power systems. • Achieve the basic knowledge about the configuration and the operation of hydro power plants, photovoltaic and wind power generation systems. Applying Knowledge and understanding • Achieve the ability to calculate the performance of prime movers (turbines) or operating machines (pumps, compressors, fans). • Achieve the ability to choose the pump or the fan required by hydraulic circuits or ventilation systems. • Achieve the ability to describe the working principle and the thermodynamic cycle of steam power plants, gas turbines, combined cycles and combined heat and power systems. • Achieve the ability to evaluate the main performance (efficiency, power output and net energy production) of power generation plants or combined heat and power systems. • Achieve the ability to evaluate the main performance of hydro, photovoltaic and wind power generation plants according to the primary source availability. • Achieve the ability to evaluate the energy production cost of a power generation plant. Making judgements • Achieve the ability to find technical and cost data from the equipment suppliers to perform a comparative assessment (qualitative and quantitative) between different energy power generation systems and find the most effective solution. Communication skills • Achieve the ability to represent the scheme and the main transformations of fluid machines and thermodynamic cycles of power generation plants. • Achieve the ability to describe the configuration and the operation of fluid machines and power generation plants. Learning skills • Achieve the ability to use the contents of other courses to enhance the knowledge in the field of fluid machines and power generation plants. • The course will also allow to consolidate the skills required by the master’s degree courses as well as for the professional training.

## Prerequisites

It is required a suitable knowledge of math (algebra, derivative and simple integrals), physics and thermodynamics. Fundamentals of chemistry are also useful.

## Teaching methods

The course has a total duration of 90 hours, 60 hours of lectures and 30 hours of practical exercises. On the basis of the “Manifesto degli Studi” of the University of Cagliari, Lectures will be prevalently held in classrooms, also integrated with online teaching resources, by using specific online platforms managed by the University of Cagliari. Lectures will be held even with the use of Power Point presentations and other documents usable online. The practical exercises are solved in the classroom by the teacher and concern the design and the performance evaluation of single components and overall energy systems. For each topic will also be assigned to the students a specific homework which includes practical problems similar to those solved in the classroom by the teacher.

## Assessment methods

The final examination includes a written test and an oral examination. The written test, lasting max 2 hours, includes 5-6 practical problems similar to those solved in the classroom. The course includes intermediate written test. A positive evaluation during the intermediate test will exempt from the final written test. The oral examination concerns the description of plant schemes, mode of operation and performance evaluation of fluid machines and energy systems. The final mark is reported on a 30 basis and depends on the marks of the written test (50%) and oral examination (50%). The mark of the written test is reported on a 30 basis and is the weighted average of the single problems. The weights depend on the complexity and difficulty level of each problem. The rank of 18/30 is assigned for an elementary level of knowledge/ability while the rank of 30/30 is assigned for an excellent level of knowledge/ability. The following criteria are applied for the evaluation of the written test: a) suitability of the solution process, b) suitability of assumptions, c) Correctness of calculations. The mark of the oral examination test is reported on a 30 basis. The rank of 18/30 is assigned for an elementary level of knowledge/ability while the rank of 30/30 is assigned for an excellent level of knowledge/ability. The following criteria are applied for the evaluation of the oral examination: a) Correctness and completeness of answers, b) Analysis of the problem, c) language skills, d) Effectiveness of presentation. Criteria a) and b) are strictly required to pass the examination.
