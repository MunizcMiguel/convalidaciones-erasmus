## Contents

- INTRODUCTION. Definition, the product life cycle, the main manufacturing processes, the relationship between processes and materials, the technological system, and the accuracy of transformations. - METAL CASTING. Fundamentals of casting and classification, mold, pattern, core, undercut, pouring of the molten metal, cooling effects, and castability. Expendable mold casting processes: sand casting and mold making, other expendable mold casting processes. Permanent mold casting processes: the basic permanent-mold process, low-pressure casting, die casting (cold-chamber and hot-chamber die casting). - METAL FORMING. Fundamentals of plasticity, constitutive models, tension test, axisymmetric and plane strain compression tests, friction, workability, classification of metalworking processes. Forging: definitions, forgeability, forging machines, factors affecting the die filling, flashing, open die forging, close die forging and other related processes, flash formation, pre-forming, forging forces. Sheet forming: anisotropy and formability of sheet metals. Shearing, blanking, and punching. Sheet bending: V bending, elastic spring back, bending force, and other bending processes. Deep drawing of axisymmetric parts: engineering analysis, choice of process parameters, calculation of the blank diameter. Deep drawing of non-axisymmetric parts and sheet stamping. - MATERIAL REMOVAL PROCESSES. Definitions, cutting and feed motions, cutting parameters, theory of chip formation in metal machining, actual chip formation, types of chip, build-up edge, cutting forces, specific energy, thermal aspects in cutting, three-dimensional cutting, machinability, tool wear, and tool life, cutting tool technology. - Welding: general aspects of welding. Welding techniques: Arc welding, TIG, MIG, MAG, diffusion bonding, chemical, laser welding, braising, and solid-state welding FSW.

## Learning objectives

Knowledge of foundry technology as a method to vary the shape of semi-finished products and the consequent choice of different casting techniques. Ability to recognize and avoid the most common defects of castings. Knowledge of the mechanism of plastic deformation of metallic materials and the most common processing techniques. The course enables students to acquire advanced knowledge of the processes required to transform raw material into finished products with market value, through processes carried out on machines or systems. This knowledge, by integrating notions acquired in the courses of material science, machine design, and mechanical drawing, will form the insights that will enrich knowledge and operational capabilities in the field of manufacturing processes in mechanics, so that the student acquires a clear awareness of the wider multidisciplinary context of engineering.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of fundamental aspects concerning chemical bonds, state of matter, the most common engineering metallic materials, heat treatments, technical drawings, and stress and strain states is required.

## Teaching methods

There are 80 hours of classroom lectures and 10 hours of tutorials, some numerical in the classroom or the laboratory, with illustrations of the most common machine tools and their operating principle.

## Assessment methods

The examination consists of a written test characterized by 10 multi-choice and 4 open-answer questions and/or exercises. Optional oral integration allows for expressing better the preparation highlighted in the written test. It is planned to carry out tests in the itinerary, to be done individually or in groups, during teaching considering the descriptors of Dublin. The student must be able to analyze the main processes used in the manufacturing industry, with reference to their characteristics and the choice of process parameters, applying, in autonomy, the methodologies and own tools of mechanical technology. Aspects such as the mastery of technical language and the clarity of exposure to the topics discussed will also be evaluated. In addition, it will be necessary to demonstrate the ability to properly use the knowledge acquired in solving simple problems. The ability to independently address the subjects covered by applying the methodologies and tools of mechanical technology, the clarity of exposure, and the mastery of technical language are valued. Online auto-evaluation tests are carried out on the Moodle platform with multi-choice quiz questions that provide a statistical analysis of the level of learning related to each topic. For allocating the final grade, Multiple-choice questions weigh 10/30, while the three open-ended questions submitted in the examination test have a weight of 5 points each. Students who demonstrate sufficient capacity to respond to all the questions formulated will achieve the minimum vote, equal to eighteen points. The maximum remarks, equal to thirty points cum laude, will be attributed to students who have demonstrated complete mastery of the subject, exposed in full autonomy and with appropriate technical language. Mid-term exams are available during the course.
