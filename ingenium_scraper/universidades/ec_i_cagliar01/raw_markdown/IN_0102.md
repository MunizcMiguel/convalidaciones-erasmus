# CONSTRUCTION THEORY (IN/0102)

## Contents

- Forces and moments, couples of forces, equivalent force systems (2h lesson). - Constraints, external loads, structural elements, materials (2h lesson). - Geometry of masses: static moments, centres of mass (or centroids), Varignon’s theorem, moments of inertia, Huygens’ theorem, principal moments and directions, Mohr’s representation (7h lesson; 7h tutorial). - Kinematic analysis of beam systems: degrees of freedom and of constraints, internal and external constraints, statically under-constrained/determinate/indeterminate structures, theorems of kinematic chains (3h lesson; 4h tutorial). - Static analysis of beam systems: equations of statics, support reactions (2h lesson). - Statically determinate structures: determination of support reactions and internal actions (6h lesson; 6h tutorial). - Mechanics of solids: analysis of deformation (small displacements kinematics, components of rigid motion and deformation, principal components and directions of deformation, compatibility conditions), analysis of stress (stress tensor, Cauchy’s theorem, principal components and directions of stress, invariants of the tensor, Mohr’s representations, plane stress states), equations of balance, boundary conditions (9h lesson). - Basic laws of elasticity: elastic constitutive law, special case of isotropy, Hooke’s law, Lamé’s constants, engineering meaning of elastic constants (2h lesson). - De Saint Venant’s problems: axial force, pure and bi-axial bending, coupled axial force and bending, shear, torsion (12h lesson). - Principle of Virtual Work: determination of hyperstatic support reactions and displacements (5h lesson; 8h tutorial). - Equations of the elastic curve (4h lesson; 5h tutorial). - Instability: Euler’s theory (6h lesson).

## Reference texts

- Strength of materials. 1976. S. Timoshenko. Krieger Publishing Company. - Theory of Elasticity. 1970. S. Timoshenko and J. N. Goodier. McGraw Hill.

## Learning objectives

I - Knowledge and understanding: The student will gain a basic understanding of the mechanical behaviour within the elastic range of plane structures subjected to point and distributed loads, and of the determination of stress and strain states in elastic solids, with the help of advanced textbooks. II - Applying knowledge and understanding: The student will acquire knowledge and skills that will enable him/her to analyse the structural behaviour and the stress/strain state of plane structures in the linear elastic range, solving the proposed problems with a professional approach. III - Making judgements: The student will gain the autonomous ability to identify and propose appropriate procedures for the calculation of stresses and strains in both statically determinate and statically indeterminate plane structures operating in the linear elastic range. IV - Communication skills: The student will gain the capability of describing to specialists and non-specialists the problems to tackle and the solutions to adopt concerning the stress-strain analysis of plane structures, making use of valid technical reasoning. V - Learning skills: The student will gain the ability to deal with typical issues related to the study of plane structures operating in the elastic range, developing the necessary theoretical skills to undertake more advanced studies in the future.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written and oral test. In particular, in the written test three questions will be asked: 1) calculation of static moments, centroid’s position, moments of second order and principal axes of inertia of a plane figure; 2) solution of a plane isostatic structure; 3) solution of a plane hyperstatic structure. The oral test will be based on the determination of the stress state in a cross-section of a simple structure and on a question concerning the theory developed during the frontal lectures. The maximum mark of the written test will be 30. In order to be admitted to the oral test, the student will have to pass the written test with a minimum mark of 18/30. In the overall evaluation, the final mark will take into account the following aspects: 1) ability to calculate the support reactions and internal actions in a structure, both isostatic and hyperstatic; 2) ability to calculate the displacement of a point in a statically determinate structure; 3) ability to calculate the geometrical features of a plane figure; 4) ability to evaluate the stress state in a prismatic beam. In order to pass the exam with the minimum mark (18/30), the student will have to show at least a basic knowledge of statics and elastic constitutive laws, as well as calculate the internal actions of a structure drawing the corresponding diagrams and determine the stress state in a prismatic beam. In order to pass the exam with the maximum mark (30/30 e lode), the student will have to show an excellent knowledge of all the topics covered during the course. In cases of necessity related to the containment measures of the Covid-19 pandemic, different methods of learning assessment will be adopted, such as written and oral exams online with the help of digital supports.
