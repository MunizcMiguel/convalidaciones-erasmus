## Contents

The course addresses the main aspects of integrated and flexible logistics systems, with a particular focus on the optimization of physical and information flows, automation, and process standardization. The program is structured around the following topics: 1.Fundamentals of Integrated and Flexible Logistics Definition and role of the logistics function in production and distribution systems; Integration of logistics processes and automation of flows; Flexibility as a response to demand variability and cross-functionality of the logistics role; Definition of production systems and classification of products, production systems, and service facilities. 2.Evolution of Production Systems and Enabling Technologies Integrated logistics and the role of CIM systems; Introduction to Group Technology and Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS); Industrial applications of work islands and flexible manufacturing systems. 3.Product Innovation and Packaging Product life cycle and its impact on logistics decisions; Product and packaging design and development; Management of pallets and load units, container standardization, volumetric efficiency, and packaging materials; Case studies and practical exercises on packaging. 4.Layout and Design of Logistics Systems Analysis of production system layouts and impact on logistics decisions; Layout study and design through Flow Value Analysis (FVA); Sizing and optimization of internal warehouse and production flows. 5.Transport and Material Handling Systems Traditional material handling equipment (forklifts, rigid conveyors, elevators, pneumatic and hydraulic systems); Flexible automated guided vehicles (AGVs), features, computerized control, and design criteria; Economic analysis and sizing of transport systems. 6.Warehousing and Storage Functions and types of warehouses, storage methods, and design criteria; Automated high-density warehouses, management of stacker cranes, and cycle time evaluation; Automation of information flows and integration with production systems. 7.Distribution Logistics and Transportation Distribution flows, transport mode characteristics, and intermodality; Combined road-rail, road-air, and maritime transport; Logistics platforms and the role of infrastructure in service competitiveness. 8.Information Flow Management and Production Planning MRP, JIT, and kanban systems for material requirements planning and production scheduling; Aggregate planning, Master Production Schedule, and material planning; Simulation of logistics systems and evaluation of performance parameters. 9.Maintenance and Spare Parts Management Role of maintenance in integrated production and logistics systems; Spare parts management, coding, classification, and procurement policies; Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) and tools for optimizing system availability.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide specific knowledge in the field of logistics engineering and management, introducing the main tools for the analysis, design, and optimization of material handling, storage, and distribution processes. Particular emphasis is placed on the integration of physical and information flows, the role of flexibility as a response to market variability, the impact of layout and packaging on logistics choices, as well as the use of innovative technologies for decision support and performance improvement. The intended learning outcomes are as follows: Knowledge and Understanding • Acquire fundamental knowledge and the ability to interpret the characteristics and objectives of integrated logistics in production and distribution systems. • Gain knowledge of the principles of classification of production systems, warehouses, and both traditional and advanced logistic equipment (e.g., AGVs, automated warehouses). • Acquire a basic understanding of the strategic role of packaging and container standardization in logistics decision-making. • Develop knowledge of production information flows, with particular reference to MRP, JIT, and kanban systems. • Understand the key concepts related to the impact of production layout on logistics performance and the principles of flexible design. Applying Knowledge and Understanding • Develop the ability to analyze and design layouts and material handling systems consistent with different production and distribution typologies. • Acquire the ability to evaluate and select packaging and load unit solutions aimed at improving logistics flow efficiency. • Apply methods and tools for material requirements planning and production scheduling. • Develop practical skills in sizing logistics systems (warehouses, AGVs, FMS, assembly lines), taking into account both technical and economic aspects. Making Judgments • Acquire the ability to evaluate the performance of a logistics system in terms of flexibility, efficiency, and costs, and to propose appropriate improvements. • Identify the interconnections between logistics management, layout design, service level, and economic–environmental sustainability. Communication Skills • Acquire the ability to prepare technical reports on the design and evaluation of material handling and storage systems. • Present the results of logistics analyses and simulations, justifying design choices and their operational and economic impacts. • Develop the ability to communicate effectively using technical and professional terminology within the field of logistics systems management. Learning Skills • Acquire the ability to integrate logistics knowledge with other subjects within the degree program (production, maintenance, safety, sustainability). • Develop the capacity to use the acquired methods and techniques as a foundation for further study and professional development, with a particular focus on innovative and digital technologies for Logistics 4.0 and 5.0.

## Prerequisites

Students are expected to possess adequate knowledge and skills in fundamental mathematical tools (algebra, basic derivatives and integrals) as well as in elementary statistical methods. The course is closely related to the course on Production Planning and Control (Programmazione e Controllo della Produzione.).

## Teaching methods

The course consists of lectures, numerical exercises, and the discussion of business cases. It also includes guest lectures by industry managers and visits to industrial plants.

## Assessment methods

The student's proficiency is tested through a written examination of the content of the lectures and exercise sessions covering the whole syllabus. During the course, there will be two partial written intermediate evaluation tests. The test is passed if the student in both parts receives a rating of at least 18/30. The final vote is the arithmetic average of the two partial written tests (rounded up). Those who were not satisfied with the marks obtained in partial examinations may repeat the written test. The repetition of a written test implies the annulment of previous written examination. The student must prove to have reached sufficient learning outcomes as knowledge and comprehension of the main criteria and methods to support engineering and management decisions for industrial maintenance and industrial safety. For the evaluation of the examination, the determination of the score takes into account the following factors: a) Correctness and completeness of responses, b) Ability to analyze the problem, c) Language property, d) Clarity of writing e) Adequacy of the solution process, f) Adequacy of hypotheses, g) Correctness of the calculations, e) Clarity in the representation and discussion of results. These elements contribute in an equal manner to the formulation of the score concerning the exercises. The mark of the examination test is reported on a 30 basis. The grade of 18/30 is awarded when the demonstrated knowledge/skills are at least elementary, 30/30 cum laude is awarded when the displayed knowledge is excellent.
