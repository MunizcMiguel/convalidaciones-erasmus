## Contents

BUSINESS INVESTMENTS AND ECONOMIC-FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY (48 hours of lectures and 12 hours of classroom exercises) * Introductory elements: choice problems; investments and costs; remuneration of unavailable money. * Interest: the loan transaction; cash flow diagram; simple interest; compound interest; nominal annual interest rate; effective interest rate. * Principles of economic and financial equivalence: equivalence concept; calculation of equivalence with a single factor; calculation of the equivalence with several factors; use of linear interpolation; financial tables. * Mortgages: add-on typology; mortgages loan typology; fixed interest rate mortgages; variable interest rate mortgages; mortgage balance; the financial bubble of subprime mortgages. * Bonds: investment opportunities; bonds and shares in comparison; zero coupon bonds; bonds with a payment coupon determined by interests; project bonds. * Inflation: price index; inflation rate; the effects on cash flows. * Analysis of private sector investments: investment decisions; accounting methodology; financial methodology; pay-back period; net present value (NPV); future value; annual equivalent; internal rate of return (IRR); minimum attractive rate of return; weighted average cost of capital; the classification of investment projects; the choice between the different investment alternatives: comparisons based on total and incremental values; the comparison between investment alternatives of different duration. * Investments in the public sector: public administration and social welfare; cost-benefit analysis: aggregate ratio, net ratio and Lorie-Savage ratio; cost-effectiveness analysis: fixed cost method and fixed effectiveness method. * Financial statements: characteristics and structure of financial statements; balance sheet; income statement. * Taxation systems in Italy: main taxes of the Italian system; tax on the income of physical persons; corporate income tax; value added tax: basic method and tax method. * Project financing: the profitability concept; type of works; economic and financial sustainability; public-private partnership; law references; viability test; economic and financial analysis; profitability indicators; debt coverage indicators; economic and financial model. MICROECONOMIC ASPECTS RELATED TO INVESTMENT PROJECTS (16 hours of lectures and 4 hours of classroom exercises) * Microeconomics in the context of business and management engineering. * Perfect competition. * Elasticity. * Theory of consumer behavior. * Enterprise theory. DEVELOPMENT OF ECONOMIC-FINANCIAL MODELS OF INVESTMENT PROJECTS THROUGH PROJECT FINANCING IN THE INDUSTRIAL SECTOR, ASSESSMENT OF THEIR FEASIBILITY, AND VERIFICATION OF THE ROBUSTNESS OF THE RESULTS OBTAINED (8 hours of lectures and 2 hours of classroom exercises) * Selection of the investment project in the industrial sector. * Development of the economic-financial model in project financing: revenue projections; cost projections; income statement; cash flows. * Assessment of economic-financial feasibility. * Verification of the robustness of the results obtained: sensitivity analysis; scenario analysis.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide the basic skills of business and management engineering relating to the sustainability of industrial investment projects. In particular, the course aims to provide the basic notions of loan methods as well as repayment of capital, analysis of investments in the private and public sector, project financing, public-private partnership techniques, financial statement, different taxation systems in Italy, and microeconomic aspects related to investment projects. Furthermore, the course seeks to equip students with the fundamental skills for developing economic-financial models of investment projects in the industrial sector through project financing, for assessing their feasibility, and to verify the robustness of the results obtained through sensitivity and scenario analysis. Teaching methods will allow students: - To acquire the aforementioned basic skills, to understand the structures and economic-financial evaluations of investment projects in the private and public industrial sector as well as the sustainability of the same projects (knowledge and understanding). - To apply the basic knowledge and understandings acquired in the industrial sector, with the support of the problem-solving skills and the basic quantitative tools acquired (applying knowledge and understanding). - To develop its own independent judgment in relation to the economic-financial evaluation of investment projects in the industrial sector (making judgements). - To develop their communication skills, in relation to the basic knowledge and understandings acquired, also with the aid of graphical and mathematical tools (communication skills). Finally, the final exam will allow the verification of the students' learning ability (learning skills).

## Assessment methods

The verification of the learning is carried out through a final examination consisting of a written test and a subsequent oral test. The written test is composed of number 8 exercises (number 6 exercises concerning the first part of the course and number 2 exercises concerning the second part of the course) and the time available to the students for the development of the same test is 120 minutes. The evaluation of the written test is expressed in twentieths. To be admitted to the oral test, it is necessary to obtain a minimum score of 12/20 in the written test. The oral examination consists of two questions (one on the first part of the course and one on the second part), aimed at verifying the level of knowledge demonstrated in the written test, and the presentation of a case study in the industrial sector, related to the third part of the course. The assessment of the oral examination is expressed in tenths. The final grade of the exam is expressed in thirtieths and is obtained by summing the evaluations obtained in the written and oral exam. The minimum final grade required to pass the exam is 18/30. Students will have to demonstrate that they know and can apply the basic knowledge of economic and management engineering covered by the course, have mastered technical language and are able to explain the topics covered clearly. Furthermore, they will have to demonstrate the ability to appropriately use the acquired knowledge to solve specific problems as well as to face autonomously the treated topics applying the methodologies and tools of the course. The minimum grade (18/30) will be achieved by students who demonstrate all the above in a sufficient manner. The maximum grade (30/30 with honors) will be achieved by students who demonstrate all the above in an excellent manner. A written mid-term exam is available for students attending the course.
