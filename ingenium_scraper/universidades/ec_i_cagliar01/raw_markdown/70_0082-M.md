## Contents

The course is divided into 5 parts (on 5 distinct themes) as specified below: 1. General Principles of analysis and evaluation of energy systems and their performance. Primary energy sources, energy statistics. Energy equation for internal and external combustion systems. Thermodynamics review. Analysis of thermodynamic power cycles in the ideal, limit and real conditions. Efficiency of a thermal power plant. Utilization factor of a power plant, cost of energy produced. (20h les. - 6h ex.). 2. Steam turbine plants. Rankine and Hirn cycles. Efficiency and specific work. Methods to increase efficiency. Regeneration with steam extractions. Energy balances. System configuration shemes, and component characteristics. Steam generator; steam turbine; condenser, deaerator, regenerative heat exchangers, extraction and feed pumps. Fuels for steam plants. Pollution control. (18h les. - 13h ex.). 3. Gas turbine plants. Elementary circuit and thermodynamic cycle. Efficiency and specific work of ideal, limit and real cycles. Closed circuit gas turbines. Thermodynamics regeneration. Energy balance of gas turbine plants. Power regulation. Current technologies of industrial and aero-derivative gas turbines. High temperature materials. Fuels for gas turbines. Pollution control. (14h les. - 4h ex.). 4. Notes on the combined plants and on cogeneration plants. Gas-steam combined cycle power plants; guiding principles and simplified schemes. Energy balance and efficiency of combined cycles. Plant for combined production of heat and power (CHP). Characteristic indices of cogeneration. (4h les. - 2 ex.). 5. Notes on the hydropower plants. Generalities and classification. Run-of-the-river and basin plants. Pumped-storage systems. (2h les., 1h ex.). Visit to departmental laboratories (2h) Visits to a power station. As part of the course is normally scheduled a visit to a thermal power plant. (4h).

## Learning objectives

The course is aimed at the study of the main thermal power plants for the industrial production of mechanical energy. The educational objectives and expected results are the following: 1. To acquire the fundamental knowledge and ability to interpret the structural and functional characteristics of the thermal power plants based on steam and gas turbines, their operating principles, performance, environmental implications, technological evolution. 2. To provide capacity, starting from the knowledge gained, to represent, analyze and evaluate the thermodynamic cycles and function schemes of steam and gas turbine plants, to set and solve mass and energy balances of the system and its fundamentals components, and to evaluate the performance characteristics. 3. To acquire the ability to recognize plant components and system solutions of different sizes, types, and configurations, to estimate the orders of magnitude of the different performance indices in relation to these characteristics, and to perform comparative analysis and assessment in qualitative and quantitative terms. 4. To acquire the ability to represent, describe, summarize and comment, in graphical, written, and oral form, the thermodynamic cycles, the physical processes, the functional schemes, the system configurations, the technological solutions, and the formulation of energy balances of steam and gas turbine plants and their basic components. 5. To acquire the ability to use the fundamental knowledge and methods of analysis learned for the study of the subject at a higher level, with particular reference to the study of more complex and efficient energy systems, the most advanced technologies in developing, related subjects concerning, in particular, the rational use of energy and the environmental impact of energy systems.

## Prerequisites

The student must have adequate knowledge and skills in the core subjects and specifically (but not exhaustively): Mathematics: algebraic equations, exponential and trigonometric functions, limits, derivatives and integrals. Physics: principles of mechanics, physical quantities and units of measure. Thermodynamics: principles of thermodynamics, the concept of "system" (open and closed), equations of state, state functions and thermodynamic properties of fluids, specific heat, thermodynamic diagrams (T-s, h-s, p-v), thermodynamic cycles, mass and energy balances Fluid dynamics: concept of "control volume" (open system), characteristics of one-dimensional stationary flows, balance of matter and energy applied to the control volume. The course is strongly correlated with the course of Fluid Machinery.

## Teaching methods

The 5 distinct themes which make up the course each include lectures and practical exercises in the classroom, for about 58 hours of lectures and 26 hours of practice. The course includes a visit to a thermal power plant in Sardinia, of an actual duration of about 4 hours, with emphasis on the theme of steam thermal plants; normally the visit is preceded by a seminar held by the technicians of the station. In accordance with the provisions of the University management, teaching will be provided in classroom, with the check of the attendance in class.

## Other information

The written test focuses mainly on exercises carried out in class during the course. The teacher is supported by his collaborators (research fellows and PhD students) in carrying out exercises and assistance to the study, usually available every day in normal working hours.

## Assessment methods

The examination of Energy Systems is based on a written test and an oral exam. The written test is aimed at verifying the ability to apply the knowledge and assessment tools acquired during the course to the solution of practical problems. More specifically, the test consists in performing some exercises on key exam topics (evaluation of system performance of steam and gas turbine power plants, calculation of specific consumption and costs, energy balance of plant components, etc.) along the lines of exercises in the classroom. During the course there will be two partial written intermediate evaluation tests. They are exempted from the official written test, limited to the June-July session following the end of the course, students who supported both partial tests with rating of at least 18/30. Those who were not satisfied with the marks obtained in partial examinations may repeat the written test. The repetition of a written test implies the annulment of previous written examination. The written test and the intermediate assessment tests will be approved if all the exercises of which they are composed will be carried out successfully. Written tests carried out in regular exam appeals are valid only for the session concerned (June-July, September, January-February, autumn and spring sessions reserved for off-course students). The written test expires and must in any case be repeated in the case of a negative oral examination. The evaluation of the written test takes into account the conceptual and formal correctness of procedures adopted, as well as the correct calculations, assumptions, management units of measure, clarity, etc. The sum of points obtained in the various exercises gives the mark of the written test in 30th. The oral exam focuses on the evaluation of the knowledge of the more conceptual aspects of the subject. During the oral exam, based normally on 2-3 questions on topics related to the entire program of the course, students should mainly have the following skills: - be able to graph in thermodynamic planes transformations and thermodynamic cycles of steam and gas plants in the most common configurations and in ideal, limit and real cases, be able to give their description and to indicate the method of calculation; - be able to graph and describe functional schemes of the plants; - know and be able to describe the functional characteristics of machinery and plant components devoted to the main thermodynamic transformations of the working fluids; - have mastered the basic knowledge of thermodynamics recalled during the course (principles of thermodynamics, state variables, ideal gas law, equations of thermodynamic transformations, etc.); - be able to identify the boundaries of a system (from single components to the entire system) for the determination of mass and energy balances; - be able to apply and solve the mass and energy balance of systems with multiple flows in the absence and in the presence of chemical reactions; - be familiar with the units of measure of the physical magnitudes that characterize the processes of fluid machines and energy systems and their components in general; - know the order of magnitude of the main operating parameters (pressure, temperature, etc.) and of the main functional characteristics (efficiency, power, etc.) of the energy systems studied during the course and their individual components. For the purposes of the overall assessment prevails, usually, the result of the oral exam.
