### Learning objectives

The course is divided into two modules: Economics and Business Economics. The Economics module intends to provide the student with the theoretical and applied tools necessary to understand the economic behavior of subjects operating in product and production factor markets, with particular attention to that of businesses. At the end of the course the student must, therefore, possess knowledge of the economic principles underlying the functioning of markets, as well as the ability to use these principles to understand the essential aspects of the functioning of an economic system. The Business Economics module provides basic knowledge of business economics, through the use of examples and exercises taken from real business operations. In the initial part of the Business Economics module, a general notion of a company is provided and the structural and behavioral characteristics of the different types of companies are analysed, with systemic logic: supply and production for market exchange. The main management operations that characterize the life of companies are analysed, with particular reference to businesses, also observing the dynamics of economic and financial flows and the conditions that characterize operating economically. Subsequently, the basic elements of the company information system and of the accounting information subsystem in particular are presented, with reference to the logic of the surveys and quantitative determinations for the determination of operating income and working capital. The elements for reading the financial statements of a commercial company will be provided. Finally, the problem of the economic nature and the criteria for determining production costs is introduced. The expected learning outcomes can be defined based on the following Dublin Descriptors, according to the following scheme: 1) Knowledge and understanding. At the end of the course, students will know the microeconomic models for analyzing consumer spending and business production decisions. Additionally, students will learn about the implications of market failures. The course also allows the understanding of economic-business phenomena and the fundamental accounting methods and tools and allows the acquisition of a language specific to economic-business disciplines; 2) Ability to apply knowledge and understanding. At the end of the course, students will be able to analyze the production decisions of companies so as to understand the real dynamics taking place in the markets. The course also allows students to learn the basic elements relating to company accounting systems and provides the essential elements for reading and understanding the financial statements; 3) Independent judgment: At the end of the course, students will be able to interpret the data relating to real market dynamics and express autonomous judgments on the implications deriving from the choices of economic agents; the course also improves the ability to interpret accounting results, to formulate independent judgments and solutions, and to interpret data of economic-business relevance, also through examples. 4) Communication skills. Students will be encouraged to intervene in class and, furthermore, the course helps to improve students' communication skills through open-ended questions to answer and through the optional oral test. 5) Learning ability. The course develops the skills necessary to take further exams, particularly in economics and professional accounting subjects.

### Teaching methods

The teaching for this module consists of 60 hours of lectures (30 for each module), including thematic exercises aimed at applying the knowledge acquired during the lessons. Further comprehensive exercises and exam simulations will be conducted.

### Other information

Additional material will be provided to students, such as further readings and exercises, and slides used during lessons. Moreover, seminars with the participation of entrepreneurs and professionals may be organized to better understand the practical implications of the topics covered in the program.

### Assessment methods

Learning is verified through: - A written test consisting of open-ended questions on the topics of the program with respect to both modules, in which the student must demonstrate knowledge and correct application of the principles and methods learned during the course. Consistent with the descriptors identified in the training objectives, the following will be evaluated: 1. Clarity in expressing theoretical content (knowledge and understanding evaluation). 2) the ability to rework concepts and explain them, including through comparison with real cases faced in class or explored by the student individually (assessment of ability to apply knowledge and understanding). 3. Ability to adequately understand and interpret basic concepts of economics and business economics (judgment autonomy evaluation). 4) the clarity of exposition, ability to synthesize, and mastery in expounding the concepts learned (assessment communication skills) 5) knowledge of the theoretical models learned (assessment of learning ability). The exam score is expressed in thirtieths. The range for the final grade, upon passing the exam, spans: - From 18/30: for an elementary level of subject knowledge, where the student can only frame the topic of economic-business phenomena and demonstrates barely sufficient knowledge of accounting tools and methods, developing the paper with barely sufficient language proficiency. - To 30/30, with possible honors, if the student can logically and coherently systematize the knowledge presumed to have been acquired during the course, both in the context of economic-business phenomena and concerning accounting tools and methods, supporting the analysis with an excellent elaboration of the expressed concepts and an adequate mastery of technical and economic language.
