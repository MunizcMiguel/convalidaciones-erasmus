## Contents

Initially, the course focused on the the kinematic analysis of structures. Then, the statics of rigid bodies are examined, i.e., the calculation of internal actions and stresses. Finally, the principles of mechanical design and the criteria for selecting materials are covered. The course content is structured around the following points: • Kinematic analysis of the rigid body (5 hours); • Analysis of isostatic structures: support reactions and calculation of internal actions (12 hours); • Geometry of areas (4 hours); • Characteristics of stresses and De Saint Venant's cases: beams subjected to normal, bending, shear, and torsional forces; representation of stress states (Mohr's circles) (11 hours); • The mechanical properties of materials, mechanical tests for tension, compression, bending, and impact (7 hours + 15 hours in the laboratory); • Principle of Virtual Work: calculation of displacements and hyperstatic structures (11 hours); • Design and verification of mechanical components: • Strength criteria (maximum normal stress, maximum shear stress, maximum distortion energy, Coulomb's criterion) (8 hours); • Fatigue behavior (12 hours); • Material selection criteria: Ashby diagrams (6 hours);

## Learning objectives

According to the objectives of the Bachelor's Degree in Mechanical and Management Engineering, the goal of the course is to provide students with the theoretical and practical foundations of structural design methods, based on the mechanics of solids and structures, and the strength of mechanical elements subjected to static and fatigue loads. Starting from the phenomenological description of the mechanical behavior of materials in the linear-elastic range, various analysis methods for one-dimensional structures are illustrated and discussed. The course's learning objectives are as follows: • Knowledge and understanding. • Acquiring knowledge of the theoretical foundations of the mechanical behavior of materials for structural use and their experimental characterization under simple loading conditions. • Acquiring fundamental knowledge of the procedures for predicting the stress state, deformability, and strength of one-dimensional mechanical parts subjected to static loads. • Applying knowledge and understanding. • The student will acquire the necessary knowledge to identify and analyze the relationships between the material's constitutive properties and the structural behavior of the mechanical component. • The student will acquire the ability to analyze the deformation and stress states of structural elements to predict strength. • Making judgments. • The student will acquire the ability to identify procedures for characterizing the mechanical response of materials, identifying the most significant properties for selecting materials for use. • The student will gain the ability to assess the deformability and strength properties of mechanical components for a given structural application. • Communication skills. • Acquiring the ability to communicate with both specialists and non-specialists about the requirements and performance associated with specific mechanical properties of structural materials, explaining the experimental methodologies required for their characterization, and discussing possible design approaches for their use in srtuctural components. • Learning skills. • The student will be able to address original and innovative aspects related to the characterization, selection, and use of materials for the design, verification, and realization of structural components subjected to static loads with a high degree of autonomy.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of a series of lectures immediately followed by numerical examples and exercises for the application and direct verification of the presented theoretical concepts and calculation procedures The course includes a series of laboratory sessions for the direct application of the concepts related to the material characterizion. For the topics covered in the laboratory, a written report is required. Submission and approval of the report are necessary conditions for registration for the exam. Attending the lessons is mandatory.

## Assessment methods

To verify the actual acquisition of the expected knowledge and skills, the final exam consists of a written test structured in two parts: • Exercises. • Theoretical questions with open-ended answers. The overall score is the sum of the points obtained in the applied part (maximum score of 21) and in the theoretical part (maximum score of 10). Thus, the maximum score is 30. To pass the exam, it is necessary to achieve a passing grade in both parts, with a total score greater than or equal to 18. Therefore, if 21 points are assigned to the applied exercises and 9 to the theoretical part, the passing grade is reached by obtaining at least 12 points in the exercises and at least 5.5 points in the theory section. The evaluation of the written test will take into account the following aspects: appropriateness of assumptions and hypotheses, correctness of the solution procedure, and accuracy in performing calculations. Similarly, the evaluation of the theoretical test will depend on the following aspects: ability to analyze and discuss the problem, correctness and completeness of the answers, and clarity of exposition. No oral examination is foreseen as a complement to the written test.
