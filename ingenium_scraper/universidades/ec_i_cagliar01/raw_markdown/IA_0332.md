## Contents

GENERAL ASPECTS OF MANUFACTURING. Role and evolution of manufacturing, technical and management functions, planning and production control, decision-making process. DEMAND MANAGEMENT. General aspects, techniques of demand forecasting, qualitative methods, quantitative methods (causal and time series models), and application cases. INDEPENDENT DEMAND MATERIAL MANAGEMENT. The role of the stocks, stock accounting systems, classification methods, inventory costs, and models for management of continuous and independent demand materials (fixed order quantity and fixed order interval models). AGGREGATE PRODUCTION PLANNING. Fundamental aspects, aims, aggregate production planning process, quantitative models, disaggregation, and application cases. MASTER PRODUCTION SCHEDULING. Definitions, ATP stocks calculation, review of MPS, and application cases. MATERIAL REQUIREMENT PLANNING. MRP systems, input/output of MRP, processing of MRP plans, MRP update, uncertainty management in MRP, lot sizing, nervousness of MRP systems, and application cases. CAPACITY PLANNING. General aspects, techniques of capacity planning, input/output control, and application cases.

## Learning objectives

The course enables students to acquire advanced knowledge of all the necessary activities required to plan and control manufacturing production. This knowledge, by integrating the knowledge gained in the teachings of technologies and manufacturing systems, industrial equipment and industrial logistics, will form the insights that will enrich the understanding of the main management policies of production and for production planning tools in the medium/long term, so that the student acquires a clear awareness of the wider multidisciplinary context of engineering.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of manufacturing processes and the most commonly used production systems in the modern manufacturing industry is required. Basic knowledge of micro and macro economics is also required.

## Teaching methods

The course has a total duration of 60 hours, of which 45 are dedicated to lectures and 15 to practical exercises. In accordance with the provisions of the Study Programme, teaching will be delivered primarily in person, potentially integrated with online strategies, to ensure innovative and inclusive access. In this regard, handouts, PowerPoint presentations, and other documentation available online will also be utilised. The practical exercises involve the instructor working through practical problems related to the programming of the master production schedule, linking it to MRP and the assignment of individual jobs (machine/man) with the priority order.

## Assessment methods

The final exam consists of a written test on topics pertaining to the entire course syllabus, comprising questions on the theoretical topics of the course and exercises on the practical parts. During the semester, two interim written assessment tests are also conducted. Passing these tests with a grade of at least 18/30 exempts the student from taking the full exam. The content of the interim tests is similar to that of the final exam. The evaluation of the written test takes into account the acquisition of basic knowledge of the topics covered, the clarity of presentation, the formal and conceptual correctness of the procedures used, as well as the accuracy of the calculations and assumptions made. The grade for the written test is expressed in thirtieths. A grade of 18/30 is awarded when the knowledge and skills demonstrated are at least elementary, while a grade of 30/30, with possible honors (cum laude), is awarded when the knowledge is excellent. It is possible to integrate the examination by an oral examination.
