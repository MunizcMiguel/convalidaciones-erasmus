## Learning objectives

According to the educational objectives of the degree course in Mechanical Engineering, the course of fluid machinery aims to provide the fundamental knowledge for the study of fluid machinery and the understanding of their operation. The main expected learning outcomes for the course of Fluid Machines are the following: To know and understand the fundamental principles of fluid machines operation. Acquire the knowledge to solve problems related to the operation and use of fluid machines Understand the laws of thermodynamic transformations and fluid dynamic processes that occur in fluid machines. Acquire the skills necessary to perform energy assessments in engineering applications on fluid machines. Acquire the ability to make hypotheses, assumptions and simplifications of the processes that take place in fluid machines and that involve mass, energy and momentum balances Acquire the ability to graphically represent on thermodynamic planes the main transformations associated with the operation of fluid machines and the corresponding thermodynamic cycles they perform.

## Prerequisites

he knowledge of basic algebra, mathematical analysis, trigonometry, operations between vectors, gradient operators, divergence and rotor are essential. Knowledge of Fluid Dynamics content with particular importance to: - Fluid properties - Kinematics and fluid dynamics: volumetric and mass flow rate concept. - Equation of motion for perfect fluids and Bernoulli equation. - Lagrangian and Eulerian point of view and Reynolds transport theorem. - Conservation equations (mass, momentum and energy). - Laminar and turbulent flow regimes, - Flow in the ducts and head losses (Moody's diagram, the boundary layer). Knowledge of Thermodynamic content and in particular - Thermodynamic properties of fluids, and thermodynamic planes - State equation and ideal gas transformations - Principles of thermodynamics for closed systems. - Internal energy and enthalpy of the ideal gas - Reversible and irreversible processes

## Teaching methods

The course includes a series of lectures accompanied by illustrative examples and numerical applications of the topics covered. Additional material regarding numerical examples and exercises, slides, graphs diagrams, and tables are provided in files on the website teacher. Photos and schemes of real components of different fluid machines are also presented and discussed during the lesson to the class. Students are also regularly invited to answer questions that require critical reflection on the physical phenomena that govern the operation of machines and on the corresponding analytical equations. In particular, the course is divided into the following main topics: - Elements of Thermodynamics and Fluid Dynamics. (5 hours of lecture) - The volumetric machines. (20 hours of lectures) - The Turbomachinery: turbines and compressors. (50 hours of lectures) - Internal combustion engines. (15 hours). Additional 30 hours of numerical exercises are provided during the tutorial activity. The ways of teaching will be indicated by the Manifesto degli studi.

## Assessment methods

The examination needs, first of all, the overcoming of a written test, consisting of numerical exercises on the subjects of the course. This allow access to the oral examination which determines the final evaluation of the exam. Then, the student before to support the oral examination must demonstrate to be able to perform engineering calculations, solving simple and basic problems, and to apply the rules on the units of measure for physical quantities and the basic principles governing the operation of machines. The items of the written exam (at the end of the course) relate to the main topics of the course: Volumetric Machines, Turbomachines and Internal Combustion Engines. Written tests are a test of skills acquired by students but do not provide quantitative assessment of the exam. To pass the written test it is necessary to obtain a score higher than 50% of the maximum points that can be awarded overall for the test. This test provides only the eligibility to support the oral exam. The oral exam, instead, must test the student's ability to use the correct technical terminology and verify if the physical phenomena that govern the operation of the machines have been well assimilated and fully understood. To pass the exam it is also important that the student proves to know and is able to apply the general and fundamental laws of fluid dynamics and thermodynamics. Evaluation is obtained by expressing a total score of the oral exam as a sum of the score assigned to each of the three questions posed. A maximum score of 10 points for each question can be assigned. Questions concern the three main areas of the course: volumetric machines, turbomachines and internal combustion engines.
