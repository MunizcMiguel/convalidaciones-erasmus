## Learning objectives

The course in "Instrumentation & Measurement for Information Engineering" aims to provide second-year students with the fundamental concepts of measurements in general and those of the information sector in particular. The course analyses the problems of analog signal conditioning, illustrates the operation and performance of basic instrumentation, with particular reference to digital instrumentation, and provides the basis for continuous learning through the correct interpretation of technical bibliography, manufacturers' manuals, technical and legal standards. In more detail, the educational objectives of the course can be expressed according to the following five descriptors: - Knowledge and understanding Knowledge and understanding of the fundamental theoretical and applied aspects of measurements in the field of information in general, and those based on digital instruments in particular. - Ability to apply knowledge and understanding Ability to understand the performance of basic instrumentation and to choose measurement instrumentation based on technical and economic considerations. Awareness of the importance of safe use of a measurement system. - Autonomy of judgment. Ability to correctly interpret the results provided by a measurement system, based on the characteristics of its components. Ability to manage results, including assessing decision-making risks arising, for example, from the use of non-traceable instrumentation. - Communication skills. Ability to communicate information and discuss problems and solutions with specialist and nonspecialist interlocutors. - Learning skills Ability to learn continuously through the correct interpretation of technical bibliography, manufacturers' manuals, technical and legal standards.

## Teaching methods

The course has an overall duration of 60 hours. 48 hours consist of lessons and classroom exercises, during which the teacher presents the subjects indicated in the program and discusses some numerical examples. The remaining 12 hours involve experimental work in the laboratory, where the students, supervised by the teacher, use the basic measurement instrumentation (multimeters, oscilloscopes, data acquisition boards).

## Other information

The teacher provides the slides used for the presentation of lessons in electronic format. Datasheets of measurement devices and international standards are also presented and discussed.

## Assessment methods

The acquisition of the learning outcomes is verified through a written exam, during which students are asked to answer questions on some of the theory topics of the course or to solve numerical excersises, and an oral exam, during which students are asked (one or two questions) to discuss some of the topics covered in the course (theory and/or laboratory). The score of the examination is provided by a grade out of thirty, which is the average of the two marks. To pass the exam, thus reporting a mark of not less than 18/30, the student must demonstrate a basic knowledge of all the topics discussed. To achieve a score of 30/30 with honors, the student must demonstrate an excellent knowledge of the topics discussed. The assessment takes into account also the capacity of both present with rigor and effectiveness the theoretical subjects and highlight their practical aspects.
