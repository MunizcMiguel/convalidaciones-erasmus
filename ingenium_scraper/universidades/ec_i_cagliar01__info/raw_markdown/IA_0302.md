## Contents

Introduction to the course: electrical power systems Fundamentals on power systems: transmission, distribution systems and final use of electricity. Main components of electrical systems (transformers, generators, lines and loads) Fundamentals on operation and management of power systems (control and management systems, protection system, safety and reliability, electrical blackouts). Evolution of energy systems Enabling technologies and tools for the energy transition. Energy demand and energy systems. Conventional and renewable power plants. Study of the effects of the widespread use of network-connected distributed generation. The evolution of electricity demand. Connection rules of active and passive users. Integration of renewable and distributed resources in the electrical grid. Electrification of final consumption. The smart grid Definitions. Motivations for Smart grid. Smart grid architecture. Demand side management. Smart grid control and management systems (centralized, decentralized, hierarchical, etc.). Telecommunications and digitization systems for smart grids. Protection systems for smart grids. Enabling technologies. Innovative operation schemes for future power systems (microgrids, energy communities, etc.). Planning of smart grids. Overview of smart grid economics (regulation and market models).

## Learning objectives

The course aims at giving the basic knowledge about the operation, management, and planning of electrical power systems, with particular reference to the smart grid concept. The course content helps develop skills in enabling technologies and tools for the energy transition, with an assessment of the use of electricity as the main energy carrier. It also leverages cross-disciplinary skills acquired by the student in other courses, focusing on the ICT sector and the need for the digitization of essential infrastructure. With reference to the Dublin descriptors, the educational objectives and expected outcomes of the course are: - Knowledge and understanding deep knowledge and understanding of the fundamental theoretical and practical aspects related to smart grid planning and management. - Applying knowledge and understanding Capability to design the architecture of an intelligent management system of energy networks according to the paradigm of smart grids. - Making judgements Capability to independently evaluate and choose enabling technology solutions to achieve intelligent management goals. - Communication skills Capability to communicate technical information both orally and in writing. Ability to discuss problems and solutions with specialists and non-specialists about the planning, design and operation of modern distribution systems. - Learning skills Capability of continuous learning through the proper interpretation of scientific and technical literature, manuals of manufacturers and technical standards.

The course is based on a traditional teaching approach: lecturers (about 45 hours), exercises also using specific software and technical visits (up to 15 hours). Teaching will be provided according to the “Manifesto Generale degli Studi”. The details will be published before the beginning of the course. Methods and techniques for on-site interaction with students: 1) Dialogue and discussion, stimulated by teacher questions 2) Communication using e-mail and notices on the teacher's web page https://unica.it/unica/page/it/[docente]_mocci 3) Communication on request of face to face meetings Interaction between methods and techniques for teaching and learning: Classical teaching will be completed with the exploitation of audio-visual supports, computers and tablets.

## Assessment methods

The exam may consist of written intermediate tests with theoretical and practical questions during the course and an oral test on the topics of the course. Alternatively, one oral test only can be taken at the end of the course. Participation in the intermediate tests is allowed only to the students that regularly attend the lessons. Tests will be used by students for self-assessment; not sufficient results can be recovered at the final examination. Minimum grade (18/30) is given when the level reached is basic, maximum grade (30/30) when the results are outstanding, and all abilities achieved. The final examination is in the form of an oral examination to evaluate the communication skills and technical knowledge on topics not covered during the previous tests. For students who decided not to participate in tests, the final examination also aims to assess the applying knowledge and understanding skills with specific written tests and exercises.
