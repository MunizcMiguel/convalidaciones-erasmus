## Contents

Fundamentals of circuit theory (lecture 6 hours; exercise 2 hours) Voltage and current, Kirchhoff's first and second laws, Components and gates, Descriptive variables, Power and energy, Conventions. General properties of components and circuits, basis of definition. Resistor, Capacitor, Inductor, Independent Voltage and Current Generators, Controlled Generators, Operational Amplifier, Mutually Coupled Inductors, Ideal Transformer. Real components. Steady-state circuit analysis (9 hours; 6 hours) Components in series and in parallel. Star-triangle transformation; Topological notions. Methods of analysis: meshes and knots. Tellegen's theorem and principle of superposition of effects. Thevenin's and Norton's theorems. Maximum power transfer theorem. Analysis of circuits in sinusoidal regime (lecture 9 hours; exercise 6 hours) Presence of the time variable in the analysis of linear and permanent circuits. Sinusoidal and phasor functions. Properties of phasors and phasor of derivative. The circuit transformed into the domain of the phasors. Impedance and admittance. Phasors' method. Analysis of networks in sinusoidal regime. Theorems. Power and energy in sinusoidal steady state, Complex power, Passive bipoles, Conservation of complex power (Boucherot), Theorem of maximum active power transfer, Superposition of powers, The problem of power factor correction. Analysis of dual-port networks. (lecture 4 hours; exercise 2 hours) Impedance matrix. Admittance matrix. Hybrid parameters. Thevenin and Norton's theorems on two-port networks. Frequency response. (lecture 4 hours; exercise 2 hours) Resonant circuits. Passive filters. Active filters. Analysis of circuits in dynamic operation (lecture 12 hours; exercise 8 hours; laboratory 10 hours) Time domain analysis, input/output relationship and equations of state. First-order and second-order examples. Main imprinted signals: unit impulse, unit step, sine wave. Analysis in the domain of the Laplace variable: references to Laplace transforms, transformation of component equations and topological equations. Network functions: circuit excitation and response, impulse response, properties of network functions. Stability. Electrical Circuits Laboratory.

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course of Circuit Theory is to provide the student with knowledge, skills and abilities consistent with the educational objectives of the Degree Course in Electronic, Computer and Telecommunications Engineering, declined according to the five Dublin Descriptors, and detailed below. Knowledge and understanding The student will acquire knowledge and understanding of the constitutive relationships of lumped-parameter components, of the fundamental relationships of circuit theory, of theorems and methods for the analysis of steady-state circuits, of the symbolic method, of theorems and methods for the analysis of circuits in sinusoidal regime, of the state variable approach for the analysis of the dynamical behavior of a circuit, the use of the Laplace transform for circuit analysis, the frequency analysis of a circuit. Applying knowledge and understanding The student will be able to apply his/her knowledge and understanding to: analyze a circuit with concentrated parameters in steady, sinusoidal and periodic conditions; derive the complete response of a circuit in the time domain and in the domain of the Laplace variable. He/she will be able to demonstrate a professional approach to the modeling of an electromagnetic device using lumped parameter circuit representation and the analysis of simple passive and active filters. Making judgements The student will be able to evaluate the applicability of theorems and methods for the analysis of lumped-parameter circuits to the analysis of electrical devices both in steady state and in dynamical behavior. He will be able to construct the relative lumped parameter model for devices of medium complexity and will be able to solve the related model with the tools of circuit theory. He/she will be able to perform frequency analysis of circuits and will be able to recognize the type of a passive and active filter. He will have developed his own autonomy of judgment that will allow him to clearly express technical concepts inherent in the study of electrical circuits and will be able to solve circuit problems never solved before. Finally, the student will have developed the ability to critically evaluate the results of circuit analysis. Communication skills The didactic approach and the methods of ascertaining the acquired knowledge will accustom the student to communicate the notions and methods learned, as well as to formalize the problems in terms of circuit models with concentrated parameters and to discuss the related solutions to specialist and non-specialist interlocutors. Learning skills The didactic approach of the course will enable the student to integrate the knowledge acquired from other courses (in particular Mathematics and Physics, Systems Analysis and Measurements) as well as from various sources in order to achieve a broad vision of the problems related to the analysis of electrical circuits. This approach will allow them to develop the necessary skills to face subsequent courses with a high degree of autonomy.

## Prerequisites

In order to successfully undertake the study of Circuit Theory, the student must have an adequate knowledge of the fundamental methodological aspects that distinguish the basic sciences (mathematical analysis, geometry, physics). In particular, the knowledge of: matrix algebra in the real and complex domain, derivatives, integrals, ordinary differential equations, Fourier series, Laplace transforms, fundamental laws of electromagnetism is of fundamental importance. The skills acquired from previous courses concern the ability to: be able to solve systems of algebraic equations in real and complex algebra, know how to derive and integrate functions, know how to solve systems of differential equations, know how to apply Laplace transforms and anti-transforms. To be able to formulate the relationships between electrical quantities in resistors, capacitors, and inductors. The competencies acquired in the previous preparatory courses are essential for the understanding, interpretation, critical analysis and resolution of problems of medium difficulty in the field of circuit theory. It is recommended to have passed the following exams: Mathematical Analysis 2, Applied Mathematics, Physics 2, as well as the exams indicated among the prerequisites of the Course of Study: Mathematical Analysis 1 and Physics 1.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of 80 hours of lectures, 44 of which are dedicated to theory, 26 to exercises and 10 to the laboratory. Teaching Methods: lectures with the help of slides made available to students at the beginning of the course. The exercises are carried out by solving exercises on the blackboard related to the Circuit Theory explained above.

## Assessment methods

The methods used to ascertain the actual acquisition by students of the expected learning outcomes consist of: - Learning tests during the year: 2 intermediate partial tests in written form consisting of the resolution of a series of exercises on the different parts of the course program. In particular, with the written test the student will have to demonstrate the knowledge of the basic techniques and methodologies for: the time-domain analysis of single-phase and three-phase linear circuits and their dynamic operation; the analysis of the same circuits in the frequency domain and in the Laplace variable domain; the analysis of magnetic circuits. The mid-term tests are reserved for attending students and are exhaustive for the assessment of learning outcomes. - Written and oral exam during the exam sessions open to all students, even those who did not participate in the intermediate tests, or did not achieve positive results, or want to renounce the recognition of intermediate results. The written test consists of solving a series of exercises on the course program. In particular, with the written test the student will have to demonstrate the knowledge of the basic techniques and methodologies for: the time-domain analysis of single-phase linear circuits at steady state and in their dynamic operation; the analysis of the same circuits in the frequency domain and in the Laplace variable domain. During the oral exam, the student will have to demonstrate the ability to critically discuss the exercises carried out during the written test. They will also have to demonstrate that they have understood the fundamental relations of circuit theory, theorems and methods for circuit analysis both with the symbolic method and with the state variable approach, and with the use of the Laplace transform. This type of written and oral exam aims to ascertain the effective acquisition by the student of knowledge, skills and abilities consistent with the educational objectives of the course as well as to increase his/her communication skills. - The final grade is expressed in thirtieths, weighing the grade attributed to the different exercises from time to time based on the effort required for their resolution both in terms of content and computational complexity. The evaluation of 18/30 is conferred when the knowledge/skills of the subject are at least elementary, the evaluation of 30/30 with possible honors, when the knowledge is excellent.
