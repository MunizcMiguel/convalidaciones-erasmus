### Contents

The course is structured into three modules, one covering analog circuits at the operating block level, one covering circuits seen at the transistor level and one dedicated to laboratory activities. The first two modules dedicate examples and exercises to practical applications of electronics while the third provides direct operational skills. Applied Electronics Module Introduction to analog electronic systems; references to the theory of circuits and elementary components (resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors); circuits with capacitors (RC network, passive filters); circuits with diodes (half-wave rectifier, bridge rectifier, LED and photodiodes); general information on operational amplifiers; circuits with operational amplifiers (inverting, non-inverting, adder, subtractor) and related non-idealities; filters with operational amplifiers; signal conversion (analog-digital and digital-analog); multi-stage circuits; oscillators. Electronic Circuit Design Module Basics of digital electronics; physical realization of logic gates: static and dynamic concepts; combinatorial and sequential logic; synchronous and asynchronous systems; architectures and operating principles of processors; timing; I/O interface; peripherals. Electronics Laboratory Module Use of laboratory instruments (signal generator, oscilloscope, multimeter, power supply, breadboards); creation and testing of simple analog circuits based on operational amplifiers and their characterization; notes on the design and manufacturing of printed boards.

### Learning objectives

The course aims to provide the skills to understand the basic functioning of electronic systems, with particular emphasis on analogue ones, and their impact on the characteristics of the systems in which they are used. Through the analysis and design of simple circuits, examined both at block diagram and transistor level, it will be possible to understand the link between the electronics within the systems and the functioning of the systems themselves. - Knowledge and understanding: at the end of the course the students will have acquired knowledge of the main circuit diagrams for signal processing used in electronics; they will know the metrics used to evaluate their performance; they will be able to analyze simple circuits to evaluate their functionality and performance; they will know circuit simulation tools and design methodologies; - Ability to apply knowledge and understanding: the knowledge acquired by students will allow them, at the end of the course, to design simple signal acquisition and conditioning systems; they will be able to dimension amplification and filtering circuits, both using block diagrams and circuit diagrams at transistor level; they will be able to choose the most suitable circuit topologies for the problem to be solved; they will be able to simulate a circuit to verify its functionality; - Independence of judgement: at the end of the course the students will be able to carry out a comparative evaluation of both circuit and block diagram solutions in order to make the best design and development choices; - Communication skills: students will be able to express themselves adequately using the appropriate terminology to describe performance specifications and technical requirements of electronic circuits. They will have acquired an appropriate technical language that allows them to communicate adequately, especially with specialist interlocutors; - Learning skills: students will acquire the skills to obtain and understand the necessary technical information from datasheets, operating manuals and commercial catalogues. They will also acquire the ability to update their skills to technological developments, to understand the functioning of new circuit solutions and to adapt to new development systems.

### Prerequisites

To successfully follow the course, knowledge of computer science, mathematics, physics as well as circuit theory and electronic devices, is required.

### Teaching methods

### Assessment methods

The assessment is based on the verification of the ability to analyze known circuits and design analogue and digital circuits based on the specifications provided. The complete exam takes place through a written test and an oral test. The written test, lasting 3 hours, involves carrying out 4 analysis and/or design exercises (2 for each of the applied electronics and electronic circuit design modules); during the test it will not be possible to consult notes, texts or material, because the ability to memorize and reconstruct the analytical reasoning that leads to the formulas is also subject to evaluation. The subsequent oral test is based on an interview in which the various topics of the course will be discussed. The final grade is given by the arithmetic average of the scores of the two tests, expressed out of 30 and to access the interview it is necessary to achieve at least 15/30 in the written test. For students who follow the course it will be possible to complete the exam through two intermediate tests carried out during the partial tests calendar. Each test includes 4 circuit analysis and/or synthesis exercises, 2 for each module (applied electronics and electronic circuit design); the duration is 3 hours, and it will not be possible to consult notes, texts or material. The final grade is given by the arithmetic mean of the results of the two intermediate tests, each of which is evaluated out of 30. To pass the exam, a score of at least 15/30 is required in each intermediate test. The objective of the written tests is to verify the ability to analyze and design both analogue and digital circuits or systems. Analysis: given a circuit diagram, it will be required to analyze its operation in relation to specific parameters of merit; for example, given a logic gate you will be asked to identify the propagation time, or given an analog filter you will be asked to find its frequency response and gain. Project: given the operating specifications of a circuit or system, it will be required to define the circuit diagram and size the components (resistors, capacitors, transistors); for example, given the specifications in terms of gain of an amplifier, find the values of the resistances of the feedback network, or given gain and power, size the transistors of a single-stage amplifier topology. The oral test aims to verify knowledge of circuit theory, design techniques and to verify whether the ability to solve the exercises is accompanied by an ability to understand the mechanisms underlying the design techniques. The laboratory activities of the third module will be evaluated through quizzes proposed during the course. Attendance (maximum 25% of absences) and the grade obtained in the quizzes will allow you to obtain a score from -1 to 2 which will be added to the grade obtained in the intermediate tests or in the final exam, provided that this grade is at least equal to 18/30. Attendance at the laboratory, with a maximum of 25% absences, is mandatory to pass the exam.
