## Contents

LabVIEW Fundamentals, 36 hours. The first 36 hours of the course are structured to follow the contents of National Instruments LabVIEW Core 1 and LabVIEW Core2. The following topics will be presented: the front panel and the block diagram; the control and function palette; Debugging and Troubleshooting; the main structures (Loops, Case, Sequences); Arrays and Clusters; data types, including Waveforms; logical functions; programming methods with different Design Patterns; state machine-based design; local and global variables; methods for saving data to files and displaying saved data; file management; SubVIs; Channels; user interfaces and events; programming practices for code reuse and readability. Data acquisition and measurement laboratory, 24 hours. Throughout the course, several practical exercises and the development of examples are planned, such as: simulation of test signals; continuous acquisitions; monitoring of operating parameters; signal analysis and processing algorithms; signal processing via FFT; development of a distributed measurement architecture. Measurement systems and architectures will be presented, and in particular: stand alone and modular instrumentation (through systems based on low-cost devices, multifunction data acquisition cards, cRIO and LXI systems).

## Learning objectives

The course explores the LabVIEW environment and the possibilities of acquisition and analysis through modular data flow programming. The course deals with the development of single-channel and multi-channel data acquisition applications from various measurement instruments, with measurement analysis and writing of results. At the end of the course, the student will therefore be able to create applications, based on design models or customized, to acquire, process, analyze, visualize and archive real-world data. One of the objectives of the course is to guide students towards obtaining the "Certified LabVIEW Associate Developer", CLAD, certification, which is the entry- level certification in LabVIEW programming. The educational goals of the course can be expressed according to the following five descriptors: - Knowledge and understanding In-depth knowledge and understanding of the fundamental theoretical and applicative aspects of the LabVIEW development environment. - Applying knowledge and understanding Ability to set up the design and management of even complex measurement systems. - Making Judgements Ability to correctly interpret the results provided by a measurement system. - Communication Ability to communicate information both orally and in writing with the right level of detail. Ability to discuss problems and solutions with specialist and non-specialist interlocutors. - Learning skills Ability to continuously learn, through the correct interpretation of technical bibliography and manufacturers' manuals.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test and an oral exam. The written test, a multiple-choice test, is aimed at evaluating LabVIEW programming skills through evaluation tests of the same type as the CLAD. The oral exam typically includes two questions that may concern programming techniques and data acquisition and processing methods. The evaluation rewards the ability to rigorously and effectively present the topics, to connect them together and to highlight their peculiarities. The exam score is awarded through a grade expressed in thirtieths, averaged between the written and oral, with a positive evaluation starting from the grade of 18/30, in the case of basic knowledge/skills. To achieve a score of 30/30 with honors, the student must demonstrate excellent knowledge of the topics discussed. In particular, with the exam, the possibility of accessing the NI CLAD exam will be evaluated: https://www.ni.com/en/shop/services/education-services/certification-program.html
