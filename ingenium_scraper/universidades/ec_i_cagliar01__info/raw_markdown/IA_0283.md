## Learning objectives

The course aims to strengthen programming skills, especially in the C language, and knowledge of the fundamental algorithms and data structures for information processing. The course will also provide a brief introduction to the Python programming language. Knowledge and understanding At the end of the course the student will have a good understanding of algorithms, data structures and their implementation in C for solving problems of medium difficulty. He will also have a good understanding of the concept of computational complexity and of the main methods for its computation. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding skills At the end of the course the student will be able to: - formulate algorithms for solving problems of medium difficulty, making choices also in consideration of the computational complexity of the algorithms - apply the concepts learned in the development of C and Python programs Judgment autonomy At the end of the course the student is expected to be able to: - evaluate the pros and cons of using a specific algorithm to solve a given problem - evaluate the computational complexity of the algorithms Communication skills At the end of the course, the student will have acquired adequate linguistic skills in relation to the course content, so that it can be fully understood by an interlocutor with adequate technical knowledge and to transmit the essential concepts even to a non technically competent interlocutor. Ability to learn independently At the end of the course, the student should have the ability to deepen his knowledge in the field of algorithm and data structure design. The student will be able to independently extend his/her algorithm design skills.

## Prerequisites

Strict prerequisites in terms of programming skills and knowledge of the C language, in particular the ability to develop simple C programs, using conditional and iterative constructs, simple and structured data types, standard I/O, text files and functions. In detail, the following skills, gained in previous courses, will be considered acquired: • Syntax, data types and basic constructs of the C language. • Knowledge of simple (int, float, double, char) and structured (vectors, struct) data types. • Knowledge and understanding of pointers and pointer arithmetic. • Knowledge of functions and understanding of the parameter passing mechanism. • Knowledge of dynamic memory management (allocation and de-allocation of memory) • Knowledge of linked lists in C (implemented by records and pointers) and their management • File operations in C.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test and an oral test. The written test consists of some exercises including both programming exercises and theory questions. The evaluation is objective, defined on the basis of the percentage of correctness of each answer. If the technical conditions allow it, the test will be carried out on a PC using a suitable software platform which will be illustrated during the course. You will be allowed to take the test using your own PC. In this case, it is the student's responsibility to verify that their PC can correctly connect to the platform and make sure they have all the information relating to their institutional account with them, which is essential for connection. The use of the PC is authorized exclusively for the purpose of carrying out the work. Any other use is prohibited. Alternatively, the student can use paper (provided by the teacher) and pen. The student will have to carry out the test independently without communicating with anyone present in the classroom or remotely. Before the time allotted for the test expires, the student must submit his/her solution according to the rules that will be indicated by the teacher. During the course, a delivery simulation of the solution will be carried out on the chosen platform. This simulation will be repeated online once in January, before the January – February exam session, and once before the June – July exam session. During the exam, the instructions in this regard will be briefly summarized. Solutions that do not include all the relevant student information (name, surname and ID number) will not be corrected. Solutions delivered late with respect to the deadline or which should be copies of other solutions will also not be corrected (in the latter case, none of the solutions in question will be evaluated). Oral exam. In case of doubts in the evaluation of the assignments, the teacher will invite the student for an oral exam that will touch all the parts of the program. If the oral exam is evaluated NOT SUFFICIENT, the entire exam will be considered insufficient. The student also has the right to request an oral evaluation, in the event that she/she believes that the evaluation of the written test does not reflect her/his preparation. In this case, the written test will contribute to 70% of the final mark and the oral test, IF SUFFICIENT, will contribute to 30% of the final mark. If the oral exam is evaluated NOT SUFFICIENT, the entire exam will be considered insufficient. By way of example, the marks obtained in writing and the maximum final mark obtainable after an excellent oral exam are reported below. 18 -> 22 19 -> 23 20 -> 23 21 -> 24 22 -> 25 23 -> 25 24 -> 26 25 -> 27 26 -> 28 27 -> 28 28 -> 29 29 -> 30 + L 30 -> 30 + L 31 -> 30 + L Test on the go. There will be an ongoing test to be held on the dates set by the Study Programme. This test will be written and will concern the carrying out of exercises in relation to the part of the program carried out up to the date scheduled for the ongoing test. If the student obtains at least a sufficient evaluation (not less than 18/30) in the intermediate test, she will be able to carry out a final exam in reduced form which does not include the topics already covered in the intermediate test. In this case, the final mark will take into account the mark obtained in the intermediate test and the mark obtained in the reduced final test. The student can take advantage of this opportunity until the January-February session.
