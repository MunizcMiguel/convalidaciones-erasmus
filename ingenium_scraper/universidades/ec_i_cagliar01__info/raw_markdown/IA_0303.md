## Contents

The course objective is to give the basic knowledge required to understand, analize and use ICT and electronic systems based on the use of electromagnetic field at RF and microwave frequencies. The syllabus is as follows. Maxwell equations, materials, energy and power theorems. Eementary sources, near and far field. Wave propagation. Plane wave, polarization, propagation in material media, plane interfaces. Propagation in real media. Ideal transmission lines. Propagation of digital signals. Antennas. Antena parameters. Transmitting and receiving antennas. Free-space radio-links. Field sensors. RFID and NFC.

## Learning objectives

A significant number of services and systems, not only related to the information engineering, rely their operations upon the use of electromagnetic fields. The general objective of the course is therefore to provide the fundamental knowledge and skills of the so-called "electromagnetic fields engineering". They are required to understand and analyze those systems and services, as well as to develop the first competences needed to integrate them into more complex systems. The detailed objectives are: Knowledge and understanding: students must know the properties of electromagnetic fields and the main devices that use them and be able to correctly evaluate the effects of electromagnetic phenomena on the behavior of ICT (and, more generally, electronic) equipments. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding: at the end of the course the student will have developed the ability to find and apply standard solutions to the different cases that he may face, and to evaluate their feasibility. Learning skills: the knowledge acquired will allow the student to subsequently deal with the study and integration of equipment, systems and services that use electromagnetic fields; these will also be useful to those who will not deal directly with these activities, as it will allow future engineers to interact efficiently with the designers of electromagnetic devices, which are increasingly widespread in all electronic and ICT systems, as well as in many systems of diagnostics and measurement, particularly in the physical and biomedical fields.

## Prerequisites

A good knowledge in college math and physics and in applied math (with a focus on differential and integral calculus in 1,2,3 variables, vector algebra and analysis, complex number and complex exponentials, simple differential equations, Fourier transform) are required to face the course and to pass the exam. Moreover, the course require a good knowledge and ability on circuit theory, above all on AC circuits.

## Teaching methods

The development of classroom lectures (around 2/3 of the total hours) will be organized so that all topics will be connected in an unique discourse. In this way, the reasoning abilities of the students will be developed. The connections with other courses, or with the professional activities, will be enlighted. The numerical applications (around 1/3 of the total hours) will be devised to develop the ability to apply concepts introduced and tools built up in the course to problems, relevant for Information Engineers, related to the syllabus. During the first week or so of lectures the main results of vector algebra and analysis, and of complex number computations will be summarized. The knowledge of these topics will be tested before, and after, these lectures. Teaching will be done in the classroom. Some lectures could be integrated with multimedia materials or with streaming.

## Other information

All the lecture notes will be available, togheter with the numerical exercises developed, on the Moodle site ofthe course (elearning.unica.it). A number of further exam problems will be given, too. Some of them will be complemented with full solutions, or hints fo solutions, while other contains only some results or none at all, to allow students to develop their own procedures and become able to assess the relative correctness.

## Assessment methods

A written test carried out during the exam sessions. The exam will include both multiple-choice questions (5 questions, 2.5 points each), and numerical exercises (typically 3-4, with scores from 3 to 9 points, for a total of 21) of the type carried out in classroom. Only calculators and the summary of main formulas, that will be provided, can be used during the exam. This exam is aimed at assessing the knowledge and, respectively, the skills acquired during the course. The complete solution of some exercises (typically those with a higher score) will also require having acquired the basic competences required by the learning objectives. To pass the exam it will be necessary to have obtained at least 5 points in the multiple choice questions part and at least 8 points in the numerical exercise part, and clearly a final score of 18 or more. The final score is the sum of the results of the two parts, and of 0,15 points, rounded to the closest integer. To obtain "30 cum laude" it will be necessary to reach at least a fila score of at least 32 points.
