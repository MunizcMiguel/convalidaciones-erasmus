# Motion and Vibrations

Studiepunten (ECTS): 6

## Course objectives

Objectives
The student is able to select an appropriate system for motion or force transmission (linkage mechanism or cam–follower mechanism) and design this system from both a kinematic and dynamic perspective. The student can select and apply procedures for the analysis and synthesis of mechanisms in both 2D and 3D.
The student is able to select suitable motion profiles for cam–follower systems or servo-driven actuators, while taking into account the vibration phenomena caused by these motion profiles in flexible follower systems.
The student is able to formulate and solve the dynamic equations for linear vibration problems. The student can assess vibration-related problems, and can propose and develop vibration suppression measures.
The student is able to use numerical tools (MATLAB) for the kinematic and dynamic analysis of mechanisms and for analyzing simplified models of complex vibration problems. The student can verify the obtained solutions and results through validation calculations and critically evaluate them based on physical insight and technological constraints. The student can independently perform and report these numerical analyses in a clear and well-structured report.
Knowledge
The role of kinematic and dynamic analysis and synthesis in machine design.
Procedures for mechanism analysis and synthesis.
Familiarity with commonly used elements and systems for motion or force transmission, with emphasis on linkage mechanisms and cam–follower mechanisms.
Design of motion profiles (for cam–follower systems or servo-driven actuators), with special attention to the dynamic phenomena caused by motion in flexible follower systems (i.e., the connection with vibration analysis).
Fundamental equations, common terminology, the effects of mechanical vibrations on humans and machines, procedures for analysis and synthesis, and principles of vibration suppression.
Skills
Applying techniques known from the Mechanics course for the kinematic and dynamic analysis of machine mechanisms (primarily planar mechanisms).
Using computer-aided tools for kinematic and dynamic calculations and for dynamic simulation (Applied Motion, MATLAB), including time-domain simulation of a nonlinear system.
Formulating a design problem as an optimization problem.
Formulating and solving the dynamic equilibrium equations for linear vibration problems; designing vibration-reduction measures; assessing vibration problems (disturbance and risk); using numerical tools (MATLAB); and employing simplified models for complex vibration problems.
Attitudes
A critical and creative mindset, including during the analysis of existing mechanisms:
Is this optimal? How could it be improved?
Critical interpretation of computer-generated results based on physical insight.
Attention to physical interpretation and technological constraints.
Attention to units and dimensional consistency.
Attention to clear and effective reporting.

## Entry requirements

Mechanics (kinetics, statics and dynamics) in the surface and in space; to solve regular linear differential comparisons with constant coefficients, basis of strength study.

## Content

The Vibrations component is designed as an introduction to mechanical vibrations. The emphasis is on developing physical insight into the subject matter and understanding its implications for the design of mechanical structures. Upon completion of the course, students are expected to be able to solve simple practical vibration problems exactly and to propose approximate solutions for more complex vibration problems. Both discrete and continuous systems are covered in the course, while solution methods based on the Finite Element Method (FEM) are not addressed.
The Vibrations lecture course consists of the following topics:
Lecture 1:
Introduction – examples of vibrations in machines and vehicles
Lecture 2:
Single-degree-of-freedom systems (free response)
Lecture 3:
Single-degree-of-freedom systems (forced response under harmonic excitation)
Lecture 4:
Single-degree-of-freedom systems (forced response under random excitation)
Lectures 5 and 6:
Multi-degree-of-freedom systems (free response, forced response, modal transformation)
Lecture 7:
Continuous systems
Lecture 8:
Vibration isolation and vibration suppression: design aspects – effects of vibrations on humans
Lecture 9:
Vibrations in rotating machinery and internal combustion engines – imbalance and balancing techniques.

## Content

The mechanical vibrations exercices consist of four computer laboratory sessions in which students, under supervision, use MATLAB to solve simple vibration problems. These sessions provide hands-on experience with the analysis and solution of vibration-related engineering problems using numerical methods and computational tools.

## Explanation

Part 1: Motion
The evaluation occurs by means of two assignments in which the students solve a problem in groups of two, using software packages on the PC while manually verifying the results. The two assignments are related to:
- kinematic and dynamic analysis of a mechanism (the mechanism can be freely chosen)
- design of a ridge-follower system including the analysis of motion and the forces in a flexible follower mechanism.
The assignments are introduced by exercise sessions. The students will write a report on these that will be corrected and returned. A first evaluation occurs by means of this written report. Afterwards, there is an evaluating conversation with the teacher, students and guiding assistants, after which the final marks will be determined. This conversation will take place before the exam period.
In the case of a resit, the students will revise their work and there will be a second evaluating conversation.
The time that is provided for these assignments is 74 hours a student.
Part 2: Mechanical Vibrations:
Oral exam with written preparation. Use of formula sheet and access to Matlab.
If the faculty decides that due to force majeure the preparation time has to be limited to less than one hour, the oral exam will be replaced by a written exam. The impact of this decision will be described on Toledo/Ultra.
The marks on both parts will be added together with equal weight. If the mark on one or both parts is less than 10/20, the total mark is rounded down. If the mark on one part is less than 8 on 20, the total mark will be limited to 9/20.
