## Prerequisites

The course is not delivered via e-learning.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The learning assessment consists of an oral exam, consisting of a discussion on the topics covered in the course (including laboratory sessions) and the solution of a dimensioning problem. Some oral questions may therefore require the execution of short calculations. For students with disabilities/invalidities or specific learning disabilities (SLD), the exam procedures will be adapted in accordance with the university guidelines.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

In order to successfully pass the assessment of learning, the student must demonstrate, through the trials described above, that he/she possesses the knowledge and methodological skills for the characterization of the main telecommunication systems, the definition of their requirements and performance, the selection of the most suitable solution in relation to specific application needs. To successfully pass the oral exam, the student will demonstrate an overall knowledge of the course content (including lab sessions), presented in a sufficiently corrected way with the use of proper technical terminology. The highest rating is achieved by demonstrating a thorough understanding of the course content, exposed with complete appropriateness of technical language.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

A mark of thirty is given, with possible praise. The minimum mark for passing the exam is 18/30.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

For the overall outcome of the exam to be fully positive, the student is required to achieve at least sufficiency (equal to eighteen points) in the oral test. The highest rating is achieved by demonstrating a thorough understanding of the course content. Praise is given to students who, having performed the tests correctly and completely, have demonstrated a particular brilliance in oral, and in the solution of the exercises.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

Through the study of the most important wireless communication systems, the course enables students to acquire knowledge needed to understand the issues relating to transmission of information, and to know how to apply appropriate design procedures of wireless communication systems. The course, by integrating the knowledge gained in previous courses like electromagnetic fields, telecommunications, and signal theory, provides the student with a solid technical background on communication systems.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

The student should be able to correctly interpret the causes of degradation and hindrance to information transmission, also analyzed in other courses, and implement appropriate design choices commonly addressed in engineering practice related to wireless communications. This ability will be developed in practice through a number of vocational skills, such as: 1. the ability to correctly choose the appropriate transmission medium, and the associated system architecture, to meet the system requirements; 2. the ability to identify and properly quantify the parameters necessary for the dimensioning of the system; 3. the ability to identify the causes of performance degradation, and the suitable countermeasures.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

Training activities and laboratory sessions will help improve the student’s ability to learn independently and to draw conclusions. Both the student’s ability to communicate and his/her independence of judgment in general, will be stimulated and refined, through oral discussions.

## Contents

Classrooms: - Block diagram of a wireless communication system. - Principles of antennas. - Basic notions of modulations. - Attenuation and noise, SNR. - Distortion, ISI. - Interference. - Up and Down conversion. - Carrier coupling. - Oscillators and frequency stability. - Radio channel characteristics, channel models (Flat and Selective fading, time-varying channels). - Diversity. - Channel coding (basic notions). - Rx sensitivity. - Link budget. - Adaptive Modulation and Coding. - Spread spectrum and multiple access techniques: Principles of DS-SS and CDMA. - Equalization techniques (IF and baseband). - Radio mobile systems, operational principles. - OFDM. Software exercises: - Simulink Models of Wireless Technologies. Lab activities.
