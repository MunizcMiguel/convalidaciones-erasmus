## Prerequisites

Theoretical and practical lectures: 60 hours. Exercises: 4 hours. Laboratory: 8 hours.

No

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The evaluation method comprises two tests: - a written paper, consisting in the development of a VHDL-based design project on a topic agreed in advance with the professor. Alternatively, the student may opt for written exercises to be solved on the spot in the classroom. The exercises may deal with both design and analysis problems related to the circuits and the topics covered during the lectures; - an oral test, which includes the discussion of the written paper and of some of the other topics dealt with in the course. If necessary, the level of knowledge of the essential prerequisites for mastering the subject may also be tested. The oral test must be undertaken during the same exam session as the written test. For those students with a disability or specific learning disorder (SLD), the examination procedures will be adapted in the light of the provisions of the University guidelines.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

The two tests aim at evaluating both the completeness of the knowledge acquired by the student on the subject and the topics covered during the course, and the student's ability of applying the acquired knowledge to the solution of engineering problems regarding the design and the analysis of digital circuits.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

The design project is evaluated according to its methodological correctness and to the degree of mastering of the subject demonstrated in its development, as will be assessed during the oral discussion. The alternative exercise-based paper will be marked in thirties, as resulting from the sum of the marks of each exercise, which vary according to the difficultly level and, for those exercises that require numerical results, half of the available marks will be assigned according to the correctness of the numerical results, the other half according to the correctness and completeness of the solution procedure adopted by the student. The oral test will directly be evaluated with a mark from 0 to 30 that equally takes into consideration the correctness, completeness, and clarity of the discussion.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

Since the oral test includes the discussion of the written paper, the final mark will be the mark obtained in the oral test. Honors will be granted to students that, having obtained the maximum mark in the oral test, have also demonstrated an excellent mastering of the subject and the ability of facing problems even if not directly discussed during the lectures. The mark obtained in the written paper is only used as a preliminary evaluation that can be used by the student to assess their preparation, and as a starting point to direct the beginning of the oral test.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The students will deepen their knowledge and understanding of the behaviour and usage of digital electronic circuits and systems (memories, microcontrollers, reconfigurable platforms), as well as the basis of their design and programming techniques.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

Students must be able to design digital electronic circuits, both by choosing discrete components from available commercial offerings, and using software or hardware programmable components, using languages such as C and VHDL. The student must also be able to apply the acquired knowledge to understand the workings of the buses most commonly used to interconnect electronic systems and devices.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The solution of real-world design problems, that include the detailed analysis of the digital portions of a "case study" (whose analog part is dealt with in the "Analog Electronic Circuits and Systems" course) stimulates the student's ability to integrate knowledge acquired in this and in preceding courses, so as to reach a complete and rational design solution, based on the available scientific literature and device data sheets.

## Contents

Theoretical lectures: • Electrical characteristics of logic gates: - Propagation delay times, power consumption, buffers, transmission gates, clock feedthrough. - Tri-state and open-drain gates. • Combinatorial logic: - Decoders, multiplexers, demultiplexers. - Arithmetic circuits: adders, subtractors, multipliers, comparators, shifters. • Sequential circuits: - Latches and flip-flops. - Registers and counters. • Memories: - Organization, decoders, basic memory cell, sense amplifiers. - Non-volatile memories: FLASH and EEPROM. - Static RAM: 6 transistor cell. - Dynamic RAM: 1 transistor cell, - Memory interfaces: parallel (SRAM, DRAM, NAND) and serial (SPI, I2C) buses. • Programmable devices: - FPGA architecture and design methods. • Designing with VHDL: - Language fundamentals. - Examples of combinatorial networks (adders). - Implementation of peripherals (SPI and I2C transceivers). - Interfacing to microcontrollers. • Design guidelines: - Synchronous circuits, clock buffering, synchronizers, reset. - Test circuits and interfaces. • Case study: - Design of the digital part of an ECG signal acquisition system. Exercises: • Design of buffers and simple digital circuits. • Serial bus data analysis. Laboratory: • Experimental characterization of logic levels. • HW/SW cosimulation of an I2C bus and EEPROM memories. • Simulation of the case study.
