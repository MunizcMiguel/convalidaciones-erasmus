## Prerequisites

Fundamental concepts of circuit theory. Diode and transistor. Single transistor amplifiers. Complex numbers, Laplace Transform, and differential equations with constant coefficients and separable variables.

## Teaching methods

72 hours of lectures

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The assessment of students' learning is structured into two parts: A written exam, consisting of solving a circuit, to be completed in two hours. The exercise will be divided into multiple parts, with the solution to each part potentially being necessary for the following ones; An oral exam, consisting of a discussion on one or more topics covered in the course; during the oral exam, students may also be asked to solve simple exercises. Passing the written exam is a prerequisite for taking the oral exam. The oral exam must be taken in the same session as the written exam. If the oral exam result is negative, students must retake the written exam as well. For students with disabilities or specific learning disorders (SLD), the examination methods will be adapted in accordance with the university's guidelines (https://www.univpm.it/Entra/Elenco_siti_tematici_1/Disabilita_e_DSA_Servizio_di_accoglienza).

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

To pass the exam, students must demonstrate the ability to analyze, solve, and understand the usefulness of circuits belonging to the classes studied during the course (e.g., operational amplifier circuits, multi-stage feedback amplifiers, oscillators). They must also show an understanding of the methods for analyzing circuit performance (e.g., DC and AC analysis, analysis in the Laplace domain or real frequency domain, time constant method). This cannot be achieved without knowledge of the operation of the devices presented during the course, nor without a solid understanding of basic Electrical Engineering concepts (Kirchhoff's Laws, nodal analysis, substitution theorems, two-port network representation) or Mathematical Analysis (complex numbers, derivatives, integrals, first-order differential equations) acquired in other courses.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

Each exam is graded on a scale of thirty. The ability to analyze and solve circuits will be assessed through the written exam. The knowledge and understanding of the topics covered in the course will be assessed through the oral exam.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

For the overall evaluation to be positive, the student must achieve a not severely insufficient grade in the oral exam and a passing final grade. The grade for the written exam is determined by the weighted average of the grades for each part of the proposed exercise. The final grade is assigned based on the weighted average of the grades from the written and oral exams, with the oral exam carrying double the weight of the written exam. Honors are reserved for students who, having completed all exams correctly and thoroughly, have demonstrated exceptional brilliance in the oral presentation and the preparation of the written work.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The course aims to provide knowledge in one of the key areas of Electronics: analog circuits. Building on the basic circuit theory knowledge acquired in previous courses, the main objective is to deepen the understanding of linear and non-linear analog circuits based on diodes, transistors, and operational amplifiers. This objective is expressed through the learning and comprehension of the fundamentals of analysis and design of essential analog circuit blocks: feedback amplifiers, operational amplifier circuits, oscillators, and power supplies.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

The acquired knowledge will be tested through the analysis of analog circuits, the design of fundamental analog circuit blocks, exercises, and simulations based on Spice.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The solution of design problems derived from real- world scenarios, which includes an in-depth analysis of the analog part of a 'case study' (with the digital part covered in the Advanced Digital Systems course), will stimulate the student's ability to integrate the knowledge acquired in this and previous courses, in order to arrive at a complete and well-reasoned design solution, based on scientific literature and device datasheets.

## Contents

1) Review of Analog Circuit Analysis and Electronic Devices Analysis of nonlinear circuits: linearization, direct-current (DC) analysis, and small-signal (AC) analysis. Review of circuit theory. Review of semiconductor physics. Diode and MOSFET: operating principles. 2) Diode and MOSFET Diode: static characteristic, large-signal models. Circuits with diodes: rectifiers, limiters. Zener diode. MOSFET: large-signal model, operating modes, small-signal model. Single-transistor amplifiers. Multistage amplifiers. 3) Differential Amplifier Differential amplifier: DC and AC analysis, differential gain, common-mode gain, CMRR, and emitter degeneration. Current mirrors and active loads in differential amplifiers. 4) Frequency Response Bode plots. Network functions and cutoff frequencies. Time-constant method. Cutoff frequencies of single-transistor amplifiers, cascode amplifiers, and the common-drain/common-source configuration. 5) Feedback General negative-feedback configuration and its properties. Stability in feedback circuits: Nyquist criterion and stability analysis using Bode plots. Compensation. Loop-gain method. Rosenstark method. Middlebrook method. 6) Operational Amplifier Operational-amplifier characteristics: DC and AC characteristics. Circuit models of the operational amplifier. Fundamental operational-amplifier circuits: linear amplifiers, comparators, Schmitt triggers, integrators, and differentiators. Effects of operational-amplifier nonidealities on circuits. 7) Case Study: Analysis and Design of an Analog Front End for Biomedical Signal Acquisition (ECG). 8) Oscillators Quasi-sinusoidal oscillators: determination of the oscillation frequency by linearization, the loop-gain method, and the characteristic-equation method. Three-point oscillators. Gouriet-Clapp oscillator. Quartz oscillators: equivalent circuit and resonance. Relaxation oscillators. Relaxation oscillators with current-controlled two-terminal elements. Relaxation oscillator with an operational amplifier. 9) Phase-Locked Loops (PLLs): Architecture and Operating Principles Introduction to PLLs. Architecture: phase detector, phase/frequency detector, VCO, and frequency dividers. PLL dynamics. 10) Output Stages Power output stages: Class A and Class B. The practical sessions include circuit analysis and verification using SPICE simulation.
