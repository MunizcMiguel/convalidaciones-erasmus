## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The examination is oral and it consists in three questions about all the topics. If required, the questions that require some calculation execution will be answered in a written form during the oral examination itself. The questions that require to draw some block diagrams, electric schemes, graphics, and the execution of analytical demonstrations will be answered in a written form during the oral examination itself. Real cases analysed during the course could require to provide a written report by the student. This report could be used to formulate one question during the examination. For those students with a disability or specific learning disorder (SDA), the examination procedures will be adapted in the light of the provisions of the University guidelines (https://www.univpm.it/Entra/Elenco_siti_tematici_1/Disabilita_e_DSA_Servizio_di_accoglienza/L/1)

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

To obtain a positive rating, the student must prove a sufficient knowledge about the course topics. Students must explain the topics in a sufficiently corrected way using adequate technical words and phrases. The ability to demonstrate the electromagnetic compatibility principles will be checked, together with the ability to put in relationship several topics and to analyse real practical cases. The highest score will be obtained showing a deep knowledge of the topics presented using a valuable technical language.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

A score ranging from zero up to ten will be assigned to each question. The score will be given in thirtieths, with a distinction if deserved.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

The final score is the sum of the score of all questions. The examination is passed if the total score will be equal or greater than eighteen. The “30/30 with distinction” score is reserved to those students who, in addition to a correct and complete answer to all questions, will show a strong ability and autonomy in theoretical demonstrations.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The course enables students to understand the concept of the electromagnetic environment, the functional coexistence of electronic devices, and the nature of interference between devices and systems, both electrical and electronic. Students will explore and deepen their understanding of electromagnetic interference (EMI) emissions and immunity to environmental interference. They will examine the risks associated with conducted and radiated emissions from devices, focusing on their potential impact on both equipment and individuals exposed in occupational settings. Specifically, students will learn to distinguish between direct risks, stemming from exposure to electromagnetic fields, and indirect risks, related to interference affecting equipment critical to human safety. Furthermore, the course provides an overview of the main international and national standards on electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) and safety, along with the methodologies for measurement and compliance verification.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

The student will develop the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to identify in realistic situations the emission responsible, the victim and the coupling mechanism of the interference capable of degrading the performance of the apparatus or system under examination. The student will apply the knowledge to the analysis of potentially dangerous situations even for the operators who use the equipment, with the ability to formulate possible mitigation solutions both in terms of filtering and shielding of electromagnetic interference. The student will be able to identify and define specific risk measurement and assessment techniques and procedures.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The course content and teaching methodologies highlight the connections with other subjects. The student is stimulated to a global approach to each problem by making a synthesis effort. This is particularly important to improve the student learning capability. The study of real-life cases will allow the opinion sharing among students and will stimulate the team working methodology so improving the student communication and interaction skills.

## Contents

CLASSROOM LESSONS 1. Electromagnetic interference. 1a. Interference generation mechanisms. 1b. Conducted and radiated propagation and coupling mechanisms. 1c. Emission and susceptibility of electronic analog and digital equipment for communication, industrial and power applications. 2. Electromagnetic safety. 2a. Direct safety due to human exposure to intentional and non-intentional sources. 2b. Indirect safety due to electromagnetic susceptibility of equipments. 2c. National and international standards for electromagnetic compatibility and safety assessment. 3. Evaluation techniques. 3a. Interference evaluation and measurement techniques. 3b. Sensors and antennas. 3c. Instrumentation for electromagnetic interference and human exposure measurements. 4. Mitigation and protection techniques. 4a. Filtering actions. 4b. Shielding actions: passive and active shielding. LABORATORY LESSONS Laboratory lessons will be carried out in front of typical test benches adopted for the compatibility and safety assessment, according to international standards (IEC/CISPR/EN) and national standards, so that the student will have complete perception of instrumentation, connections, and typical issues in measurement procedures. More precisely: narrowband measurements of emissions and human exposure to em fields, broadband measurements of emissions and human exposure to em fields, susceptibility measurements of electronic equipments.
