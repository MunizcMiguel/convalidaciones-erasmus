## Contents

1. Physical quantities and units of measurement (1 ECTS): Fundamental and derived physical quantities. Units of measurement. The international system. Dimensional analysis. Conversion of units of measurement. Scientific notation. Calculations of orders of magnitude. Coordinate systems. Scalar and vector quantities and their properties. Components of a vector and versors. Operations with vectors. 2. Kinematics (1 ECTS): Reference systems. Position, trajectory. Average and instantaneous velocity. Average, instantaneous, tangential and radial acceleration. Motion diagram. One-dimensional motion. Uniform rectilinear motion, uniformly accelerated motion. Falling body. Motion in two dimensions. Parabolic motion. Periodic motion, period, and frequency. Simple harmonic motion. Uniform circular motion. Position, velocit,y and angular acceleration; motion in non-inertial reference systems; relationships between rotational and translational quantities. 3. Dynamics: Newton's forces and laws (1 ECTS): Concept of force. Newton's three laws. Gravitational force. Difference between mass and weight. Normal force. Centripetal force. Elastic force. Frictional forces. Apparent forces. Central forces. Surface tension forces. Equilibrium conditions of a rigid body. Examples of rigid bodies in static equilibrium. Elastic properties of solids. 4. Dynamics: work and energy (1 ECTS): Mechanical work. Translational kinetic energy and the kinetic energy theorem. Conservative forces and potential energy. Mechanical energy and its conservation. Friction and dissipation of energy. Isolated and non-isolated systems. Work of non-conservative forces. Power. Introduciotn to rotational motions. 5. Momentum and collisions (1 ECTS): Momentum. Impulse of a force. Principle of conservation of momentum. Systems of material points. Center of mass and barycenter. Collisions in one dimension. Elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions. Deformable systems. Coefficient of restitution. Energy transfer through collisions. 6. Fluid mechanics: hydrostatics and fluid dynamics (1 ECTS): Density. Pressure. Pascal's principle. Hydrostatic pressure and Stevino's law. Hydrostatic equilibrium equation. Barometric equation (hypsometric formula). Pascal's principle. Archimedes' law and buoyancy. Ideal fluids. Flow rate of a fluid. Continuity equation. Bernoulli's equation and its applications. The Venturi tube and the Pitot tube. Real fluids. Viscosity and turbulent motion. Reynolds coefficient. Resistance of a duct. Poiseuille's law. Hydraulic duct circuits.

## Learning objectives

In line with the objectives of the Degree Course, the teaching aims to provide a basic understanding of the foundations of Classical Mechanics, as well as the ability to reason scientifically and apply abstract mathematical models and concepts to real and concrete scientific problems. Furthermore, the student will acquire a conscious operational ability in solving simple numerical exercises. Knowledge and understanding At the end of the teaching period, the student will have to learn: a) the basics of the scientific method, common to all experimental disciplines, and b) a significant selection of Classical Mechanics topics, also presented through everyday life experiences or applications to the medical engineering sector. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding At the end of the teaching period, the student will be able to: a) analyze a problem, b) identify the physical laws that govern the phenomena involved, and c) arrive at the definition of adequate solution strategies. Autonomy of judgment At the end of the teaching period, the student will be equipped with useful schemes to be able to independently express evaluations on problems of reasonable complexity. Communication skills At the end of the teaching period, the student will be able to use correct terminology and technical-scientific language appropriate to the treatment of the topics learned. Learning skills At the end of the teaching period, the student will be able to independently deepen the skills acquired and use tools for the continuous updating of their knowledge.

## Prerequisites

Good knowledge of elementary mathematics (algebra, geometry and trigonometry) is required; fundamental notions of differential and integral calculus.

## Teaching methods

The course contents are presented through lectures, with the aid of slides. Each topic is illustrated with various examples and applications. Numerical exercises will be carried out with various degrees of difficulty, similar to those that students will have to face in the written test. The presentations of each of the teaching units listed above will be uploaded on the Moodle platform, together with a vast repertoire of exercise tracks that the student can download to independently verify the skills acquired during the course. Attendance at lessons is not mandatory, but is strongly recommended for achieving the educational objectives of the course.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test followed by an oral test. The written test (duration 2 hours) consists of the resolution, justified and commented in a clear manner, of three problems of Mechanics. Students who pass the written test (minimum threshold to reach 18/30) will be admitted to take the subsequent oral test, which consists, starting from a discussion of the previous written test, of the illustration of some of the main topics covered in the program. The skills, rigor, and language properties presented during the oral test will be evaluated. The final evaluation will be expressed in thirtieths.
