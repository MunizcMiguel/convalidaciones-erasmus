### Contents

2 ECTS THE STRUCTURE OF MATTER: The structure of atoms. The chemical bond. Lewis's symbolism. Classification of the bonds. The ionic bond. Valence bond theory, hybridization, molecular orbital theory. The metallic bond and band theory, conductors, semiconductors and insulators. 2 ECTS THE CHEMICAL THERMODYNAMICS: the first principle of thermodynamics and thermochemistry, the second principle of thermodynamics and the spontaneity of chemical/biochemical reactions. Heterogeneous equilibrium. Phase diagrams. Gases. 1 ECTS THE CHEMICAL EQUILIBRATION: homogeneous equilibria in the gas phase. Pure liquids liquid solutions. Homogeneous equilibria in aqueous solution. 1 ECTS THE CHEMICAL KINETICS: the rate of a chemical reaction, reaction orders, effects of temperature on rate, complex and enzymatic reactions, mechanisms of reactions. 1 CFU CRYSTALLINE AND AMORPHIC STRUCTURE OF MATERIALS: Bravais lattices, main metal structures, comparison of CFC, EC, CCC structures. Planar volume density, polymorphism and allotropy. Crystal structure analysis. 1 CFU THERMICALLY ACTIVATED PROCESSES: solidification of metals, solid metal solutions. Analysis of defects. Diffusion in solids. ceramic materials. 1 CFU POLYMERIC MATERIALS: Polymerization reactions, industrial processes for polymerization. Characteristics of polymers. Technologies for processing polymeric materials. Biological materials and biomaterials. 1CFU REACTORS: Ideal batch reactor. cross flow stationary reactor, piston flow stationary reactor, Design of a reactor. 1CFU BIOREACTORS: General principles of biotechnology. Differences between bioreactor and fermenter. Fluidized bed bioreactors. 1 CFU Classroom exercises on all topics covered and laboratory experiences.

### Reference texts

Peter Atkins, Physical Chemistry, Zanichelli. The textbook is only a general-purpose guide; the lecturer will provide handouts on all topics covered in the course. Handouts will be provided via Moodle platform.

### Learning objectives

The objective of the course, administered over a full year, is to provide students with the fundamentals of chemical reactions useful for the identification of material properties and for the design of reactors and bioreactors. Specific Learning Objectives Specific objectives include: understanding the mechanisms underlying chemical reactions; experimental determination of kinetic parameters; analysis of complex kinetic patterns; identification of characteristics underlying material properties; understanding thermal effects in materials formation; evaluation and optimization of different reactor configurations.

### Prerequisites

Basic mathematics, algebraic and transcendent equations, logarithms, exponentials and graphing of points

### Teaching methods

Lectures are conducted through participatory lecture with direct student intervention in the lecture. Classroom exercises consist of numerical exercises appropriately chosen to understand the level of difficulty encountered by students and consequently reshape teaching to reduce any impacts on learning. Laboratory experiences will be designed so that students can be the architects of their own learning. Attendance at the course, although not mandatory, is strongly recommended. The lecturer will make handouts on all course topics available on the Moodle platform. The lecturer is available daily for any clarification of specific topics. If foreign students are present, the lecturer is available to conduct the lectures in English

### Assessment methods

Knowledge verification In addition to the theoretical lectures, classroom exercises are conducted for the purpose of verifying the learning of the knowledge described above. These exercises may involve both a numerical solution and the setting of the solution method from a theoretical point of view as well as the development of laboratory methodologies. Skills Students will acquire the following skills: - understand the structure of matter; - Predict the behavior of materials on the basis of chemical structure; - Setting up and solving the fundamental equations describing different configurations of homogeneous and heterogeneous chemical reactors; and - Analysis of experimental data and their interpretation for the purpose of developing homogeneous and heterogeneous kinetic models - Optimization of reactor configuration, including consideration of thermal effects. Methods of testing knowledge and understanding In order to make the conduct of the examination easier, students are given the opportunity to pass the first part of the course (first semester) through an ad hoc written test. The proposed exercises are developed to verify, both qualitatively and quantitatively, the acquisition of the above skills and the autonomy of judgment and communication achieved. The result of the test, expressed in thirtieths, will be averaged with the result of the test at the end of the second semester. For those who fail or do not take the test, they will be allowed to take the written test at the end of the course on all topics covered in both the first and second semesters. Behaviors Students will acquire sensitivity: - in carrying out activities to analyze experimental data and interpret them - in understanding the effect of various phenomena present in chemical reactors - in evaluating optimal operating conditions Ability to apply knowledge and understanding Students will gain sensitivity: - in performing activities of analyzing experimental data and interpreting them - in understanding the effect of various phenomena present in chemical reactors - in the evaluation of optimal operating conditions the exercises offered in the written exam are designed to test the acquisition of the above behaviors. Mode of testing behaviors The exercises offered in the written examination and the corresponding oral test are developed in such a way that the acquisition of the above knowledge and understanding can be tested.
