## Learning objectives

In line with the objectives set out by the relevant degree program, the course introduces the student to the identification of simplified structural models, using the tools of rigid and deformable body mechanics. In particular, the student is introduced to understanding problems related to the equilibrium of a body, methods of analysis, and the determination of internal stress characteristics, integrated with knowledge of kinematics and the tools of structural analysis for rigid and deformable systems. Special attention is given to the stress and strain state of a body or system of bodies in relation to the material properties derived from characterization tests such as tensile and/or surface hardness tests. The Structural Mechanics course prepares students to design safe and functional medical devices, optimizing the design and improving the performance of devices through solid structural analysis. This aligns with the goal of training engineers capable of contributing to innovation in the healthcare sector by designing advanced solutions. The minimum requirements are considered met if the knowledge and understanding abilities are adequate to describe, address, and solve simple problems related to the equilibrium of isostatic, hyperstatic, or unstable systems, particularly in the context of human body kinematics. Specific objectives are related to the knowledge and understanding of the basic tools of Structural Mechanics. In particular, the student should be able to apply the knowledge gained to simple solid mechanics problems with autonomy in choosing the solution methodology. Furthermore, the student should acquire the ability to independently research and learn solutions for problems not previously addressed or innovative, and be able to present them appropriately.

## Teaching methods

Lectures and exercises. Attendance at lectures is not mandatory, but it is strongly recommended to achieve the course's educational objectives, as it allows for a quicker learning of terminology and basic concepts, encouraging the cognitive potential needed for independent study of theoretical content and its possible implementation. The construction of students' self-assessment skills is carried out through examples of resistance calculations of mechanical components, in which they evaluate, provide feedback on the work of their peers and receive feedback from their classmates, these latter skills being considered fundamental in everyday life and professional contexts. The course will be held in Italian but students who request it can take the exam in English. The teacher will recommend an appropriate text in the same language..

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test and an oral test. The written test lasts 2 hours and consists of practical exercises that require the solution of problems related to the topics covered in class. This test aims to evaluate the knowledge and understanding skills applied and the student's independent judgment. The grade of the written test is expressed in thirtieths. Students who obtain a grade higher than 18/30 in the written test access the oral test. The oral test consists of a first part in which the written test is corrected and discussed and, if this is sufficient, we move on to the discussion of topics covered in class in order to evaluate both the student's communication skills and his/her learning ability. The final grade will not be given by the average of the two tests but by the overall evaluation of the skills acquired by the student
