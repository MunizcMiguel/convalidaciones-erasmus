## Contents

1. Sets, Relations (2 cfu) Sets, representations and operations; Properties of operations between sets. De Morgan's laws; The set of parts; Cartesian product between sets; Binary relations; Properties of binary relations of a set in itself (reflexive, symmetric, transitive, antireflexive, antisymmetric); Equivalence relations, quotient sets. Order and strict order relations; Correspondences (functions) between sets; injective, surjective correspondences; biunivocal correspondences; Characterization of infinite sets. Numerical sets: N, Z, Q, R. Peano axioms (construction of N); Construction of Z, Q and R. Operations in N, Z, Q, R; Countable sets: equipotency between N, Z, Q. Non-countability of R. 2. Real functions: (3 cfu) Cartesian coordinate system. Intervals of real numbers. Real function of a real variable; Domain and codomain of a function. Injective, surjective and bijective function. Inverse of a function. Bounded, periodic, even, odd functions. Composite functions. Increasing and decreasing functions. Composite functions. Classifications of elementary functions, algebraic functions and transcendental functions. Properties and graphs of algebraic functions: whole rationals, fractional rationals, irrationals. Transcendental functions. Properties and graphs of exponential and logarithmic functions. Periodic functions, period and frequency. Trigonometric functions. Invertibility of trigonometric functions: arcsine, arccosine, arctangent function. Hyperbolic functions. Equations and inequalities: algebraic, logarithmic, exponential 3. Complex Numbers: (1cfu) The imaginary unit. Imaginary numbers. Complex numbers. The set of complex numbers. Algebraic form, Geometric representation of complex numbers, Trigonometric form of complex numbers: from algebraic to trigonometric form and vice versa. Exponential form: Euler formulas. 4. Limits and continuity (2 cfu) Notions of topology of the numerical line. Numerical sequences. Limits of sequences. From the limits of sequences to the limits of functions. Continuous functions; Discontinuity. 5. Differential calculus (1cfu) Derivatives and differentials. Application of the derivative. Taylor polynomial with Peano remainder and Lagrange remainder. Mac Laurin formula. 6. Integral calculus (1cfu) Definite integrals. Integrability of continuous functions. Primitives of a function. Indefinite integrals. Calculation of areas of plane figures. Improper integrals. 7. Numerical series: (1cfu) Numerical series. Geometric series. Harmonic series. Generalized harmonic series. Series with non-negative signs. Taylor series, Taylor series developability theorem. 8 Differential equations. (1cfu) Differential equations. Order and types. General integral. Cauchy problems. First order linear, Bernoulli and separable variable eqs. Second order differential eqs. Homogeneous and non-homogeneous with constant coefficients.

## Learning objectives

In order to have a solid basic education in engineering disciplines, students must know the rudiments of Mathematical Analysis. To this end, it is necessary to review basic notions of Mathematics. Provide the first knowledge of mathematical analysis essential for a student of a scientific degree course with particular attention to applications in Engineering. Insist on the formalism and rigor of mathematical reasoning. At the end of the course, students will know the theoretical and practical aspects inherent to numerical sets, combinatorial calculus, elementary functions, complex numbers, numerical sequences, limits, derivatives, integrals, differential equations, numerical series. At the end of the course, students will have experimented through exercises and discussions in the classroom with the resolution of equations in the field of complex numbers, behaviors of elementary functions, notable points, calculations of limits, derivatives and integrals, series expansions, resolution of differential equations both on direct questions and inverse problems or when instrumental to the resolution of problems. Students will achieve the necessary skills to be able to solve exercises even in a different way from how learned in class. Preference for a demonstration of a theorem carried out in a different way from how shown by the teacher in class. Students will be able to use appropriate language in stating theorems, performing demonstrations, specifying definitions. He/she will also be able to express the meaning of concepts, theorems, definitions using informal language. Student will have the ability to identify the mathematical tool useful for solving a specific problem within the scope of the exam program
