## Contents

1. Electrical phenomena (1cfu): - Experimental aspects; electric charge; Coulomb’s law and electric field; overlapping principle; continuous charge distributions; flow of a vector field; Gauss’s law (first Maxwell equation); electric field lines; integral and local form of Gauss’s law; divergence theorem; motion of charged particles in electrostatic fields. 2. Electrostatic potential (1cfu): - Local relationship between electric field and potential by gradient operation; operational definition of the electrostatic potential; calculation of potential in some significant cases; calculation of potential differences by means of the line integral of the electric field; Maxwell’s second equation for the electrostatic field; Poisson and Laplace equations. 3. Conductors, capacitors and stationary currents (1 cfu) - Electrostatic field in conductors; capacity, potential and electrostatic energy of a charged capacitor; flat and cylindrical capacitors; series and parallel capacitors; capacitors with dielectric; electric fields in matter. - Intensity of electric current in conductors; current density vector; conservation principle of the electrical charge; electromotive force generators; Ohm’s and Kirchhoff’s laws; Joule effect; series and parallel electrical resistances; non-stationary phenomena; time constant in RC circuits. 4. Stationary magnetic fields (1 cfu) - Sources of the magnetic field and experimental aspects; Lorentz force; Biot-Savart law; I and II Laplace laws; definition of the ampere; magnetic dipole moment of a coil; circuitry of a vector field; Ampere and Stokes theorems; rotor and divergence of the magnetic field; equations of magnetostatics in integral and differential forms; magnetic field of a solenoid; magnetic field produced by a moving charge; work carried out by the Lorentz force; magnetic properties of matter; orbital magnetic dipole and spin moment in the atom; effects of a magnetic field on different substances; diamagnetism, paramagnetism and ferromagnetism; magnetization vector; magnetic susceptibility and magnetic permeability; magnetic field conditions and magnetic induction field at the interface between isotropic and homogeneous magnetic substances; magnetic hysteresis; vector potential. 5. Electromagnetic induction (1 cfu) - Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction; Lenz’s law; induction due to relative motion; rotating coil and alternators; electric field rotor; mutual induction and self-induction; foucault currents; inductances in series and in parallel; energy stored by an inductance; oscillating circuits; alternating currents; overall impedance of a RLC circuit; displacement current; Maxwell’s equations; Ampere-Maxwell’s law; fundamental laws of electromagnetism; electromagnetic fields in matter; electromagnetic potentials. 6. Electromagnetic waves (1cfu): - Equation of electromagnetic waves in vacuum; plane and spherical waves; electric and magnetic fields in plane waves; linear, circular and elliptical polarization of electromagnetic waves; energy and pulses carried by electromagnetic fields; Poynting vector; emission of electromagnetic radiation from accelerated charges; irradiation of an oscillating charge and dipole radiation; the electromagnetic spectrum. Elements of modern physics; the limits of classical physics; black body radiation; from Galileo to Einstein; uncertainty principle and wave-particle dualism; applications to industry, spectroscopy, medicine, microscopy, computer science, electronics, navigation.

## Learning objectives

The purpose of the Physics II course is to teach the basics of classical electromagnetism, both in vacuum and in isotropic and homogeneous media, so as to allow the student to face problems of electromagnetism and apply the laws acquired to solve them. Classical electromagnetism is the basis of other courses in the degree programme in Medical Engineering, such as electrotechnics and electronics. The course includes both theory and practice. Knowledge and understanding At the end of the teaching period, the student will have learned: a) the properties of electrical phenomena in vacuum and matter and their interpretation through the concept of electric field and electric potential; b) the properties of magnetic phenomena in vacuum and matter and the interaction between magnetic field and magnetic moment of atoms; c) the profound interdependence of electric and magnetic fields which vary over time and how one should speak of electromagnetic interaction and electromagnetic phenomena, leading to the prediction and discovery of electromagnetic waves. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding At the end of the teaching period, the student will have acquired: a) the ability to analyse and solve simple problems concerning electrical or magnetic phenomena such as electric conduction, the calculation of the electric and magnetic field in space and the calculation of the forces of interaction between electrical charges or between wires crossed by current and external magnetic fields; b) analytical capacity which allows to break down a problem into sub-fields, which that can be addressed through the skills acquired. Autonomy of judgment At the end of the teaching period, the student will be equipped with useful schemes to be able to independently express assessments on problems of discrete complexity. Communication skills At the end of the teaching period, the student will be able to use correct terminology and a technical-scientific language appropriate to the treatment of the topics learned. Ability to learn At the end of the teaching period, the student will be able to independently deepen the skills acquired and use tools for continuous updating of their knowledge.

## Prerequisites

The basic mathematics required to be able to follow the course successfully are: reference systems in cartesian, polar and cylindrical coordinates, trigonometry, vector calculation, integral and differential calculation of functions of several real variables. Principal topics in the Physics I course, such as kinematics and dynamics, are also fundamental. During the course some basic notions of vector calculation, differential vector operators are recalled, as well as theorems in vector calculation such as those of Gauss and Stokes are presented.

## Teaching methods

The course is delivered through face-to-face lectures which address both theoretical subjects and practical problems aimed at clarifying theoretical topics. During the lectures examples of exercises of the same type as those of the final test will be illustrated. Some simple demonstration experiments are planned to be carried out in the classroom.

## Assessment methods

The purpose of the final examination is to verify the level of achievement of the teaching objectives indicated above. It consists of a written test divided into three numerical exercises with the aim of determining the level of knowledge acquired. The above test is preparatory to the oral test, which can only be taken if a score of at least 18/30 has been achieved. The oral test aims to assess the ability to use the knowledge acquired to analyze and solve simple problems related to electrical or magnetic phenomena in vacuum and material media as well as the skills and/or competencies acquired for the analysis of DC and AC electrical circuits. For attending students only, there is the possibility of producing short written reports, which can be evaluated, on the experiments presented in class. The final score, defined at the end of the oral test and expressed in thirty-ths, does not result from the arithmetic mean of the previous tests.
