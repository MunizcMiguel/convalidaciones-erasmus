# Software Engineering

ECTS: 6 · Year 3 · Semester 5

## Objectives (learning outcomes)

After having completed this course unit, the student should be able to:

OA1. Apply an engineering process to the production of software;

OA2. Produce software in large groups;

OA3. Apply principles of software engineering such as software requirements analysis, software analysis and design, implementation, testing, code inspection, configuration management, build, delivery and deployment;

OA4. Software product quality assessment and improvement.

## Programme (contents)

CP1 - Software engineering processes

CP2 - Configuration management and build automation

CP3 - Software requirements

CP4 - Software testing

CP5 - Software quality

CP6 - Software delivery and evolution

## Evaluation (assessment)

ASSESSMENT THROUGHOUT THE SEMESTER

In accordance with Article 7A of the RGACC, assessment will be carried out throughout the semester, using the following two types of instruments:

• Six (6) fortnightly practical assignments, weighted at 30% (maximum of 6 points in total). Each of these guides will introduce the use of at least one new tool in the context of a project (group work) throughout the semester, which may be differentiated according to the students' degree (LEI, LIGE, and LETI) for motivation purposes.

• Two (2) individual written assessment tests, each worth 35% (maximum of 7 points each), one taken during the mid-semester break and the other coinciding with the first exam period. Each of these tests will have two components, one focusing on practical learning conveyed through the completion of the scripts and the other focusing on theoretical content, both worth half the mark of the corresponding written test.

Both the set of 6 practical scripts and the set of 2 tests must have a minimum grade of half the maximum score to pass, i.e., 3 points in the case of scripts and 7 points in the case of tests. Each script must be submitted within a maximum of 2 weeks after its release. Final grades above 16 points will be confirmed in an individual oral exam held immediately after the second test.

SECOND ASSESSMENT PERIOD

Pursuant to Article 13, paragraph 2, of the RGACC, this course does not include an exam-based assessment; therefore, the second assessment period will allow students to complete their assessment over the course of the semester, either to pass the course or to improve their grade. This second-session assessment will consist of a single individual written test, and students may choose one of three options:

i) a 1.5-hour test covering the material from the first test (maximum score of 7, corresponding to 35% of the final grade);

ii) a 1.5-hour test covering the material from the second test (maximum score of 7, accounting for 35% of the final grade);

iii) a 3-hour test covering the entire course material (maximum score of 14, accounting for 70% of the final grade).

These tests will consist of two components: one focusing on practical learning demonstrated through the completion of the assignments, and the other focusing on theoretical content, with each accounting for approximately half of the written test’s grade.

The remaining 30% of the final grade in the second assessment period (maximum of 6 points) corresponds to the score obtained on the 6 assignments completed throughout the semester.

The rules regarding the minimum passing grade for the second assessment period are the same as those for the first assessment period.

The assessment scheme is the same as for the second assessment period.
