The texts and teaching materials will be provided through the course web page.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide the student with the basics of web programming. The main components of such systems are illustrated, and design guidelines useful for designing a web server are presented. To reinforce the concepts presented in class, the techniques analyzed will be demonstrated through the practical implementation of a sample system. By the end of the course, students will know the main techniques for implementing the system components and the methods necessary for their integration to develop a complete server system. * Knowledge and Understanding: The student will be able to understand and design the main components that form a web server. * Applying Knowledge and Understanding: The student will be able to use the necessary tools to create the components of a web server, both to identify solutions and to justify, support, and argue their design choices. * Autonomy of Judgment: The student will be able to analyze and design basic web systems, considering criteria such as resource requests and system response speed, and to identify and gather additional information to achieve greater awareness if the acquired knowledge is insufficient to meet the required requirements. * Communication Skills: The student will be able to present the functionalities of a web system and to communicate implementation issues related to the design and resource availability required for implementing a web system. * Learning Skills: The student will be able to evaluate and recognize the acquired knowledge and to search for additional solutions through bibliographic research to pursue further studies, such as master's degree courses.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of logic and computer science. Previous knowledge of programming languages is recommended but not mandatory.

## Teaching methods

The course is organized into approximately 40 hours of theory and 20 hours of laboratory, in which the various components of a sample web service will be designed to apply the theoretical knowledge provided through lectures. The laboratory hours are considered as a simulation of the exam.

## Assessment methods

The assessment test consists of two phases: - an oral test, including theoretical questions and design exercises related to the development of web system components, which will contribute to 2/3 of the final grade; - the development of a group software project, using the tools illustrated in the course, which will contribute to 1/3 of the final grade. The oral test consists of questions and requires the completion of some exercises (e.g., the design of a functionality of a web service) aimed at verifying the actual acquisition of the expected learning outcomes, covering most of the program at various levels of detail. The student's ability to pursue the educational objectives listed in the course description will be evaluated. The evaluation is objective, defined based on the percentage of correctness of each answer (each associated with a fixed score, unknown to the candidate, based on the difficulty level). To pass the exam, the student must receive a score of no less than 18/30 and must demonstrate sufficient knowledge of the topics to be able to argue the answers and solutions adopted for the proposed design. The test is evaluated with a maximum score of 32. If the score is greater than or equal to 31, honors are awarded. The student is encouraged to go beyond simply studying to solve the single exercise through problems requiring the ability to transition from the abstract concept to its practical application. The student is always invited to justify the provided answer. This approach makes it possible to evaluate the knowledge and ability to apply it to problems of different complexity, and also to assess communication skills and judgment autonomy. The number of exam sessions is defined according to the Faculty regulations.
