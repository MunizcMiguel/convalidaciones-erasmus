## Contents

The course is organized into five modules. The first three modules focus on the main types of digital media: images, audio, and video. The fourth module covers quality assessment methods for digital media, while the fifth module provides skills related to traditional and immersive multimedia applications. Image Module Image acquisition and representation; Image compression and encoding (lossy, lossless, JPEG); Image processing techniques. Audio Module Audio acquisition and representation; Audio compression and encoding (PCM, MPEGX); Audio processing techniques. Video Module Video acquisition and representation; Video compression and encoding (MPEGX, H.26X); Video processing techniques. Digital Media Quality Assessment Module Subjective methods; Quality of Experience; Objective metrics (No-Reference, Reduced-Reference, Full-Reference). Traditional and Immersive Multimedia Applications Module Video streaming (DASH); 360° video; Point clouds; Point cloud compression and encoding (V-PCC, G-PCC).

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide skills related to digital media (audio, images, video), focusing on the main technologies and solutions for their representation, compression, encoding, and processing. -Knowledge and understanding: by the end of the course, students will have acquired knowledge of techniques for representing, compressing, encoding, and processing digital media (audio, images, video). They will understand quality measurement metrics for digital media and be familiar with both traditional and immersive multimedia applications. -Applying Knowledge and understanding: the knowledge acquired will enable students to select and apply appropriate techniques for the representation, compression, and processing of digital media. They will be able to choose and apply suitable quality measurement metrics and understand how traditional and immersive multimedia applications work. -Making judgment: by the end of the course, students will be able to identify the most suitable approaches and technologies for representing, compressing, processing, and measuring the quality of digital media. -Communication skills: students will be able to express themselves appropriately using the correct terminology to describe digital media and their representation, compression, and processing techniques. They will have acquired a suitable technical vocabulary to communicate effectively, especially with specialized audiences. -Learning skills: students will gain the ability to understand how traditional and immersive multimedia applications work and are designed. They will also develop the capacity to update their skills in line with technological advancements, understand new multimedia solutions, and adapt to new development systems.

## Prerequisites

To successfully follow the course, students are expected to have prior knowledge from foundational courses in computer science, telecommunication networks, and telecommunication systems. Additionally, a basic understanding of programming languages and MATLAB is required.

## Teaching methods

Lectures (40 hours) using slides that include theoretical explanations accompanied by practical examples and in-depth exercises; practical sessions with MATLAB (20 hours).

## Other information

All materials (slides, exercises) are available on the course Team within the university’s Microsoft Teams platform.

## Assessment methods

The evaluation is based on verifying the student’s understanding of both theoretical and practical concepts covered in the course. The final exam consists of a 2-hour written test, which includes both open-ended and multiple-choice questions. These questions may cover both theoretical concepts and practical exercises.
