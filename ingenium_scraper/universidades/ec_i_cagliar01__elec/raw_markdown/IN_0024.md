# POWER ELECTRONICS (IN/0024)

- **Grading:** Voto Finale
- **Attendance:** Mandatory
- **Teaching methods:** Practise, Lesson

## Contents

Electronic Power Systems ( 4 hours lessons) Classification of power electronic systems and converters. Circuit theory, power and power factor in the presence of current and voltage harmonic distortion. Semiconductor devices (4 hours lesson, 2 hours tutorial/exercises) Physics of Semiconductor (Diodes, Thyristor, BJT, MOSFET, etc.). Power dissipated in conduction and in switching. Diode rectifiers (6 hours lessons, 5 hours of tutorial/exercises) Basic concepts. Single-phase and three-phase bridge rectifiers. Non instantaneous switching of the line inductors. Single-phase and three-phase bridge rectifiers. Phase control thyristor converters (6 hours lessons, 5 hours tutorial/exercises). Single-phase and three-phase bridge thyristor converters. Line inductance. Overview of the recommended technical standards. DC/DC converters (8 hours lessons, 4 hours tutorial/exercises). DC / DC converter (buck, boost and buck-boost) continuous and discontinuous conduction modes and output filters. Four quadrant DC / DC converter, pulse width modulation (PWM). DC / AC converters (8 hours lessons, 4 hours tutorial/exercises) Voltage source and current source inverters. Pulse width modulation (PWM). Single-phase half-bridge and full-bridge inverter. Hysteresis regulators. Design criteria for converters (4 hour lesson) Functions and types of snubber. R-C type snubber for diodes and power switches. Pilot circuits

## Reference texts

1)Power Electronics “Converter Applications and Design”-Mohan, Undeland,Robbins 2)Switching Power supply design - Abraham I. Pressman

## Learning objectives

Knowledge and understanding: knowledge and understanding of the theoretical aspects of power devices and arrangements for the static conversion of electricity with a view to the energy transition. - Applying knowledge and understanding: ability to analyze structures used for static electricity conversion in the civil and / or industrial sector and to assess their impact on the electricity grid and the environment. - Making judgements: ability to correctly evaluate the performance of static energy conversion systems based on their topology. - Communication skills: ability to carry out technical discussions both of the problems inherent to the conversion system under examination and of the possible solutions to be undertaken to improve efficiency. - Learning skills: continuous learning skills, using the correct interpretation of the scientific literature of the sector;

## Assessment methods

The course includes an oral exam, usually consisting of two questions, aimed at verifying the actual acquisition of the expected learning outcomes. The exam covers most of the topics discussed in class, addressing various levels of detail and including both simple application exercises and theoretical questions. To pass the exam, the student must correctly answer all the questions; otherwise, the exam will be considered insufficient. During the oral exam, the student is always required to justify their answers, either through a mathematical calculation process or clear explanations. This approach allows for the assessment of the acquired competencies, the ability to apply them to engineering problems according to the level achieved, as well as communication skills and independent judgment. The number of exam sessions is defined in accordance with the Faculty regulations. Extra exam sessions for students who are behind schedule can be requested from the instructor for justified reasons.
