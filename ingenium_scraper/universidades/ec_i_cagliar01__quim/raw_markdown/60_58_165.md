## Contents

Introduction: Main classes of biological molecules; energetic metabolism and ATP; recall about water molecule properties ((hydrogen bonds, water interactions with polar, apolar and amphipathic solutes); recalls about acid-base equilibria and buffers. Amino acids: classification and nomenclature of standard alpha-amino acids; acid-base properties and titration curves; peptide bond; polypeptide conformations; polypeptide ionization; protein structure levels; secondary structure elements, fibrous proteins: alpha-keratin, collagen, fibroin; globular proteins; proteins denaturation. Myoglobin and Hemoglobin: oxygen transport; Hemoglobin cooperative binding; the Hill’s and the Adair’s models; the Bohr effect; modulation of Hemoglobin by CO2, H+ and 2,3-BPG; molecular basis of sickle-cell anemia. Main enzymic mechanisms; vitamins, cofactors, coenzymes; enzymatic activity regulation and allosteric modulation; fundamentals of enzyme inhibition: competitive, uncompetitive and mixed inhibition. Carbohydrates: classification and nomenclature of monosaccharides; cyclic hemiacetals and hemiketals formation; anomers and epimers; acetals and ketals formation; the glycosidic bond, disaccharides; polysaccharides: cellulose, amylose, amylopectin, glycogen. Lipids: classification and nomenclature of fatty acids and lipids; triacylglycerols, glycerophospholipids, glycolipids, sphingolipids, and wax esters; sterols; physical properties and aggregation states; biological membranes. Metabolism: general overview; ATP and phosphoryl-transfer; main redox coenzymes; Glycolysis; homolactic and alcoholic fermentation; Gluconeogenesis.Pyruvate oxidative decarboxylation; Citric acid cycle; Respiratory chain; Oxidative phosphorylation; lipids digestion and absorption; beta-oxidation of fatty acids; amino acids transamination, oxidative deamination; Urea cycle.

## Learning objectives

Knowledge: the Student will know structures and properties of the main classes of biological molecules such as proteins, carbohydrates and lipids, and moreover the main degradative pathways involved in the energetic metabolism. Skills: the Student will be able to apply her/his previus knowledge in the fields of General, Organic and Physical Chemistry to a physiological context. Behavior: the Student will be able to integrate the acquired knowledge within a coherent frame.

## Prerequisites

The Student should be proficient in General and Organic Chemistry. A good Physical-Chemistry background completes the prerequisites. STUDENTS MUST PASS THE EXAMINATION OF ORGANIC CHEMISTRY PRIOR TO THAT OF BIOCHEMISTRY.

## Teaching methods

The course stands along 12 weeks with 48 hours of lectures (4 hours/week). Lectures are carried out by using Power Point slides, also taking advantage from animations to visualize reaction mechanism and 3D molecular models.

## Other information

The slides projected during the lectures will be provided to the students. They are merely intended as a study guideline. Slides are neither to be considered as alternative nor additional to the suggested textbook in any way.

## Assessment methods

Description: Examination has the form of a colloquium. Aims: Examinations are aimed to a correct and unbiased evaluation of the preparation level reached by the Student, i.e. the achievements of the formative targets required: a) acquisition of a notion baggage coherent and consistent; b) logical integration among the acquired notions, to build a logical and cognitive path, suitable for reaching competence; c) Ability in developing the acquired skills to continue the study or to apply them at work or as professional. Modes: The examination is an individual colloquium, also requiring the use of graphics, equations, schemes, flux diagrams. From general to particular, the student will move among basic concepts to proceed in depth within the topic. Final decision: The final decision takes into account different factors: a) Meaningfulness; b) Knowledge of the scientific language related to the specific course; c) Knowledge of the specific course contents; d) Ability in correlating notions and place them into a coherent frame; e) Ability in relating different frames within a comprehensive and consistent view; f) ability in the use of graphics, equations, schemes, flux diagrams, chemical structural formulae,equations.
