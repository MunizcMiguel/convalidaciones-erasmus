## Learning objectives

THE COURSE MATHEMATICS I IS DESIGNED TO PROVIDE A RIGOROUS FOUNDATION IN THE FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPTS OF MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS AND CALCULUS, WITH PARTICULAR EMPHASIS ON THEIR PHYSICAL AND GEOMETRICAL INTERPRETATIONS. THE COURSE IS STRUCTURED AROUND THE FOLLOWING EDUCATIONAL OBJECTIVES: -) KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING STUDENTS ARE EXPECTED TO ACQUIRE A SOLID UNDERSTANDING OF THE FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPTS OF MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS, INCLUDING NUMBER SETS, REAL-VALUED FUNCTIONS, SEQUENCES OF REAL NUMBERS, LIMITS, CONTINUITY, DERIVATIVES, THE FUNDAMENTAL THEOREMS OF DIFFERENTIAL CALCULUS, AND THE GRAPHICAL ANALYSIS OF FUNCTIONS. THE COURSE ALSO COVERS SINGLE-VARIABLE INTEGRATION, NUMERICAL SERIES OF REAL NUMBERS, AND THE CANONICAL FORMS OF CONIC SECTIONS. -) APPLYING KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING STUDENTS ARE EXPECTED TO DEVELOP MATHEMATICAL ARGUMENTS IN A RIGOROUS AND COHERENT MANNER, AND TO APPLY THE THEOREMS AND RULES STUDIED TO PROBLEM SOLVING. THEY SHOULD BE ABLE TO PERFORM CALCULATIONS INVOLVING LIMITS, DERIVATIVES, AND INTEGRALS (BOTH INDEFINITE AND DEFINITE). -) LEARNING SKILLS STUDENTS ARE EXPECTED TO DEVELOP THE LEARNING SKILLS NECESSARY TO PURSUE SUBSEQUENT STUDIES WITH A HIGH DEGREE OF AUTONOMY, AND TO CRITICALLY ADDRESS MORE GENERAL PROBLEMS.

## Prerequisites

FOR THE SUCCESSFUL ACHIEVEMENT OF THE COURSE OBJECTIVES, STUDENTS ARE REQUIRED TO POSSESS THE FOLLOWING PREREQUISITES: – KNOWLEDGE OF ALGEBRA, WITH PARTICULAR REFERENCE TO ALGEBRAIC, LOGARITHMIC, AND EXPONENTIAL EQUATIONS AND INEQUALITIES; – KNOWLEDGE OF TRIGONOMETRY AND GONIOMETRY, WITH PARTICULAR REFERENCE TO THE BASIC GONIOMETRIC FUNCTIONS.

## Assessment methods

THE EXAMINATION CONSISTS OF BOTH A WRITTEN TEST AND AN ORAL EXAMINATION. IN ORDER TO PASS THE EXAM, IT IS NECESSARY TO PASS BOTH PARTS. THE WRITTEN TEST CONSISTS OF SOLVING THREE EXERCISES RANDOMLY SELECTED FROM THE FOLLOWING TYPES COVERED DURING THE COURSE: DETERMINATION OF THE DOMAIN OF A SCALAR FUNCTION (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 12 POINTS); SOLUTION OF AN EQUATION IN THE COMPLEX FIELD (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 6 POINTS); CALCULATION OF THE LIMIT OF A FUNCTION IN AN INDETERMINATE FORM (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 12 POINTS); STUDY OF THE MAIN PROPERTIES OF A SCALAR FUNCTION (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 12 POINTS); INTEGRATION (DEFINITE OR INDEFINITE) OF A SCALAR FUNCTION (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 12 POINTS); DETERMINATION OF THE NATURE OF A NUMERICAL SERIES (UP TO A MAXIMUM OF 6 POINTS). THE TIME AVAILABLE FOR THE WRITTEN TEST IS GENERALLY ONE HOUR AND THIRTY MINUTES. THE GRADE FOR THE WRITTEN TEST RANGES FROM 1 TO 30 (SUM OF THE SCORES OBTAINED IN THE INDIVIDUAL EXERCISES ACCORDING TO THE CRITERIA SPECIFIED ABOVE) AND DEPENDS ON THE STUDENT’S ABILITY TO APPLY KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING. IN ORDER TO BE ADMITTED TO THE ORAL EXAMINATION, STUDENTS MUST OBTAIN A GRADE GREATER THAN OR EQUAL TO 18, ENSURING AN ADEQUATE MINIMUM LEVEL OF LEARNING. THE WRITTEN TEST GRADE IS NOT RETAINED FOR SUBSEQUENT EXAM SESSIONS. THE ORAL EXAMINATION (WHICH MAY ONLY BE TAKEN AFTER PASSING THE WRITTEN TEST), LASTING APPROXIMATELY 30 MINUTES, FOCUSES ON DEFINITIONS, STATEMENTS, AND PROOFS OF THE THEOREMS COVERED DURING THE COURSE. IT IS AIMED AT ASSESSING METHODOLOGICAL AND EXPOSITORY RIGOR, APPROPRIATE MATHEMATICAL LANGUAGE, AND SYNTHESIS SKILLS. THE ORAL EXAMINATION IS CONSIDERED PASSED IF THE STUDENT DEMONSTRATES SUFFICIENT KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING AT LEAST OF THE DEFINITIONS AND STATEMENTS OF THE MAIN THEOREMS RELATED TO THE FOLLOWING TOPICS: ELEMENTARY FUNCTIONS, LIMITS OF FUNCTIONS, DERIVATIVES OF FUNCTIONS, DEFINITE AND INDEFINITE INTEGRATION. THE FINAL GRADE, EXPRESSED ON A SCALE OF THIRTY WITH POSSIBLE HONORS (“CUM LAUDE”), IS DETERMINED STARTING FROM THE WRITTEN TEST GRADE AND THEN NORMALLY ADJUSTED UPWARD OR DOWNWARD ON THE BASIS OF THE ORAL EXAMINATION. A STUDENT WHO PASSES THE MATHEMATICS I EXAMINATION WILL THEREFORE AT LEAST BE ABLE TO IDENTIFY THE FUNDAMENTAL PROPERTIES OF ELEMENTARY FUNCTIONS, UNDERSTAND AND COMPUTE LIMITS, DERIVATIVES, AND INTEGRALS, AND ANALYZE FUNCTIONS OF ONE REAL VARIABLE. EXCELLENCE, CORRESPONDING TO THE AWARD OF HONORS (“CUM LAUDE”), IS ACHIEVED WHEN THE STUDENT’S KNOWLEDGE COVERS THE ENTIRE SYLLABUS, CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING IS COMPLETE AND IN-DEPTH, PROBLEM-SOLVING PROCEDURES ARE FULLY APPROPRIATE TO THE NATURE OF THE PROBLEMS, AND PERFORMANCE IN TRANSFERABLE SKILLS IS GENERALLY EXCELLENT.
