## Learning objectives

The course will provide a comprehensive knowledge of differential and integral calculus for real and vector-valued multivariable functions. Priority will be given to the understanding and the ability of applying the definitions and the main results, rather than focusing on the proofs (however, some of them will be discussed in detail). A large number of examples and exercises will be provided: at the end of the course students will be proficient in the main theoretical notions and will be able to make computations involving directional and partial derivatives, multivariable integrals, line and surface integrals.

## Contents

• Curves in R^N. Continuous curve, support of a curve; simple and closed curve. Parametrizations of a line segment, a circle, and an ellipse; graph of a function as a curve; curve in R^2 in polar form. Regular and piecewise regular curves. Tangent vector. Length of a rectifiable curve. Equivalent curves and changes of parametrization. Arc-length parameter. Line integral of the first kind and its geometric and physical meaning. Invariance of first-kind line integrals under equivalent parametrizations and reversal of orientation.  
  
• Functions of several variables. Open ball in R^N. Definition of limits. Continuity of functions of several variables. Interior, exterior, and boundary points. Open, closed, bounded, and connected sets. Neighborhood of a point. Properties of open and closed sets. Interior, boundary, and closure of a set. Weierstrass theorem. Intermediate value theorem for zeros.  
  
• Differential calculus for scalar-valued functions. Partial derivatives. Gradient. Differentiability at a point. Differentiability implies existence of partial derivatives and continuity. Tangent hyperplane. Total differential theorem (sufficient condition for differentiability). Directional derivative. Gradient formula. Differentiation of composite functions. Orthogonality of the gradient to level sets. A function with zero gradient on a connected open set is constant. Higher-order partial derivatives. Schwarz’s theorem. Hessian matrix. Second-order Taylor formula with remainder in Lagrange form and Peano form. Definition of local/absolute/strict maximum and minimum points; critical points. Fermat’s theorem. Positive definite, negative definite, positive semidefinite, negative semidefinite, and indefinite quadratic forms. Eigenvalue criterion. Criterion based on leading principal minors. Saddle points. Second-order necessary condition and sufficient condition for local minima/maxima. Classification of critical points using the Hessian matrix.  
  
• Vector-valued functions. Continuity and differentiability of vector-valued functions of several real variables. Jacobian matrix. Differential. Chain rule for composite functions. Coordinate transformations in R^2 and R^3.  
  
• Surfaces in R^3. Definition of a surface. Coordinate curves. Regular surfaces and singular points. Tangent plane. Unit normal vector. Cartesian surfaces. Surfaces of revolution.  
  
• Applications of differential calculus. Implicit functions: Dini’s theorem, existence and continuity of the implicit function. Formula for the derivative of an implicit function. Extension to several variables. Constrained extrema. Definition of a constrained extremum point. Parametric method (when the constraint can be solved explicitly). Method of Lagrange multipliers. Lagrangian function. Lagrange multipliers in the case of M constraints.  
  
• Multivariable integral calculus. Definition of the Riemann integral in several variables and its characterizations. Peano–Jordan measure: measurable sets and sets of measure zero. Integrability of continuous and piecewise continuous functions. Properties of the integral and of measure. Mean value theorem. Reduction theorem. Cavalieri’s principle. Integration by slicing and by layers. Volume of solids of revolution. Normal domains and integration over normal domains. Change-of-variables theorem. Polar coordinates in the plane and spherical and cylindrical coordinates in space. Pappus–Guldinus theorem. Applications: computation of centroids and moments of inertia.  
  
• Vector fields. Definition of vector and scalar fields. Differential operators: gradient, Laplacian, curl, and divergence. Identities involving differential operators. Irrotational and solenoidal fields. Line integral of the second kind of a vector field: work and circulation. Conservative fields and potentials. Conservative and irrotational fields: computation of a potential. Gauss–Green formulas and the divergence theorem in the plane. Area and surface integrals. Oriented surfaces and flux through a surface. Divergence theorem. Stokes’ theorem.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test. The exam time is at most 3 hours. In the test students will be asked to solve some exercises and to answer some theoretical questions. During the exam books, notes, calculators are not allowed. The exam is passed if the final score is greater than or equal to 18/30. The results of the test will be communicated by email. An oral exam may be required by the exam committee in some cases.
