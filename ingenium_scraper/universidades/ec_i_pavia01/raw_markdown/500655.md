### Learning objectives

The course is divided into two modules. The first module, taught by [docente], focuses on the fundamentals of computer science, the principles underlying the operation and organization of computing systems, computer networks and the Internet, as well as the fundamentals of algorithms and programming. The second module, taught by [docente], focuses on the principles of programming.  
  
The primary objectives of the course are to provide students with the fundamental principles of computer science and with an understanding of the logical foundations underlying the operation and organization of computing systems, while developing their ability to apply this knowledge to solve simple computational and programming problems.  
  
Upon successful completion of the course, students will have acquired the skills necessary to understand the main mechanisms of a computing system, describe and analyze simple algorithms, develop basic programs, and address computational problems of limited complexity, providing a foundation for the study of more advanced programming problems and techniques.

### Contents

The fundamental concepts of computer science, the role of computers, and the main areas of application are introduced. The main approaches to the representation and storage of information within computers are presented, with particular emphasis on the representation of numerical and textual data and the fundamental principles of information encoding. The principles underlying data processing and the operation of computing systems are introduced. The fundamental concepts of computer networks and the Internet are also presented. The concept of an algorithm is introduced, together with the main elements required to describe and solve problems using computational procedures. The fundamental concepts of programming languages and the process of translating and executing programs are also introduced. The practical part of the course is devoted to programming in Python.  
The course covers the main commands for file management in a Unix environment and describes the operation of compilers and interpreters as tools for program development. Data Structures: Key data structures are examined from the perspectives of both their abstract definition and their actual implementation on computing systems. Programming Principles: The principles of computer programming are introduced. Specific focus is placed on problem formalization, algorithm design and coding using structured programming methodologies and techniques, as well as criteria for program analysis, testing, and validation. The goal is to equip students with practical software development skills and an appreciation for the importance of good documentation and application reliability. The C Language: The programming module covers programming principles and provides a detailed description of C language syntax. Theoretical instruction is complemented by practical sessions in the Basic Computer Science laboratory, where solutions to problems involving numerical computing and data structures are presented. Specifically, students implement algorithms for sorting, table searching, data structure management, and more.

### Assessment methods

The examination consists of one assessment for Module A, taught by [docente], and one assessment for Module B, taught by [docente].  
  
For Module A, a written examination is required, consisting of multiple-choice questions and exercises covering the topics addressed during the course, including those covered in the practical Python programming exercises. Each question or exercise is assigned a score of 1, 2, or 3 points, based on the correctness and completeness of the student's answer. The grade for Module A is determined by the sum of the points obtained in the individual questions and exercises. The module is passed by achieving a minimum score of 18 points. A score of 31 points corresponds to a grade of 30 cum laude.  
  
The examination for Module B consists of a practical test to be carried out on the computers available in the Computer Science teaching laboratories and a programming theory test assessing the theoretical concepts presented during the course taught by [docente]. The practical test requires the implementation of an algorithm for the proposed problem, the coding of the corresponding program in C, its compilation, and debugging in the Computer Science teaching laboratories. The overall result of the programming assessment is the weighted average of the programming theory test and the practical test, with weights of 1/3 and 2/3, respectively.  
  
The assessments for the two modules may be taken in different examination sessions. The examination is passed upon obtaining a passing grade (18) in both assessments. The overall examination grade is the average of the grades obtained in the assessments for the two modules. Once passed, an assessment remains valid for two calendar years from the date on which it was passed. If a student retakes an assessment, the previously obtained result, even if passing, is forfeited.

### Assessment methods

The exam consists of a practical programming test and a programming theory test.  
  
The practical test is carried out in the computer science classrooms C2-C3. The test involves the solution of a problem through the realization of a program in C language. The duration of the practical test is 3 hours. During the test it is possible to use the notes and the textbook, but not the exercise book and other documents containing solved exercises.  
  
The programming theory test is carried out consists of questions and exercises on UNIX commands, flowcharts, and the C language.  
  
The overall grade of the part of the course relating to programming is the average between the grade of the practical part and that of the programming theory (weights 2/3 and 1/3 respectively).  
  
The overall grade of the Fundamentals of Computer Science exam is therefore composed of 3 parts: 1) the practical programming test 2) the programming theory test ([docente]) and 3) the Danish theoretical test. The three tests can be taken at any time, independently of each other. The result of a test that has already been passed remains valid for 1 year. If a test that has already been passed is taken again, e.g. to improve the previous result, that previous result is canceled, even if the most recent grade is insufficient.

## Partition: 8215-2

### Language

Italian

### Prerequisites

Those required for the subscription to the University courses.

### Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide students with the fundamentals of computer science, introducing the principles underlying the operation and organization of computing systems and developing the ability to apply this knowledge to simple computational problems. The theoretical part introduces the main concepts related to data representation and storage, data processing, computer networks and the Internet, while also providing the foundations for understanding algorithms and programming languages. The practical part focuses on Python programming and aims to develop students’ ability to translate simple problems into programs. In particular, the course covers variables and data types, conditionals and loops, strings and input handling, functions, scoping and abstraction, structured data types and mutability, higher-order functions, as well as fundamental testing and debugging techniques. The concepts of exceptions and assertions are also introduced for error handling and for verifying program behavior. By the end of the course, students will have acquired a solid understanding of the fundamental concepts of computer science and will be able to understand the main mechanisms of a computing system, describe and analyze simple algorithms, and develop basic programs in Python. These skills will provide a foundation for subsequent courses in their degree program and for independently exploring more advanced topics in computer science.

### Contents

The course introduces the fundamental concepts of computer science, the role of computers, and their main application areas, with particular reference to the concepts of information, data, processing, and computing systems. The main approaches to the representation and storage of information in computers are presented, with particular emphasis on the representation of numerical and textual data and the fundamental principles of information encoding. The principles underlying data processing and the operation of computing systems are introduced, with particular reference to the representation of operations and program execution. The course also introduces the fundamental concepts of computer networks and the Internet, including the main network devices, protocols, and services, as well as the basic principles of communication between systems. The concept of an algorithm is introduced, together with the main elements required to describe and solve problems through computational procedures. The fundamental concepts of programming languages and the process of translating and executing programs are also presented. The practical part focuses on Python programming and includes an introduction to the programming environment, variables, data types, and basic operations. Conditional and iterative structures, string operations, and user input are introduced. The course also covers the definition and use of functions, scoping and abstraction, as well as structured data types, mutability, and higher-order functions. Finally, fundamental testing and debugging techniques are introduced, together with exception handling and assertions for verifying program behavior.
