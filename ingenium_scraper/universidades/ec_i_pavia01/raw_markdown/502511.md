## Partition: 10313-1

### Language

Italian

### Prerequisites

Students are expected to be familiar with the working principles of diodes and MOS transistors, the use of Fourier transform, Laplace transform, Bode plots and complex numbers.  
Students are expected to be able to perform AC and DC, small and large signal analysis of simple electronic circuits, and to understand the structure and the operating principle of the basic amplification stages including operational amplifiers and MOS transistors.

### Learning objectives

The course aims to complete the basic training of the student in analog electronics and to introduce him to the laboratory activity, with the use of the measurement equipment and the circuit simulator Spice. At the end of the course the student should have acquired knowledge on the architectures of multi-stage amplifiers using both MOS and BJT transistors and a deep knowledge of the frequency behavior of the elementary devices and basic circuits; he/she must have a deep understanding of the negative feedback concepts, of the stability problems in amplifiers with feedback and of the techniques to compensate them.

### Contents

Review of amplification stages with single transistor. Behavior of the transistors at high frequency, the high frequency equivalent circuit for MOS and BJT. Analysis in the high frequency of the basic stages of amplification. The cascode stage. Differential stage BJT and MOS. Review of current mirrors. Multistage amplifiers. The feedback: basic concepts, negative and positive feedback. The negative reaction applied to the amplifiers. Calculation of the loop gain. Stability of amplifiers with feedback. Frequency compensation.

### Teaching methods

Lectures (hours/year in lecture theatre): 32 Practical class (hours/year in lecture theatre): 13 Practicals / Workshops (hours/year in lecture theatre): 16 The lecture are done sing the black board and sometime integrate with foils. The practical classes are done using the black board and they involves reviewing topics to be known solving examples of analytical calculations. and verifying the knowledge acquired by the students. The workshops are done with the use of the circuit simulator and the measuring lab equipment.

### Reference texts

A.S. Sedra, K.C. Smith. Microelectronics Circuits. Spice manual; Data Sheets e Applications of electronic components.

### Assessment methods

The exam consists of an oral exam for each of the two modules (A and B). Generally, the oral exam is intended to verify the student's understanding of the course topics and their ability to solve circuit problems.  
At the end of each module, there will also be an optional written exam "in itinere" covering the topics of the module just completed. Each written exam "in itinere" includes two or three circuit problems and ten multiple-choice questions.  
If the written exam is passed, the oral exam for the corresponding module becomes optional.  
If the student passes both the "in itinere" written exams and does not take the oral exams, the grade is calculated based on the average of the written exam scores.  
The student may, however, take the oral exam in order to improve their grade on the written "in itinere" exam(s). The oral exam may be taken in the same session as the written exam or in a subsequent session.  
The oral exam remains mandatory for modules for which the student does not take or pass the written exams "in itinere". The final grade is the average of the grades obtained in the two modules plus a score ranging between zero and one point based on the evaluation of the laboratory activity reports.

## Partition: 10313-2

### Language

Italian

### Prerequisites

Knowledge of circuit theory and applied electronics (analog) with particular reference to the MOS and BJT transistors, operational amplifiers, Bode plots. Familiarity with the use of the Fourier transform, the Laplace transform, and the complex numbers. Contents of the Modulus A of this course.

### Learning objectives

The course aims to complete the basic training of the student in analog and mixed signal electronics making use of an intensive laboratory measurement activity and of circuit simulation. At the end of the course the student should have acquired knowledge on architectures of simple electronics systems, ranging from multistage linear and non linear circuits, signal conditioning stages, waveform generators. He/she will know the systems for the signal conversion from analog to digital and vice versa and the theory of noise in circuits.

### Contents

(1) Internal structure of bipolar and MOS transistor operational amplifiers. Single-stage and two-stage operational amplifiers. Frequency-response compensation.  
(2) Class A and Class B power amplifier stages. Power transistors and thermal management/heatsink design.  
(3) Analog filters. Filter classification. The approximation problem: Butterworth and Chebyshev filters. First- and second-order transfer functions and their implementation using operational amplifiers. Introduction to the implementation of Gm-C and switched-capacitor filters.  
(4) Electronic noise. Analytical tools for electronic noise analysis. Noise sources in electronic components. Calculation of the input-referred noise in amplifiers.  
(5) Oscillators. Sinusoidal oscillators: operating principles and analysis of RC, LC, and crystal oscillator configurations. Relaxation oscillators and waveform generators: operating principles and analysis of the most common circuit configurations.

### Teaching methods

Lectures (hours/year in class): 22  
Problem-solving sessions (hours/year in class): 15  
Practical laboratory activities (hours/year in class): 43  
  
Lectures are delivered with the aid of presentation slides. Problem-solving sessions include a review of prerequisite topics, analytical problem solving, and weekly assessment of students’ learning. Practical activities are carried out using both circuit simulation software and laboratory measurement equipment.

### Reference texts

A.S. Sedra, K.C. Smith. Microelectronics Circuits. Spice manual; Data Sheets e Applications of electronic components. Plus notes from the instructors.

### Assessment methods

The examination consists of an oral examination. In general, the oral examination is aimed at assessing the student’s understanding of the topics covered in the course and their ability to solve circuit-related problems.  
At the end of the lectures for each module, an optional midterm written examination will also be offered, covering the topics of the module just completed. Each midterm written examination consists of 2 or 3 circuit problems and 10 multiple-choice questions.  
If the midterm examination is passed, the oral examination for the corresponding module becomes optional.  
Students may nevertheless take the oral examination in order to improve the grade obtained in the midterm examination. The oral examination may be taken during the same examination session as the midterm examination or during a subsequent session.  
The oral examination remains mandatory if the student does not take or does not pass the midterm examination.  
The final grade is calculated as the average of the grades obtained in the two modules, increased by a score ranging from zero to one point, based on the assessment of the reports submitted for the laboratory activities.
