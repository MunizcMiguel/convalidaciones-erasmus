### Prerequisites

Basic knowledge on mechanics, geometry and calculus

### Teaching methods

• Theory: 50 hours • Exercises and practical activities: 22 hours The course is taught in person and combines lectures, exercises, problem-solving activities, and opportunities for interactive and individual learning. In the classroom, special attention is given to understanding mechanical models, selecting appropriate analytical methods, developing calculation procedures and critically assessing the obtained results. Exercises may include laboratory-like activities based on the observation and analysis of mechanical components, mechanisms and simple mechanical systems, aimed at connecting theoretical models with the behaviour of real systems. Interactive tools for formative assessment of learning may also be used during classes. Teaching activities may be complemented by seminars and talks given by experts from industry and the professional world, either in person or remotely, as well as, where possible, by technical visits to companies or facilities relevant to the course topics. In-person activities are complemented by teaching materials available on the University’s Moodle platform, including handouts, exercises, multimedia materials, videos and other selected resources. Such materials support the autonomous study of selected topics, as well as the preparation, consolidation and further study of the activities carried out in class.

### E-LEARNING

The course is not offered via e-learning.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The level of student learning is assessed through two tests, taken in the same examination session: • an individual written test, in person and under supervision, consisting of the complete solution of an exercise from the official course exercise collection, including model formulation, solution procedure, calculations and numerical results; • an oral examination covering the entire syllabus, assessing knowledge and understanding of machine mechanics, mechanisms and mechanical components, as well as appropriate technical terminology and critical reasoning. The oral examination may include a brief discussion of the written test to verify understanding. Foreign-language students may take the exam in English upon request. For students with disabilities/invalidities or specific learning disorders who have requested support from the University Disability/DSA Info Point, examination methods will be adapted according to University guidelines.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

In order to pass the examination, students must demonstrate understanding of the fundamental course concepts and basic skills in the kinematics, statics and dynamics of machines, including vibrations, and in the main mechanisms. The written test assesses correct formulation of the mechanical model, selection and application of analysis methods, correctness of calculations and results, and their physical and engineering consistency. The oral examination assesses knowledge and understanding of the course topics, the ability to establish connections among them, and appropriate use of technical terminology. The highest grade is awarded to students demonstrating excellent analytical and calculation skills and, in the oral examination, thorough knowledge, critical reasoning, methodological rigor and command of technical terminology.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

A thirty-point scale is used for grading, with possible honours.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

For the overall assessment to be positive, students must achieve at least 16 points out of 30 in the written test and a sufficient assessment in the oral examination, as well as an overall grade of at least 18 points out of 30. The final grade is obtained by combining the assessments of previous parts with the following weights: • written test: 40%; • oral exam: 60%;

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The module explains the principles that underlie the operation of machinery and describes the most important mechanisms used in industrial applications. The course gives a basic engineering education in the industrial field and the student achieves basic technical training in mechanics of machines. Such knowledge will be useful for young graduates to interface with specialists from other engineering fields. In this way, students will acquire knowledge that will help them in the future to develop projects using appropriate methodologies, techniques and methods.

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

The course aims to provide students with the ability to solve mechanical engineering problems using the typical methods of Mechanics of Machines. By acquiring the ability to develop models,, students will be able to approach the main problems of machine mechanics, with particular reference to the kinematic, static and dynamic analysis of machines and simple mechanical systems. Particular attention is devoted to the ability to correctly formulate a problem, identify appropriate assumptions and models, select suitable analysis tools, quantitatively develop the solution and critically assess the physical and engineering consistency of the obtained results. Furthermore, students will develop the ability to recognize the most important types of mechanical components and understand the fundamental aspects of their operation.

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The analytical nature of the most frequent problems in machine mechanics allows students to develop an aptitude for logical reasoning firmly based on the scientific method, mainly due to the methodological rigor of the subject, as well as to train their ability to concentrate. The proposed activities also aim at developing the ability to critically address problems that cannot be immediately reduced to a predefined procedure, by verifying the consistency and reliability of the available data, formulating assumptions, selecting appropriate analysis tools and verifying the consistency of the obtained results.

### Contents

Theory: • Kinematic pairs and mechanisms • Contact forces and friction • Kinematics and statics of linkages • Dynamics of mechanical systems • Mechanical vibrations • Gears • Power transmissions • Joints, clutches, brakes and hoists Exercises and practical activities: • Joints, degrees of freedom, contact mechanics • Kinematic, static and dynamic modeling • Mechanical vibrations • Power flow in mechanical transmissions • Forces in gears
