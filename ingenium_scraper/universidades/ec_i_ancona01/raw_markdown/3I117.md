## Partition: ANALISI MATEMATICA 1 (Cognomi A-L)

### Prerequisites

Algebraic calculus and analytic geometry.

### Teaching methods

Theory lessons: 72 hours

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

The student will be assessed through the following tests: * An online test with questions about the prerequisites needed to understand the course material; * A written test with exercises that will test the ability to solve problems by using the techniques learned during the course; * An oral test in which the student will be asked to present some of the theoretical. For students with disabilities/invalidities or specific learning disorders (DSA), who have submitted a request for support to take the specific exam at the Disability/DSA Info Point of the University, the exam methods will be adapted in light of what is recommended by the University guidelines (https://www.univpm.it/Entra/Servizi_agli_studenti/Disabilita_e_DSA_Servizio_di_accoglienza)

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

To successfully pass the exam, the student must prove, through the tests listed above, that he has understood the fundamental concept presented during the course; that he has acquired the techniques of differential and integral calculus, and that he is able to set problems and solve them by suitable application of the techniques and methods learned during the course. The maximum grade is given to students who, in the written test, demonstrate excellent ability and full autonomy in solving the proposed problems and, in the oral test, demonstrate a thorough knowledge of the concepts presented during the course, methodological rigor and appropriateness of scientific language.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

A grade out of thirty is awarded, possibly with honour.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

Students will be admitted to the written examination only after passing the online preliminary test on the course prerequisites. They may proceed to the oral examination only after passing the written examination. Both examinations are deemed to have been passed only if the student achieves a minimum mark of 18/30. The final grade is determined through a comparative assessment of the written and oral examinations. Honours are awarded to students who have completed the examinations correctly and comprehensively and have demonstrated exceptional ability and independence of thought.

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The course aims to provide theoretical, methodological, and applied knowledge of Mathematical Analysis, enabling students—together with the subsequent course in Mathematical Analysis 2—to acquire the mathematical tools required for engineering applications. In particular, students will gain knowledge of the basic principles of differential calculus and integration theory for functions of one variable, and a range of applications will be presented.

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

To develop the ability to apply mathematical methods to model, analyse, and solve problems, the course will introduce the classical results of Mathematical Analysis, together with numerous applications. This learning pathway will enable students to develop the ability to analyse problems, identify different solution methods, and select the most appropriate approach.

### LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The skills acquired during the course will be essential for undertaking subsequent courses. Independently solving a wide range of problems and exercises, followed by their correction and discussion, will enhance students’ learning skills and their ability to make independent judgements. Presenting the topics covered and using the specialised language of foundational disciplines will also help develop their communication skills.

### Contents

The set of real numbers and its properties. Complex numbers: algebraic, polar, and exponential forms. Limits of numerical sequences. Numerical series and convergence tests. Functions of a real variable and limits of real-valued functions. Continuous functions and their properties. Differential calculus for functions of one real variable. Graphical analysis of real-valued functions. Selected optimization problems. L’Hôpital’s rule and Taylor’s theorem. Integral calculus for real-valued functions: definite integrals and their properties. Indefinite integrals and methods of integration. Improper integrals and convergence criteria. Power series and Taylor series. Introduction to Fourier series.
