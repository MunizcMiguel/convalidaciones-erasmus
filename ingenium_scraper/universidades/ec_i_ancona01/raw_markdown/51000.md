## Prerequisites

Differential and integral calculus in one and several variables; differential equations; curves and surfaces in space; matrices and eigenvalues problems; elementary classical mechanics.

## Teaching methods

Theoretical lectures: 48 hours.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Methods

Assessment will consist of a written practical test, which may also include theoretical questions, and a written and/or oral theoretical test, which may also require the solution of exercises. The two tests may also take place at the same time. For students with disabilities/invalidities or specific learning disorders (DSA), who have submitted a request for support to take the specific exam at the Disability/DSA Info Point of the University, the exam methods will be adapted in light of what is recommended by the University guidelines (https://www.univpm.it/Entra/Servizi_agli_studenti/Disabilita_e_DSA_Servizio_di_accoglienza).

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Evaluation Criteria

In the exams the student must show good understanding of the theoretical subjects of the course and the ability to use them for the modeling and the resolution of problems. In the practical test the ability to apply the techniques learned during the course towards the solution of practical problems will be assessed, while the theoretical test will establish the theoretical knowledge.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Learning Measurement Criteria

The final grade is awarded on a scale of 30, with the possibility of honours.

## DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXAMINATION - Final Mark Allocation Criteria

The written test gives a maximum mark of 24 points. Those who pass the written exam with a sufficient score of 18 are given the opportunity to choose between two oral methods: a) oral examination limited to the discussion of the written text, which would allow the vote to be confirmed with a possible variation of between minus and plus 2 points b) extended oral exam, with 3 questions, which would allow an increase between more or less 3 points per question.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Knowledge and Understanding

The aim of the course is that of providing the theoretical, methodological and practical elements of mechanics from the mathematical-physics perspective, with the objective of modelling analyzing and solving engineering problems. In particular, the kinematics, the statics and the dynamics of point-mass systems will be covered. The basic elements of Lagrangian mechanics will be covered as well.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Capacity to apply Knowledge and Understanding

The course will include many examples and exercises aimed at developing a thorough understanding of its topics and the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to real mechanical systems. In particular, the ability to use mathematical tools in engineering problems will be developed through the following steps: 1. being able to write the equations of motion for point-mass systems and rigid bodies using both Newtonian and Lagrangian approaches; 2. being able to solve the equations of motion in some important cases; 3. being able to determine the equilibrium configurations of the most important mechanical systems and study their stability; 4. being able to calculate the inertia matrix of a general rigid body and determine its principal axes of inertia. The knowledge and skills acquired in this course are essential for successfully undertaking subsequent courses in Structural Mechanics, Mechanics of Machines, and Machine Design.

## LEARNING OUTCOMES - Transversal Skills

The expertise acquired in this course will be needed in order to study the material of later courses, particularly of building science, machine-building and mechanics applied to machines. Individual and collective problem-solving sessions will improve the ability to develop independent thought and learning capabilities. Oral presentations of the topics taught in the course, with the language proper of the basic disciplines of the degree course will help developing communication skills

## Contents

Mathematical prerequisites. Point and rigid body kinematics. Kinematics of relative motions. Constrained systems. Geometry of masses. Kinematics of masses. Forces, Work, Power. Law of Mechanics. Statics, equilibria and stability. Principle of virtual work. Dynamics of the material point, of systems and rigid body. Mechanics in non-inertial systems. Lagrangian mechanics. Harmonic oscillator.
