## Contents

TLS/SSL. IPsec. VPN and tunnelling. DNSsec. Firewalls and NIDSs. Security in wireless networks based on 802.11 standard. Bluetooth security. Mobile code and security Viruses and related threats. ## Learning objectives

The course is intended for third-year students in the Computer Engineering degree program and aims to provide an overview of the main technologies currently used to implement network security and, more generally, information systems. The topics covered will therefore provide the technological foundation for working in the world of cybersecurity, enabling students to understand the main risks and challenges. Knowledge and understanding: By the end of the course, the student will possess a basic understanding of cybersecurity principles, security models, and major cyber threats. Furthermore, the student will be familiar with key techniques for protecting systems and networks. Applying knowledge and understanding: The student will be able to analyze vulnerabilities and assess the risks associated with cyber threats using fundamental cybersecurity methodologies and tools. Making judgments: By the end of the course, the student will be able to evaluate and devise simple security countermeasures for various application scenarios, balancing trade-offs between security requirements, performance, usability, and cost. Communication skills: By the end of the course, the student will be able to effectively communicate cybersecurity issues and solutions to both technical and non-technical audiences. Learning skills: By the end of the course, the student will have developed the ability to independently keep up to date with the evolution of major threats, technologies, and countermeasures in the field of cybersecurity.

## Prerequisites

To properly understand the topics covered, students must be familiar with the operation of modern PCs and the architecture of the Internet. Consequently, the following subjects are considered foundational: theoretical principles of operating systems; the architecture and operation of computer networks; and principles of network programming. The official prerequisite for this course is: Networked Systems Programming.

## Teaching methods

The course consists primarily of classroom lectures. Specifically, lectures will account for 95% of the course, while the remaining 5% will be taken up by practical exercises. Attendance is not mandatory, but participation in the learning activities is strongly encouraged. Teaching materials are available on the University e-learning platform Handy. Students with valid certifications for learning disabilities (SLD), disabilities, or other educational needs are encouraged to contact the professor or the Degree Course/Department Disability Coordinator at the beginning of the course to discuss possible teaching methods that, while respecting the course objectives, take into account individual learning needs.

## Assessment methods

Students must develop a thesis of at least 30 pages on one of the course topics of their choice. The thesis must be submitted via email to the professor at least 10 days before the exam session in which the student wishes to participate. The thesis is evaluated and a grade out of 30 is proposed to the student before the exam session. The student may accept or reject the proposed grade. If the grade is accepted, the exam is considered passed and an oral exam is not required. If the grade is rejected, the student must supplement the thesis or write another on a new topic and resubmit at the next exam session. The following factors contribute to the grade assigned to the thesis: (i) the quality of the content; (ii) the use of technical language and overall expressive ability; (iii) the ability to provide examples; and (iv) originality.
