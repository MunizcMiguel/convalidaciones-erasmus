## Contents

The course is divided into four sections. The first part recalls some basic concepts provided in electronic measurement courses. The concepts of metrology chain, components of a measurement system and their functionality, sensors and transducers are recalled and extended. Subsequently, the characteristics of automatic measurement systems and data acquisition systems are presented. The definitions of automatic measurement system, data acquisition system, distributed measurement system are provided; the standard model for the measurement instrumentation is presented; the elementary components of the previously defined systems are also presented: acquisition section, processing section, presentation section. The elementary concepts for the design of a data acquisition system are finally introduced. The second part of the course is dedicated to the study of the programming interfaces of the measurement instrumentation. The course introduces the IEEE 488, RS-232, RS-422, RS-449, RS-485 and USB interfaces, and the VXI and PXI systems, describing the electrical, mechanical and functional specifications of the main interface systems. The communication protocols of the IEEE 488 and RS-232C interfaces are studied in detail. The third part is dedicated to the presentation of the fundamental characteristics of the LabVIEW development environment, illustrating the programming of the data acquisition boards and of the IEEE 488 and RS-232C interfaces. Ample space is dedicated to experimental activities in laboratory, dedicating the last, and largest, part of the course to a laboratory project to be developed using Labview.

## Learning objectives

The Measurement Software course is designed to provide students with the knowledge needed to design and implement automatic measurement systems. In particular, the course focuses on developing the ability to independently create virtual instrumentation using the LabVIEW environment. By the end of the course, students will have acquired the knowledge and skills necessary to design and implement simple automated measurement systems based on virtual instrumentation, integrating data acquisition devices, communication interfaces, and dedicated software development environments. Knowledge and Understanding Students will be able to: • describe the architecture and operating principles of automatic measurement systems and data acquisition systems; • understand the structure of a metrological chain and the role of the main components of a measurement system; • understand the basic characteristics of the main communication interfaces for electronic instrumentation (IEEE 488, RS-232, RS-422, RS-449, RS-485, USB) and of VXI and PXI modular systems; • understand the principles of virtual instrumentation and the LabVIEW development environment, with particular reference to the programming of instruments and data acquisition systems. Applying Knowledge and Understanding The student will be able to: • design simple automated measurement systems based on application specifications; • develop software applications in the LabVIEW environment for the acquisition, processing, and visualization of measurement data; • program data acquisition devices and measurement instruments using the main communication interfaces; • integrate hardware and software to create virtual instruments capable of automating experimental and measurement procedures. Making Judgments Students will develop the ability to: • evaluate the most appropriate hardware and software solutions for implementing an automated measurement system; • identify the main critical issues in communication between instruments, in device configuration, and in data acquisition; • critically analyze the results obtained during experimental activities, identifying any anomalies and proposing corrective actions in simplified contexts. Communication Skills The student will be able to: • document design and experimental activities using appropriate technical language; • present and discuss the design choices made and the solutions developed as part of the final project; • collaborate effectively within a work group, contributing to the planning, development, and validation of the project; • clearly communicate results, issues, and solutions related to the design of automatic measurement systems. Learning Skills By the end of the course, students will have developed the ability to: • independently acquire skills in using new software tools and data acquisition devices by consulting technical manuals and specialized documentation; • stay up to date on developments in virtual instrumentation technologies and automated measurement systems; • address simple design problems by integrating knowledge from the fields of electronic measurement, programming, and hardware-software interfacing; • apply the methodologies learned to the development of similar applications in various contexts within information engineering.

## Teaching methods

Lectures in the classroom, laboratory exercises, and final group project work. Upon completion of the project, students will prepare a technical report to be discussed during the exam. In line with the total number of course hours, the instructional activities are roughly divided as follows: Lectures: approximately 40% Lab activities: approximately 60% Teaching materials and guidelines for independent study are available on the University’s Handy e-learning platform, which is also accessible to working students and non-attending students. Students with valid documentation regarding specific learning disabilities (SLD), disabilities, or other educational needs are encouraged to contact the instructor or the disability coordinator for the degree program or department at the beginning of the course to arrange any teaching accommodations that, while adhering to the course objectives, take individual learning needs into account.

## Assessment methods

Oral exam. Two-thirds of the grade will be based on the completeness and accuracy of the project solution, and one-third on the clarity and completeness of the presentation of theoretical concepts. The successful completion of the project work and the submission of the final technical report are required to take the exam.
