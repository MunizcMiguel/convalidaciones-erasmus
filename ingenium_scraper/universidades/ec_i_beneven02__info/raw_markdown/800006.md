## Contents

Introduction to the enterprise. The enterprise, its objectives, and key business activities; the relationship between organization, resources, and economic results. Economic and organizational terminology. 2. Feasibility elements of business initiatives. Preliminary analysis of the market, potential customers, and alternatives; factors influencing location choice. Relationship between costs, revenues, and activity volumes; break-even analysis. Main types of investment and introduction to business performance indicators. 3. Process-based organization and management systems: Business processes, responsibilities, and inter-functional coordination. Purpose and structure of a management system: goal setting, activity planning, results monitoring, and improvement. Introduction to management system standards, with examples regarding quality, the environment, and information security. In-depth look at quality management systems: customer requirements, product/service and process quality, non-conformity management and performance evaluation, quality-to-price ratio, and performance trends. 4. Elements of business economics and financial statement analysis. Classification of business costs; purpose, general principles, and structure of the annual financial statements. Introduction to the balance sheet and the income statement; interpretation of key items and basic reclassification of the income statement. Economic, asset-based, and financial indicators. The cash flow statement, cash flows, and the distinction between economic results and cash availability. 5. Organizational structures and people. Business functions and the distribution of tasks and responsibilities; main organizational structure models. The individual within the organization and the fundamentals of human resource management; vertical and horizontal coordination and integration. Examples also drawn from companies developing IT products and services.

## Learning objectives

This course provides the foundational knowledge and tools required to understand how a business operates, as well as the relationships between an organization, its resources, and its economic outcomes. It covers the essential elements of business feasibility, process-based organization, management and quality systems, business economics, financial statements, and human resource management—incorporating examples from companies that develop IT products and services. The course content is organized into five areas: business and corporate activities; project/initiative feasibility; management processes and systems; costs, financial statements, and performance indicators; and organizational structures and human resources. D1. Knowledge and understanding Students will be able to: • identify key business activities and functions, and understand their relationship to resources and outcomes; • grasp the elements of feasibility, the interplay between costs, revenues, and volume, and the concept of the break-even point; • understand the fundamentals of management systems, with a specific focus on quality, non-conformity, and performance evaluation; • comprehend the essential structure of financial statements, key performance indicators, and various organizational models. D2. Applying knowledge and understanding Students will be able to: • describe business processes, functions, tasks, and responsibilities; • perform basic break-even analyses and interpret key line items in financial statements; • analyze business performance indicators and identify quality-related aspects within a process, product, or service. D3. Making judgments Students will be able to: • evaluate the feasibility of an initiative based on available data; • interpret economic, asset-related, and financial information; • identify critical quality-related issues and compare the characteristics of different organizational models. D4. Communication skills Students will be able to: • describe a business, its processes, and its functions using appropriate professional terminology. • present the results of simple economic analyses and comment on financial statement information and aspects of quality management. D5. Learning skills Students will develop the ability to: • connect organization, management, resources, and economic outcomes; • apply the knowledge acquired to business examples and use it to independently explore the course topics further. The course is worth 6 CFU (48 hours) and takes place in the second semester.

## Assessment methods

Achievement of the learning outcomes will be assessed through an oral examination, which will evaluate students’ knowledge of the course topics and their ability to apply it to theoretical questions and numerical problems. The oral examination will consist of three questions. Each answer will be awarded a score from 0 to 10, based on the accuracy of the content, clarity of presentation and depth of understanding. The final grade, expressed out of 30, will be the sum of the scores obtained. The examination is passed with a minimum grade of 18/30. Students attending classes may take two midterm tests, consisting of open-ended and multiple-choice questions. For students who take both tests, the final grade will be the arithmetic mean of the two scores, expressed out of 30. The examination is passed with a minimum average of 18/30; in this case, the oral examination is optional. Students with valid documentation relating to specific learning disorders (SLD), disabilities or other educational needs may contact the lecturer and the degree programme or department disability support contact at the beginning of the course to discuss suitable assessment arrangements. These arrangements will take individual needs into account while preserving the learning outcomes of the course.
