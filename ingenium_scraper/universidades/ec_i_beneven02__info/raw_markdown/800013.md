## Contents

The curriculum for this 6-ECTS credit course encompasses the progressive coverage of the following topics: - Introduction to operating systems and hardware review; - Core concepts and structure of operating systems; - Processes and threads; - CPU scheduling; - Synchronization and inter-process communication; - Deadlock; - Memory management; - File system; - Shell scripting; - Input/output and mass storage; - Virtualization techniques (e.g., hypervisors and containers); - Security issues; - Advanced topics and practical exercises using the Linux system.

## Reference texts

The primary required textbook is: Modern Operating Systems, A. S. Tanenbaum, H. Bos, 5th edition, Pearson (2023). This text is supplemented by the lecture presentations. Practice materials, in-depth readings, and online resources will be recommended by the instructor and specified within the presentations. These materials are designed to support individual study and are equally beneficial for working and non-attending students.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide foundational theoretical and practical knowledge on the structure, architecture, and internal mechanisms of traditional operating systems. The objective is to enable students to understand how hardware is managed, virtualized, and protected, while developing practical systems programming skills through in-depth analysis and hands-on exercises utilizing the Linux operating system. The objective is to provide the student with: D1. Knowledge and Understanding Comprehensive understanding of the internal architecture, key components, and process lifecycle of an operating system. Acquisition of models for memory management, file systems, I/O management, security, and virtualization technologies. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding Ability to solve academic and practical concurrency problems (such as mutual exclusion and the producer-consumer problem). Proficiency in utilizing the Linux command-line interface (shell) to navigate the file system, manage permissions and users, and automate tasks through shell scripting. D3. Making Judgments Ability to evaluate and select the most appropriate scheduling or memory management strategies based on hardware and software constraints. Proficiency in critically analyzing the advantages and disadvantages of diverse virtualization technologies. D4. Communication Skills Ability to articulate concepts related to operating system operations with technical linguistic precision. Proficiency in documenting configuration choices or developed scripts during practical exercises in a clear and rigorous manner. D5. Learning Skills Development of the capacity to independently consult technical manuals and official documentation (such as Linux man pages). Acquisition of a methodological approach to operating system management, fostering autonomous learning of new technologies and software platforms.

## Assessment methods

The learning assessment is designed to verify the achievement of the course learning objectives and to evaluate the student’s ability to effectively and consciously apply the concepts and tools presented throughout the course. The final examination consists of a written assessment (containing short numerical problems or multiple-choice questions), a computer-based programming exam, and an oral examination. The computer-based programming exam assesses the ability to write high-level code, such as C or shell scripts, to solve problems covered during the course. The oral examination evaluates the student’s knowledge of the topics listed in the syllabus, their ability to synthesize and connect different course themes, and their mastery of technical terminology. The final grade is awarded on a 30-point scale. Grading criteria include: the accuracy and completeness of the written problem solutions, the correctness and completeness of the programming exam, the relevance and accuracy of the oral responses, the quality of reasoning, the appropriate use of technical language, and the ability to integrate the topics covered. Erasmus or international mobility students may arrange linguistic support with the instructor prior to the examination, provided it remains compatible with the course learning objectives. Students with valid documentation for learning disabilities, medical conditions, or other special educational needs are encouraged to contact both the instructor and the department’s disability service coordinator before the exam to arrange any necessary accommodations that account for their individual needs.
