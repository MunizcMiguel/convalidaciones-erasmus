## Learning objectives

The course aims at - providing the students a methodological basis, which, through the study of mechanics and electromagnetism, allows them to understand the steps of the scientific experimental method as well as the structure and the logical organization of scientific theories, contributing to develop their abilities in problem solving. - providing the knowledge of the main principles, theorems and experiments of classical mechanics and classical electromagnetism. - providing the instruments to understand the historical evolution of the interpretation models of reality, highlighting their importance, limits and incremental refinement. In line with the Dublin descriptors, the expected learning outcomes are as follows. D1. Knowledge and understanding By the end of the course, students will be able to: know and understand the principles, laws, and fundamental theorems of kinematics and dynamics of a point particle and systems of particles, electrostatics, magnetostatics, electromagnetic induction, Maxwell’s equations, and electromagnetic waves; understand the role of the experimental method and the logical structure of a physical theory; master the tools of vector and differential calculus necessary for their description. D2. Applying knowledge and understanding Students will be able to apply: the laws of mechanics and electromagnetism to the solution of problems and exercises; conservation principles and general theorems (for example Gauss’s law) to model concrete physical situations; techniques of vector and differential calculus to determine, for example, the electric and magnetic fields in significant configurations, selecting appropriate hypotheses and approximations. D3. Making judgements Students will be able to evaluate and interpret: the validity and limits of applicability of a physical model; the dimensional consistency and order of magnitude of obtained results; the distinction between hypotheses, thesis, approximations, and logical-mathematical steps in the proof of a theorem; the physical meaning of experimental results in the context of current physical theories. D4. Communication skills Students will be able to: communicate, in written and oral form and using rigorous scientific language and appropriate terminology, concepts, methods, and results of classical physics related to the topics included in the syllabus; present and discuss the proof of a theorem by distinguishing its hypotheses and theses; describe the aims, methods, and results of an experiment and its interpretation. D5. Learning skills Students will develop the ability to learn independently and to consult scientific texts critically; to address new problems and application contexts with a rigorous method; to continue autonomously the study of physics and of the disciplines that use it in later courses of the Degree Course in Computer Engineering.

## Assessment methods

Assessment of learning is designed consistently with the expected learning outcomes, expressed through the Dublin descriptors, and is based on declared evaluation criteria made known to students at the beginning of the course, so as to ensure transparency, fairness, and coherence between objectives, learning activities, and assessment. The exam consists of a written test, which is a prerequisite for the oral test. Written test. Aimed at verifying the conscious use of calculation techniques and tools and the correct application of solution procedures (descriptors D1 and D2), it consists of three problems — two on mechanics topics and one on electromagnetism — to be solved in one hour. The assessment is expressed according to the following criteria, communicated in advance to students: • A — three problems solved correctly: excellent - test passed with a bonus of 3 points that will be added to the vote of the oral test • B — two problems solved correctly: optimum - test passed without any bonus. • C — one problem solved correctly: test passed with a grade of 18/30, which contributes to the average together with the outcome of the oral test. • D — no problem solved correctly: test not passed. Passing the written test allows the student to take the oral test on any exam date throughout the academic year. Oral test. Aimed at evaluating the degree of acquisition of the syllabus contents (D1), the autonomy of judgement in distinguishing hypotheses, theses, approximations, and logical-mathematical steps in the proof of a theorem (D3), and communication skills in presenting, with rigorous scientific language, the aims, methods, and results of an experiment and their interpretation in the context of current physical theories (D4). The oral test also makes it possible to assess the student’s ability to connect topics independently and to recognize the limits of validity of the models studied (D3 and D5). Final grade. The grade is expressed out of thirty and coherently combines the results of the written and oral tests, according to the criteria stated above; honours are awarded in the presence of complete mastery of the contents, rigor in formalism, and full critical independence. Inclusivity and equal opportunities. Students with valid certifications for specific learning disorders (DSA), disabilities, or other educational needs are invited to contact the professor or the disability representative of the degree programme/department before the assessment, in order to agree on any assessment arrangements that, while respecting the course objectives, take account of individual learning needs.
