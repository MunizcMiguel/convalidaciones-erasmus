## Learning objectives

The course provides students with fundamental knowledge of the operating principles of telecommunication systems. In particular, it covers digital modulation, the characterization and modelling of noise in communication systems, signal reception techniques in the presence of noise and interference, and the basic principles of equalization for compensating for distortions introduced by the transmission channel. By the end of the course, students will have acquired the theoretical tools required to understand, model, and analyze the main telecommunication systems operating both at baseband and in passband. This knowledge provides a solid foundation for undertaking more advanced courses and for understanding the main applications in the field.

## Skills

By the end of the course, students will be able to: 1) model and analyze analog and digital communication systems operating both at baseband and in passband; 2) compare different communication systems in terms of spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and overall performance; 3) represent and assess the effects of distortions and impairments introduced by the transmission channel on the received signal; 4) select the most appropriate transmission technique according to system requirements, such as data transmission rate and bit error probability, and the available resources, such as bandwidth and transmit power; 5) apply the analysis methods learned to the evaluation of simple telecommunication systems in practical contexts.

## Co-requisites

This background supports the understanding of the mathematical models and circuit-related aspects used in the analysis of signals and communication systems, thereby facilitating the learning and application of the course contents.

## Assessment methods

The examination consists of an oral test covering the main topics of the course, including analog and digital modulation, analog-to-digital signal conversion, channel equalization techniques, and the effects of noise in communication systems.

During the oral examination, students will be assessed on their understanding of the theoretical foundations, their ability to apply the concepts learned, the clarity of their presentation, and the appropriate use of technical terminology. They may also be asked to solve and discuss exercises or problems. Where necessary, the exercises may be completed in written form as an integral part of the oral examination.

The examination is considered passed if the student demonstrates an adequate command of the fundamental concepts, the ability to reason and establish connections among the different topics covered in the course, as well as clarity and rigour in presenting the material.

## Prerequisites

A good knowledge of the fundamental principles of signal theory is required, with particular reference to the spectral analysis of deterministic signals and stochastic processes. Students are also expected to have a solid command of complex numbers, a good familiarity with phasor analysis, and knowledge of the basic mathematical tools used in the analysis of linear systems. This background provides the necessary foundation for understanding the theoretical and practical aspects of signal modulation, transmission, and reception in telecommunication systems.

## Contents

The course is the first core subject in the Telecommunications Engineering curriculum. Its main objective is to provide a systematic and rigorous introduction to the fundamental concepts of analog and digital communications, laying the theoretical foundations for the study of more advanced techniques and systems.

The program is organized into the following parts:

**1) Characterization of Noise in Communication Systems**

Power spectral density dissipated on a load, available power spectral density of a generator, available power gain of a two-port network, Nyquist theorem for noisy linear networks, equivalent noise temperature and noise figure, Friis formula, equivalent noise bandwidth, communication systems over radio and cable channels, repeaters for analog and digital signals.

**2) Analog-to-Digital Conversion of a Signal**

PCM technique: uniform and non-uniform quantization, compressor and expander, sample interpolation. Performance of a PCM system: quantization noise and effect of transmission channel errors. Performance curves of PCM systems.

**3) Theory of Digital Modulations**

PAM modulation: analytical signal model and its power spectral density, structure of modulator and demodulator, Nyquist condition for intersymbol interference (ISI) elimination, design of transmit and receive filters. Raised cosine pulses. QAM and PSK modulations: analytical signal model and its power spectral density, structure of modulator and demodulator, effect of phase error in carrier reconstruction. Decision regions and bit error probability calculation.

**4) Channel Equalization Techniques**

Wireless channel model: multipath propagation, coherence bandwidth and coherence time. Statistical models of indoor, typical urban, and hilly terrain channels. Linear equalizers in the time domain. Criteria for setting equalizer coefficients: Zero-Forcing and Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE). Performance of equalization techniques.
