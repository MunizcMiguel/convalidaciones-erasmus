## Teaching methods

- **Course Structure**

1. The course involves classroom lectures. Attendance is highly recommended.
2. A smaller portion of the lectures will be dedicated to explaining fundamental theoretical concepts. Most of the time, however, will be spent on performing and thoroughly analyzing exercises.

- **Organizing Individual Study**

For effective study, students should:

1. Familiarize yourself with basic notions: make sure you thoroughly understand all definitions by testing them with relevant examples and counter-examples.
2. Understand fundamental theorems: "Is all the specific terminology in the statement known? Are the theorem's hypotheses necessary? How does this theorem connect to other topics in the course?"
3. Test yourselves with exercises: solve the assigned homework exercises and analyze your progress week by week.

## Contents

**Vector spaces and linear applications**

- Fields and vector spaces. Vector subspaces.
- Linear dependence and linear independence. Generators. Bases and components of a vector with respect to a basis. Dimension of a vector space and a vector subspace. Span of a set of vectors.
- Sum and intersection of vector subspaces. Grassmann formula. Direct sum of subspaces and components of a vector with respect to a direct sum.
- Linear applications. Matrix associated with a linear application after choosing a basis in the domain and codomain.
- Operations between matrices: sum, product by a scalar, product. Transpose and inverse of a matrix. Computing the inverse matrix through the Gauss-Jordan algorithm and the matrix of cofactors.
- Change-of-basis matrix. Matrix similarity.
- Kernel and image of a linear application. Rank-nullity theorem. Relations between injectivity, surjectivity, and dimensions of domain and codomain for linear applications.
- Determinant of a matrix: definition, main properties, existence, uniqueness. Computation through the Gauss algorithm and Laplace expansion. Determinant of the transpose, the inverse, and the product of matrices.
- Rank of a matrix. Computation of the rank through minors and the Gauss algorithm.
- Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, eigenspaces. Algebraic and geometric multiplicity of an eigenvalue.
- Minimal polynomial, characteristic polynomial. Relations between coefficients of the characteristic polynomial, trace, determinant, and eigenvalues.
- Diagonalization over the field of real and complex numbers. Symmetric applications and symmetric matrices. Spectral theorem.

**Scalar product and quadratic forms**

- Canonical scalar product in R^n. Norm and distance.
- Orthogonal and orthonormal bases. Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process.
- Orthogonal matrices.
- Orthogonal of a subspace. Orthogonal projections onto subspaces.
- Quadratic forms and corresponding symmetric matrices. Definition and signature.
- Methods for determining the signature of a quadratic form: completing the square, sign of the eigenvalues, Sylvester's method (signs of minors), Descartes' method (sign of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial).

**Analytic geometry**

- Geometric vectors in the plane, in the space, and more generally in R^n.
- Analytic geometry in the plane. Cartesian and parametric representation of lines. Angles and distance.
- Analytic geometry in space. Cartesian and parametric representation of lines and planes. Angles and distance between lines and planes.
- Cartesian and parametric representation of affine subspaces of R^n.
- Affine transformations and isometries in R^n.

**Linear systems**

- Writing a linear system in terms of matrices and vectors. Interpretation in terms of linear combinations, Span, and linear applications.
- General structure of the set of solutions of a (homogeneous and non-homogeneous) linear system.
- Matrices in reduced row echelon form. Solution of a linear system through the Gauss algorithm.
- Solvability of a linear system and rank: Rouché-Capelli theorem.
- Cramer's method for linear systems.
