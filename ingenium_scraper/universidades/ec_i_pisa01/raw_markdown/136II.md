## Learning objectives

The student will develop knowledge of basic electronics, from semiconductor physics to well-known analog and digital circuits, with emphasis on their use. In particular, traditional electronic devices such as diodes, bipolar transistors and field-effect transistors will be addressed and their use in multiple circuit topologies will be analyzed. The student will learn fundamental concepts such as the bias point, small-signal equivalent circuit and frequency behavior of electronic circuits, greatly developing his or her circuit analysis skills. Through the study of reaction theory, the student will gain considerable theoretical knowledge that will enable him or her not only to analyze circuits seen in class, but also new ones. In addition, the student will learn about the figures of merit and tradeoffs present in the design of many types of circuits such as amplifiers, filters, oscillators, comparators and multi-vibrator circuits. Finally, the student will receive the fundamentals of Boolean algebra and elementary combinatorial and sequential blocks to build digital networks, along with an introduction to digital memories.

## Skills

The student will be able to analyze basic analog and digital circuits, recognizing their figures of merit and evaluating their performance critically. The student will also be able to size circuit components to achieve the required performance.

## Assessment methods

The examination consists of two tests: a written test lasting 3 hours, and an oral test approximately lasting 1 hour. Both tests cover the entire program taught in class. Specifically, the written consists of 4 exercises, in which both analysis and design of analog and digital circuits may be required. The oral test typically consists of 3 questions to assess theoretical knowledge on different topics, which may range from device physics to analog and digital circuits.

## Assessment criteria of knowledge

The student must demonstrate an understanding of the basic knowledge and be able to apply it while solving the exercises in the written test. During the oral test, the student must be able to describe in depth the circuits and concepts presented in class.

## Contents

The course is divided into three main parts: physics of semiconductor devices, analog electronics and digital electronics. The first part introduces the fundamental physical concepts on which traditional electronic devices such as diodes, bipolar transistors and field-effect transistors are based. Once their working principle is known, their use in electronics and their modeling in circuits is introduced.

In the second part, several fundamental analog electronic circuits are analyzed such as: diode-based circuits (such as peak detector, envelope detector...), single-transistor amplifier stages (BJT/MOSFET), linear circuits based on operational amplifier (inverting and non-inverting amplifier, integrator, differentiator, filters, sinusoidal oscillators... ) with emphasis on op-amp non-idealities (finite gain, finite input resistance, nonzero output resistance, offset and bias currents, dominant pole response), study of network feedback by means of generalized theorem, nonlinear circuits based on op-amp (comparator, multivibrator, triangle wave generator).

Finally, in the third part of the course, an introduction to digital electronics is made, starting with Boolean algebra, fundamental theorems and synthesis of logic functions. Elementary logic gates and their fundamental parameters are studied, and from these, complex logic gates and combinational circuits such as multiplexers, demultiplexers, decoders, encoders, and finally sequential circuits such as latch and flip-flops are constructed. Finally, the best-known digital memory architectures (ROM and RAM) and the popular 555 timer are introduced.

The lectures also include demonstration of the operation of some circuits by electrical simulation with LTSpice.
