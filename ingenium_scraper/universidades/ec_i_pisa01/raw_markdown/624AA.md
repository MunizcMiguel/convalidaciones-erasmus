## Learning objectives

By the end of the course, the student must be familiar with and able to use the tools of **multivariable analysis**.

Specifically, this includes:

- Multivariable differential calculus (including optimization problems)
- Multiple integrals
- Line and surface integrals

## Bibliography

M.Ghisi, M.Gobbino, Analisi Matematica II, Schede ed Esercizi, editrice Esculapio

The teacher will make his lecture notes available to the students.

## Skills

At the end of the course, the student should be able to solve exercises on the following topics:

- study of functions in several variables,
- solving optimization problems in several variables,
- calculating multiple integrals,
- calculating integrals on curves and surfaces,
- calculating the work of a vector field along a curve and the flux through a surface.

## Teaching methods

- **Course Structure**

1. The course involves classroom lectures. Attendance is highly recommended.
2. A smaller portion of the lectures will be dedicated to explaining fundamental theoretical concepts. Most of the time, however, will be spent on performing and thoroughly analyzing exercises.

- **Organizing Individual Study**

For effective study, students should:

1. Familiarize yourself with basic notions: make sure you thoroughly understand all definitions by testing them with relevant examples and counter-examples.
2. Understand fundamental theorems: "Is all the specific terminology in the statement known? Are the theorem's hypotheses necessary? How does this theorem connect to other topics in the course?"
3. Test yourselves with exercises: solve the assigned homework exercises and analyze your progress week by week.

## Teaching methods

During the course, the student should develop critical thinking towards the basic notion of differential and integral calculus in several variables and consequently be able to tackle and solve simple problems in an independent way.

## Prerequisites

- Basic mathematics, and in particular drawing subsets of the plane described through equations/inequalities, and solving systems of equations.
- Real analysis for one-variable functions (differential calculus, limits, integral calculus).
- Basic linear algebra (vectors, analytic geometry in the plane and in the space, matrices, quadratic forms).

## Contents

- Differential calculus for scalar functions of several variables: continuity, partial and directional derivatives, gradient, Hessian matrix, first- and second-order Taylor formulas.
- Identification of critical points of a function and constrained critical points (via Lagrange multipliers); study of critical points via the Hessian matrix, identification of maximum and minimum points.
- Partial derivatives and gradients of vector functions.
- Integrals of several variables: definition and calculation methods; change-of-variable formula; volume of solids and of solids of revolution, differentiation of integrals
- Curves in the plane and in space: parameterizations, tangent line and tangent unit vector, length, integral of a function along a curve, work of a vector field along a curve.
- Conservative fields and potentials.
- Surfaces in space: parameterizations, tangent plane and normal vector, area, area of surfaces of revolution, integral of a function on a surface, flux of a vector field through a surface.
- Gauss-Green theorem, divergence theorem, Stokes' theorem.
