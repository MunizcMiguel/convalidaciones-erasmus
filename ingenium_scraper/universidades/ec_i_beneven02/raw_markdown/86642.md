## Contents

The course is structured as follows: Smart Sensors (2.5 ECTS): general characteristics and applications (IoT, biomedical, etc.); multifunctional materials and macro/mesoscopic description of phenomena (magnetostriction, piezoelectricity, etc.); linear and nonlinear modeling. Energy Harvesting (2 ECTS): general characteristics and applications; devices for energy harvesting (photovoltaic panels, vibrational harvesters, etc.); modeling. Laboratory Sessions (1.5 ECTS): hands-on activities on smart sensors and energy harvesting devices.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to introduce innovative sensors based on so-called smart materials, which are capable of coupling different physical quantities (mechanical, electromagnetic, thermal, etc.) within their constitutive relations. These materials are also referred to as “multifunctional” and can be employed for actuation, sensing, and energy conversion purposes, combining efficiency and speed in precision applications or in harsh environments where conventional techniques are ineffective. Energy harvesting represents the future technique for powering wireless sensors. The course provides an introduction to both energy harvesting and smart materials (e.g., photovoltaic, magnetostrictive, piezoelectric, magnetothermal, etc.). The teaching approach follows a problem-solving methodology: methods and concepts are presented as solutions to practical application challenges.

The course is problem-oriented, introducing analysis and design techniques and methodologies through practical application examples.
