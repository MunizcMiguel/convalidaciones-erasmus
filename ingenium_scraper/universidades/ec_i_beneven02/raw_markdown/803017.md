## Contents

General properties of living organisms. Cellular organization of living organisms: Differences between prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Cellular cycles. The concept of genotype and phenotype. MACROMOLECULES -CARBOHYDRATES Definition and naming of carbohydrates. Structure of monosaccharides and their properties. Conformational isomerism. Derivatives of monosaccharides: Acids (aldonic, sugar and uronic acids), deoxy derivatives, amino derivatives. The glycosidic bond. Disaccharides: maltose, lactose and sucrose. Polysaccharides: structural and storage polysaccharides. The structure of starch, glycogen and cellulose. Glycoproteins and proteoglycans. -LIPIDS AND BIOLOGICAL MEMBRANES General biological properties of lipids and their classification. Triglycerides, phospholipids, sphingolipids, steroids. Properties of triglycerides and polar lipids. Structure and function of biological membranes: the fluid mosaic model. - NUCLEIC ACIDS Purine and pyrimidine bases, nucleosides and nucleotides. Structure of ribonucleotides and deoxyribonucleotides. Characteristics and properties of the phosphoester bond. Structure of DNA: the Watson and Crick model and other types of double helixes. Structure of eukaryotic chromosomes: histones, nucleosomes and higher levels of chromatin organization. Types of RNA and their structure: messenger RNA, ribosomal RNA and transfer RNA. EXPRESSION AND TRANSMISSION OF GENETIC INFORMATION DNA replication in prokaryotes and eukaryotes. RNA synthesis (transcription) in prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Regulation of transcription in prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Post-transcriptional modifications of RNA. Mechanisms of protein synthesis. Transfer RNA and its aminoacylation. Structure and function of ribosomes. Energy requirements for protein synthesis and control mechanisms for the accuracy of synthesis. - PROTEINS Amino acids: structure and chemical properties. The peptide bond and its properties. Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure of proteins. General properties and functions of proteins. Globular Proteins (hemoglobin, myoglobin e immunoglobulins) and fibrillar proteins ( collagen, keratin, elastin, fibroin, actin and myosin). Methods for the purification and analysis of proteins (colorimetric, chromatographic and electrophoretic methods). Immunological methods for the identification and quantification of proteins. Immunoblotting and ELISA and lateral flow assay. METABOLIC AND CATABOLIC REACTIONS, CARBOHYDRATE, LIPID AND PROTEIN METABOLISM - METABOLISM The concept of metabolism: catabolism and anabolism. The concept of a metabolic pathway. Thermodynamics of Metabolic Pathways. ATP and other high-energy hydrolysis compounds. Coupled reactions: the central role of ATP in energy metabolism. The hydrolytic and oxidative stages of catabolism. The energetic utilization of reduced coenzymes: NAD and FAD. Enzymes: definition, general properties and classification. Thermodynamic analysis of chemical reactions. Mechanisms of enzymatic catalysis. Models of enzyme kinetics and regulation: the Mentens and Michaelis model and the characteristics of competitive and non-competitive inhibition; the allosteric model and the characteristics of allosteric regulation. The biochemical concept of respiration. Anabolic strategies: carbon source and reducing power. General description of the most important metabolic pathways. Detailed description of three metabolic pathways: Glycolysis, Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation. Overview of the Catabolism of Lipid; Protein and nucleic acid. Anabolic pathways: gluconeogenesis and fatty acid synthesis

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide fundamental biochemical knowledge on the structure and function of biological macromolecules and explore the major metabolic pathways. This knowledge is essential for understanding biological processes and for designing materials and devices for biomedical applications at the nano, micro, and nanoscale.

## Assessment methods

The students' learning outcomes are evaluated by the teacher through an oral examination of biochemistry learning on the basis of the program carried out during the course. It will also allow for the assessment of the student's communication skills, proper use of language, and ability to organize the presentation of topics independently, as well as verify the student's ability to apply the acquired skills to more complex systems still related to the course content. The final grade will be expressed out of thirty.
