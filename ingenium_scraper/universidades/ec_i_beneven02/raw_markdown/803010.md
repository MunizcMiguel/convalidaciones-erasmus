## Contents

Sampling theory. Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT). Use of DFT for filtering. Sequence expansion and decimation. Interpolation. Fast DFT computation (FFT). Numerical simulation of analog systems. DFT-based spectral analysis. Use of windows. Resolution and sidelobes. The zeta transform. Finite impulse response (FIR) and infinite impulse response (IIR) filters design. Simulation and analysis of random sequences. Monte Carlo simulation. Correlated Gaussian variables. CDF estimation. Estimation of the autocorrelation function of a sequence. Non-parametric spectral analysis. Periodogram. Spectral resolution and dispersion. Matched filter and its properties. Lab Introduction to signal processing using Matlab. Processing of analog signals. Simulation and analysis of random variables. Estimation of CDF from iid sequences. Spectrum estimation. Applications to biomedical signals. The teaching activities are approximately distributed as follows Lectures: 65% Seminars: 5% Supervised exercise sessions: 30%

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide students with the fundamental knowledge and methodological tools required to understand and analyze digital signal processing systems and techniques. By the end of the course, students will be able to describe and apply the main methods for the deterministic analysis of one-dimensional signals and systems. Particular attention will be devoted to audio signals, biomedical signals, and data acquired from advanced sensing systems. The course will also introduce the main concepts of statistical signal processing, enabling students to understand the role of probabilistic and statistical models in the analysis of signals affected by noise. In the final part of the course, the acquired knowledge and skills will be applied to wireless and biomedical signal processing, with the aim of developing the ability to address real-world problems involving signal analysis and interpretation, to justify the methodological choices adopted, and to communicate the results of the analysis appropriately.

## Prerequisites

It is expected that the student is familiar with methods of signal theory and probability theory. At least a basic knowledge of programming methods is also required.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of two parts. The first part, which accounts for 70% of the final grade, focuses on the analytical and methodological aspects of the course, as well as on assessing the student ability to apply signal and system analysis methods. The second part, which accounts for 30% of the final grade, consists of a discussion of the homework assigned during the course and is aimed at assessing the student&#39;s ability to apply numerical signal processing techniques using a computer, with particular reference to MATLAB, as well as to interpret and critically discuss the results obtained and the methodological choices adopted. The overall assessment takes into account the student knowledge about the theoretical content, his ability to develop and justify analytical procedures, the use of numerical tools, and clarity of presentation in discussing the results.
