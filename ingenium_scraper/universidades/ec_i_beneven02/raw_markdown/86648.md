## Contents

Introduction to biomaterials and medical devices History and evolution, definition and classification criteria (material/organism interaction, application, chemical nature), design principles. Issues related to compatibility, reliability, and factors influencing the success of implants will also be addressed. Properties of biomaterials Analysis of biological properties (biocompatibility, toxicity), bulk properties (mechanical and viscoelastic), and surface properties, with an introduction to the main surface analysis techniques. Tissue–biomaterial interactions Study of the role of surface characteristics in the interaction with tissues and in biological response processes. Classes of materials for biomedical applications Metals, ceramics, and polymers: distinctive features and fields of application. Examples of medical devices Presentation of concrete case studies based on metallic, ceramic, and polymeric materials. Laboratory activities The laboratory sessions are aimed at the characterization of biomaterials.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the fundamental knowledge of biomaterials required to understand the relationship between the structure, properties, and performance of materials used in biomedical applications. The course also aims to develop students' ability to analyze the interactions between biomaterials and biological systems, understand the criteria for selecting and designing materials for clinical applications, and acquire the theoretical foundations necessary to evaluate the chemical, physical, mechanical, and biological properties of biomaterials in relation to their use in medical devices. Expected Learning Outcomes D1. Knowledge and Understanding knowledge of the main classes of biomaterials (metals, ceramics, polymers, and composite materials) and their structural characteristics; understanding of the relationship between the structure, properties, and performance of biomaterials; knowledge of the fundamental principles governing the interaction between biomaterials and the physiological environment, with particular emphasis on biocompatibility and biofunctionality; knowledge of the principal surface modification techniques and of the chemical, physical, mechanical, and biological methods used for biomaterial characterization; knowledge of the criteria for selecting biomaterials for major biomedical applications and the design of medical devices. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding ability to correlate the structure, properties, and performance of biomaterials with specific biomedical applications; ability to critically evaluate the interactions between biomaterials and biological systems; ability to identify the most suitable biomaterial for a given application by considering functional, biological, and technological requirements; ability to interpret the results obtained from the main biomaterial characterization techniques; ability to apply the acquired knowledge to solve simple problems related to the design and development of biomedical devices. D3. Making Judgements ability to critically analyze the properties of biomaterials and their behavior in biological environments; ability to evaluate the advantages, limitations, and potential of different classes of biomaterials for specific clinical applications; ability to identify appropriate criteria for the selection and use of biomaterials in medical devices. D4. Communication Skills ability to describe the characteristics, properties, and applications of biomaterials using appropriate scientific language and technical terminology; ability to clearly and rigorously present the fundamental principles underlying the design, characterization, and application of biomaterials. D5. Learning Skills ability to use the acquired knowledge as a foundation for further learning in biomedical engineering, advanced biomaterials, and regenerative medicine; ability to independently deepen the topics covered in the course through the use of university-level textbooks and the relevant scientific literature.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the achievement of the learning objectives will be conducted through an oral examination. The examination includes a PowerPoint presentation of approximately 15 minutes, which constitutes the main component of the assessment (50%). Students are required to select a class of materials or a specific material and present it by describing its chemical structure, morphology, and microscopic and/or nanoscopic organisation, together with its main physicochemical properties. Particular emphasis should be placed on properties that are relevant to applications and, where appropriate, on significant quantitative parameters. During the presentation, the structure–property relationship must be discussed, highlighting how the structural features of the material determine its functional properties. The presentation must also include examples of applications reported in the scientific literature, with appropriate citation of selected review articles. During the oral examination, follow-up questions will be asked to assess the student’s critical understanding of the selected topic. The discussion will also include questions related to the laboratory activities carried out during the course, focusing on the underlying principles, objectives, experimental techniques, and interpretation of results. Further questions may address the general topics of the course, with the aim of evaluating the student’s command of the theoretical concepts and their ability to establish connections between the different subjects covered. The final mark will be expressed on a thirty-point scale.
