## Contents

Course program Introduction to electronics Signals. Frequency spectrum of signals. Analog and Digital Signals. Amplifiers and their circuit models. Frequency response of amplifiers. Differential amplifier. Integrator and Derivator. Real operational amplifier. Diodes Ideal diode. Voltage-current characteristic: the forward bias region, the reverse bias region, the breakdown region. Models of the direct characteristic of the diode. Trimming and coupling circuits. MOS Field Effect Transistors (MOSFETs) Structure and physical functioning of the enrichment and depletion MOSFET. Polarization of a MOS amplifier circuit. Small signal operation and models. Fundamental configurations of MOSFET amplifiers with resistive and active load. Digital circuits Introduction to digital electronics. Fundamentals of digital electronics: logic inverter as a fundamental binary element. Elementary logic gates. Transfer characteristic of a logic inverter. Nominal logic levels. Noise margins. Times of propagation. Power dissipation. Propagation delay. Integrated circuit technologies Moore's law: integrated circuits integration scales. Silicon: main material in electronic and optical devices. Processes for manufacturing the P-N junction. Oxidation, Deposition (CVD, PVD - Electron beam), Lithography and photolithography. X-ray and electron beam lithography. Etching: Dry and wet etching. Drug. Implantation. MOS, nMOS and CMOS process. MOS transistor Structure of the MOS transistor. Threshold voltage and current-voltage characteristics. Capacity and tracing of the MOS device. NMOS logic gates NMOS inverter with resistive load. MOS devices as active loads. Inverters with NMOS active load. Transfer characteristics and logic levels. Noise margins and minimization of the inverter area. Circuit capacity, dynamic analysis and propagation times. Dissipated power. NMOS elementary logic gates. CMOS logic gates CMOS Inverter, Transfer Characteristics and Noise Margins. Dynamic behavior and propagation times. dissipated power. CMOS elementary logic gates. Fan-in and Fan-out of the CMOS ports. Output separator stages. Scaling of CMOS circuits. Advanced logic circuits Input / output interconnection circuits. A-O-I ports. Combinatorial circuits. NOR, NAND, XOR gate. Adding and subtracting circuits. Circuit comparators. Circuits encoders and decoders. Multiplexer to demultiplexer circuits. Sequential circuits. Bistable circuits. the bistable SR. Synchronized flip-flops. Flip-flop JK. Flip-flop Master-Slave. Flip-flop D-T. Programmable logic circuits Introduction. Programmable Logic Arrays (PLA), PAL Devices. Two-step logic. Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs). Programming techniques.

## Learning objectives

The educational targets of the module is to provide the student with the basics for the analysis of elementary electronic circuits, both analog and digital. To this aim, the characteristics of the basic electronic devices are introduced: diode, MOS transistor and bipolar transistor and their applications are studied in the logic circuits and in the amplifiers.

## Prerequisites

Prerequisites - Basic mathematical and physical knowledge. - Differential equations resolution and Fourier and Laplace transform applications. - Fundamental elements of electrical engineering knowledge. - Fundamentals of automatic knowledge (Bode diagrams, Feedback). Preparatory: Electrotechnical

## Teaching methods

Teaching Methods Lectures (48 hours), classroom tutorials (9 hours), and laboratory sessions (18 hours). These activities are designed to provide students with the theoretical knowledge and practical skills required for the design, analysis, sizing, assembly, and characterization of both analog and digital electronic circuits. Laboratory activities are carried out in small student groups. Classroom Tutorials The course includes three 3-hour classroom tutorial sessions focused on the analysis of amplifier circuits and rectifier circuits through guided problem-solving exercises. Laboratory Sessions The course also includes six laboratory sessions, for a total of 18 hours, held at the Multifunctional Laboratory of the University of Sannio. Laboratory activities are conducted in groups of three or four students. The laboratory topics are: Introduction to laboratory instrumentation and measurement techniques; Operational amplifier circuits in inverting and non-inverting configurations; Half-wave rectifier and smoothing filter; 4–6. Design, implementation, and testing of Arduino-based Internet of Things (IoT) platforms.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the objectives achievement set will take place through an oral interview, aimed at assessing the of topics knowledge degree, the ability to apply them, the critical spirit with which the student faces the analysis of electronic circuits and clarity of exposure. In evaluating the oral exam will take into account: a) the maturity reached on the course contents, b) the oral exposure quality, c) the critical spirit with which the topics are addressed, d) judgment autonomy, e) the mastery of scientific language. The oral examination may include in the discussion aspects related to the classroom exercises and to the projects that the student has carried out during the laboratory exercises that the student will present during the examination to the commission. In this case the oral test is based on the quality of the report and on the student's knowledge of the subject taught during the course and the critical spirit with which he will present the report: the final grade, expressed out of thirty, will be composed on the basis of the degree of achievement of previous requisites of the oral exam. The praise can be had in the case of an oral test that fully satisfies the evaluation criteria.
