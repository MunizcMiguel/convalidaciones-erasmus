## Contents

The theoretical part includes the study of statistic methods for the analysis of experimental data. Units and references. Analogue and digital electronic instrumentation (voltmeters, ammeters, multimeters, counters). Measurement methods (Wheatstone bridge, a.c. bridges). Hints on automatic measurement systems.

## Learning objectives

D1 - Knowledge and Understanding At the end of the course, students will have acquired knowledge of the theoretical principles of electronic measurements, methodologies for the processing of experimental data, and the fundamentals of measurement signal acquisition and processing. They will understand the operating principles of the main digital electronic instrumentation used in measurement laboratories, as well as the criteria for evaluating measurement uncertainty and data reliability. D2 - Applying Knowledge and Understanding Students will be able to use digital electronic instrumentation to acquire, process, and analyze measurement signals by applying appropriate experimental data processing methodologies. They will also be able to design and carry out basic measurement procedures, interpret the obtained results, and assess measurement quality through the analysis of the main sources of error and measurement uncertainty. D3 - Making Judgements Students will develop the ability to critically evaluate the reliability of experimental data, identify the main sources of error in measurement procedures, and select the most appropriate measurement techniques and instrumentation for a given application. They will be able to formulate technical judgments regarding the quality of experimental results and their consistency with theoretical models. D4 - Communication Skills Students will be able to document and present experimental activities in a clear and rigorous manner, using appropriate technical terminology. They will be capable of interpreting and discussing measurement results through technical reports, graphs, and tables, effectively communicating the adopted methodologies and the conclusions reached. D5 - Learning Skills Students will acquire the skills necessary to independently deepen their knowledge of measurement methodologies, signal processing techniques, and more advanced data acquisition tools, enabling them to adapt to the continuous evolution of technologies employed in electronic measurements and engineering practice.
