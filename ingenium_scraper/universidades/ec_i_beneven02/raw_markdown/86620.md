## Contents

1. Electronic Measurement Instrumentation for Biomedical Applications (Theory and Lab – 16 hours) Contents: - Measurement methods and characterization techniques for DAQ systems: - Measuring noise and distortion in biomedical DAQ. - Evaluating bandwidth and sampling frequency. - Calibration techniques to ensure accuracy (e.g., for ECG, EEG). - Transfer function: Experimental determination of the transfer function in an electronics lab (e.g., with a signal generator, oscilloscope, spectrum analyzer). - Theoretical modeling (e.g., frequency response of a sensor amplifier). - Applications: Characterization of DAQ systems for biomedical signals. - Biocompatibility and safety (ISO 13485, IEC 60601, MDR standards). - Medical applications: Monitoring, telemedicine, diagnostics. Objectives: - Model a biomedical measurement chain, including the DAQ. - Calculate and interpret figures of merit (e.g., SNR). - Characterize DAQ systems using experimental techniques. - Determine the transfer function in a laboratory setting. - Evaluate and model the measurement uncertainty of a biomedical measurement chain. 2. Design of Data Acquisition Systems (DAQ) for Medical Applications (Theory and Lab – 16 hours) Contents: Low-power DAQ architecture: - Design considerations for reduced power consumption: power management techniques (e.g., sleep mode, duty cycling). - Importance of low power supply noise: effects of noise on the accuracy of biomedical signals. - Power supply figures of merit: - Power Supply Rejection Ratio (PSSR): Definition, measurement, and impact on medical DAQ (e.g., reducing power supply interference). - Other parameters: ripple, voltage stability, energy efficiency. - Characterization techniques: measuring PSSR with a spectrum analyzer, power supply noise testing. Measurements for communication protocols in medical DAQ systems: - I2C: Low power, suitable for local sensors (e.g., temperature sensors). - SPI: High speed for multi-parameter DAQ (e.g., ECG, EEG). - Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE): Wireless connectivity for wearable devices. - LoRa: Long-range communication for remote monitoring in telemedicine. - Comparison: power consumption, latency, reliability. Measured data visualization interfaces: Designing dashboards (virtual instrumentation) for real-time monitoring. Tools: Python applications. Requirements: usability, real-time updates, compatibility with medical standards. Real-time monitoring and measurement systems: - Requirements: low latency, high precision, safety. - Examples: DAQ for multi-parameter monitoring, low-power wearable systems for telemedicine. - Techniques: data buffering, edge processing to reduce transmission. Medical applications: - Real-time monitoring. - Low-power wearable devices for real-time monitoring. - Remote systems. Objectives: - Design low-power, low-noise DAQ systems for biomedical signals. - Characterize the power supply with figures of merit like PSSR. - Develop virtual instrumentation interfaces for real-time data visualization and monitoring.

## Learning objectives

Apply measurement methods to characterize biomedical Data Acquisition Systems (DAQ), evaluating key performance metrics (e.g., SNR, PSSR, uncertainty). Perform calibration of DAQ systems and medical instruments against gold standard references, understanding the importance of these measurements for validating accuracy and regulatory compliance. Design and numerically model biomedical measurement circuits for medical applications. Apply Digital Signal Processing (DSP) techniques (e.g., FIR/IIR, adaptive filters) and machine learning to analyze calibrated biomedical signals. Develop practical skills in simulation, circuit design, and calibration of smart measurement systems. Analyze intelligent sensors and systems within the context of calibration and validation for medical applications.
