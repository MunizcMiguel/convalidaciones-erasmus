## Contents

Course Program: Introduction to Bioengineering. Definition of Bioengineering, situation in Italy and Europe, fields of application, and objectives. Models in physiology and medicine: Central and peripheral nervous system. Neurons and synapses. Membrane potential and action potential generation. Conduction velocity and its measurement. Sensory system: sensory transducers. Muscular system: mechanical model of the muscle, contraction, simple shock, tetanus, and active state. Musculoskeletal and postural system: feedback control, reflex arc, spindles, and Golgi tendon organ. Electrical nerve and muscle stimulation. Cardiac and vascular system: mechanical function of the heart, cardiac electrical activity, elements of hemodynamics. Biomedical instrumentation: Origin and collection of physiological signals. Issues related to signal transduction, preparation, and processing. Bioimaging instrumentation: overview of radiological equipment and optical and electron microscopy. Amplifiers for biomedical use. Electrocardiogram (ECG), electroencephalogram (EEG), electromyogram (EMG), pressure, flow, respiration, temperature. Equipment for monitoring key vital signs. Ultrasound and flow assessment devices. Pacemakers, defibrillators, and cardioverters. Hemodialysis and ventilators. Biomedical Signal and Data Processing: Introduction to biological signals. Morphological and bandwidth characteristics of the main biomedical signals. Issues about biomedical signal and data acquisition, conditioning, sampling, quantization, and storage. Digital signal processing in the time and frequency domains. Methods for analyzing correlated and uncorrelated signals. Analysis of electrocardiographic, pressure, flow, electroencephalographic, evoked potential, and electromyographic signals. Principles of electrical safety.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to introduce students to bioengineering, providing them with the basic knowledge necessary to understand biomedical systems and the fundamentals of biomedical instrumentation. In particular, the following will be introduced: the elements for understanding the main physiological systems, from an anatomical and functional point of view, and the origin of physiological signals, including through the use of models; the main systems for the collection and presentation of pathophysiological signals and for monitoring and functional assistance; techniques for processing biomedical data and signals; biomedical instrumentation.

## Prerequisites

- Basic knowledge of mathematics and physics. - Solving differential equations and applications of Fourier and Laplace transforms. - Knowledge of the fundamental elements of electrical engineering. - Knowledge of the fundamentals of automation.

## Teaching methods

Teaching methods: - Theoretical lessons, classroom exercises, and laboratory exercises. - Classroom exercises: Exercises on physiological signals, images, and biomedical signals. Exercises on biomedical instrumentation. - Laboratory exercises: Examples of biomedical signal processing.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the achievement of the set objectives will take place through an oral exam, aimed at evaluating the degree of knowledge of the topics, the ability to apply them, the critical spirit with which the student approaches the analysis of electronic circuits, and the clarity of presentation. The oral exam will be assessed taking into account: a) the level of maturity achieved in relation to the course content, b) the quality of the oral presentation, c) the critical spirit with which the topics are approached, d) the independence of judgment, e) the mastery of scientific language. The oral exam may include aspects related to classroom exercises and projects that the student has carried out during the laboratories, which the student will present to the commission during the exam. In this case, the oral exam grade is based on the quality of the presentation and the student's knowledge of the subject taught during the course, as well as the critical spirit with which they present the report: the final grade, expressed in thirtieths, will be based on the degree to which the above requirements of the oral exam are met. Honours may be awarded in the case of an oral exam that fully satisfies the assessment criteria.
