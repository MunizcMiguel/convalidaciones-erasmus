## Contents

1. Foundations and Maxwell's equations: - Vector calculus review: gradient, divergence, curl, and their physical meaning. - Maxwell's equations in time and frequency domains. Speed of light in vacuum. - 1D wave equation and its solution. Maxwell's equations in matter, constitutive relations, and physical realizability conditions. - Fundamental theorems of electromagnetics: Poynting theorem, reciprocity, equivalence, and duality. - Propagation in plasmas: cold collisionless plasma model and ionospheric propagation; wave packet, phase and group velocities. 2. Transmission line theory: - Distributed-parameter circuit model and TEM modes. Transmission line equations in time and frequency domains. - Reflection coefficient, standing wave ratio, and impedance transformation formulas. - Short-circuited and open-circuited lines. - Impedance matching techniques: matched lines, quarter-wave transformer, and single-stub tuning. 3. Radiation and antenna fundamentals: - Retarded electrodynamic potentials and electromagnetic radiation in free space. - Fundamental parameters of transmitting antennas: effective length, radiation vector, directivity, gain, and effective area. - Main lobe characterization for directive antennas and basic principles of receiving antennas.

## Learning objectives

The course provides the fundamental concepts of electromagnetics, guided and free-space wave propagation, and the analysis of basic devices (transmission lines and antennas). Upon completing the course, students will acquire: - D1 (Knowledge and understanding): Maxwell&#39;s equations, electromagnetic wave propagation, transmission line theory, and basic antenna parameters. - D2 (Applying knowledge): solving simple propagation problems and evaluating operational parameters of transmission lines (impedance matching). - D3 (Making judgements): ability to choose between lumped-circuit and distributed-parameter models for simple high-frequency problems. - D4 (Communication skills): clear presentation of concepts using appropriate scientific and technical terminology. - D5 (Learning skills): ability to consult introductory technical textbooks and attend advanced courses in electronic and biomedical engineering.

## Prerequisites

Passing the basic Mathematics and Physics exams scheduled in the first two years of the degree course is strongly recommended. Fundamental knowledge of basic circuit theory and Electrical Engineering is also important.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of classroom lectures supported by slides and multimedia material, numerical exercises and computer-based simulations, as well as project activities and case studies (individual and group work). Supporting learning materials are made available on the University e-learning platform.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of an oral examination aimed at assessing the comprehension of course contents through questions of gradual difficulty. The final grade, expressed on a 30-point scale (minimum passing grade 18/30), takes into account: - level of understanding of theoretical and methodological concepts; - clarity of presentation and proper use of technical terminology; - ability to address simple sensing problems. Honors (cum laude) are awarded in case of outstanding preparation and comprehensive mastery of the subject.
