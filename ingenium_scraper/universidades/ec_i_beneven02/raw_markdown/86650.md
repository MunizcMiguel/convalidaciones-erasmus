## Contents

The course program covers the following topics: 1. Structure of materials and defects Crystal structure of solids; atomic bonding and its influence on properties; point and linear defects; diffusion in solids (Fick's laws); microstructure-property relationships. Exercises: theoretical density, vacancy concentration, diffusion applications. 2. Mechanical properties and service behavior Stress-strain curves; elastic modulus, yielding, fracture; brittle and ductile fracture; fatigue and creep introduction; corrosion and degradation. Exercises: stress/strain calculations, yield verification, fatigue analysis. 3. Electrical and dielectric properties Conduction in metals, band theory introduction; semiconductor materials; temperature effects on resistivity; dielectrics, polarization, electric breakdown. Exercises: resistivity, thermal variation, capacitance calculations. 4. Thermal properties and thermo-mechanical coupling Thermal conductivity, thermal expansion, thermal stresses; thermal cycling reliability. Exercises: heat flux and expansion mismatch. 5. Polymers, biomaterials, and material selection Polymer structure and properties; glass transition; degradation; biomaterials (metals, polymers, ceramics); selection criteria and performance indices. Final exercise: material selection case study.

## Learning objectives

The course provides the fundamentals to understand the relationships between structure, properties, and performance under operating conditions of materials used in electronic and biomedical engineering. Particular attention is devoted to mechanical, electrical, and thermal properties, the role of defects and microstructure, service behavior, and material selection criteria. D1. Knowledge and understanding: At the end of the course, students will be able to describe the relationship between microscopic structure and macroscopic properties and evaluate the impact of defects and microstructure on conductivity, strength, and diffusion. D2. Applying knowledge and understanding: Students will be able to calculate fundamental quantities such as density, resistivity, strain, and thermal flux, and perform preliminary material selection for electronic or biomedical applications. D3. Making judgements: Students will be able to evaluate material behavior under service conditions (mechanical load, temperature, biological environment) and select appropriate materials based on design constraints. D4. Communication skills: Students will be able to communicate relationships between microstructure, properties, and performance using proper technical terminology. D5. Learning skills: Students will develop learning skills to independently study advanced materials and methodically approach specialized courses.

## Assessment methods

The final examination consists of a written test (~2 hours) comprising: • Numerical problems; • Open-ended theoretical questions; • A short case study on material selection. An optional oral examination may be integrated upon request by the student or established by the professor if necessary. Assessment criteria: (i) relevance and correctness of answers; (ii) numerical calculation accuracy; (iii) technical terminology; (iv) ability to connect structure, properties, and performance. Grading is expressed on a 30-point scale. Students with certified disabilities or learning disorders should contact the professor prior to the examination to arrange appropriate assessment conditions.
