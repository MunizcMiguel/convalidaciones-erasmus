## Contents

GENERAL CHEMISTRY MATTER composition : pure substances and mixtures; elements and compounds. The atoms. Elementary particles. The atomic nucleus, isotopes. ATOM quantization of energy. The electronic structure of atoms . The periodic system and the periodic properties of the elements (atomic radius, effective nuclear charge, ionization energy, affinity for the electron, electronegativity). CHEMICAL BONDS: The ionic bond: crystalline structure and properties of ionic compounds, ionic radii, lattice energy. The covalent bond: molecules. Molecular geometry and properties of molecular substances. Covalent solids. The metallic bond: Metals (conductors) and semiconductors. Intermolecular forces, hydrogen bonds. STATES OF AGGREGATION OF MATTER. Description of the gaseous state using the equation of state for perfect gases. Notes on real gases. Description of liquid and solid states. ELEMENTS OF CHEMICAL THERMODYNAMICS. First law of thermodynamics: energy, heat and work. Enthalpy and heat of reaction Entropy and second law of thermodynamics. Gibbs free energy. Equilibrium constant. Prediction of spontaneity of chemical reactions. ELEMENTS OF KINETICS. Reaction rate and its dependence on temperature and concentrations of reactants. The collision model in the description of chemical reactions. The catalysts. CHEMICAL REACTIONS IN AQUEOUS SOLUTION. Definition of acids and bases. Acid-base equilibria in aqueous solution. The ionic product of water and the pH value. Oxidation-reduction reactions. ORGANIC CHEMISTRY CARBON AND ITS COMPOUNDS. Classes of substances and functional groups, nomenclature, physical properties, isomerism, stereoisomerism and chirality. REACTIVITY OF THE FOLLOWING CLASSES OF COMPOUNDS: Alkanes, alkenes, alkyne halogen derivatives, aromatic compounds, alcohols, thiols, ethers, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and derivatives, amines

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the fundamental principles of General and Organic Chemistry required to understand the relationship between the microscopic structure of matter and its chemical and physical properties. It also aims to develop students&amp;amp;#39; ability to interpret the main chemical phenomena, understand the structure and properties of compounds of biological interest, and acquire the theoretical foundations necessary to describe the reactivity of the major classes of organic compounds. Expected Learning Outcomes D1. Knowledge and Understanding  Knowledge of the fundamental principles of the atomic and molecular structure of matter.  Understanding of the combining ratios between elements and the nature of the chemical bond.  Knowledge of the molecular geometry and properties of the main inorganic and organic compounds.  Knowledge of the principles of chemical equilibrium, acid-base equilibria, and oxidation-reduction reactions.  Knowledge of the structure, nomenclature, physical properties, and reactivity of the major classes of organic compounds. D2. Applying Knowledge and Understanding  Ability to balance chemical reactions, including acid-base and oxidation-reduction reactions.  Ability to predict the spontaneity of chemical reactions based on thermodynamic principles.  Ability to solve quantitative problems involving chemical equilibria and pH calculations.  Ability to identify functional groups and correlate the structure of organic compounds with their properties and reactivity.  Ability to apply the acquired knowledge to the interpretation of simple chemical systems of biological interest. D3. Making Judgements  Ability to critically analyze chemical phenomena and interpret basic experimental data.  Ability to identify the most appropriate theoretical model for describing and solving simple problems in General Chemistry. D4. Communication Skills  Ability to describe chemical phenomena, properties, and transformations using appropriate scientific language and terminology.  Ability to clearly and logically present the fundamental concepts of General and Organic Chemistry. D5. Learning Skills  Ability to use the acquired knowledge as a foundation for the study of biological, biochemical, and biotechnological disciplines.  Ability to independently deepen the topics covered in the course through the use of university textbooks and introductory scientific literature.

## Teaching methods

Lectures (70%) and exercises (30%). Exercises are done especially in the classroom, solving application problems on the topics covered. Students with valid certifications of disability and/or special educational needs (BES) are invited to contact the course instructor and/or the Department/Course of Study Disability and BES Coordinator at the beginning of the course to discuss appropriate teaching arrangements, in accordance with the intended learning outcomes of the course.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of the achievement of the intended learning outcomes will be carried out through a written examination, which is a prerequisite for admission to the oral examination. The written examination includes, in varying proportions, numerical problems on chemical reactions and exercises on the nomenclature and reactivity of chemical compounds. These problems are intended to assess the student&#39;s ability to apply the concepts acquired through the solution of numerical exercises. A minimum mark of 18/30 in the written examination is required for admission to the oral examination. The oral examination will consist of: - discussion of the written examination; - questions on the theoretical aspects of the topics covered in the course and reported in the detailed course syllabus, with the aim of assessing the student&#39;s level of knowledge and understanding of the theoretical and methodological contents of the course. The oral examination will also assess the student&#39;s ability to communicate using appropriate scientific terminology and to present topics in an autonomous and well-organized manner, as well as the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to more complex systems that are nevertheless related to the course syllabus. The final examination grade will be expressed on a 30-point scale and will take into account the overall performance in both the written and oral examinations, although it will not be calculated as the arithmetic mean of the two marks. The assessment will be based on the following criteria:(i) relevance of the answers to the questions asked; (ii) quality of the contents; (iii) appropriate use of technical terminology and overall communication skills; (iv) ability to establish connections with other topics covered in the course; (v) ability to provide appropriate examples.
