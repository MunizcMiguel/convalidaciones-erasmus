## Learning objectives

The main objective of the course is to provide, after having introduced the operating principle and the analytical and circuit description of the main semiconductor electronic devices, the fundamental tools for the design and the analysis of electronic circuits for the analogical treatment of the information, with particular reference to signal amplification. The main knowledge acquired will be: - operating principles of semiconductor devices; - analysis of analog electronic circuits; - frequency response of amplifiers in the small signals approximation; - processing of analog signals using operational amplifiers; - basic elements on the power supply circuits.

## Prerequisites

The course requires knowledge of the contents of the " BASIC ELECTRIC CIRCUIT THEORY " course and in particular of the following topics: - circuit components and their constitutive equations; - Kirchhoff's laws and their application; - Thevenin and Norton theorems. Furthermore, it is necessary to have acquired and assimilated the knowledge of the following topics: - transfer functions and harmonic response; - signals in the time and frequency domain.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of: - a series of theoretical lessons on all course topics; - numerical exercises focused on the design and analysis of fundamental electronic circuits; - laboratory exercises carried out by the students and concerning the application of the topics studied during the course.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test and, if admitted, an oral examination. The written test assesses the student's ability to solve simple problems of analysis and synthesis of electronic circuits using the methods learned in the course. The oral examination includes three theoretical questions on all the topics covered in the course and aims to evaluate the student's level of knowledge and his/her ability to connect and compare the different topics and issues covered in the theoretical classes. The score obtained in the written test serves as the basis for determining the final grade, which may increase and/or decrease depending on the score obtained in the oral examination.
