## Contents

The course program covers the following topics: Mechanics of particles and Systems: Scientific Method: Model and Experiment. Dimensional Relations and Scaling Laws. Vectors. Material Points. Law of Motion. Trajectory. Velocity. Acceleration. Motions. Galileo&#39;s Principle of Relativity. Inertial and Non-Inertial Reference Systems. Force and Momentum. Newton&#39;s Laws. Angular Momentum. Momentum of a Force. Apparent Forces. Gravitation. Work. Power. Kinetic Energy. Conservative and Non-Conservative Forces. Potential Energy. Dynamics of Systems. Statics and Dynamics of Rigid Bodies. Elements of Fluid Mechanics: Fluid Statics. Pressure. Stevin&#39;s Law. Archimedes&#39; Law. Elements of Fluid Dynamics. Bernoulli&#39;s Relation. Real Fluids. Thermodynamics: Temperature, Thermal Equilibrium, Thermometers. Ideal and Real Gases. Work. Heat. Principles of Thermodynamics. Elementary microscopic interpretation of thermal phenomena. Electromagnetism Electrostatics in a vacuum. Coulomb force and electric field. Gauss&#39;s theorem. Electric potential and Poisson&#39;s equation. Electric dipole. Capacitance and capacitors. Polarization in a dielectric. Electric current. Drude model for conductors. Resistance and Ohm&#39;s law. Generators and electromotive force. Kirchhoff&#39;s laws. Magnetostatics in a vacuum. Lorentz force. Laplace&#39;s formulas. Ampere&#39;s law of circulation. Motion of charges subjected to the Lorentz force. Hall effect. Vector potential and gauge transformations. Magnetism in matter. Faraday-Neumann law. Self-induction. Displacement current. Maxwell&#39;s equations. Electromagnetic waves.

## Learning objectives

The overall objective is to provide students with the fundamental concepts of classical mechanics, thermodynamics, and electromagnetism, starting with methodological and phenomenological aspects and including the historical evolution of models for interpreting reality, highlighting their importance, limitations, and progressive refinement. At the end of the course, students will be able to: - understand the most common phenomena in nature, describe them analytically, and anticipate their evolution; - apply the studied physical laws to solve simple problems; - interpret the most common phenomena in nature and trace them back to the underlying physical principles; - state and demonstrate the principles of classical mechanics and the basic notions of fluids, gravitation, thermodynamics and electromagnetism; - develop the ability to understand and describe a new phenomenon in nature, drawing inspiration from the phases of the experimental method and the logical organization of scientific theories.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of algebraic calculus, trigonometry, plane and solid geometry, and the graphical representation of mathematical functions is needed.

## Teaching methods

Teaching is delivered through lectures, consisting of 80% explanations and demonstrations of course topics and 20% practical exercises consisting of exercises or simple experiments. The Handy platform is used to provide the detailed syllabus, sample exams, and to submit simple questions for students to answer during the course. Students with certified SLD, disabilities, or special educational needs should contact the professor and department coordinator early in the course to agree on suitable teaching accommodations that align with learning objectives.

## Assessment methods

The final exam consists in a written and and oral test. The written test consists in three exercises (two on mechanics and one on electromagnetism) to be solved in one hour and a half. The result of the test can vary from A to C, depending on how many problems are correctly solved (A: 3 exercises correctly solved, test is passed - B: 2 exercises correctly solved, test is passed - C: test failed). The oral test aims at evaluating the degree of understanding the contents of the course. The maximum final grade that can be achieved is influenced by the written test grade: if A, the maximum final grade is 30, if B the maximum final grade is 24. It is possible to spit the final exam in two parts: a first one on the Mechanics and a second one on the Electromagnetism. Students with certified SLD, disabilities, or special educational needs should contact the professor and department coordinator early in the course to arrange suitable exam accommodations.
