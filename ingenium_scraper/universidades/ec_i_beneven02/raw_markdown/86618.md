## Contents

1. Introduction to bioelectronics: Introduction to bioelectronics and bioelectronic devices for diagnostics (sensors) and therapy (actuators). Biosensors for measuring chemical, physical, and physiological parameters of clinical interest. Overview of biological recognition elements, labeled and label-free approaches. Transduction mechanisms and principles, with particular attention to those of an electrochemical and optical nature. Properties of biosensors (sensitivity, selectivity, reproducibility, stability, linearity, etc.) and evaluation of performance and technical limitations. Commercial, regulatory, ethical, and practical obstacles in the development of bioelectronic devices in the medical and healthcare fields. 2. Laboratory exercises on biosensors. At least four laboratory exercises will be carried out aimed at the analysis and characterization of different types of biosensors based on electrochemical and optical transduction mechanisms. The exercises, to be carried out in groups of students, are aimed at designing and conducting experiments aimed at measuring, analyzing, and interpreting parameters of real clinical interest. At the end of each exercise, students will prepare a technical report in the form of slides or posters, which will be discussed with their classmates and during the exam. 3. Study of a scientific paper: The teacher assigns students a scientific paper from an international journal related to a topic covered in the course, which they critically examine and discuss with their classmates through a presentation or poster.

## Reference texts

## Learning objectives

The main objective of the course is to teach students, mainly through laboratory experience, the fundamental concepts of bioelectronics, understood as a discipline that applies the principles and technologies of electronics to biology, medicine, and life sciences. The course aims to provide students with the tools necessary for the analysis, design, and characterization of bioelectronic devices, and more specifically biosensors, for application in the medical and healthcare sectors.

## Prerequisites

The course consists of classroom lectures (15 hours) and laboratory exercises (57 hours).

## Assessment methods

Oral exam aimed at assessing the degree of knowledge of the topics covered and the exercises conducted during the course. During the oral exam, the student will be asked to comment on technical reports (slides showing the main results achieved) prepared as a group at the end of each lab exercise. To pass the exam, students must demonstrate knowledge of the principles of transduction and the configurations of the various bioelectronic devices analyzed, understanding their performance and technical limitations.
