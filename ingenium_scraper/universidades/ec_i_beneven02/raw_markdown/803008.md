## Contents

The detailed teaching program includes the following topics, divided into Part 1 (deterministic signals) and Part 2 (random signals): Part 1 Introduction to the study of signals: random and deterministic, analogical, sampled, quantized, numerical. Elementary signals with continuous time and discrete time. Definition and properties of the Dirac impulse. Periodic signals. Energy and power of a signal. Operations on signals (translation, reflection, change of scale, scalar product, orthogonality, convolution, correlation). Correlation functions. Basic properties Systems and analysis in the time domain. System properties. Impulsive response of an LTI system. ARMA systems. I-O links for LTI systems. Systems and analysis in the frequency domain. Fourier transform. Properties. LTI systems and frequency response. Definition of Band. Energy Spectral Density (ESD) and Power Spectral Density (PSD) for deterministic signals. Mutual PSD and ESD. Parseval and Wiener-Khintchine theorems for deterministic signals. Sampling. Shannon&amp;#39;s theorem of ideal sampling. Complete and synthetic statistical characterization. Part 2 Random processes/signals and random variables. Definitions. Probability: Axiomatic, classical and frequentistic definition. Independent and correlated events. Conditional probability. Basic laws of conditional probability. Random variables: Discrete random variables, pmf. Continuous random variables, pdf. CDF. Joint and conditional distributions. Independent random variables. Transformations of random variables, Position and scale transformation. Gaussian variables. Expectation and moments: Definition and meaning of statistical expectation. Fundamental theorem for the expectation. Moments of a random variable, mean square value and variance. Conditional expectation. Joint moments: correlation and covariance. Incorrelation and independence. Random vectors: mean, correlation matrix and covariance matrix. Linear combination of random variables: mean and variance. Properties of Gaussian vectors, linear combination of Gaussian variables. Course closing with some insights of most common random processes/signals (Poisson, Bernoulli, Binomial, Cauchy, Uniform, etc.).

## Learning objectives

The overall goal is to provide the student with: - basic knowledge of probabilities and random variables, and of the analysis of signals and systems in the time and frequency domain. The student will be able to process deterministic signals in the case of discrete and continuous time, in the time and frequency domain. The student will be introduced to the analysis of random signals with a specific focus on random variables. Class participation will be encouraged to develop the student's communication skills, as well as their critical judgment in evaluating classmates and collectively discussing topics.

## Assessment methods

Verification is done with a written test. You can divide the test into two parts. Intracourse, non-mandatory tests are also provided for both the first and second parts, in order to facilitate and encourage continuous, lesson-by-lesson study. There is no oral test. Grade assignment methods (intermediate and final): each test consists of a certain number of exercises with assigned scores. The test is considered passed if after the correction of the test, the total score is bigger than or reaches 18/30. The proposed final grade is based on the arithmetic mean of the scores achieved in the individual tests.
