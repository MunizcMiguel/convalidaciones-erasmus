## Contents

The course syllabus covers the following topics: The concepts of system and model. The Laplace transform: exponential, step, ramp, impulse. Theorems on the Laplace transform: real derivation, final value, initial value, real integration. Anti-Laplace transform: decomposition with residues. Transfer function and impulse response function. Linearization of nonlinear dynamical systems. Block diagrams. Models of electrical, mechanical, electromechanical, thermal and hydraulic systems. Time response to impulse, step, ramp, sinusoid. First order, second order and higher order systems. Transient and steady state response. Frequency response. Bode diagrams. Nonlinear systems and linearization: electric circuit with constant-power load, predator-prey model, SIR model. Discrete-time systems. Simulation of dynamical systems with Matlab and Simulink.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide a unitary method of classification and study of cause-effect relationships in dynamic systems through the use of mathematical models. The models of interest describe the input-output or input-state-output behavior of continuous-time and dicrete-time dynamical systems through the use of differential equations and transfer functions. Models of electrical, mechanical, electronic, thermal ans hydraulic systems are proposed. The methods for the analysis of linear and time-invariant dynamical systems in the time domain and in the frequency domain are deepened. The ability to represent nonlinear systems through linearization is acquired by analyzing case studies: an electrical circuit with a constant-power load, a predator-prey model, and an SIR model. The exercises allow you to acquire skills on the analysis of dynamic systems through computer simulation. D1. Knowledge and understanding By the end of the course, the student will be able to: recognize the dynamic nature of a system based on its input-output behavior. D2. Applying knowledge and understanding The student will be able to apply: time-domain and frequency-domain models to electrical and mechanical systems. D3. Making judgments The student will be able to evaluate/interpret: the importance of the dynamic analysis of a physical phenomenon. D4. Communication skills The student will be able to communicate: the behavioral characteristics of a dynamic system. D5. Learning skills The student will develop the ability to: explore the dynamic behavior of systems in greater depth, including through numerical analysis.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of the basic concepts and techniques of mathematical analysis: study and plotting of functions, derivation and integration. Basic elements of physics: Newton equation of translational and rotational dynamics, Kirchhoff laws. To sit the final proficiency exam, it is necessary to have previously passed the following exams: Mathematics and Physics.

## Assessment methods

The final examination consists of a written test and an oral interview designed to assess knowledge of the topics covered in the syllabus and, more broadly, the achievement of the course learning objectives. Assessment criteria include: (i) the relevance of answers to the questions asked; (ii) the quality of the content; (iii) the appropriate use of technical terminology and overall expressive ability; (iv) the ability to connect the topics covered in the syllabus; (v) the ability to provide examples. Student performance contributes to the final grade based on the following percentage weightings: written test = 20% of the overall assessment modeling = 20% of the overall assessment numerical simulation = 20% of the overall assessment time-domain analysis = 20% of the overall assessment frequency-domain analysis = 20% of the overall assessment Students with valid certification regarding specific learning disorders (DSA), disabilities, or other educational needs are encouraged to contact the lecturer or the designated disability representative for the degree program/department prior to the assessment. This is to agree upon any assessment arrangements that—while respecting the course objectives—take individual learning needs into account.
