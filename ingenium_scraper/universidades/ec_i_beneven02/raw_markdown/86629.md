## Integrated course (common part) / Partition: 8077-1 / Partition: 8077-2

### Contents

Introduction (course outline) (MOD 1) RF Transceiver architecture - Power Transfer Problem - RLC Networks - Series/Parallel Transformation - Impedance matching networks - Passive components on integrated circuits - Transmission lines (basic concepts) - Review on MOSFET amplifiers - MOSFET at HF - Bandwidth estimation techniques (OCTC method) - Noise in passive circuits - MOSFET noise - Low noise amplifier - Mixers - Oscillators - Phase locked loop - Frequency Synthesizers (basics concepts) - Power Amplifiers (class A, B, C, AB) LTSPICE Laboratory Exercise: The design of passive filters and multistage MOSFET amplifier. (MOD 2) Transmit/receive antennas and their characteristic parameters. Link budget. Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) systems: basic operation, applications in LF, HF, and UHF bands, tag and reader types. Overview of key IoT technologies: Bluetooth, WiFi, ZigBee, Z-Wave, UWB. Wireless power transfer and energy harvesting. Radar: operating principles, Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave mode. On-chip radar architectures for automotive applications. Antenna measurement and characterization. MATLAB antenna simulations. Measurement of millimeter-wave radar-on-chip (120 GHz) for automotive applications, using MATLAB.

### Learning objectives

The main goal of this course is to train students to analyze and design electronic circuits at high frequency and antennas, with an emphasis on integrated circuits for wireless communication transceiver and radar.

### Prerequisites

Prerequisites: Analog Electronics, Electrical Measurements, and Electromagnetic Fields. Knowledge of concepts of Digital Electronics and Dynamic Systems is recommended.

### Teaching methods

Classroom lectures. (MOD 1) There will be 4 Lab Exercises concerning numerical simulations of RF electronic circuits with the aid of a PC. (MOD 2) Measurements are planned for 120 GHz radar (silicon radar) in an automotive environment and comparison with MATLAB-based numerical codes. Antennas such as dipoles and Yagi-Uda antennas will also be measured in the laboratory and compared with antenna toolbox codes.

### Assessment methods

Oral exam on the course topics including tests on lab exercises. To pass the exam, the student is required to demonstrate that he/she has acquired the fundamental concepts on the analysis and design of the main blocks of a RF transceiver system, and the related circuit components, in particular, on integrated circuits for wireless transceiver and radar systems. Students may take the exam for both modules in a single session or in two separate sessions. The final grade will be calculated as the average of the grades earned on the individual modules.
