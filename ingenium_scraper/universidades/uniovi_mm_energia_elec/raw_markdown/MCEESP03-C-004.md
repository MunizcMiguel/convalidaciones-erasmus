## Contextualización

**The Master’s degree:**

The main goal of the Master’s Degree in “Electrical Energy Conversion and Power Systems” (EECPS Master) is the training of qualified staff in areas related to electrical energy management, emphasizing in power systems for renewable energies. The Master presents a double approach: scientific and professional. In the scientific thread, training focuses on the design of two main applications: Electrical Power Systems and Electrical and Hybrid Traction Systems. On the other hand, in the professional thread, training is focused on the management of electrical energy. Thus, the subjects of this thread have been designed attending to two main issues, such as the management of energy in large consumers and the generation and transmission of electrical energy in a liberalized market. Three main lines have been considered as keystones in the Master:

- Electrical Power Systems
- Electrical and Hybrid Vehicles
- Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energies

**The first semester:**

The first semester is intended to provide a uniform level of knowledge among students with different basic training. This equalization term offers a set of optional courses designed to promote homogenization among students' knowledge. The teaching committee will study every application form independently, selecting 27 ECTS credits for every student among the optional courses. The additional 3 ECTS are dedicated to a compulsory introductory subject, called “Introduction to power systems, renewable energies, electrical traction, and energy efficiency”.

**The subject:**

This subject deals with the efficient real-time implementation of control systems that have been covered in "Control of Electromechanical Systems". The basis of the digital control theory is provided in the first part of the course. A discussion is then established for the selection of an appropriate hardware platform for a given problem. This will be the nexus between this subject and the one tough earlier in the semester, "Microcontrollers". The basic concepts of digital filters (FIR/IIR) are introduced and then, frequency-domain-based filters are developed. At the end of the subject, a topic about applications is given so the students can apply the previously acquired knowledge.

## Contenidos

1. Introduction to digital control systems.
2. Discrete-time models of continuous-time controllers.
3. Introduction to discrete-time controller design.
4. PID controllers: digital implementation and saturation of the controller ouput.
5. Digital implementation of cascaded control.
6. Implementation issues: sensors, filter and actuator effects.
7. Introduction to real-time digital signal processing.
8. Digital filters:
   1. Introduction to digital filters: FIR/IIR filters.
   2. IIR design by discretization of continuous-time filters.
   3. FIR filter design.
   4. Filter selection criteria.
9. Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT/FFT)
10. C language implementation of digital control systems.

## Evaluación del aprendizaje

**Ordinary Call**

**Assessable Elements**

The ordinary assessment consists of the following evaluable elements:

- **Final Written Exam (EX): 40%** A written exam conducted at the end of the teaching period. The exam may include:
  - Multiple‑choice questions
  - Short open‑ended questions
  - Computer‑oriented problem‑solving **A minimum score of 4/10 is required to pass this component.**
- **Final Assignment — 40% (25% documentation + 15% presentation)** The assignment consists of:
  - A written report (approx. 8 pages) representing **25%** of the final grade.
  - A **15‑minute presentation** representing **15%** of the final grade. The project may address topics from **any of the subjects included in this guide**. **A minimum score of 4/10 is required to pass this component.**
- **Practical Cases (PC): 10%** Completion of **three practical cases** on the control of electromechanical systems. Simulations will preferably use **MATLAB**, although other tools are permitted if effective. No minimum score is required unless otherwise stated.
- **Attendance and Active Participation (AT): up to 10%** Attendance alone does **not** guarantee points. Active participation is assessed primarily through questions, comments, and interaction related to the three practical cases. Passive attendance will generally **not** be considered.

**Grading**

If all minimum requirements are met, the final grade for the ordinary call is calculated as:

Final Grade = (0.40 × EX) + (0.40 × Assignment) + (0.10 × PC) + (0.10 × AT)

Where:

Assignment = (0.25 × Documentation) + (0.15 × Presentation)

If any of the minimum scores required in EX or Assignment are **not** attained, the final grade for the call will be:

min( Final Grade , 4.0 )

**Considerations**

If the teaching staff has any doubts regarding the authorship of submitted work or suspects the use of unauthorised assistance (e.g. external help), the student may be required to complete an **individual oral defence** of the work.  
Additional considerations may be introduced by the teaching staff when necessary.

**Extraordinary Call**

The extraordinary call follows **the same structure and weighting** as the ordinary call.

All components (EX, Assignment, Practical Cases, Participation) contribute with the **same percentages** and the **same minimum requirements**, and the final grade is computed using the same formula.

**Differentiated Assessment**

Differentiated assessment will be provided in accordance with University regulations.  
Each case will be examined individually, and a suitable assessment plan will be proposed considering the student’s circumstances, provided it remains aligned with the learning outcomes of the subject.
