## Contextualización

The Master’s course:

The main goal of the Master’s Degree in “Electrical Energy Conversion and Power Systems” (EECPS Master) is the training of qualified staff in areas related to electrical energy management, emphasizing in power systems for renewable energies. The Master presents a double approach: scientific and professional. In the scientific thread, training focuses on the design of two main applications: Electrical Power Systems and Electrical and Hybrid Traction Systems. On the other hand, in the professional thread, training is focused on the management of electrical energy. Thus, the subjects of this thread have been designed attending to two main issues, such as the management of energy in large consumers and the generation and transmission of electrical energy in a liberalized market. Three main lines have been considered as keystones in the Master:

- Electrical Power Systems
- Electrical and Hybrid Vehicles
- Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energies

The third semester:

The third term has been designed according to two possible tracks: professional and research. The first one is focused on the acquisition of the required competences for the management of electric energy, with a special emphasis on energy efficiency and renewable energies. The second track approaches the technology development and the industrial design established in the specific competences of the master lines:

"Power systems" and "Electric / Hybrid vehicle". This subject belongs to this latter track.

The subject:

This subject covers the simulation of complex vehicular systems, including Full Electric Vehicles and Hybrid Vehicles. It covers the steady state and transient response of the full system, the closed loop operation of the elements of the system, the power balance between different generators and loads, the bidirectional power flow, and so on.

The subject is aimed to complement the knowledge and skills acquired in the rest of the subjects that are simultaneous to this one within the scientific-technical research track, mainly "Power Systems in hybrid (HEVs) and electric (EV) vehicles” and “Energy storing and recovering in power systems and HEV and EV”.

Teaching activities may be carried out in collaboration with universities of the INGENIUM alliance, such as lectures, seminars and/or joint assignments or projects. Such collaboration may entail specific adjustments to the methodology and/or assessment systems.

## Contenidos

Course Contents:

**Module 1:** Introduction

- Overview. HEV/EV needs and objectives for power conversion.
- An overview on available simulation platforms and their interfaces.

**Module 2:** Battery charging systems:

- Dynamic modelling and simulation of traction batteries.
- Simulation of power stages for inductive charging: resonant converters

**Module 3: Simulation of EV/HEV** **Drivetrains**:

- Simulation of DC/DC bidirectional power converters.
- Simulation of DC/AC bidirectional power converters.
- Simulation of mechanical subsystems: internal combustion engine (ICE) and vehicle dynamics.
- Multi-domain simulations (electrical/mechanical).
- Simulation of power sharing interfaces for HEV.

## Evaluación del aprendizaje

Students will have to develop two different individual simulation projects to carry out.

Two written reports will be uploaded at a given date, along with the necessary files for proper simulation of the work. As an option, the students will have a feedback (first revision) with an initial mark. If the student carries out the mandatory/suggested changes given in the first evaluation, and uploads a final version within a given later date, the mark will be revised.

The students might be requested to make a presentation of the project showing the simulation files, the main plots and graphs, conclusions, etc., using, if considered convenient, a PowerPoint presentation. After the presentation, the student should answer any eventual question by the teachers.

***Late computer projects or reports will not generally be accepted.***

The students will have a written/oral exam about the course contents.

The reports and project including active participation during the classes will account for 40%

The presentation will account for 20%

The exam will account for 40%

For the case of students in part-time dedication scheme, a specific differentiated assessment procedure ("evaluación diferenciada") is put in place. Students under this scheme will not have a mark on the "active participation during the classes" share on the reports and projects qualification, and the total grade will be calculated proportional to the remaining percentages. The oral tests for these students will be scheduled on a specific agreed date, and online oral presentation will be available. Also, the rest of the tests might be moved to a specific date if not attendance to the original date is justified.

**Ordinary assessment**

**Assessment elements**

The ordinary assessment consists of the following elements:

**Projects and reports (PR) (40%):**  
Students will develop two individual simulation projects during the semester. Written reports must be submitted on the specified dates, including all necessary simulation files. Feedback may be provided after an initial submission, allowing students to improve and resubmit their work. These projects assess the practical application of course contents. A minimum mark of 5 out of 10 is required. Active participation during classes, including involvement in discussions, activities, and continuous engagement, will be considered in this element.

**Oral presentation (OP) (20%):**  
Students may be required to present their projects orally, including simulation results, main findings, and conclusions. The presentation may be supported by visual materials (e.g., slides). A minimum mark of 5 out of 10 is required.

**Exams (EX) (40%):**  
Written and/or oral exams will be conducted to assess the theoretical and practical knowledge acquired throughout the course. A minimum mark of 5 out of 10 is required.

**Grading**

If all minimum requirements are met, the final grade is calculated as:

Final grade=0.40⋅PR+0.20⋅OP+0.40⋅EX

In case the minimum mark is not achieved in any of the mandatory elements (PR, OP, EX), the final grade will be the minimum between 4.0 and the result of the above formula.

**Considerations**

In case of doubts regarding the authorship of any assessment activity or the use of unauthorized resources (e.g., AI tools), the student may be required to carry out an oral defense.

Late submissions will not be accepted unless duly justified.

Assessment will be continuous and differentiated, taking into account the different learning outcomes and the individual characteristics of students. When necessary, adaptations will be implemented without altering the core learning objectives. In cases where students cannot attend regularly due to justified circumstances (e.g., employment), alternative assessment measures may be applied, such as adapted deadlines or alternative formats, while maintaining the same evaluation criteria.

As an exception, the “Participation and continuous assessment (PA)” component will be adapted in such cases and will be evaluated through participation in individual tutorials and interaction via official communication channels.

**Extraordinary assessment**

As in the ordinary, the extraordinary assessment consists of a final exam (EX) covering all course contents, a project (PR) and an oral presentation (OP).

The final grade will be calculated as in section "Grading".

**Considerations**

In case of doubts regarding the authorship of any assessment activity or the use of unauthorized resources (e.g., AI tools), the student may be required to carry out an oral defense. ***Late computer projects or reports will not generally be accepted.***
