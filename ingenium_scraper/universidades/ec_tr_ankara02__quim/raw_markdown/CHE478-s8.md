### Objectives of the Course

Simulating the behavior of a chemical or physical process with computer support and controlling the processes accompanied by these simulations.

### Course Content

Simulation of the behavior of dynamic systems using computer assistance. Examples for reaction engineering, thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, heat transfer, mass transfer, and separation processes. Examination of Laplace and time-domain dynamics. Application examples for feedback controllers and different control systems.

## Course Learning Outcomes: Upon the successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

| No | Learning Outcomes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding the development of mathematical models describing chemical process dynamics. |
| 2 | Understanding the application of mathematical methods to engineering problems using chemical engineering examples. |
| 3 | Understanding analytical and computer simulation techniques for ordinary differential equations. |
| 4 | Understanding the dynamic behavior of linear 1st and 2nd order systems. |
| 5 | Understanding the dynamics of process control and controlled processes. |
| 6 | Understanding the differences between steady-state and unsteady-state behavior. |
| 7 | Understanding the role of mathematical models used in chemical engineering in simulation. |
| 8 | Recognizing the effects of linear system parameters on system responses, including system stability. |
| 9 | Understanding the fundamentals of using Simulink for simulating dynamic systems. |
