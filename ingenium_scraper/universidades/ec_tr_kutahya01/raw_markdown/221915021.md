### Objectives of the Course

The aim is to provide the ability to analyze the kinematic and dynamic characteristics of mechanisms. In this context, the goal is to teach methods for analyzing the path, speed, and acceleration of machine systems, modeling the static and dynamic forces acting on systems, mass balancing and flywheel calculations, and vibration control, enabling their application in solving complex engineering problems.

### Course Content

The course covers the analysis of degrees of freedom, position, velocity, and acceleration of mechanisms; static and dynamic force interactions in systems; mass balancing principles; and flywheel calculations for energy management. Furthermore, analytical solutions to dynamic stresses on shafts and the mechanical vibration behavior of single and multi-degree-of-freedom systems form the core content of the course.

### Course Methods and Techniques

The course is conducted through lectures, question-and-answer, problem-solving, and applied examples. Kinematic and dynamic analysis of mechanisms, force analysis, balancing, vibration, and critical speed topics are covered using analytical, graphical, and matrix methods. Newton-Euler and Lagrange equations are utilized. Laboratory experiments, case studies, project-based learning, group work, and presentations are employed; computer-aided analysis/simulation tools are used when necessary.
