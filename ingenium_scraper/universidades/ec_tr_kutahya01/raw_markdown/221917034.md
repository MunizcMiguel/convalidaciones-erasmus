### Objectives of the Course

To help students comprehend the fundamental principles and theory of automatic control systems.

To provide the ability to model, analyze, and design control systems.

To develop the capability to apply this knowledge to real-world problems, including industrial applications.

### Course Content

Basic Concepts: Open/closed-loop systems, history and importance of control systems.

System Modeling: Block diagrams, signal flow graphs, Laplace transforms, and transfer functions.

Time and Frequency Domain Analysis: Time response, stability (Routh-Hurwitz), root locus, Bode and Nyquist plots.

Controller Design: Principles and advantages of P, PI, PD, PID controllers.

Advanced Topics: State-space analysis, linearization of non-linear systems.

Application Areas: Hydraulic/pneumatic systems and industrial control applications.

### Course Methods and Techniques

Theoretical Lecture: In-class presentation of fundamental concepts and equations.

Problem Solving: Solving analytical problems and practical examples related to weekly topics.

Visual Tools: Use of graphical analysis methods such as block diagrams, root locus, and Bode plots.

Case Studies: Discussion of real-world application examples from industry.

Q&A and Review: Interactive review sessions for general evaluation and exam preparation at the end of the semester.
