### Objectives of the Course

The aim of this course is to provide mechanical engineering students with fundamental knowledge about air pollution and control techniques. The course covers topics starting from the formation of air pollution, pollutants, sources, pollutant dispersion, measurement methods, pollution control principles, air quality management, and relevant legislation.

### Course Content

Learning about air pollution, control techniques, and legislation.

### Course Methods and Techniques

The course is conducted through lectures, question-and-answer, problem-solving, and discussion. Sources, characteristics, dispersion, and effects of air pollutants, along with control technologies (cyclones, electrostatic precipitators, bag filters, scrubbers, adsorption, catalytic converters, etc.) are covered through case studies and computational examples. Applications include emission calculations, dispersion modeling, and control device design/selection.

## Course Learning Outcomes: Upon the successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

| No | Learning Outcomes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Explain the types, sources, formation mechanisms, and atmospheric behavior of air pollutants. |
| 2 | Interpret the effects of air pollution on human health, ecosystems, materials, and climate, along with relevant legislation and standards. |
| 3 | Determine emission rates and pollutant concentrations using mass balances and basic calculation methods. |
| 4 | Analyze factors affecting the atmospheric dispersion of air pollutants and apply simple dispersion models. |
| 5 | Explain the operating principles, design parameters, and selection criteria of control technologies for particulate and gaseous pollutants. |
| 6 | Select, size, and evaluate the performance of an appropriate control technology/system for a given source in terms of efficiency, cost, and environmental impacts. |
