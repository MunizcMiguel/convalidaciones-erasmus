### Objectives of the Course

The aim of this course is to equip mechanical engineering students with the engineering principles of renewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass, and hydrogen), thermodynamic cycles used in energy conversion, fluid-mechanical systems, and the design fundamentals of associated machinery. It aims to enable students to develop sustainable energy solutions, conduct energy efficiency analyses, perform technical and economic comparisons of different sources, and integrate these systems into existing engineering infrastructure and power grids.

### Course Content

The course covers the concept of energy, sustainability, the global energy outlook, and the environmental and economic dimensions of renewable energy. Topics include solar energy (thermal collectors, photovoltaic panels, concentrated solar power plants, and thermal storage), wind energy (aerodynamic principles, horizontal/vertical axis turbines, blade profile design, site selection and wind measurement), hydraulic energy (Pelton, Francis, and Kaplan turbines, pumped-storage hydroelectric plants), geothermal energy (cycle types, heat pumps, and direct use), biomass energy (combustion, gasification, pyrolysis, and biofuel production processes), and hydrogen energy.

### Course Methods and Techniques

The course will be conducted through theoretical lectures complemented by applied and interactive methods specific to the mechanical engineering discipline. Topics will be enriched with visual materials, animations, and real power plant data.
