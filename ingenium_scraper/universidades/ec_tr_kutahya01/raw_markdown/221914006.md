### Objectives of the Course

The aim is to teach students kinetics and kinematics of particle (and system of particles) together with underlying principles of dynamics. The student should gain practical approach to dynamics problems at the end of the course.

### Course Content

- Basic Concepts

- Kinematics of Particle

- Normal and Tangential Coordinates

- Relative Motion

- Kinetics of Particle

- Work and Energy

- Impulse and Momentum

- Conservation of Momentum

### Course Methods and Techniques

The course is conducted through lectures, question-and-answer, problem-solving, and applied examples. Particle and rigid-body dynamics are covered using Newton’s laws, work-energy, and impulse-momentum methods. Free-body diagrams, analytical and numerical solutions are used.

## Course Learning Outcomes: Upon the successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

| No | Learning Outcomes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Explain and apply the relationships among position, velocity, and acceleration in particle kinematics using Cartesian, normal-tangential, and polar coordinate systems. |
| 2 | Formulate and solve equations of motion for particles using Newton’s laws and free-body diagrams. |
| 3 | Apply work-energy and impulse-momentum methods to particle motion, and interpret conservation laws of energy and momentum. |
| 4 | Analyze translation, rotation, and general planar motion in rigid-body kinematics, including angular velocity, angular acceleration, and relative motion concepts. |
| 5 | Formulate mass moment of inertia, force-moment equations, and planar equations of motion in rigid-body dynamics. |
| 6 | Theoretically model and analyze the dynamic behavior of mechanical systems, interpret the results, and apply them to engineering problems. |
