### Objectives of the Course

With this course; the fundamentals of the energy sector, how structural and strategic changes in the energy sector affect industries and investment decisions will be investigated, the unique dynamics and financialization of energy products in international capital markets will be explained, and the impact of sustainable energy on financial decision-making will be discussed.

### Course Content

Acquiring knowledge about energy efficiency and energy economics, and being familiar with the legislation

### Course Methods and Techniques

The course is conducted through lectures, question-and-answer, discussion, case studies, and project-based learning. Energy efficiency policies, legislation, standards, and international frameworks are examined. Students work on sectoral, building, or industrial cases using techniques such as energy auditing/benchmarking, SWOT analysis, scenario analysis, cost-benefit analysis, roadmapping, and action plan development. Group work, presentations, reporting, and feedback techniques are employed.

## Course Learning Outcomes: Upon the successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

| No | Learning Outcomes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Explain the fundamental concepts, policies, legislation, standards, and international frameworks related to energy efficiency. |
| 2 | Analyze energy use in an organization, sector, building, or process; interpret energy audit and benchmarking data to determine efficiency potential. |
| 3 | Apply SWOT, scenario, cost-benefit, and prioritization analyses to develop energy efficiency strategies. |
| 4 | Propose energy efficiency strategies by considering economic, environmental, sustainability, health-safety, and legal requirements. |
| 5 | Prepare an implementable action plan/roadmap including targets, responsibilities, timeline, and performance indicators. |
| 6 | Report, present, and effectively communicate energy efficiency strategy and action plan studies individually and in teams with stakeholders. |
