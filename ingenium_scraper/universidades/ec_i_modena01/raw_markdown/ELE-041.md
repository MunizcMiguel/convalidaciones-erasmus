## Contents

The concept of wave and propagation (1 ECTS) The wave as a disturbance capable of propagating and performing work at a distance. Co-propagating and counter-propagating waves. Superposition principle. Damped and undamped sinusoidal waves, standing waves. Plane and spherical waves. Transmission lines (2 ECTS) Circuit representation of transmission lines. Time-domain transmission line equations. Descriptive parameters in the time domain. Bounce diagrams. Frequency-domain transmission line equations. Descriptive parameters in the frequency domain. Smith chart. Power and impedance matching. Quarter-wave transformers. Scattering parameters. Maxwell’s equations (1 ECTS) Review of vector analysis. Electromagnetic properties of materials. Maxwell’s equations in static and time-varying fields. Free-space propagation (2 ECTS) One-dimensional plane waves. Propagation in good dielectrics and good conductors. Poynting vector in time and frequency domains. Active and reactive power. Reflection at a discontinuity. Antennas (3 ECTS) The antenna as a transformer between guided and free waves. Elementary or Hertzian dipole. Near and far field of an antenna. Antenna characteristics. Aperture antennas, horn antennas, and parabolic antennas. Antenna arrays. Radio link design and Friis formula. Radar equation. Power transmission in the near field. Introduction to electromagnetic compatibility issues. Waveguides (1 ECTS) The concept of waveguide and waveguide modes. Cutoff frequency and single-mode conditions. Rectangular metallic waveguides and optical fibers. Laboratory activities (2 ECTS) Use of an electromagnetic simulator to study transmission lines, free-space propagation, and antennas. Use of network analyzers to measure characteristic parameters of transmission lines and antennas.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide basic technical knowledge and skills in electromagnetic propagation, both guided and in free space, as well as in antennas. Specifically, it aims to develop the ability to analyze and design, also using electromagnetic simulators and basic instrumentation, transmission lines, metallic waveguides, optical fibers, basic antennas, and radio links.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of electromagnetism provided by an introductory physics course and of DC and AC circuit theory.

## Teaching methods

The teaching methods include: 1) Lectures; 2) Exercises solved by the instructor in class; 3) Exercises completed independently by students (homework); 4) Periodic self-assessment by students; 5) Laboratory activities in groups of 2–3 students. The course is organized into weekly modules. The first hours are dedicated to introducing the topic, including in-class demonstrations, theoretical framing, development of mathematical models, and their phenomenological interpretation. This is followed by exercise sessions and laboratory activities. Weekly homework includes multiple-choice quizzes on exercises similar to those done in class and theoretical questions related to the week's topic. Homework is completed independently via the Moodle platform and must be submitted within the assigned week. At the end of each of the three main topics—Transmission Lines; Maxwell’s Equations and Free-Space Propagation; Antennas and Waveguides—students complete a self-assessment activity via Moodle, answering questions and solving numerical exercises through multiple-choice quizzes. Each lab group also submits a report on the activities and results obtained.

## Other information

Upon completion of the course, participation in the described teaching methods, and successful assessment, the student will be able to: 1) Knowledge and Understanding skills: Understand physical phenomena, basic mathematical models, and key descriptive parameters of propagation (lines, waveguides, free-space) and radiation (antennas). Comprehend and use information from textbooks or technical reports on transmission lines, waveguides, free-space propagation, and antennas. 2) Applying Knowledge and Understanding Understand technical descriptions of simple systems involving transmission lines, waveguides, free-space propagation, and antennas. Identify and apply appropriate mathematical models, basic methodologies, and descriptive parameters for analyzing, describing, or designing electromagnetic systems. Use an electromagnetic simulator to analyze and design simple systems involving transmission lines and antennas. 3) Judgment Skills Critically evaluate the concepts and analysis tools presented during the course. Assess the results of mathematical models and analysis methods, considering the approximations made. Analyze and critically evaluate results from electromagnetic simulators and network analyzer measurements, considering non-idealities. 4) Communication Skills Present knowledge clearly and logically, using appropriate technical language. Effectively communicate results from simulations or laboratory measurements.

## Assessment methods

Assessment is based on a written exam lasting approximately 75 minutes, consisting of either two exercises and one open-ended theoretical question or three exercises covering the three main topics. The final grade is determined by the written exam score, increased by a variable percentage based on homework, self-assessments, and lab reports. Homework can increase the exam score by 0% to 5%, depending on the number of correct answers. The three self-assessments can increase the score by 0% to 10%, based on the score obtained in each. The three lab reports can contribute an additional 0% to 5%, depending on the number of positively evaluated reports. A total score (written exam plus bonuses) equal to or greater than 33/30 will result in honors being awarded.
