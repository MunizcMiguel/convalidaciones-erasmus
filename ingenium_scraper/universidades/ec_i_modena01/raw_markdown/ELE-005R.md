## Learning objectives

Knowledge and capacity of application of principles of basic physics, especially classical mechanics (kinematics and dynamics of particles and rigid body) and electromagnetism (electric force, electric field, magnetic induction field, electric currents, Maxwell's equations in integral form) in vacuum.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of calculus as learnt during the first year of engineering studies: complex numbers, limits, derivatives, integrals, study of functions. Knowledge of plane geometry. Calculation of areas and volumes of simple shapes. Trigonometry.

## Teaching methods

Lectures also assisted with audiovisual tools, analysis of case studies. Attendance at lectures is not mandatory but highly recommended. Teaching is in Italian.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: Through lectures students learn the basic principles of physics, with particular emphasis on classical mechanics and electromagnetism in vacuum. Applying knowledge and understanding: through exercises and numerical problems, the student is able to apply the acquired knowledge for predicting physical phenomena inherent in classical physics, mechanics and electromagnetism in vacuum. Making judgments: Through exercises and self study the student is able to understand, critically discuss and present the results obtained and the approximations made. Communication skills: The preparation for the written exam allows the student to develop the ability to expose risults correctly, effectively and concisely, to express the concepts learned with appropriate language and to hold a discussion about the topics covered. Learning skills: the activities described allow the students to acquire the methodological tools to continue their studies and to be able to arrange their own upgrade.

## Assessment methods

Written Examination composed of a calculation part (2 problems) and a part of theory (5 open questions). A maximum of 15 points is assigned for each problem and a maximum of 6 points for each open-response of theory. The test is passed if at least 18 points are obtained in both parts. The final vote is obtained as the average between the two parts. Scores are assigned on the basis of the formal correctness and completeness of the response (80% of the evaluation) and of the technical language and clarity of expression (20% of the evaluation) It will not be possible in any way to consult notes or texts during the test. Exams take place according to the academic calendar; students wishing to take the exam must register for each scheduled session via the ESSE3 system. Test results are typically released within 15 days and communicated via ESSE3.
