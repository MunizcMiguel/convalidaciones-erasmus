## Contents

Fundamentals of measurement science: basis of statistics, statistical analysis of the results of repeated tests and evaluation of the measurement uncertainty, uncertainty propagation (2 CFU) Electrical measurements and related methods, devices, and calculations to measure DC and AC voltages, current, and resistance. (1 CFU) Analysis of signals in the time domain: oscilloscope. Operating principle, time base, channel y, channel x (1 CFU) Analysis of signals in the frequency domain: spectrum analyzer. Operating principle, application, performance (1 CFU) A/D converters, digital multimeter, PC-based acquisition systems and signal processing Counter/frequency meter (1 CFU) LABORATORY: Experiences aimed at measuring basic electrical quantities (3 CFU)

## Learning objectives

Aim of the Misure Elettroniche (6CFU) course is to give a basic preparation concerning the measurement methods and electronic instrumentation. The Lab (3 CFU, optional for the students of INGEGNERIA INFORMATICA (D.M.270/04)) adds knowledge on Labview and concretely experimenting the theory aspects.

## Prerequisites

Basic behavior of physics and electronics

## Teaching methods

All classes throughout the academic year will be held in person. A variety of exercises will be worked through during practical sessions, while laboratory activities are designed to provide students with opportunities to consolidate and apply their theoretical knowledge. In preparation for the examination, the material covered and explanations provided during lectures should be complemented by the study of the recommended textbooks.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding skills: Through lectures and in-class open discussions, the student understands the methodology to analyze measuring systems, he/she learns how to understand technical papers and how to read the data sheet of the most popular electronic instruments. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding: By means of the in-class open discussions and the preparation process to the examination, the student learns how to apply his/her knowledge to understand the operating principle of the most popular electronic instruments; Communication skills: Through preparation process to the examination, the student develops the ability to express concepts learnt during the course with the appropriate language Learning skills: The described activities will allow the student to learn the theoretical and methodological tools to independently carry on his/her own updates.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of two in-person tests: the first is mandatory while the second is optional. The mandatory test is written, lasts two hours, and consists of: (i) two questions mainly focus on the ability to solve practical problems; these questions require numerical steps. These two questions are assigned a score of 11 points each. (ii) five multiple-choice closed questions. These questions have five answer options each, only one of which is correct. The correct answer is awarded 2 points, the answer not given 0 and the wrong answer -1. It is not allowed to consult textbooks or notes during the written test while a pocket calculator is required. After the written test, each student is assigned a score on a 0-30 scale. If the score is at least 18/30, the student can ask to access the second part of the exam. The optional part of the exam is oral and consists of further questions and problems concerning theoretical and practical problems. After this test, the student is assigned a second score between -2/30 and +2/30. For the verification of the skills acquired, a mandatory laboratory activity is required. At the end of each laboratory activity, a report is required awarded assigning a grade. The overall score is calculated as the algebraic sum of the two scores. The final grade is calculated as 0.8 * (overall exam score) + 0.2 * (laboratory score), where the laboratory score is assigned based on the laboratory reports.
