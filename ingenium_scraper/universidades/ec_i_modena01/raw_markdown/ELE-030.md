## Contents

The course content can be divided into the following main topics: • Introduction to microelectronics and semiconductor physics (1.5 CFU) • Electronic devices: diode, BJT, MOSFET (2.5 CFU) • Small-signal model (0.5 CFU) • Biasing, basic stages, and cascode (1.5 CFU) • Current mirrors (0.5 CFU) • Differential amplifiers (1 CFU) • Frequency response (1.5 CFU) • Feedback (2 CFU) • Basic circuits with operational amplifiers (1 CFU)

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide the knowledge necessary to understand the physical operation of key non-linear electronic devices—such as transistors and diodes—and their application in the implementation of analog electronic circuits. The course will examine the fundamental building blocks of analog electronic circuits at the transistor level, focusing on the mathematical derivation of their key circuit properties. Additionally, the course will cover analog circuit analysis methodologies, which will be applied through practical exercises. In terms of the Dublin Descriptors, the course objectives can be summarized as follows: Knowledge and understanding: acquiring fundamental concepts regarding the physical operation of key non-linear electronic devices (transistors and diodes) and their application in analog electronic circuits. Applying knowledge and understanding: using analytical techniques and development methodologies to gain proficiency in the analysis and design of analog electronic circuits. Making judgments: ability to design independently based on one's own knowledge and experience, even with incomplete specifications; ability to organize the development stages of an analog circuit. Communication skills: ability to present design considerations to both technical and non-technical audiences. Learning skills: acquiring the skills necessary to tackle design problems.

## Prerequisites

In order to successfully attend the course, the student should have basic knowledge of electrical engineering, physics, mathematical analysis, and algebra.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of in-person lessons—comprising both lectures on theoretical concepts and the explanation and solving of exercises—conducted in Italian. It aims to provide students with the knowledge necessary to understand the physical operation of key non-linear electronic devices, such as transistors and diodes, and their application in the design of analog electronic circuits. Course content may be supplemented or modified based on changing circumstances and student feedback. Teaching materials, including the slides used during lectures and any additional resources, will be made available on Microsoft Teams or Moodle.

## Other information

Knowldege and understanding (First descriptor) At the end of the course the student will have to demonstrate knowledge of the basic polarization techniques of a transistor and the genesis of the small signal equivalent models of transistors and diodes. The student will also have to demonstrate knowledge of elementary assemblies with their fundamental properties, such as gain and input and output resistances. The student will have to demonstrate knowledge of the main techniques for analyzing the frequency response of an analog circuit. The student will have to demonstrate knowledge of the concept of feedback applied to an electronic amplifier with its effects, advantages and disadvantages on its fundamental properties, such as gain, input and output resistances, band, linearity, phase margin. The student will have to demonstrate knowledge of the analysis techniques of a feedback amplifier as well as of the main compensation techniques. Applying knowledge and understanding (Second descriptor) At the end of the course the student must be able to read the schematic of a simple amplifier recognizing the elementary assemblies of which it is made up. The student should be able to calculate the polarization point of the circuit and deduce the main small signal properties, such as gain, input and output resistances. The student must be able to devise and support arguments also relating to properties such as bandwidth, stability and compensation. Making judgements (Third descriptor) At the end of the course the student must be able to collect and interpret the most relevant aspects of an electronic amplifier, possibly hypothesizing improvement solutions. Communication skills (Fourth descriptor) At the end of the course, the student must demonstrate that he is able to present the concepts described by the First Descriptor, possibly integrating the presentation with his ideas. Furthermore, the student must demonstrate that he is able to concisely explain possible solutions to circuit problems, which may arise during the oral exam. Learning skills (Fifth descriptor) At the end of the course, the student should be able to undertake subsequent studies with a high degree of autonomy as well as gain knowledge, with the support of advanced books, of some advances themes in their field of study.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test followed by an oral test; the two parts are inseparable. Admission to the oral test is contingent upon passing the written test. Failure to pass the oral test results in the invalidation of the written test, which must then be retaken. The written test lasts three hours. It involves the analytical solution of analog circuits and aims to assess the student's ability to apply the knowledge acquired to the analysis of such circuits. The oral test is a structured interview, led by the examining board, lasting approximately 30 minutes. It is designed to evaluate the candidate's overall theoretical knowledge, as well as their ability to present information clearly and rigorously and to draw connections between the various topics and concepts covered in the course. Exams will be held during the standard examination periods. There are no mid-term tests during the course. Assessments are graded on a scale of thirty. The final grade is the average of the marks obtained in the two tests and will be announced at the conclusion of the entire examination process.
