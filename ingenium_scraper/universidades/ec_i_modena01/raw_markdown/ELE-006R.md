## Contents

FIRST CONCEPTUAL BLOCK: STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF MATTER (2 CFU): Atoms, molecules, ions and chemical structures. Chemical bonds in and between molecules: liquids, solids and gases and their transformation and reactivity. Fundamentals of organic chemistry: classification of functional groups. The IUPAC international nomenclature. SECOND CONCEPTUAL BLOCK: CHEMICAL SUBSTANCES AND THEIR TRANSFORMATIONS (1 CFU): Properties of metals, insulators and semiconductors. Thermodynamics and reference to phase transition diagrams. THIRD CONCEPTUAL BLOCK: CHEMICAL REACTIVITY (3 CFU): The nature of chemical reactions: moles, molecular weights and grams as units for quantitative description of chemical transformations, with special emphasis on combustion reactions. Chemical equilibrium and spontaneous chemical reactions in the gas phase and aqueous media. Electrochemistry and chemical reactions involving electrical work. NOTE: - Exercises on the main topics covered, self-assessment exercises and collaborative correction. - It is possible to spend more time on some topics than estimated on the basis of student feedback.

## Learning objectives

The aim of the Chemistry Course is to provide the knowledge of the fundamentals of chemistry and the strategies for using chemical models to solve simple problems. The teaching approach is the "learning by doing" one, therefore the experimental practice in the chemistry laboratory is strongly integrated with the theoretical aspects of the Course programme. Such an approach aims to stimulate students' ability to abstract and apply theoretical models.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of chemistry, physics and mathematics acquired at secondary school is required. Recommended: University course in Mathematical Analysis I (with exercises).

## Teaching methods

Lectures are given in the presence of students, according to university regulations, using slides and involving students in simple chemical experiments. If access to the chemistry laboratory is available, the experience will be proposed only to the students of Electronic Engineering. The laboratory activity is considered fundamental to the achievement of the learning objectives of the course. The final assessment consists of two parts: one relating to theory and exercises and the other relating to laboratory activities. The laboratory activity is assessed by means of a report which the student can only produce if he/she carries out the activity at times other than the official timetable (i.e. during make-up laboratory time). In the absence of this report, the grade will be determined by the theory and practical part only and cannot exceed 28/30.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: Through lectures, guided numerical exercises, group activities developed in the laboratory and group discussions, the student learns the main methods of modern chemistry and acquires the ability to understand the patterns of inorganic systems of interest in electronics. Application of knowledge and understanding: Through guided numerical exercises in the classroom and laboratory exercises, the student will be able to apply the acquired knowledge to the characterisation of matter (mainly semiconductors) from various chemical and physico-chemical aspects. Making judgements: Through written reports of laboratory experiments, the student will be able to understand, critically discuss and present the results obtained and the approximations made. Communication skills: Written reports and group discussions on laboratory work will develop the ability to present the data obtained in an effective and concise way, to express the concepts learnt in an appropriate language and to lead a discussion on the topics covered. Learning skills: the activities described allow students to acquire the methodological tools to continue their studies and to organise their own updating. Abstraction: The general laws of chemistry are presented through laboratory experiments. The experimental data must then be interpreted and generalised by the student in abstract patterns.

## Assessment methods

The assessment methodology is applied throughout the course, as students are regularly asked to solve simple problems during lectures. In addition, three self-assessment tests are provided through the course Moodle platform (one at the beginning of the course, one midway through the semester, and a third at the end of the course), which students complete independently. The examination consists of a written test lasting approximately 60 minutes and covering all topics discussed during the lectures. The test includes numerical exercises and theoretical questions in the form of multiple-choice quizzes. Students are required to solve problems related to the following topics: 1) Balancing chemical reactions and calculating reaction yields. 2) IUPAC and traditional nomenclature of inorganic compounds, as well as traditional nomenclature of common organic compounds; 3) Homogeneous chemical equilibrium in the gas phase and relationships among thermodynamic equilibrium constants; 4) pH calculations for strong and weak monoprotic acids and bases; 5) Solubility product calculations; 6) Oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions, the use of standard reduction potential tables, the relationship between redox reactions and electrochemical cells, and the Nernst equation. Practice tests designed to help students prepare for the written examination are also available online through the course Moodle platform. An oral examination may also be conducted in person to supplement the grade obtained in the written test. During the oral examination, students are asked to present and discuss the fundamental models of modern chemistry, illustrating their application through examples relevant to different engineering fields. Examination results are communicated within two weeks of the test date through publication on the ESSE3 student information system.
