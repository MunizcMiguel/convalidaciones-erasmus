## Contents

LINEAR ALGEBRA (6 CFU) Fundamental algebraic structures: groups and fields. Vector spaces and subspaces. Fundamental models. Intersection and sum of subspaces. Systems of generators. Linear dependence and independence of vectors. Bases and dimension of a vectorial space (16 hours) Matrices. Matrix operations. Special matrices. Determinant of a matrix. Properties of the determinant and methods for computing the determinant. The inverse of a matrix (9 hours) Linear transformations and their properties. The matrix of a linear transformation (5 hours) Rank of a matrix. Algorithms for computing the rank of a matrix. Systems of linear equations. Cramer linear systems. Algorithms for the resolution of linear systems (10 hours) Similar matrices. Diagonalizable matrices. Eigenvectors, eigenvalues and eigenspaces (6 hours) Inner product. The length of a vector. Angle between two vectors. Orthogonal vectors. Orthonormal bases. Gram-Schmidt method. Orthogonal complement of a vectorial Euclidean subspace. Outer product (8 hours) EUCLIDEAN GEOMETRY (2 CFU) The real Euclidean plane. Parametric and cartesian representations of a line of the Euclidean plane. Reciprocal position of lines. Parallelism and orthogonality of lines. Euclidean distance. Areas. Plane transformations. Polar coordinates (7 hours) The real three-dimensional Euclidean space. Parametric and cartesian representations of lines and planes. Reciprocal positions of lines, planes, line and plane. Parallelism and orthogonality of lines, planes, between line and plane. Distances. Angles. Volumes. (11 hours) CONICS IN EUCLIDEAN PLANE (1 CFU) Conic theory. Projective extension of a Euclidean space; points at infinity. Homogeneous and non-homogeneous equations of a conic. Tangent and polar of a conic. Classification of conics of the Euclidean plane. Centre and axes. Canonical equations. Foci and directrices of a non-degenerate conic (9 hours)

## Learning objectives

To give the basic knowledge and operative methods of linear algebra and their application to Euclidean geometry of dimension two and three, At the end of the teaching the student will be able to - recognize and apply different methods to solve typical problems of linear algebra and Euclidean geometry -discuss the topics of the course with appropriate language and correct mathematical formalism -develop in depth skills on topics related to those presented in the course For a thorough description of the aims of this activity consult the section "Expected Results".

## Prerequisites

Basic skills in mathematics and set theory from high school

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test and an oral test. The written exam (2 hours) is a multiple choice test of 11 questions: 3 theoretical questions and 8 exercises . The score is: 3 points for each correct answer, -1 points for each incorrect answer, 0 points for questions without answer. The written test is passed with a score grater than or equal to 15. Once the written test is passed, the student can access the oral test. There are two intermediate written tests during the course: if the student passes them he has direct access to the oral test. The interview focuses on theoretical arguments of the program and is aimed at verifying the level of knowledge and understanding of the topics of the program, the ability to use mathematical language and to apply correctly the hypothetical deductive method. The final grade depends on the oral exam, while taking into consideration also the written grade. A basic knowledge of the topics and a sufficient ability to apply the knowledge corresponds to the minimum grade (18/30), a full knowledge of the topics and an excellent ability to apply the knowledge corresponds to the maximum grade (30/30 cum laude); the graduation of the intermediate grades will be based on the achievement of the expected learning outcomes, the ability to re-elaborate and the mastery of the topics covered.
