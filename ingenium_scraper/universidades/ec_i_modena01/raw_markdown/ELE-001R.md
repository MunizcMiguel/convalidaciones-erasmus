## Contents

Topics and their allotted time could be slightly modified based on feedback from the class. 63 hours corresponding to 7 credits are dedicated to the following topics 1-Upper extreme and lower extreme. Axiom of completeness. 2-Sequences. Calculus of limits. The number of Nepero. Infinitesimal and infinite sequences. 3- Bounded, symmetric, monotone, periodic functions. Limit and continuity. Discontinuous functions. Asymptotes. Composite and invertible functions. Theorem of zeros, of Weierstrass and theorem of intermediate values. Limits of extra interest. Infinite and infinitesimal functions. 4- Derivative and tangent. Angular points, cusps and flexes with vertical tangent. Continuity and differentiability. Rules of derivation and fundamental derivatives. Stationary points and local extremes. Primitives. Monotonicity test. Search of local and global minima and maxima. De L'Hospital rule. Second derivatives, concavity and convexity. Flexes. 5- The Reimann integral. Fundamental theorem of integral calculus. Integration by parts and substitution. Improper integrals. 18 hours corresponding to 2 credits are dedicated to the following topics 7-Convergent, divergent and irregular number series. Geometric and harmonic series. 8-Taylor-MacLaurin formula with the rest according to Peano and according to Lagrange. Taylor series. 9- Complex numbers in algebraic, trigonometric, and exponential form. Gauss plane. Operations with complex numbers.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide (1) the basic knowledge: of differential and integral calculus for functions of a real variable; (2) the ability to analyze mathematical problems that can be approached with these mathematical tools allowing the student student to develop a mentality oriented toward problem solving. At the end of the course, the student will be able to understand the basic concepts of the theory of limits and differential and integral calculus in a real variable, know the main properties of the field of complex numbers, the series operation, the approximation of functions of a real variable by the use of appropriate polynomials, analyze the graph of a function of a real variable and deal with simple optimization problems.

## Prerequisites

Main operations between sets. The sets of natural, integer, rational, real numbers and their main properties. Polynomial algebra. Algebraic equations and inequalities. Powers, roots and logarithms. Trigonometric functions. Equations of lines and conics as geometric loci. No Additional Educational Obligations (OFA) are required to enter the exam.

## Teaching methods

Teaching is delivered in Italian through face-to-face in-person lectures. The lectures include a theoretical part and an exercise part. The theoretical part consolidates the student's understanding and knowledge of the topics presented. The exercises are aimed at refining the student's application skills and are devoted to solving exercises on all the topics in the program. Attendance at lectures is not compulsory but strongly recommended. Lectures are conducted in the classroom and are supported by tutoring activities. The following will be made available in the course moodle platform: lecture notes, slides, exercise sheets and self-assessment quizzes.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: through classroom lessons, the student learns the main methods of mathematical modeling based on the tools of mathematical analysis. Applied knowledge and understanding: through practical exercises, the student is able to apply the acquired knowledge to solve problems that require typical tools of mathematical analysis. Learning skills: the students are able to independently use the acquired methodological tools useful for continuing their studies and updating their knowledge and skills. Autonomy of judgment: the student develops the ability to independently choose the methods of analysis and solution of problems relating to the course program. Communication skills: the student develops the ability to clearly explain the topics covered in the course.

## Assessment methods

The exam takes place at the end of the course. It includes: 1. a written test aimed at verifying the achievement by the student of the ability to independently analyze and solve mathematical analysis problems using differential and integral calculus; 2. an oral test aimed at ascertaining the knowledge and understanding of the course contents, the ability to connect and apply knowledge, the command of language. Only students who have no additional educational obligations are eligible to take the written examination. The written test, lasting 120 minutes, consists of four exercises whose correct resolution involves the same score. During the test, it is not allowed to use: notes, books, handouts or manuals; it is possible to make use of a not scientific calculator. The results of the written test are communicated through ESSE3 on average within one week from the date of the test. The oral exam can be accessed only after passing the written exam. A passed written test remains valid throughout the exam session in which it was held (January-February or June-September). The oral test generally consists of three theoretical questions concerning: definitions, examples, illustration of properties, statements of propositions and theorems, their related proofs. Part of this test may also take place in written form. It will be, on average, 40 minutes in length. The grade of the exam is the result of the arithmetic average of the evaluations of the two tests. The rules illustrated apply to face-to-face exams. Times and methods may vary as a result of contingent situations that force and deliver exams online Students enrolled in the first year of the course can earn credits related to this course by passing two partial written tests. The first partial test will be scheduled during the class suspension period of the first semester; the second test will take place, immediately after the conclusion of the semester. Each of the two partial tests, both lasting150 minutes, consists of two exercises and two theory questions. The four questions thus present all have equal importance in achieving the final grade. It is not permitted during the tests to use: notes, books, handouts or textbooks; use of a non-scientific calculator is permitted. The results of the partial tests are communicated through ESSE3, on average within three weeks of the date of the performance in the case of the first test and on average within one week for the second test. One can only enter the second partial test if the score of the first test is at least 18/30 has no additional training obligations. The exam is considered passed if both partial tests are sufficient and the final grade will be obtained from their arithmetic avarage.
