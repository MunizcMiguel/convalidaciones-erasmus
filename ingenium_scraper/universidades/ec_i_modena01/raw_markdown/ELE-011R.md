## Contents

## Learning objectives

At the end of this course, the student should be able to: a) know how to use some mathematical tools developed for the analysis of deterministic and random signals; b) know how to apply these tools to the analysis of: 1) analog-digital and digital-analog conversion; 2) analog and digital modulations; 3) noise processes and their effects in communication systems.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of the basics of calculus and statistics (probability, random variables and random vectors).

## Teaching methods

Learning is based on both following online lectures and attending lectures in a classroom. Attendance of lectures: optional. Teaching strategies: pre-recorded online lectures for theory, classroom summary lectures, and classroom exercises for learning MATLAB. Language: Italian.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: through online and classroom lectures, and MATLAB training each student learns the main methods for the analysis of signals and communications systems, and their implementation, and acquires the ability to understand the technical literature concerning signal processing and communication systems. Applying knowledge and understanding: through MATLAB training, each student learns to apply the acquired knowledge to the analysis of signals and communications systems. Making judgments: through the interaction with the teacher during MATLAB training, students learn to critically analyze the obtained results, and to present them. Communication skills: the interactions with the teacher during MATLAB training allow students to develop the ability to illustrate the obtained results in an effective and concise way, to express the learned concepts in an appropriate language and to discuss the covered topics. Learning skills: the activities described above allow students to learn various methodological tools which are useful to continue their studies and update their own knowledge in the fields of signal analysis and communication systems.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of a written test, followed by an oral test. The written test, if carried out in a classroom, consists of 4 open questions, which concern different topics covered in the course. Its total duration is 160 minutes. Furthermore, during its development, the consultation of teaching material (texts, notes, slides, ...) is not permitted. Distinct questions are given similar grades; any difference between grades is motivated by their level of difficulty. The overall grade assigned to a written test carried out correctly in all its parts is equal to 33 (30 cum laude). The grades are made available to the students not later than one week after the written test; they are informed through esse3. The achievement of a sufficient score in the written test represents a necessary condition for the oral test. The oral test aims at deepening the student's level of knowledge and at evaluating his/her exposition skills. The questions formulated during the oral test aim both at deepening the content of the questions of the written test, and assessing the overall level of learning and understanding of additional topics covered in the course. The final grade represents the aggregate of the grades assigned tp: 1) the written exam; 2) the oral exam; and 3) two exercises completed in the MATLAB environment prior to the written exam or during the course.
