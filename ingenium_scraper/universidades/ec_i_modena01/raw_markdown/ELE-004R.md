## Learning objectives

The course will provide: basic knowledge of differential and integral calculus for functions of several real variables with real and vector values, the understanding of conservative vector fields and the presentation of the main methodological tools for the study of differential equations. More specifically, the expected learning objectives after the completion of the course and passing the exam are, with reference to the Dublin descriptors, the following: Knowledge and understanding: 1. Knowledge and understanding of differential calculus in several variables for functions to real values. 2. Knowledge and understanding of integral calculus in several variables for real-valued functions. 3. Knowledge and understanding of curves and surfaces in the space. 4. Knowledge and understanding of conservative vector fields. 5. Knowledge and understanding of linear ordinary differential equations and separable variable differential equations. 6. Ability to analyze and solve problems related to the study of functions of several real variables. 7. Ability to analyze and solve problems related to the integral calculation of functions of several real variables. 8. Ability to calculate the length of a curve and the area of a surface. 9. Ability to recognize a conservative vector field and compute its potential. 10. Ability to solve problems through the use of differential equations.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of topics covered in Mathematical Analysis I is required, with emphasis on differential and integral calculus for functions of one variable, and numerical successions and series. Knowledge of topics in Linear Algebra is also required.

## Teaching methods

Teaching is delivered in Italian through face-to-face in-person lectures. The lectures include a theoretical part and an exercise part. The theoretical part consolidates the student's understanding and knowledge of the topics presented. The exercises are aimed at refining the student's application skills and are devoted to solving exercises on all the topics in the program. Attendance at lectures is not compulsory but strongly recommended. Lectures are conducted in the classroom and are supported by tutoring activities. The following will be made available in the course moodle platform: lecture notes, slides and exercise sheets.

## Assessment methods

The exam takes place at the end of the course. It includes: 1. a written test aimed at verifying the achievement by the student of the ability to independently analyze and solve mathematical analysis problems using differential and integral calculus in several variables both forscalar and vector-valued funcions and differential equations; 2. an oral test aimed at ascertaining the knowledge and understanding of the course contents, the ability to connect and apply knowledge, the command of language. Only students who have no additional educational obligations are eligible to take the written examination. The written test, lasting 120 minutes, consists of four exercises whose correct resolution involves the same score. During the test, it is not allowed to use: notes, books, handouts or manuals; it is possible to make use of a not scientific calculator. The results of the written test are communicated through ESSE3 on average within one week from the date of the test. The oral exam can be accessed only after passing the written exam. A passed written test remains valid throughout the exam session in which it was held (January-February or June-September). The oral test generally consists of three theoretical questions concerning: definitions, examples, illustration of properties, statements of propositions and theorems. Proof may be required of some theorems, specified during the course. Part of this test may also take place in written form. The oral proof must be taken in the same examination session in which the written proof was passed. It will be, on average, 40 minutes in length. The examination grade will be the result of the weighted average of the evaluations of the two tests with the following weights: - Written test 1/3 - Oral test: 2/3. Two intermediate written tests are scheduled; the first will take place during the teaching break of the semester and the second immediately after the end of the semester. The tests are reserved for students who have already acquired credits in Mathematical Analysis I. Each of the two 90-minute tests consists of three exercises, all of equal importance. It is not permitted, during their development, to use: notes, books, handouts or manuals; it is allowed to make use of a non-scientific calculator. Passing both of these tests permits exemption from the written examination (with a grade equal to the arithmetic mean of the two tests) provided that the oral examination is taken in the summer session. The exam grade will be the result of the weighted average of the assessments of the two tests with the following weights: - Written test 1/3 - Oral test: 2/3.
