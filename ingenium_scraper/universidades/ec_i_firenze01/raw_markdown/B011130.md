### Language

Italian

### Learning objectives

The course aims to develop students' knowledge of Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tools and their role throughout the product development cycle. Upon successful completion of the course, students will have acquired both the theoretical knowledge required to select the most appropriate geometric modelling strategy for a given mechanical assembly and the practical skills needed to effectively use feature-based parametric solid modelling software.  
  
In particular, the course provides the following knowledge:  
  
cc3 – Systematic knowledge of the key aspects of mechanical design in industrial engineering and the related methodologies. In particular, students will understand the most appropriate methods for defining a product and its characteristics. The study of mechanical components and assemblies, their basic sizing, and their graphical representation constitutes the main area of knowledge and understanding.  
  
cc8 – Understanding of the broader multidisciplinary context of engineering, with particular emphasis on problem-solving approaches that start from the identification of a problem, investigate its causes, and identify appropriate multidisciplinary solutions.  
  
cc9 – Knowledge of information technologies and their role in supporting engineering design activities.  
  
cc12 – Knowledge of the most appropriate manufacturing processes for the production of mechanical components.  
  
The course develops the following application skills:  
  
ca3 – Ability to select and apply appropriate analytical, modelling, and verification methods for the design and analysis of machines and mechanical systems, including the interpretation and production of detail and assembly drawings using CAD software, as well as the functional design of mechanical systems by applying the principles of kinematics, kinetostatics, statics, and dynamics.  
  
ca7 – Ability to select and use appropriate tools, equipment, and methods for the design and production of products and services, with particular reference to three-dimensional CAD modelling software and technical information management systems.  
  
ca8 – Ability to combine theoretical knowledge and practical skills in order to solve multidisciplinary engineering problems.  
  
ca10 – Ability to select the most appropriate manufacturing process for the production of mechanical components.  
  
With regard to independent judgment, the course contributes to the development of the following competencies:  
  
ag1 – Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance.  
  
ag2 – Ability to compare alternative engineering solutions and contribute to technically justified decisions by referring to engineering standards and established industrial procedures.  
  
ag3 – Ability to work collaboratively and effectively within a team, understanding the engineering context of a problem and recognizing the main interdisciplinary implications of the application domain.  
  
ag4 – Ability to organize one's work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and resources effectively, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to task prioritization.  
  
ag5 – Ability to recognize personal knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competencies by identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
  
Students will also acquire the following communication skills related to Industrial Technical Drawing:  
  
ac1 – Ability to communicate and convey information, ideas, problems, and solutions in engineering contexts to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language and referring to familiar application scenarios.  
  
ac2 – Ability to present engineering problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and conventions.  
  
ac3 – Ability to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results, and engineering solutions through the preparation of technical drawings, diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools and conventions.  
  
Upon successful completion of the course, students will also have developed the following learning skills:  
  
ap1 – Ability to acquire and update knowledge in the relevant engineering field, independently, although primarily under guidance, by using established educational resources and learning tools.  
  
ap2 – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, technical manuals, and digital resources), selecting relevant information with appropriate guidance.  
  
ap3 – Ability to learn through project-based and Problem-Based Learning (PBL) methodologies by applying acquired knowledge and engineering tools to case studies and real-world engineering problems of appropriate complexity.

### Assessment methods

The final (oral) exam involves the creation, with the help of 3D CAD software, of a machine/device design that is discussed during the examination. Open questions are then asked about basic concepts and IT techniques.The exam includes in particular the discussion of the project carried out by the student (design choices, implementation methods); open questions regarding the methodologies of design and three-dimensional and two-dimensional representation of mechanical components and assemblies.Students must demonstrate good knowledge of basic approaches for 3D modelling; good knowledge and ability to use computer tools for 3D and 2D CAD; sufficient knowledge of standards for technical representation of mechanical components/assemblies; sufficient knowledge of annotation systems for GD&T.

Assessment Criteria and Grading Rubric Fail (Insufficient) Negative evaluation due to serious conceptual errors, fragmentary or non-existent understanding of the subject matter, inability to apply knowledge, major gaps, and a lack of independence. 18–22 Sufficient or more than sufficient evaluation due to elementary knowledge of the topics, the presence of some errors, the ability to handle only simple or guided tasks, and a basic level of independence. 23–25 Satisfactory evaluation due to a satisfactory understanding of the topics, the ability to correctly apply knowledge to familiar contexts, and the ability to complete exercises independently with some inaccuracies; demonstrates a satisfactory level of independence and operational competence. 26–28 Good or very good evaluation due to a solid theoretical understanding of the discipline, the ability to connect concepts, solve problems, and provide critical analysis; ability to perform complex tasks independently, precisely, and effectively, with a good level of independence and operational competence. 29–30 and Honors (Lode)Excellent or outstanding evaluation due to complete, in-depth, and well-structured knowledge; the ability to operate independently and flexibly even in complex or new contexts; appropriate, precise, and articulate use of specialized terminology; demonstrates critical thinking, originality, and rigor in both presentation and application. Honors (Laude) is awarded for work or performance distinguished by brilliance and originality, exceeding standard curricular expectations.
