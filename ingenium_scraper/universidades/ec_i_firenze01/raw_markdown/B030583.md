## Learning objectives

The module aims to provide students with a basic knowledge of the main concepts and results of probability theory and statistics. Particular attention will be devoted to applications to concrete situations and to the development of the ability to construct, use, and interpret simple probabilistic and statistical models.

## Contents

Descriptive statistics. Basic combinatorics and axioms of probability. Conditional probability and independence. Discrete and continuous random variables. Joint and conditional distributions. Limit theorems. Statistical inference: point estimation, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing. Introduction to linear regression.

## Prerequisites

Differentiation and integration of functions in one and more variables. Limits and series. Basic notions of linear algebra.

## Teaching methods

The course will rely primarily on lecture slides, complemented by explanations and worked examples on the board. Classes will alternate between presentation of the material and in-class exercises and discussion. Students will be encouraged to participate actively. Short quizzes will also be given in each class, providing opportunities for individual reflection and discussion with classmates.

## Assessment methods

The exam consists of two parts, which take place on the same day. The first is a multiple-choice test. The second is a written test with open-ended questions, which students may take only after passing the multiple-choice test. In the written test, students will be required to demonstrate their ability to apply the results discussed in lectures to problem-solving, justify their reasoning, and using correct scientific language. They must also be able to choose a suitable probabilistic model or statistical method for the situation, check the conditions for its use, and interpret the results.

## Extended programme

Descriptive statistics: graphical and numerical summaries of data, measures of location and dispersion, and correlation. Basic combinatorics. Sample spaces and the axioms of probability. Conditional probability and independence. Random variables: distributions, mean, and variance. Discrete models: uniform, Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, and geometric distributions. Continuous models: uniform, normal, and exponential distributions. Joint and conditional distributions, covariance, and independence. Sampling and sampling distributions. Limit theorems: the weak law of large numbers and the central limit theorem. Statistical inference: point estimation, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests. Introduction to simple linear regression.
