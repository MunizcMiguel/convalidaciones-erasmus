### Language

italian/english

### Learning objectives

The course as a whole consists of two parts:  
Part A: Gas Turbines in Various Land-Based Applications  
Part B: Gas Turbines in Aeronautical Propulsion Applications  
Part A is based on course B024569 – GAS TURBINE POWERPLANTS – Part A  
Part B is set out below:  
  
The aim of the course is to provide knowledge regarding gas turbines  
in various aeronautical propulsion applications; specific  
focus is placed on the study of actual operating conditions in different application contexts  
SKILLS ACQUIRED ON COMPLETION OF THE COURSE:  
To be able to evaluate the performance of an aero-gas turbine propulsion system under real operating conditions (@B410-cc1, cc4, cc10, cc13, ca1, ca3, ca8, ca14 – @B309-cc4, ca9).  
To understand and compare different types of propulsion systems, with particular attention to combustion systems, the fuels used and their environmental impact (@B410-cc1, cc4, cc10, cc13, ca1, ca3, ca8, ca14 - @B309-cc4, ca9)  
Understand and compare the various control systems for gas turbine propulsion systems (@B410-cc1,cc4, cc10, cc13, ca1,ca3,ca8, ca14 - @B309-cc4,ca9)  
Be able to model and simulate the actual operation of an aeroengine using appropriate software, based on simple datasheets or publicly available repositories. (@B410-cc1,cc4, cc10, cc13, ca1,ca3,ca 6, ca8, ca14 - @B309-cc4,cc9,ca9)  
The preparation of a project relating to the simulation of a specific propulsion system and the oral examination, designed to assess the ability to describe and analyse the various factors influencing the actual operation of engines and their adaptation to specific operating and mission conditions through appropriate control systems, also enable students to develop, test and evaluate their own judgement (@B410 – ag1, ag5 – @B309 ag1, ag5), communication skills (@B410 – ac1, ac5 – @B309 ac1, ac5) and learning skills (@B410 – ap2 – @B309 ap2)  
For the definitions of cc, ca, ag, ac and ap, please refer to the Master Degree current programme structure.

The aim of the course (Part A and Part B) is to provide knowledge regarding:

### Assessment methods

For the sake of completeness, Parts A and B are reproduced in full; for this specific syllabus, please refer only to Part A  
  
Gas Turbine Modelling Laboratory – (Part A)  
Thermo-fluidodynamic Analysis Laboratory (numerical, experimental and design-oriented) of high-temperature machine components – (Part B) (the course includes a Part C, taught by another lecturer, to which the laboratories are extended for the experimental component)  
Oral Examination (Parts A and B+C)  
  
In the Gas Turbine Modelling Laboratory, students work in small groups (3–4) on specific case studies of gas turbine plants, using numerical O-D models of the various components. The laboratory sessions take place during the final month of the course, replacing lectures.  
In the Thermoflui-Dynamics Analysis Laboratory, students, in the classroom, examine specific case studies of high-temperature components (turbine blades, discs, combustors) in order to apply, to specific cases, the procedures for calculating the thermal load applied to a specific component and the conductive analysis of those components, with a view to calculating the temperature distribution within them (the experimental topics are covered in Part C).  
  
During the oral examination, the assignment produced in the relevant laboratory (Part A) is discussed, attendance at the prescribed laboratory sessions is verified (Parts B and C), and a detailed theoretical question is asked on the topics covered during lectures, integrating both Part B and Part C (taught by another lecturer). Should attendance at the laboratory sessions (Parts B and C) be partial or unverified, the specific skills described above are also assessed in the oral examination through further questions.
