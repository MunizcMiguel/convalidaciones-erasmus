## Learning objectives

CC5 – Knowledge of industrial electrical engineering, electrical machines, industrial robotics, and control systems employed in industrial plants.  
  
CC10 – Understanding of applicable engineering techniques and methods, their limitations, and awareness of the non-technical implications of engineering practice.  
  
CA5 – Ability to solve problems involving resistive and dynamic electrical networks, including active components; to select and use electrical machines in mechanical systems; to analyse their performance; to design automatic feedback control systems using computational tools; to understand the operation of industrial and mobile robotic systems; and to select appropriate electric, hydraulic, and pneumatic actuators for machines and industrial plants.  
  
AG1 – Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance.  
  
AG4 – Ability to organize one's work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and resources effectively, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to task prioritization.  
  
AG5 – Ability to recognize personal knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competencies by identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
  
AC1 – Ability to communicate and convey information, ideas, problems, and solutions in engineering contexts to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language and referring to familiar application scenarios.  
  
AC2 – Ability to present engineering problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and conventions.  
  
AC3 – Ability to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results, and engineering solutions through the preparation of drawings, diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools and conventions.  
  
AP1 – Ability to acquire and update knowledge in the relevant engineering field, independently, although primarily under guidance, by using established educational resources and learning tools.  
  
AP2 – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, technical manuals, and digital resources), selecting relevant information with appropriate guidance.  
  
AP3 – Ability to learn through project-based and Problem-Based Learning (PBL) methodologies by applying acquired knowledge and engineering tools to case studies and real-world engineering problems of appropriate complexity.
