## Learning objectives

The course is named “Fondamenti di Automatica” (9 ECTS credits) in degree programmes B307, B309 and B412 or “Elementi di Automatica” (6 ECTS credits) in degree programmes B309, B336 and B412. It introduces the fundamentals of modelling, analysis and control of dynamical systems, with particular emphasis on linear time-invariant and feedback systems. The general learning outcomes are common; in the 6-credit course they are pursued within a reduced scope. Outcomes or topics marked “9 credits only” are not included in Elementi di Automatica. By the end of the course, students will be able to:  
  
KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
Tuning-matrix codes: B307–ETL: CC18, CC19; B309–MEL: CC5; B412–MEM: CC01, CC02, CC03, CC16; B336–RAM: CC3.  
• describe the main mathematical representations of continuous-time dynamical systems;  
• describe the models and fundamental properties of sampled-data systems; [9 credits only]  
• explain the dynamic behaviour, stability and performance of linear time-invariant systems;  
• explain the operation of feedback control systems and the main criteria for the design of basic automatic control systems.  
  
APPLYING KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
Tuning-matrix codes: B307–ETL: CA23, CA28, CA29, CA33, CA35; B309–MEL: CA5; B412–MEM: CA01, CA03, CA06, CA07, CA08, CA09; B336–RAM: CA1, CA7.  
• derive state-space and input–output models of simple physical systems and analyse their response;  
• apply the Laplace transform and time- and frequency-domain methods to LTI systems;  
• assess stability and performance using the Routh–Hurwitz criterion and Bode plots;  
• apply Nyquist plots and criterion and the root-locus method; [9 credits only]  
• simulate dynamical and control systems in MATLAB/Simulink and compare analytical and numerical results;  
• design basic PID controllers and compensator networks subject to steady-state and dynamic specifications;  
• analyse and design basic discrete-time control systems; [9 credits only]  
• consult and use textbooks, lecture notes, manuals and technical documentation, and plan, complete and clearly document an analysis or design procedure.  
  
MAKING JUDGEMENTS

• select the analysis or design method most appropriate to the assigned problem;  
• critically interpret results, check their consistency, and discuss the assumptions and limitations of the adopted solution.  
  
COMMUNICATION SKILLS  
Tuning-matrix codes: B307–ETL: AC1; B309–MEL: AC1, AC2, AC3; B412–MEM: AC01, AC02; B336–RAM: AC3.  
• present analysis and design solutions in written and oral form, using appropriate technical terminology;

LEARNING SKILLS

## Contents

Common contents (6 and 9 credits): modelling and simulation; time- and frequency-domain analysis of continuous-time LTI systems; Routh–Hurwitz stability analysis; Bode plots; feedback systems; design of PID controllers and lead/lag compensators.  
Contents restricted to the 9-credit course: Nyquist plots and criterion; root locus; analysis and design of discrete-time systems.

## Prerequisites

Students are expected to have:  
• basic knowledge of mathematical analysis: complex numbers, derivatives and integrals, and linear differential equations;  
• basic knowledge of linear algebra: vectors, matrices, determinants, eigenvalues and systems of linear equations;  
• basic knowledge of physics and electrical circuit analysis;  
• elementary computer skills.  
  
No previous knowledge of MATLAB/Simulink is required: the tools needed for the course will be introduced during the classes.

## Teaching methods

Delivery mode: entirely face-to-face.  
  
Teaching activities include:  
• lectures introducing concepts, models, and analysis and design methods;  
• guided exercise sessions in which the methods are applied to problems of increasing difficulty;  
• MATLAB/Simulink simulations aimed at comparing analytical and numerical results and assessing stability and performance;  
• individual problem-solving activities, with in-class discussion of solution strategies and design choices;  
• guided use of textbooks, manuals and technical documentation;  
• supporting materials and activities on Moodle, including examples, solved exercises and possible formative quizzes.  
  
The combination of lectures, exercises and simulations supports the gradual transition from understanding the fundamentals to analysing and designing basic control systems. For Elementi di Automatica (6 credits), exercises and simulations do not include Nyquist plots or criterion, root locus, or the analysis or design of discrete-time systems. All planned activities are individual; no group work is included.

## Assessment methods

The assessment structure, duration, score allocation, evaluation criteria and rules for determining the final mark are identical for all degree programmes sharing the course. The scope of the assessed content is consistent with the number of credits: the examination paper explicitly identifies questions or subparts restricted to Fondamenti di Automatica (9 credits). Learning is assessed through a compulsory written test and an oral examination. The oral examination includes a compulsory discussion of the exercises completed in the written test and, at the student’s request, an optional full oral examination. The assessment jointly addresses theoretical knowledge, application skills, independent methodological judgement and clarity of technical communication.  
  
WRITTEN TEST  
The two-hour written test consists of three sections:  
1. Theoretical knowledge and conceptual understanding: multiple-choice questions on definitions, properties and fundamental concepts concerning continuous-time dynamical systems. Maximum score: 7 points (threshold: 0). For attending students, the quiz may be replaced by an exemption test whose result is valid until the end of the summer examination session and may be used to replace the quiz in one written examination only. After it has been used or has expired, the student must take the standard quiz.  
2. Analysis skills: an exercise on an LTI feedback system using Bode plots and/or the Routh–Hurwitz criterion. Parts concerning Nyquist plots and criterion and the root locus are restricted to the 9-credit course. Maximum score: 12 points.  
3. Design and problem-solving skills: design of a feedback control system using compensator networks and/or PID controllers subject to steady-state and dynamic specifications. Any analysis or design parts concerning discrete-time systems are restricted to the 9-credit course. Maximum score: 12 points.  
In the examination paper, questions or subparts on Nyquist plots and criterion, root locus, and the analysis or design of discrete-time systems are marked “9 credits only”. Students enrolled in Elementi di Automatica (6 credits) complete only the parts covered by their syllabus.  
For both courses, the maximum raw score across the three sections is 31 points; for assessment purposes, the written-test mark is capped at 30/30.  
  
Permitted materials are blank sheets, logarithmic graph paper, compensator charts provided on Moodle, one personal double-sided A4 formula sheet, a ruler and a non-programmable scientific calculator. Students with specific needs are granted the accommodations provided for by University regulations and services.  
  
ORAL EXAMINATION  
The discussion of the exercises completed in the written test is compulsory for all students and serves to verify and confirm learning. It assesses awareness of the adopted procedure, the ability to identify and correct possible errors, independent judgement and the appropriate use of technical terminology. The score of the written test is provided at the end of the discussion.  
  
At the student’s request, provided that the written-test mark is at least 14/30, an optional full oral examination may be taken. It includes:  
• open questions on fundamental theoretical and methodological topics included in the syllabus of the relevant course;  
• analysis and justification of design choices and possible alternative solutions.  
  
FINAL MARK AND ASSESSMENT CRITERIA  
Without the optional full oral examination, the final mark is the written-test mark after the compulsory discussion of the exercises. The optional full oral examination may change the written-test mark by between -5/30 and +5/30, depending on the student’s command of the subject, methodological awareness, independent judgement and quality of presentation. The final mark is expressed out of 30 and is capped at 30/30; a result below 18/30 is a fail. Honours (Lode) may be awarded for performance meeting the criteria of the highest band in the grading rubric.  
  
The assessment specifically considers:  
• correctness of results and procedures;  
• completeness of the analysis and consistency among assumptions, method and results;  
• ability to select and apply appropriate tools;  
• ability to critically interpret results and justify design choices;  
• clarity, organisation of the solution and appropriate use of technical terminology.  
  
ASSESSMENT CRITERIA AND GRADING RUBRIC  
Fail – Serious conceptual errors; fragmentary or absent understanding; inability to apply knowledge; major gaps and lack of independence.  
18–22 – Essential knowledge of the topics; some errors; ability to address only simple or guided tasks; basic independence.  
23–25 – Satisfactory understanding; correct application of knowledge in familiar contexts; independent work with some inaccuracies; satisfactory operational competence.  
26–28 – Solid theoretical understanding; ability to connect concepts, solve problems and formulate critical analyses; independent, accurate and effective completion of complex tasks.  
29–30 and Honours (Lode) – Complete, in-depth and well-structured knowledge; ability to operate independently and flexibly, including in new contexts; precise use of disciplinary language; rigour, critical thinking and originality. Honours are awarded for performance distinguished by brilliance and originality beyond standard curricular expectations.

## Extended programme

All topics are common to the 6- and 9-credit courses, except those marked [9 credits only].  
  
1. MODELLING AND SIMULATION  
• State-space and input–output representations.  
• Lumped-parameter linear models of simple electrical, mechanical, hydraulic and thermal systems.  
• Numerical simulation of dynamical systems using MATLAB and Simulink.  
  
2. ANALYSIS OF CONTINUOUS-TIME LINEAR TIME-INVARIANT SYSTEMS  
• Review of the Laplace transform and inverse transform.  
• Transfer function and impulse response.  
• Free and forced response.  
• Stability and the Routh–Hurwitz criterion.  
• Step response and time-domain performance indices.  
• Harmonic analysis and frequency response.  
• Bode plots.  
• Nyquist plots. [9 credits only]  
  
3. ANALYSIS OF FEEDBACK SYSTEMS  
• Structure and properties of feedback systems.  
• Internal stability.  
• Nyquist criterion. [9 credits only]  
• Stability margins.  
• Steady-state specifications: steady-state error and system type.  
• Time-domain dynamic specifications: overshoot, settling time and rise time.  
• Frequency-domain dynamic specifications: resonance peak, bandwidth, phase margin and crossover frequency.  
• Approximate relationships between time- and frequency-domain specifications.  
• Root locus. [9 credits only]  
  
4. DESIGN OF FEEDBACK CONTROL SYSTEMS  
• Basic compensator networks: lag and lead compensators.  
• Trial-and-error design in the frequency domain.  
• PID controllers and basic tuning criteria.  
  
5. SAMPLED-DATA SYSTEMS [9 CREDITS ONLY]  
• Models and analysis of discrete-time systems.  
• Discretisation methods.  
• Fundamentals of sampled-data control-system design.
