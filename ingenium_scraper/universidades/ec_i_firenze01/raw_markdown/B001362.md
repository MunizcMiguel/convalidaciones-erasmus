### Learning objectives

cc3 – Systematic knowledge of the key aspects of mechanical design in industrial engineering and the  
related methodologies, including the ability to identify the most appropriate methods for defining a  
product and its characteristics. Knowledge and understanding encompass materials technology, the  
mechanical design of components and assemblies, their sizing, the study of their static and dynamic  
behaviour and component interactions, manufacturing technologies, and their graphical  
representation.  
cc5 – Knowledge of industrial electrical engineering, electrical machines, industrial robotics, and  
control systems employed in industrial plants.  
cc7 – Knowledge of current topics concerning the organization and management of the technical  
aspects of production.  
cc9 – Knowledge of information technologies and their role in supporting engineering design.  
Understanding of data organization within databases and of information systems supporting  
engineering processes.  
  
ca4 – Ability to select and apply appropriate analytical, modelling, and experimental methods for the  
design, analysis, and testing of fluid machinery, thermal engines, and energy conversion systems,  
including: the application of design principles for thermal and fluid systems; the application of  
thermodynamic principles to engineering systems; the understanding of major thermodynamic cycles  
and diagrams; the identification of heat transfer mechanisms relevant to engineering applications; the  
functional design of turbomachinery, energy conversion plants, and internal combustion engines; and  
the evaluation of the energy, economic, and environmental performance of fluid machinery, thermal  
systems, and hydraulic components.  
ca6 – Ability to identify, formulate, and solve industrial engineering problems, particularly in mechanical  
engineering, by defining technical, social, health, safety, environmental, and commercial constraints  
and applying established engineering methods to develop engineering projects involving products,  
devices, machines, energy systems, and automated systems.  
ca8 – Ability to integrate theoretical knowledge and practical skills to solve multidisciplinary engineering  
problems while considering technical and non-technical constraints and collaborating effectively with  
engineers and other professionals typically involved in manufacturing industries.  
  
ag1 – Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret results, and  
draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance.  
ag2 – Ability to compare alternative engineering solutions and contribute to technically justified  
decisions by referring to engineering standards and established industrial procedures.  
ag3 – Ability to work collaboratively and effectively within a team, understanding the engineering context  
of a problem and recognizing the main interdisciplinary implications of the application domain.  
ag4 – Ability to organize one's work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and  
resources effectively, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to task  
prioritization.  
ag5 – Ability to recognize personal knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competencies by  
identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
  
ac1 – Ability to communicate and convey information, ideas, problems, and solutions in engineering  
contexts to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language and  
referring to familiar application scenarios.  
ac2 – Ability to present engineering problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and  
systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and  
conventions.  
ac3 – Ability to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results, and engineering  
solutions through the preparation of drawings, diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools  
and conventions.  
  
ap1 – Ability to acquire and update knowledge in the relevant engineering field, independently, although  
primarily under guidance, by using established educational resources and learning tools.  
ap2 – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the  
main available resources (libraries, databases, technical manuals, and digital resources), selecting  
relevant information with appropriate guidance.  
ap3 – Ability to learn through project-based and Problem-Based Learning (PBL) methodologies by  
applying acquired knowledge and engineering tools to case studies and real-world engineering  
problems of appropriate complexity.  
ap4 – Ability to understand and interpret the fundamental principles of design, manufacturing, and  
maintenance processes while operating in compliance with quality, safety, and reliability requirements  
and with reference to the main international technical and environmental standards (ISO, UNI, ASME,  
DIN, etc.).

Friction and wear.  
Kinetostatics  
Efficiency of machines and group of machines.

Gears.  
Lubrication.  
Motion transmission with flexible elements.  
Lagrangian Dynamics.  
Balancing of alternative mechanisms.

### Extended programme

1. -Presentation of the course; explanation about examinations  
Basic definitions: machine, kinematic chain, mechanism  
-Kinematic pairs, elementary kinematic pairs (rotational, helical, prismatic) and higher kinematic pairs in the plane and in three  
-dimensional space  
-computation of the degrees of freedom (DOFs) of a plane mechanism (Grübler rule) and a spatial mechanism (Kutzbach rule); Bi-dimensional examples: four-bar linkage, crank, cam follower, cam follower with roller, cross connector, cross connector with ineffective auction; spatial examples: spatial four-bar mechanism with 4 revolute pairs, spatial four-bar mechanism with two revolute, a spherical and a cylindrical joint.  
2. Sliding friction: Coulomb's law, friction coefficient and friction angle - Definition of boundary lubrication, hydrostatic lubrication (natural and forced) and hydrodynamic lubrication (hard and soft) - Rolling friction: definition of the rolling friction parameter and rolling friction coefficient. Friction between dry surfaces, Reye's hypothesis: push pin, flat sled.  
3. Reye hypothesis: brake blocks (only evolution of the pressures on the contact surface, assuming a given direction of approach) - Definition of mechanical efficiency and loss factor – Efficiency of machines in series and in parallel with examples. Efficiency in forward and reverse motion with definition of non back-driveability .  
4. Theoretical Treatment of static problems and kinetostatic - Cardinal equations of statics as a result of the dynamic equations - Rigid body subjected to two forces, two forces and a moment, three forces, three forces and a moment, four forces- Examples: four-bar mechanism and crank thrust (only ideal case).  
5. Mechanical efficiency of the inclined plane; forces exchanged in a rotational joint in the ideal case, and with friction - definition of the friction circle - efficiency of the rotational joint – efficiency of the prismatic joint - efficiency of the screw-nut pair.  
6. Efficiency of the screw-nut torque, direct and reverse motion - relationships between the characteristic angles of the thread - The wheel in locomotion: driven wheel, traction and braking – Example of vehicle moving on a horizontal road.  
7. Kinetostatics exercise: crankshaft with friction  
8. Recall kinematic circular rigid motions, rolling curve (roulette) and fixed curve in plane motions  
9. Kinematic analysis of four-bar mechanisms, analytical formulation for crankshaft  
10. Cams and tappets  
11. Kinetostatics exercise: ideal case, case with friction.  
12. Gearing, introduction to mating profiles  
13. Geometry of gears with involute teeth profile  
14. Definition of conjugate profiles and methods for their generation; definition of involute and evolute curves; spur gears with involute teeth: basic properties; geometric characteristics and design: pitch, normal pitch (module), etc.; conditions of continuity of the motion and of non-interference; hints on cutting gears; correction of the toothed wheels and mounting of the same; notes on the spur gears with helical teeth.  
15. Ordinary and planetary gear trains with 1 DOF  
16. Two-DOFs gearing: automotive differential  
17. Mechanical systems with flexible organs: transmissions with belts  
18. Ordinary and pulley blocks, belt brakes  
19. Introduction to the theory of lubrication, deduction of Reynolds’ equation starting from Navier-Stokes’ ones; application to the case of the flat slide: Reynolds’ equation  
  
20. Application to the case of the flat sled: infinitely wide sled, finite width slide, infinitely narrow sled. Reynolds equation  
21. Infinitely long rotational joint, finite length rotational joint, infinitely short rotational joint, Reynolds’ equation and examples  
22. Lubrication for juxtaposition, Reynolds’ equation and examples  
23. Thrust bearing, journal bearing  
24. Recalls of Newtonian dynamics. Recalls of Lagrangian dynamics: non-redundant formulation  
25. Lagrangian dynamics recalls: redundant formulation and examples  
26. Kinematics and dynamics of crankshaft mechanism: equation of motion  
27. Dynamic balancing of single-cylinder engines  
28. Dynamic balancing multi-cylinder engines
