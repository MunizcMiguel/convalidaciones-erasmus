## Learning objectives

KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
cc5 - Knowledge of the principles of industrial electrical engineering, electrical machines, industrial robotics, and controls used in industrial plants.  
cc10 - Understanding of applicable techniques and methods and their limitations, including awareness of the non-technical implications of engineering practice.  
  
APPLYING KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTANDING  
ca5 - The ability to solve problems related to resistive and dynamic electrical networks, even in the presence of active components; to select and use electrical machines within mechanical systems, analyzing their performance; to design automatic feedback control systems, also with the aid of computer tools; to understand the operation of manipulator and mobile robots most commonly used in industrial and non-industrial applications; to choose the appropriate drives (electrical, hydraulic, and pneumatic) for machines and plants.  
ca6 - The ability to apply one's knowledge to identify, formulate, and solve industrial and specifically mechanical engineering problems, defining specifications and technical, as well as social, health and safety, environmental, and commercial constraints, and to solve them using established methods, thereby implementing engineering projects appropriate to the developed level of knowledge and understanding. The projects may concern products, devices, machines, energy systems, or automatic systems.  
ca8 - The ability to combine theory and practice to solve multidisciplinary engineering problems, taking into account constraints of a non-technical nature, and working in collaboration with other engineers or other professionals typically found in manufacturing companies.  
  
MAKING JUDGEMENTS  
ag1 - Ability to analyze data and information using known methods and tools, interpret the results, and draw coherent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance.  
ag2 - Ability to compare different options or solutions to an engineering problem and to contribute to reasoned technical choices, referring to technical standards and standard industry procedures.  
ag3 - Ability to work in a team in a collaborative and coordinated manner, understanding the context of the engineering problem and recognizing the main interdisciplinary implications of the application field.  
ag4 - Ability to organize one's work in compliance with assigned commitments and deadlines, managing available time and resources, realistically assessing one's capabilities, and contributing to the prioritization of activities.  
ag5 - Ability to recognize one's own limitations in knowledge and the need to acquire new skills, identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
  
COMMUNICATION SKILLS  
ac1 - Ability to communicate and transfer information, ideas, problems, and solutions in the engineering field to specialist and non-specialist interlocutors, using appropriate language and referring to known application contexts.  
ac2 - Ability to present problems, solutions, analyses, and results in a clear and structured manner through written reports and oral presentations, according to standard methods and formats.  
ac3 - Ability to represent and communicate data, results, and technical solutions graphically, through the preparation of diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools and conventions.  
  
LEARNING SKILLS  
ap1 - Ability to learn and update one's knowledge in the reference engineering area, also independently but predominantly in a guided manner, using established teaching tools and resources.  
ap2 - Ability to find and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, manuals, and digital sources), selecting relevant information with the support of provided guidance.  
ap3 - Ability to learn through teaching methods based on projects and problem-solving (Problem Based Learning – PBL), applying acquired knowledge and tools to case studies and real-world problems of appropriate complexity.  
ap4 - Ability to understand and interpret the basic principles of design, production, and maintenance processes, operating in compliance with quality, safety, and reliability requirements and referring to the main technical and environmental standards (ISO, UNI, ASME, DIN, etc.).

## Contents

1. Kinematics of manipulators  
2. Differential kinematics and statics  
3. Dynamics  
4. Trajectory planning  
5. Motion control

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of:  
- Geometry and Linear Algebra;  
- Physics;  
- Analytical Dynamics (desired, not mandatory);  
- Modelling and control of LTI systems.

## Teaching methods

35 hours of ex cathedra lectures plus 15 hours of class exercises, 50 hours in total.  
According to the availability of computer laboratories, some of the class exercises  
will be given using Matlab and Simulink.  
During the course, visits to one or more companies producing, integrating, and/or using  
industrial robots will be possibly organized.

## Assessment methods

Written exam. Three different questions will be usually formulated to the student: one or two will be theoretical. The remaining questions will be on applications of theory.
