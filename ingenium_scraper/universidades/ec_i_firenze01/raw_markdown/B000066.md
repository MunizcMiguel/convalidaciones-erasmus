### Learning objectives

The course aims at providing students with the basic knowledge of chemistry essential to form a scientific knowledge to which to add the specific characteristics of the course of study. In particular, the course topics are selected in such a way as to enable students to face, understand and evaluate the chemical, thermodynamic, kinetic and catalytic aspects related to chemical processes and their optimal conduction.  
  
Students will acquire:  
cc2 (B309 and B406): Knowledge of the laws of chemistry and some laws of physics (thermodynamics) relevant to industrial engineering, together with an understanding of their role in formulating  
models that represent physical reality.  
  
Student will be able to:  
ca2 (B309 and B406): Ability to apply knowledge of chemistry to solve disciplinary problems in chemistry, applied chemistry and theoretical thermodynamics, interpreting and applying the governing physical laws in subsequent engineering applications.  
Making Judgements  
ag1 (B309 and B406): Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance.  
ag4(B309 and B406): Ability to organize one's work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and resources effectively, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to task prioritization.  
ag5 (B309 and B406): Ability to recognize personal knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competencies by identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities  
  
Communication Skills  
  
ac2(B309 and B406): Ability to present engineering problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and conventions.  
ac3 (B309 and B406): Ability to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results, and engineering solutions through the preparation of drawings, diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools  
and conventions.  
  
Learning Skills  
  
ap2 (B309 and B406): Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, technical manuals, and digital resources), selecting  
relevant information with appropriate guidance.

### Assessment methods

The student evaluation includes a written exam, followed (after a few days) by an oral exam, reserved to students who have passed the written exam. The written test is devised to verify the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to solve problems in the chemical field (ca2 capacity). The oral exam includes questions regarding the correction of written exam and the knowledge and understanding of the topics covered during the course which are particularly relevant to industrial engineering (competence cc2).  
The final assessment evaluates the written and oral exam results, as well as the student's independent judgment (ag1, ag4, ag5) and communication and learning skills (ac2, ac3, ap2).

### Extended programme

Atomic structure of matter. Atoms, ions and molecules: the atomic model of matter; the subatomic particles; isotopes, atomic weight, molecular weight, mole.  
Electronic structure of the atom. The wave-particle duality. The quantum-mechanical model of the atom. Orbitals and quantum numbers.The quantum number of spin. Polyelectronic atoms. Rules for filling the orbitals. Electronic configurations.  
Periodic table of the elements and periodic quantities.  
Introduction to chemical bonding. The covalent bond. Geometry of molecules (VSEPR theory). VB theory. MO theory (elements). Polar covalent bonds and dipole moment. The ionic bond. General characteristics of metals and metallic bonding (gas theory and band theory). Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors.  
Intermolecular forces. Intermolecular forces and states of aggregation of matter. Types of solids: metallic, ionic, covalent and molecular. Main properties of the different types of solids.  
Gaseous state. Gas law equations. Gas mixtures (partial pressures and Dalton law). State diagrams. Colligative properties.  
Elements of thermodynamics. Thermochemistry. Chemical equilibrium. Law of mass action. Le Chatelier's principle. Entropy, free energy and spontaneity.  
Chemical kinetics (elements).  
Chemical reactions. Reactions in solution (molarity and pH). Oxidation-reduction and combustion reactions. Oxidation number, balancing redox reactions.  
Electrochemistry. Galvanic cells. Standard reduction potential and the Nernst equation. Spontaneity and displacement of redox reactions. Oxidation-reduction reactions of water. Electrolysis (molten salts and aqueous solutions). Faraday laws. The phenomenon of corrosion. Strategies for corrosion protection.
