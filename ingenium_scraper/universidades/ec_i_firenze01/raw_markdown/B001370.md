## Learning objectives

The course aims at providing students with the basic knowledge required for a correct analysis and design of production systems and industrial logistics.  
Knowledge and understanding. By the end of the course, students will know: current topics in the organization and management of production factors, together with quantitative methodologies for profitability analysis and for the optimisation of production factors (CC6); the broader multidisciplinary context of engineering, with a focus on problem solving, starting from the problem to identify its causes and possible countermeasures (CC7); the techniques and methods applicable to plant and industrial logistics design — demand forecasting, layout study, warehouse sizing — together with their limitations (CC9); the non-technical implications of engineering practice, including the economic and safety implications of plant design choices (CC10).  
Applying knowledge and understanding. By the end of the course, students will be able to: select and use appropriate equipment, tools and methods for the production of goods and services, such as material handling, storage and identification systems (CA7); combine theory and practice to solve multidisciplinary plant design and management problems, taking into account both technical and non-technical constraints (CA8).  
Making judgements. Students will develop the ability to work collaboratively and effectively within a team, understanding the context of plant engineering problems and recognizing their main interdisciplinary implications (AG3).  
Communication skills. Students will be able to communicate information, problems and solutions in the field of industrial plants to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language (AC1), and to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results and solutions through diagrams, graphs and tables (AC3).  
Learning skills. Students will be able to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation (manuals, databases, digital resources), selecting relevant information (AP2), and to understand and interpret the fundamental principles of design, production and maintenance processes, operating in compliance with quality, safety and reliability requirements and with reference to the main technical standards (AP4).

## Assessment methods

The student's assessment is based on an examination test which consists of performing a written assignment. Seven exam sessions are scheduled during the academic year.  
The task consists of 10 questions covering the entire programme. The questions are of three types: simple theoretical questions (with relative weight of 1), theoretical questions of average difficulty (with relative weight of 2), articulated exercises (with relative weight of 3). For each section of the reference book (except for the introductory chapter) there is one question. The questions are different for each exam session and are different for each assignment within the same session. The sequential order of the questions is also casualized, to make each job unique.  
In order to pass the exam with the minimum score, students must have acquired a good knowledge of the subject as a whole and must be able to model an industrial engineering problem (CC6, CC7, CC9, CC10). . In order to obtain a high mark, knowledge is required to be very good and comprehensive in all subjects covered. In order to have the highest grade with honors, the student must combine previous knowledge with outstanding ability to manage the time factor, producing an excellent result in a short time (CA7, CA8).  
From the perspective of transversal skills, it is necessary for the student to be able to express technical concepts in written form (AC1), also through diagrams, graphs and tables (AC3). It is also necessary to understand the technical standards (AP4) and the scientific documentation proposed in class (AP2).

Honors (Laude) is awarded for work or performance distinguished by brilliance and originality, exceeding standard curricular expectations.
