### Language

### Learning objectives

The aim of this course is to provide knowledge of technical language and techniques of Design Technology and Industry, with particular regard to the major industry regulations, to introduce the main elements of  
machines.  
The course has the following objectives:  
- Provide the basic elements of the language of technical communication in industry, in order to put the student able to correctly perform and interpret technical drawings.  
- Describe the organs of machines and their modes of representation  
- To provide the basics of computer aided design.  
The student will acquire:  
cc3 - Systematic knowledge and understanding of the key aspects of mechanical design of industrial engineering and its methods. In particular: understanding of which are the most suitable methods in order to define a product and its characteristics; technologies for their production and their graphic representation.cc12 - knowledge of the most suitable production process for the production of mechanical components and for the representation of drawing dimensions, geometric and dimensional tolerances.  
Students will prove: ca3 - The ability to choose and apply appropriate analytical, modelling, verification and experimental methods for the interpretation and production of drawings of parts and assemblies; the basic sizing and functional verification of components and mechanical groups. ca10 - The ability to choose the most suitable production process for the production of mechanical components for their correct representation with particular reference to issues related to the definition of geometric and dimensional tolerances.  
With regard to the Transversal Competencies, the following are achievable:  
CT1: Written technical communication (reports, deliverables)  
CT4: Graphic representation and communication (drafting of diagrams, graphs and tables)  
CT5: Understanding of technical standards  
CT7: To respect commitments and deadlines

### Assessment methods

The student's assessment includes a written test in which they are proposed:1) drawing of a standard mechanical component (bearing, toothed wheel, threaded connection)(2) one or more exercises on dimensional and/or geometrical tolerancesThese exercises are designed to ascertain the achievement of cc3 competence. (3) Shot peening of a mechanical assembly4. a drawing of one or more parts of the assembly.Questions 3 and 4 are intended to ascertain at least a good level of achievement of cc3, cc8 and to verify at least a good level of achievement of ca3 capacity and a sufficient level of achievement of ca10 capacity.

Italian

### Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide students with the knowledge of the technical language and methods of Industrial Technical Drawing, with particular emphasis on the main standards governing the field, as well as an understanding of the principal machine elements and their graphical representation.  
  
More specifically, the course pursues the following objectives:  
  
to provide the fundamental principles of technical communication in the industrial engineering field, enabling students to correctly produce and interpret technical drawings;  
to describe the main machine elements and the conventions used for their graphical representation;  
to provide the fundamentals of Computer-Aided Design (CAD).  
  
In accordance with the set of Knowledge and Skills defined by the Degree Programme in compliance with the Dublin Descriptors, the course provides the following knowledge:  
  
cc3 – Systematic knowledge of the key aspects of mechanical design in industrial engineering and the related methodologies. In particular, the course focuses on the mechanical design of individual components and assemblies, their basic sizing, their graphical representation according to current Industrial Drawing standards, as well as introductory concepts concerning their static and dynamic behaviour, interactions among components, and manufacturing technologies.  
  
cc12 – Knowledge of the most suitable manufacturing processes for producing mechanical components, as well as the representation of dimensions, dimensional tolerances, and geometric tolerances in technical drawings.  
  
The course enables students to develop the following application skills:  
  
ca3 – Ability to select and apply appropriate analytical, modelling, verification, and experimental methods for the interpretation and production of detail and assembly drawings, as well as for the basic sizing and functional verification of mechanical components and assemblies.  
  
ca10 – Ability to select the most appropriate manufacturing process for mechanical components in order to ensure their correct technical representation, with particular reference to dimensional and geometric tolerances.  
  
With regard to independent judgment, the course develops the following competencies:  
  
ag1 – Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidelines.  
  
ag2 – Ability to compare different engineering solutions and contribute to technically justified decisions by referring to engineering standards and established industrial procedures.  
  
ag3 – Ability to work effectively in teams, understanding the engineering context of a problem and recognizing the main interdisciplinary implications of the application domain.  
  
ag4 – Ability to organize one's work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and resources effectively, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to task prioritization.  
  
ag5 – Ability to recognize personal knowledge limitations and the need for further learning, identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
  
Students will also acquire the following communication skills related to Industrial Technical Drawing:  
  
ac1 – Ability to communicate and convey information, ideas, problems, and solutions in engineering contexts to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language and referring to familiar application scenarios.  
  
ac2 – Ability to present engineering problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and conventions.  
  
ac3 – Ability to graphically represent and communicate technical data, results, and solutions through the preparation of technical drawings, diagrams, graphs, and tables using established standards and conventions.  
  
Upon successful completion of the course, students will also have developed the following learning skills:  
  
ap1 – Ability to independently, although primarily under guidance, acquire and update knowledge in the relevant engineering field using established educational resources and learning tools.  
  
ap2 – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, technical manuals, and digital sources), selecting relevant information with appropriate guidance.  
  
ap3 – Ability to learn through project-based and Problem-Based Learning (PBL) methodologies by applying acquired knowledge and engineering tools to case studies and real-world problems of appropriate complexity.
