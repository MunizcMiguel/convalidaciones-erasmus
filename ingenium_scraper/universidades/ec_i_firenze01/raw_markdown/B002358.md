## Contents

The course deals with the basic elements of measurement theory and the representation of the measurement result with relative uncertainty assessment. The main measuring instruments are covered, and basic knowledge is provided for the acquisition of signals from sensors. The operating principle of basic sensors are studied along with the operation of an automatic measurement system. The course includes laboratory lessons to learn dealing with measurements and instrumentations.

## Prerequisites

No special preliminary knowledge is required, except for the basic concepts of Physics and Electromagnetism of the teaching scheduled for the first year. For part 4 of the program relating to measurement uncertainty, knowledge of basic elements of statistics, the concept of random variable and the main distributions, both discrete and continuous, are required.

## Extended programme

- Step response and frequency response of 0th, 1st, and 2nd-order electrical systems. Resistive attenuators, low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, and band-stop filters.  
  
- The International System of Units.  
  
- The analog oscilloscope. Front-panel control layout. Input impedance. Vertical coupling modes (AC, DC, GND), compensated attenuator, input modes (ALT, CHOP, ADD, INV). Trigger and ramp generation circuits (main time base), ramp release modes (AUTO, NORM, SINGLE), trigger coupling (DC, AC, LF-REJ, HF-REJ). Delayed time base: waveform expansion using a dual time base and delay time measurement. HOLD-OFF adjustment (application to practical cases). Analog oscilloscope frequency response, bandwidth, step response. Rise time measurements.  
  
- Digital oscilloscopes: Advantages over analog ones. Block diagram. Real-time sampling technique. Design values for a real-time oscilloscope: choosing the sampling frequency in relation to the analog bandwidth (real-time bandwidth), memory depth. Flash A/D converter, sequential ("interleaved") sampling. Sample, Peak, and High-resolution sampling modes. Random equivalent-time sampling technique. Trigger interpolator. Frequency limits of the random equivalent-time technique. Sequential equivalent-time sampling technique. Frequency limits of the sequential equivalent-time sampling technique. Dynamic limits of sequential equivalent-time oscilloscopes. Digital oscilloscope frequency response, bandwidth, step response. Rise time measurements.  
  
- Oscilloscope voltage probes. High-impedance probes: physical and circuit model, compensation, frequency and step response for compensated and uncompensated probes, compensated probe input impedance.  
  
- Transformer current probes. Physical model and circuit representation using mutually coupled inductors. Frequency response of the current probe, transfer impedance. Relationship between the parameters of the physical model and the physical and geometric characteristics of the current probe.  
  
- Digital multimeter. Block diagram. DC and AC voltage measurement. DC and AC current measurement. Resistance measurement. Continuity and junction testing. High-sensitivity AC/DC converter schematic. Integrating A/D converter schematic. Normal-mode rejection (NMR). Common-mode rejection (CMRR, CMR). Technical specification analysis: interpretation of symbols, specification application limits (temperature and humidity ranges), instrument usage limits, accuracy limits.  
  
- Measurement uncertainty. Measurement model, probability density, assessment of category A and category B uncertainty, uncertainty propagation. Propagation of probability densities (Monte [docente] method), coverage interval.
