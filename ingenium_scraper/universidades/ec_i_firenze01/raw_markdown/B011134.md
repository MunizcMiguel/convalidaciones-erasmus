## Learning objectives

The course Industrial Energy Management aims to provide students of the Master’s Degree in Energy Engineering with an advanced and multidisciplinary preparation to understand, analyse and manage energy systems in industrial contexts, integrating technical, economic, managerial and regulatory aspects. The course develops knowledge and competences aligned with the Dublin Descriptors, with particular emphasis on energy optimisation processes and the transition towards more efficient and sustainable systems.  
  
Knowledge and understanding (CC)  
Students will acquire an in-depth understanding of the theoretical and scientific principles governing complex energy systems (CC01@ENM), including the thermodynamic, thermo-fluid dynamic, thermo-chemical and electrical phenomena that determine the operation of industrial plants (CC13@ENM, CC16@ENM). The course introduces advanced methods and tools for modelling, simulating and designing energy components and systems (CC03@ENM, CC06@ENM, CC07@ENM), together with criteria for analysing industrial processes and assessing their energy and environmental performance.  
Students will also develop a systemic understanding of the energy transition, including its regulatory, technological and methodological implications (CC09@ENM), as well as the methods needed to conceive, plan and manage complex and innovative systems and services (CC04@ENM). Moreover, the course provides methodological bases for performing experimental activities and energy diagnostics in articulated industrial contexts (CC05@ENM).  
  
Applying knowledge and understanding (CA)  
Upon completing the course, students will be able to apply the acquired knowledge to model and interpret the behaviour of complex energy systems (CA01@ENM) and to analyse components and plants using thermo-economic modelling, numerical simulation and performance evaluation tools (CA06@ENM, CA07@ENM). They will be able to conduct experiments and use advanced instrumentation for energy monitoring (CA05@ENM), critically evaluating results and associated uncertainties (CA08@ENM).  
Students will also be capable of designing, planning and managing energy conversion systems and complex industrial plants, integrating technical, economic and environmental considerations (CA14). They will learn to address typical industrial and energy engineering problems by identifying constraints, solutions and improvement opportunities (CA13@ENM, CA11@ENM). The course also enables students to assess the integration of renewable technologies and innovative energy systems with a view to sustainability and competitiveness (CA12@ENM) and to evaluate processes, technologies and plants considering their economic and managerial implications (CA10@ENM).  
The competencies acquired provide a solid foundation for further advanced studies or research activities (CA09@ENM).  
  
Judgement autonomy (AG)  
Students will develop the ability to independently analyse data, indicators and information from industrial plants and processes, formulating objective and coherent assessments (AG01@ENM). They will be able to compare alternative technical solutions and make informed decisions based on technical standards, design constraints and principles of efficiency and sustainability (AG02@ENM).  
The course supports the development of autonomy in organising activities, managing time and resources, and planning energy improvement interventions (AG04@ENM), as well as fostering awareness of the need for continuous professional development (AG05@ENM).  
  
Communication skills (AC)  
The course strengthens the student’s ability to communicate energy-related issues, analyses and solutions clearly and effectively to both specialist and non-specialist audiences (AC01@ENM), developing skills in drafting technical energy management reports and presenting energy scenarios (AC02@ENM).  
Particular attention is given to the use of graphs, diagrams, indicators and quantitative representations to illustrate performance and interventions (AC03@ENM), together with awareness of the ethical, environmental and economic implications of engineering decisions (AC04@ENM).  
  
Learning skills (AP)  
Finally, the course enhances the student’s ability to independently update their knowledge of technological, regulatory and methodological developments in the energy sector (AP01@ENM), to perform bibliographic and technical research using major scientific databases (AP02@ENM), and to learn through real-world problems and project-based activities, in line with the Problem-Based Learning approach (AP03@ENM).

## Assessment methods

Assessment Methods (English version)  
Throughout the course, thematic seminars and/or technical visits to industrial facilities are organised to complement the learning experience with professional insights and real-world case studies. Attendance is recorded and contributes to the final grade, as these activities support the student’s ability to contextualise theoretical knowledge in practical settings (CA10@ENM, CA12@ENM, AG01@ENM, AG04@ENM).  
The content of the seminars is not part of the examination, but unjustified absences negatively affect the final mark.  
  
Final Examination  
The final examination aims to verify the achievement of the expected learning outcomes and consists of the following components:  
  
1. Technical Report (students enrolled in the 9-ECTS version only)  
Students registered for the 9-ECTS version are required to produce a technical report, individually or in pairs, agreed upon with the instructor. The report may concern:  
• the energy analysis of an industrial plant, or  
• a bibliographic study on technologies or industrial energy systems.  
This activity assesses the student’s ability to:  
• apply modelling tools, performance evaluation methods, and techno-economic analysis to real systems (CA01@ENM, CA06@ENM, CA07@ENM, CA10@ENM, CA13@ENM, CA14@ENM);  
• demonstrate mastery of the engineering fundamentals of energy systems (CC01@ENM, CC03@ENM, CC06@ENM, CC07@ENM, CC13@ENM);  
• organise data, interpret results, and formulate well-founded conclusions (AG01@ENM, AG02@ENM);  
• communicate effectively through a clear and rigorous technical report (AC01@ENM, AC02@ENM, AC03@ENM);  
• conduct autonomous research and literature review (AP01@ENM, AP02@ENM).  
The report must be submitted a few days before the exam session. It is graded and contributes to the final mark. A satisfactory report is required in order to sit the oral examination.  
  
2. Oral Examination  
The oral examination consists of a technical interview aimed at verifying:  
• the student’s knowledge and understanding of the main topics covered in the course, including energy systems, conversion processes, and industrial applications (CC01@ENM, CC09@ENM, CC13@ENM, CC16@ENM, CC01@RAM, CC04@RAM, CC05@MEL);  
• the ability to apply such knowledge to solve engineering problems, possibly through short exercises similar to those tackled during classes (CA01@ENM, CA05@ENM, CA06@ENM, CA07@ENM, CA13@ENM, CA14@ENM; CA01@RAM, CA02@RAM, CA03@RAM, CA02@RAM, CA07@MEL, CA04@MEL);  
• autonomous reasoning and the capacity to evaluate alternative technical solutions and justify engineering decisions (AG01@ENM, AG02@ENM; AG05@RAM, AG04@RAM; AG01@MEL, AG02@MEL);  
• clarity of expression and ability to communicate technical concepts effectively (AC01@ENM, AC02@ENM; AC03@RAM; AC01@MEL, AC02@MEL).  
The oral examination generally includes two main questions based on the course content and class activities.  
  
Final Grade Determination  
The final grade is based on:  
• the oral examination (main component),  
• the technical report (for 9-ECTS students),  
• attendance and participation in seminars/visits.  
The final mark reflects not only theoretical knowledge but also the student's applied skills, communication abilities, and autonomous judgement, in accordance with the Dublin Descriptors (CC, CA, AG, AC, AP).  
  
Docimological evaluation table  
  
INSUFFICIENT: Negative evaluation due to serious conceptual errors, fragmentary or no understanding of the topics covered, inability to apply knowledge, serious gaps and lack of autonomy.  
  
18-22: Sufficient or more than sufficient assessment due to elementary knowledge of the topics, presence of some errors, ability to tackle only simple or guided tasks, basic level of autonomy.  
  
23-25: Discreet assessment due to satisfactory understanding of the topics, ability to correctly apply knowledge to known contexts and to approach the exercises autonomously with some inaccuracies, with a satisfactory level of autonomy and operational competence.  
  
26-28: Good or very good evaluation, due to a solid theoretical understanding of the discipline, the ability to connect concepts, solve problems and propose critical analyses, to perform complex tasks autonomously, precisely and effectively, with a good level of autonomy and operational competence.  
  
29-30 and Praise: Excellent or excellent evaluation, due to complete, in-depth and well-structured knowledge, ability to operate autonomously and flexibly, even in complex or new contexts, to use disciplinary language appropriately, precisely and articulately, to highlight critical skills, originality and rigor in exposition and application.  
  
The Praise is awarded to works or performances that stand out for their brilliance and originality, even beyond curricular expectations.

## Extended programme

Energy overview  
Energy demand. Historical trends. Overview of the main energy generation technologies. Load curve of the national electric grid.  
Economic analysis  
• Introduction to economic analysis.  
• Interest rate and investment lifetime  
• Investment and cash flow  
• CAPEX and OPEX  
• Capitalization mechanisms  
• NPV (Net Present Value), IRR (Internal Rate of Return), DPBT (Discounted Payback Time), and other economic indicators  
• Relationship between NPV and plant profitability  
• Nominal and effective interest rates  
Industrial plant components  
• Pumps  
• Description and operating principles  
• Characteristic curves  
• Characteristic curves as a function of rotational speed  
• Operating point  
• Example calculation of pump operating point  
• Pumps in series and parallel  
• Pump regulation systems  
• Cavitation  
• Fire-tube boilers  
• Boiler description  
• Fire-tube boilers  
• Thermodynamic analysis of fire-tube boilers  
• Boiler efficiency estimation  
• Safety issues related to fire-tube boilers  
• Thermal insulation  
• Heat transfer equations for conduction and convection  
• Heat transfer equations for pipes (cylindrical systems)  
• Critical insulation radius for pipes  
• Economic insulation thickness and related trends  
Characterization and monitoring of energy consumption  
• Energy consumption monitoring  
• Relationship between energy consumption and production  
• Energy drivers  
• Typical energy-consumption trends versus production  
• CUSUM technique  
• Degree-days definition  
• Typical energy-consumption trends versus climate  
• Energy performance indicators and universal diagram  
• Industrial energy management  
• Energy audit  
• Energy Manager (EM)  
• EGE (Certified Energy Management Expert)  
• ESCO (Energy Service Company)  
Energy markets  
• Electricity market.  
• Natural gas market.  
• Natural gas distribution networks.

Main industrial processes  
Analysis of energy flows in major industrial sectors: glass industry, paper industry, textile industry, and tanning industry.
