## Learning objectives

For the Energy Systems module (6 ECTS): The course aims to provide students, in relation to the learning outcomes (cc) identified for the bachelor degree, with the evaluation capability and thermodynamic sizing of energy conversion systems, with particular reference to those most commonly used in industrial plants today (cc4); to recognize and classify the main and most common energy systems, describe the operating principles of their various components, and carry out performance assessments of the plants based on energy balances, taking into account the relevant characteristics of the fluid machineries used (cc4); finally, to identify algorithmic approaches and use databases to achieve the aforementioned learning outcomes (cc9).  
With regard to the competences (ca) identified for the bachelor degree, for the Energy Systems section (6 ECTS), students are expected (ca4) to be able to: apply the principles of thermodynamics to energy systems described by the main thermodynamic cycles; correctly interpret thermodynamic diagrams; to select and apply appropriate analytical and modelling methods for thermal power plants and energy conversion systems; to assess the energy and environmental performance of conversion plants.  
With regard to the learning outcomes identified by the bachelor degree (ag), the course includes written tests (intermediate or final) on the thermodynamic analysis of specific energy systems (ag1, ag4), the methods for tests solving are presented and introduced in specific lectures, and various examples of solutions for energy plants and systems are provided, allowing sufficient time for preparation to take place in class during lesson time (ag5); furthermore, in the oral examination, students are invited to freely present a specific topic covered during the lectures, encouraging their independent ability to argue their answer (ag5).  
On the other hand, the intermediate and preliminary written tests demonstrate the communication skills defined by the bachelor degree, through the graphical and tabular representation of thermodynamic cycles, systems and results (ac2, ac3), whilst the oral examination demonstrates the ability to communicate specific issues relating to energy systems clearly and rigorously, highlighting the criteria for analysis (ac1, ac2)  
The plant engineering applications that form the basis of the additional module (3 ETCS) not only recall the knowledge (cc) and skills (ca) mentioned above, but also further foster independent judgement (ag1, ag4) and communication skills (ac2, ac3). Furthermore, as students are required to develop models of energy conversion systems independently, this enables them to practice and assess their ability to learn autonomously or with only partial guidance (ap1) and, albeit at a basic level, to gather further bibliographic information and develop the ability to solve problems and undertake simple projects (ap2, ap3)

## Assessment methods

Students can take part in 3 intermediate tests during the course. After passing the intermediate tests, the oral test (single question) and the final vote are directly accessible. If the student does not take part or pass the intermediate tests, the student must pass a written test (very similar to one of the intermediate tests) and the oral test. The tests are oriented to verify ca4 skills in close relation to the cc4 objectives. In particular, the written tests assess the ability to select and use suitable mathematical models for the description and performance estimation of thermodynamic cycles and corresponding energy systems.  
  
The oral examination, on the other hand, is designed to assess the student’s ability to describe energy systems and their components, highlighting their distinctive features and performance characteristics (cc4). The student must be able to demonstrate at least sufficient knowledge of the methods of mathematical and physical modelling of energy systems of general interest, and a sufficient knowledge of the energy components and systems described in the course, whilst details and in-depth analysis contribute to raising the final mark  
Furthermore, the written examination requires the application and demonstration of specific independent judgement in the preparation and selection of solution methods for the thermodynamic analysis of specific energy systems (ag1, ag4, ag5), whilst the oral examination assesses the student’s ability to present, independently, a specific topic covered during the lectures (ag5). Both assessments also enable the evaluation of presentation skills (ac1, ac2, ac3)  
Examination plan is published on the SOL website where each student has to sign up before exam using http://sol.unifi.it

## Extended programme

ENERGY SYSTEM section Energy Conversion national and worldwide scenario. Environmental effects of Energy Conversion. Combustion Process: Fundamentals; specific applications for Energy Conversion plants. Steam PowerPlants: thermodynamic features and components descriptions. Performance Gas Turbine PowerPlants: thermodynamic features and components descriptions. Performance Reciprocating internal combustion engines. Ideal and limit cycle for four-stroke spark ignition and ignition for compression. Real cycle and performance. Cogeneration and combined cycles. Thermodynamic advantages of cogeneration: analysis parameters and regulatory overview. Cogeneration plants with steam turbines, gas turbines and volumetric heat engines. Combined gas-steam cycles. Operating principles and types. Rankine cycles with ORC organic fluids and overview of the main solutions for the exploitation of renewable energy. POWERPLANTS section Numerical tools for modeling energy systems OpenModelica for simulating dynamic systems Applications for: - Thermodynamic transformations - Energy systems - Fluid networks - Operating machines and engines - Plant simulation
