## Partition: GEOMETRIA (Cognomi A-D)

### Language

Italian

### Learning objectives

General course objective: provide basic knowledge in the field of linear algebra (analysis of linear systems, matrix reduction and decomposition into structurally simple forms, aimed at applications) and analytic geometry (geometric interpretation of equations). Use the language of linear algebra to describe linear phenomena in the field of analytic geometry.  
  
In particular, with reference to the Dublin Descriptors, the expected learning outcomes are:  
  
[cc1] – Knowledge of mathematical principles and understanding of the role of the mathematical sciences as a tool for the analysis and solution of problems and models underlying industrial engineering;  
  
[cc7 for Gel] – Understanding of the broader multidisciplinary context of engineering;  
  
[ca1] – Ability to apply mathematical methods—particularly those related to geometry and linear algebra—to model, analyze, and solve engineering problems;  
  
[ca2, ca8 for Gel] – Ability to combine theory and practice to solve multidisciplinary engineering problems and to contribute to the implementation of engineering projects, working collaboratively with engineers and non-engineers;  
  
[ag1] – Ability to analyze data and information using established methods and tools, interpret the results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance;  
  
[ag4] – Ability to organize one's own work in compliance with assigned commitments and deadlines, managing the available time and resources, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to the prioritization of activities;  
  
[ag5] – Ability to recognize one's own knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competences, identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities;  
  
[ac2] – Ability to present problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and in a structured manner through written reports and oral presentations, according to standard formats and procedures;  
  
[ac3] – Ability to represent and communicate data, results, and technical solutions graphically through the preparation of diagrams, graphs, and tables, using established tools and conventions;  
  
[ap2] – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, manuals, and digital sources), selecting relevant information with the support of provided guidance.

### Contents

Vector spaces; linear systems of equations; affine geometry; inner products and Hermitian products; eigenvectors.

### Prerequisites

Basic mathematics, as required to pass the OFA test.

### Teaching methods

Lectures and classroom exercise sessions. The lectures combine the presentation of the theoretical part with the solution of numerous exercises and allow direct teacher–student interaction, in order to facilitate and ensure a full and concrete understanding of the subject. Moodle platform: development of online teacher–student interaction; reference texts; detailed summary of each lecture; assignment of progressively structured exercises following the development of the course; texts of the written examinations from previous years.

### Assessment methods

The final evaluation is based on a written and oral exam. Each written exam consists of 11 questions on the topics of the course. The teacher may not require the oral part, which, however, remains optional for the student. The student is expected to demonstrate a basic knowledge of the topics and be able to apply it in problem solving.

Assessment Criteria and Grading Rubric Fail (Insufficient) Negative evaluation due to serious conceptual errors, fragmentary or non-existent understanding of the subject matter, inability to apply knowledge, major gaps, and a lack of independence. 18–22 Sufficient or more than sufficient evaluation due to elementary knowledge of the topics, the presence of some errors, the ability to handle only simple or guided tasks, and a basic level of independence. 23–25 Satisfactory evaluation due to a satisfactory understanding of the topics, the ability to correctly apply knowledge to familiar contexts, and the ability to complete exercises independently with some inaccuracies; demonstrates a satisfactory level of independence and operational competence. 26–28 Good or very good evaluation due to a solid theoretical understanding of the discipline, the ability to connect concepts, solve problems, and provide critical analysis; ability to perform complex tasks independently, precisely, and effectively, with a good level of independence and operational competence. 29–30 and Honors (Lode)Excellent or outstanding evaluation due to complete, in-depth, and well-structured knowledge; the ability to operate independently and flexibly even in complex or new contexts; appropriate, precise, and articulate use of specialized terminology; demonstrates critical thinking, originality, and rigor in both presentation and application. Honors (Laude) is awarded for work or performance distinguished by brilliance and originality, exceeding standard curricular expectations.

### Extended programme

0 - Introduction and preliminaries. Complex numbers. 1 - Vectors, operations on vectors. Scalar product, vector product, triple product. Vector spaces, linear dependence, basis, dimension. Subspaces and their operations. Numerical vector spaces. 2 - Linear systems: Matrices. Operations on matrices and properties, special matrices. Determinant, rank. Linear systems of equations. Solutions of linear systems. Gauss Elimination Method. 3 - Linear analytic geometry: Parametric and Cartesian equations for subspaces. Lines, planes. Parallelism and perpendicularity. 4 - Linear metric geometry: Scalar products. Distance, angle, area, volume. 5 - Linear maps, Kernel and image. Projections and orthogonal projections. Matrices associated to linear maps. Change of basis. Eigenvectors and eigenvalues. Diagonalization. Diagonalization of symmetric matrices and spectral theorem.
