## Learning objectives

The course is meant to provide the student with an in-depth analysis on different topics related to Energy Engineering, like components design through advanced thermo-fluid-dynamics aspects and energy systems characterization, in the form of energy storage systems. The goal is to provide the student with the knowledge and the methodologies necessary to face and solve, in a proper way, problems related to the treated aspects. In accordance with the set of KNOWLEDGE AND COMPETENCES developed by the CDS, the knowledge provided during the course are: cc4: Knowledge and understanding of thermodynamics applied to energy systems and of fluid-dynamic phenomena as well as models capable of representing them. Knowledge of systems and machines for the production and conversion of energy, with particular reference to turbomachinery and industrial combustion equipment. Understanding the role of different energy technologies in ensuring the environmental and economic sustainability of production.  
In accordance with the APPLICATION CAPACITIES (in accordance with the Dublin Descriptors), the application capabilities provided during the course are: [CA4]: Applying knowledge and understanding related to analytical modelling and experimental methods to design, analyze and test fluid machines, thermal motors and energy conversion systems. This includes: the application of design criteria for technical and thermos-technical plants, fluid and energy distribution; the application of thermodynamic principles to simple systems; the understanding of the main thermodynamic cycles and the reading of thermal diagrams; the identification of significant heat transmission mechanisms for engineering applications; the analysis and functional design of equipment of mechanical interest such as turbomachinery, energy conversion systems and internal combustion engines; the evaluation of the energy, economic and environmental performance of fluid, thermal and oleo-dynamic machinery.  
With reference to Autonomy of Judgement (AG), the following descriptors apply: AG1 – Ability to analyse data and information using established methods and tools, interpret the results, and draw consistent conclusions, also with the support of provided guidance; AG2 – Ability to compare different options or solutions to an engineering problem and contribute to technically justified decisions, with reference to technical standards and established engineering procedures; AG3 – Ability to work collaboratively within a team, understanding the engineering context of the problem and recognising the main interdisciplinary implications of the application domain; AG4 – Ability to organise one's work while meeting assigned commitments and deadlines, managing available time and resources, realistically assessing one's own capabilities, and contributing to the prioritisation of activities; AG5 – Ability to recognise one's own knowledge limitations and the need to acquire new competences, identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities.  
With reference to Communication Skills (AC), the following descriptors apply:  
  
AC1 – Ability to communicate and convey information, ideas, problems, and solutions in engineering contexts to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate language and referring to familiar application contexts; AC2 – Ability to present problems, solutions, analyses, and results clearly and systematically through written reports and oral presentations, following standard formats and conventions; AC3 – Ability to represent and communicate technical data, results, and solutions graphically through the preparation of diagrams, graphs, and tables using established tools and conventions;  
With reference to Learning Skills (AP), the following descriptors apply: AP1 – Ability to acquire and update knowledge in the relevant engineering field, also independently but primarily under guidance, using established educational resources and learning tools; AP2 – Ability to identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation through the main available resources (libraries, databases, manuals, and digital sources), selecting relevant information with the support of provided guidance; AP3 – Ability to learn through project-based and Problem Based Learning (PBL) methodologies, applying acquired knowledge and tools to case studies and real engineering problems of appropriate complexity.

## Contents

- Design of components for turbomachinery: Gas turbine blades cooling - Energy systems modeling and characterization: Energy storage systems - Theory and pratical examples

## Prerequisites

- Knowledge of basic energy systems operation - Basic knowledge of thermodynamics, fluid-dynamics and heat transfer theory

## Teaching methods

Theoretical lessons and practical examples Group practice on assigned projects with periodic revisions

## Assessment methods

Oral exam aimed at reviewing the students’ project solution. Attention will be paid to the assessment of the choices made by the students for the adopted methodologies (CA4), of the critic evaluation of the results, based on the provided theoretical knowledge (CC4), of the ability to interpret results and work effectively in a team (AG1–5), of the quality of the presentation of the results (AC1–3), and of the ability to make effective use of the knowledge acquired during the course, as well as to identify and select relevant literature supporting the assigned project (AP1–3)..

## Extended programme

Gas turbine components cooling • Recall of heat transfer theory • Blade cooling theory • Examples of conventional and cooling system • Simplified design method for GT cooled components Energy storage systems (EES) • CAES (Compressed Energy Storage Systems): principle, examples and efficiency improvement methods • Modelling/verification methods for EES.
