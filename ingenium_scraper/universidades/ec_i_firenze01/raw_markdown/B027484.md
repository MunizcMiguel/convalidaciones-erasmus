## Partition: IMPIANTI INDUSTRIALI E SERVIZI DI STABILIMENTO (Componente Fittizio B)

### Language

Italian

### Learning objectives

The course aims at providing students with the basic knowledge required for a correct analysis and design of production systems and plant service facilities.  
Knowledge and understanding. By the end of the course, students will know: manufacturing systems and production processes, understanding the advantages and limitations of process and plant design choices in different application contexts (CC6); current topics concerning the organization and management of the technical factors of production (CC7); the broader multidisciplinary context of engineering, with particular emphasis on problem solving, starting from the problem to identify its causes and possible countermeasures (CC8); the techniques and methods applicable to the design of industrial plants and service facilities together with their limitations, including awareness of the non-technical implications of engineering practice (CC10).  
Applying knowledge and understanding. By the end of the course, students will be able to: select and use appropriate equipment, tools and methods for the design and production of goods and services, including calculation tools for the sizing of service facilities and logistics systems (CA7); combine theory and practice to solve multidisciplinary engineering problems, taking into account both technical and non-technical constraints and collaborating with the professionals typically involved in manufacturing companies (CA8).  
Making judgements. By the end of the course, students will be able to: analyse data and information (demand series, plant performance) using established methods and tools, interpreting results and drawing consistent conclusions (AG1); compare alternative solutions to plant engineering problems — location, layout and sizing alternatives — contributing to technically justified decisions in compliance with industry standards and procedures (AG2); work collaboratively and effectively within a team, recognizing the interdisciplinary implications of the problem addressed (AG3); organize their work while meeting assigned deadlines, managing available time and resources (AG4); recognize personal knowledge limitations, identifying appropriate information sources and guided learning opportunities (AG5).  
Communication skills. Students will be able to: communicate information, problems and solutions in the field of industrial plants to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, using appropriate technical language (AC1); present problems, analyses and results clearly and systematically through written reports in standard formats (AC2); graphically represent and communicate technical data, results and solutions through diagrams, graphs and tables (AC3).  
Learning skills. Students will be able to: acquire and update their knowledge in the field of industrial plants, also independently, using established educational resources (AP1); identify and consult bibliographic sources and technical documentation, selecting relevant information (AP2); learn through problem solving applied to real cases of appropriate complexity (AP3); understand and interpret the fundamental principles of design, production and maintenance processes, in compliance with quality, safety and reliability requirements and with reference to the main technical and environmental standards (AP4).

### Assessment methods

The student's assessment is based on an examination test which consists of performing a written assignment. Seven exam sessions are scheduled during the academic year.  
The task consists of 10 questions covering the entire programme. The questions are of three types: simple theoretical questions (with relative weight of 1), theoretical questions of average difficulty (with relative weight of 2), articulated exercises (with relative weight of 3). For each section of the reference book (except for the introductory chapter) there is one question. The questions are different for each exam session and are different for each assignment within the same session. The sequential order of the questions is also casualized, to make each job unique.  
In order to pass the exam with the minimum score, students must have acquired a good knowledge of the subject as a whole and must be able to model an industrial engineering problem (CC6, CC7, CC8, CC10). . In order to obtain a high mark, knowledge is required to be very good and comprehensive in all subjects covered. In order to have the highest grade with honors, the student must combine previous knowledge with outstanding ability to manage the time factor, producing an excellent result in a short time (CA7, CA8, AG4).  
From the perspective of transversal skills, it is necessary for the student to be able toexpress technical concepts in written form (AC1, AC2), also through diagrams, graphs and tables (AC3). It is also necessary to understand the technical standards (AP4) and the scientific documentation proposed in class (AP2).
