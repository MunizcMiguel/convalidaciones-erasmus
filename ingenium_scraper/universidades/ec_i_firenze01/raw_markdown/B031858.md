## Partition: B031859-A-L

### Language

italian

### Learning objectives

General course objective:  
Provide the basic concepts for understanding the operation and use of the most important turbomachines.  
Knowledge and Understanding:  
Knowledge of the laws of physics (mechanics and thermodynamics) relevant to the field of industrial engineering, and understanding of the role of such laws in the formulation of models representative of tangible reality (CC2); knowledge of thermodynamics and fluid dynamic phenomena, as well as the models for representing them; knowledge of systems and machines for energy production and conversion, with particular reference to turbomachinery (CC4).  
Applying Knowledge and Understanding:  
The ability to apply one's knowledge to solve turbomachinery-related problems by interpreting and using the laws that govern them (CA2). The ability to choose and apply appropriate analytical, modeling, and experimental methods to study and design fluid machines, evaluating their performance (CA4).  
Making Judgements:  
Ability to analyze data and information, compare different solutions, and arrive at applicative engineering solutions (AG1, AG2).  
Communication Skills:  
Ability to communicate information, ideas, problems, and solutions within the engineering field, presenting them clearly according to standard methods, and making use of graphs and tables where necessary (AC1, AC2, AC3).  
Learning Skills:  
Ability to acquire knowledge in the area of turbomachinery. Ability to learn through practical exercises on real case studies. Ability to understand and interpret the basic principles of turbomachinery design, their operation, and their use (AP1, AP3, AP4).

### Contents

fundamentals of turbomachinery

### Prerequisites

fluid dynamics

### Teaching methods

The course is taught mainly through lectures and exercises. Attendance is strongly recommended because the topics covered and discussed in the classroom can be assimilated more easily and are the only ones required to pass the final exam.

### Assessment methods

Oral examination.  
  
The student is asked to orally answer one or more questions to the end of assessing his/her ability to explain the course subjects (cc2, cc4).  
  
The student must demonstrate his/her ability to discuss the operation, the main issues, and the preliminary design criteria of the different kinds of turbomachinery (ca2, ca4).

## Partition: B031859-M-Z / Partition: B031860-A-L

### Language

Italiano

### Learning objectives

General course objective:  
Ability to study, describe, and predict the physical behaviour of fluids.  
Knowledge and Understanding:  
Knowledge of the laws of physics (mechanics and thermodynamics) relevant to the field of industrial fluid dynamics, and understanding of the role of such laws in the formulation of models representative of tangible reality (CC2).  
Applying Knowledge and Understanding: The ability to apply one's knowledge to solve fluid dynamics problems, in successive applicative engineering courses (CA2), by interpreting and using the laws that govern them.  
Autonomy of Judgment:  
Ability to analyze data and information using known tools, compare different solutions, and arrive at applicative solutions (AG1, AG2).  
Communication Skills:  
Ability to communicate information, ideas, problems, and solutions within the engineering field, presenting them clearly, according to standard methods, and making use of graphs and tables where necessary (AC1, AC2, AC3).  
Learning Skills:  
Ability to acquire knowledge in fluid mechanics. Ability to learn through practical exercises on real case studies. Ability to understand and interpret the basic principles of aerodynamic design (AP1, AP3, AP4).

### Contents

fundamentals of fluid dynamics

### Prerequisites

mathematics and physics

### Teaching methods

The course is taught mainly through lectures and exercises. Attendance is strongly recommended because the topics covered and discussed in the classroom can be assimilated more easily and are the only ones required to pass the final exam.

### Assessment methods

Oral examination.  
  
The student must solve a brief engineering problem involving the course subjects. The student is then asked to orally answer one or more questions to the end of assessing his/her ability to explain notions of fluid dynamics (CC2, CA2).  
  
The student must demonstrate his/her ability to discuss the course subjects and to apply methods and notions to the solutions of simplified engineering problems.  
  
Assessment Criteria and Grading Rubric  
  
Fail (Insufficient)  
Negative evaluation due to serious conceptual errors, fragmentary or non-existent understanding of the subject matter, inability to apply knowledge, major gaps, and a lack of independence.  
  
18–22  
Sufficient or more than sufficient evaluation due to elementary knowledge of the topics, the presence of some errors, the ability to handle only simple or guided tasks, and a basic level of independence.  
  
23–25  
Satisfactory evaluation due to a satisfactory understanding of the topics, the ability to correctly apply knowledge to familiar contexts, and the ability to complete exercises independently with some inaccuracies; demonstrates a satisfactory level of independence and operational competence.  
  
26–28  
Good or very good evaluation due to a solid theoretical understanding of the discipline, the ability to connect concepts, solve problems, and provide critical analysis; ability to perform complex tasks independently, precisely, and effectively, with a good level of independence and operational competence.  
  
29–30 and Honors (Lode)Excellent or outstanding evaluation due to complete, in-depth, and well-structured knowledge; the ability to operate independently and flexibly even in complex or new contexts; appropriate, precise, and articulate use of specialized terminology; demonstrates critical thinking, originality, and rigor in both presentation and application.  
Honors (Laude) is awarded for work or performance distinguished by brilliance and originality, exceeding standard curricular expectations.

### Extended programme

INTRODUCTION: Definition of fluid, integral and differential analysis methods.  
BASIC CONCEPTS: Flow as a continuum, flow and stress field, viscosity and Newtonian fluid.  
FLUID STATICS: Basic equations and buoyancy.  
BASIC EQUATIONS AND CONTROL VOLUME METHOD: Integral equations of continuity, momentum and energy.  
DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS FOR A FLUID FLOW: Continuity Equation, flow field, momentum end energy equation.  
INCOMPRESSIBLE INVISCID FLOW: Basic Equations , Euler Equations, Bernoulii Equation, total quantities.  
INTERNAL INCOMPRESSIBLE VISCOUS FLOW: Laminar and turbulent flows in pipes, losses and Moody diagram.  
EXTERNAL INCOMPRESSIBLE VISCOUS FLOW: Concept of laminar and turbulent boundary layer, transition, integral equation of the boundary layer, lift and drag.  
COMPRESSIBLE FLOW: Sound speed and Mach number. Basic equation of one-dimensional compressible flow, Fanno and Rayleigh line.

### Reference texts

Introduction to Fluid Mechanics, Fox and McDonald's, John Wiley & Sons
