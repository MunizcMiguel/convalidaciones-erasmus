## Learning objectives

In the context of Mechanical Engineering, the efficiency and reliability of many operating systems depend on the correct selection and integration of drive systems. The course provides a systematic overview of the operating principles, operating characteristics, essential components, and basic and medium-complexity circuits for electric, oleohydraulic, and pneumatic drive technologies. The perspective is characteristic of machine construction, focusing on operating principles, construction solutions, and system integration. The physical principles on which the drives base their operation, together with the characteristic quantities, will be introduced, while advanced thermal and fluid dynamic aspects do not constitute the primary element of study. The integration between various types of systems, particularly between the electrical and hydraulic sectors, will be considered, and the differences and peculiarities that guide technological choice will be analyzed, especially in applications where their uses are partially overlapping.  
  
The course aims to consolidate a systematic knowledge of the key aspects necessary for the design and analysis of drive systems (cc3, cc6). Students will acquire in-depth knowledge of the principles of electrotechnics that govern electric drives and of the control methods used in industrial plants (cc5). The capacity for mechanical study of components and systems will be developed, including the understanding of the application context, correct sizing, and graphical representation of systems, essential for technical documentation.  
Upon completion of the course, students will be able to choose and use appropriate components (ca5), fully understanding their limitations and potential applications in electrical and fluidic fields. They will be capable of identifying, formulating, and solving complex problems in the design of drive systems (ca6). This includes defining specifications, technical constraints, but also considering social, health and safety, environmental, and commercial aspects. Students will be able to create complete engineering projects, appropriate to the developed level of knowledge and understanding. The course will also allow for the development of the ability to choose and apply appropriate analytical and modeling methods (ca3) to simulate the behavior of components and systems, in order to predict and improve their performance.  
  
The course encourages the development of the following essential professional competencies:  
- Teamwork: development of the ability to work in a coordinated manner on complex projects (ct2).  
- Technical Communication: ability to develop a rigorous technical discussion, providing adequate arguments for design choices (ct3).  
- Graphical Representation: acquisition of proficiency in graphical representation and communication (drafting of diagrams, graphs, and tables) (ct4), a fundamental language in fluidics.  
- Technical Standards: understanding and application of relevant technical standards (ct5), particularly those related to the safety and installation of systems.

## Contents

Generation and distribution of compressed air. Air treatment units. Linear and rotational pneumatic actuators. Directional, regulation and safety valves.  
  
Oleodynamic pumps and motors. Cilynders. Valves. Proportional oleohydraulics. Pressure and flow control components. Tanks, pipes, seals. Hydraulic systems design.  
  
Electric servomechanisms. Motor-load coupling. Motion laws for cyclic duties. Thermal problem for electric actuators. Electric actuators. Electronic converters. Transducers

## Prerequisites

Basics of physics, basics of mechanical and electrical engineering

## Teaching methods

Classroom lessons, laboratory activities (also via dedicated hydraulic and electric system teaching equipment) and educational visits to factories.

## Assessment methods

Oral exam focusing on the topics of the course.  
Questions regarding the fundamentals of fluid power components and electric actuators.  
Then, the discussion of a practical problem concerning the design or analysis of a complex system of actuators.  
The exam will assess the assimilation of the required levels of knowledge (in particular, cc3, cc5 and cc6) and skills (ca3, ca5 and ca6) proposed in the course.
