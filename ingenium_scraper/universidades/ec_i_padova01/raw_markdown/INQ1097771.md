## Prerequisites

Fundamentals of Computer Science (mandatory prerequisite) In particular, students are expected to have already acquired the following knowledge and skills: * Ability to write simple procedural programs in any programming language. * Ability to trace and debug small programs. * Knowledge of basic data structures, such as arrays and lists. * Knowledge of the principles of object-oriented programming, including abstract classes and inheritance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to (Intended Learning Outcomes – ILOs):

1. Describe fundamental algorithmic paradigms and data structures. (Knowledge)

2. Analyze the computational complexity of algorithms. (Application)

3. Design efficient algorithms using pseudocode. (Application)

4. Compare algorithmic solutions based on their performance and constraints. (Judgement)

5. Implement algorithms in the C programming language. (Application)

6. Debug programs written in C. (Application)

## Examination methods

A mandatory written examination, consisting of three parts: 1. Theory questions (assessment of ILOs 1–2) 2. Algorithm analysis and design in pseudocode (assessment of ILOs 2–4) 3. Implementation of algorithms in C (assessment of ILOs 5–6) Oral follow-up assessment. The instructor reserves the right to require an additional oral assessment when necessary to verify that the submitted written examination accurately reflects the student’s individual knowledge and competencies. Use of Generative AI Tools * Permitted for individual study and for self-assessment of exercises completed outside the classroom. * Not permitted during the final examination.

## Assessment methods

The assessment will be based on the student’s understanding of the theoretical topics covered in the course and on their ability to apply them in the design of efficient algorithms and data structures.

## Contents

* Introduction to programming in the C language.

* Algorithm specification: computational model, computational problem, algorithm.

* Algorithm analysis: elements of combinatorics and asymptotic analysis, mathematical induction, complexity analysis, and correctness analysis.

* Trees: definitions, properties, and traversals. Binary trees.

* Priority queues and heaps.

* Maps and hash tables. Dictionaries.

* Search trees: definitions and special cases. Binary search trees; (2,4)-trees.

* Graphs: definitions, properties, data structures for graph representation, graph traversals (BFS and DFS), shortest paths, minimum spanning trees, and topological sorting.
