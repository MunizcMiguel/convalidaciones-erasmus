## Partition: TEORIA DEI CIRCUITI (A)

### Prerequisites

The course assumes basic knowledge of mathematical analysis and linear algebra.

### Learning objectives

1. Knowledge and understanding

Understand the basic properties of circuits with time-varying voltages and currents.

Know the basic methods for the analysis of linear resistive circuits of two-terminal elements) and introduction to circuits with ideal controlled sources.

Know the basic methods for phasor analysis of two-terminal elements circuits in sinusoidal steady state.

2. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding

Apply knowledge and methods to solve introductory linear circuits.

3. Independent judgment

Develop autonomous reasoning skills with reference to the introductory circuits indicated.

4. Communication skills

Correctly use the technical terminology of the field.

5. Learning skills

Develop independent study skills.

### Innovative teaching methods: Teaching and learning strategies

- Use of technologies for teaching.

### Examination methods

The exam is in person, in written form. It consists of a section with exercises and another one for the theory. Should there be any interpretive doubts regarding what is reported by the candidate in the written test, the professor may require the candidate to take an oral exam.

### Assessment methods

The exercises are intended to verify the level of skill acquired by the student to solve a linear circuit in DC steady state and in sinusoidal steady state.

The theory questions are intended to verify the level of knowledge acquired by the student on circuits.

### Contents

Introduction. Current and voltage.

Time-varying voltages and currents. Kirchhoff’s laws. N-terminal elements, two-terminal elements. Power. Topology of two-terminal element networks. Adynamic two-terminal elements.

Resistive two-terminal element circuits. Basic analysis methods and theorems.

Introduction to linear resistive circuits with ideal controlled sources.

Circuits with time-invariant voltages and currents: some first-order cases and introduction to second-order circuits.

Two-terminal element circuits in sinusoidal steady state. Phasor method. Basic analysis methods and theorems. Series RLC circuit and parallel RLC circuit in sinusoidal steady state.

The course consists of lectures in classroom where the topics of the course are presented.

## Partition: TEORIA DEI CIRCUITI (B)

### Prerequisites

Fundamentals of Mathematical Analysis, Linear Algebra and Physics

### Learning objectives

The main goal of the course is to provide the following knowledge: 1) the main properties of electrical circuits based on N-terminal elements 2) the main analysis methods of linear DC circuits containing ideal passive resistors, ideal independent voltage and current sources and, with reference only to Thévenin's theorem, also controlled sources 3) the main analysis methods of AC circuits containing ideal passive two-terminal elements (resistors, capacitors and inductors) and ideal independent voltage and current sources, through the use of phasors and the symbolic circuit 4) the analysis of some time-varying circuits, i.e., constant voltage generator with ohmic-capacitive, ohmic-inductive, and ohmic-inductive-capacitive load At the end of the course the student will be able to solve: i) DC circuits containing ideal passive resistors and ideal independent voltage and current sources ii) AC circuits containing ideal passive two-terminal elements (resistors, capacitors and inductors) and ideal independent voltage and current sources.

1. Knowledge and understanding

Understand the operation of key electrical components

Know the main techniques for analyzing electrical networks

2. Ability to apply knowledge and understanding

Solve electrical networks under steady-state and sinusoidal conditions

Solve electrical networks containing controlled sources

3. Making judgments

Critically evaluate the performance of electrical networks

Self-assess using optional exercises provided during the course

4. Communication skills

Use technical terminology correctly

Correctly use circuit diagrams

Clearly present procedures and results

5. Learning skills

Integrate theoretical and practical knowledge

Develop problem-solving skills

Utilize supporting materials (slides, exercises)

### Examination methods

Written exam with exercises and theory questions. Oral exam (supplementing the written exam) only at the teacher's request. The exercises are used to verify the level of expected skills achieved by the student and the theory questions are used to verify the level of acquired knowledge. The evaluation method is both formative and summative. The use of any artificial intelligence tool to carry out the test is prohibited.

### Assessment methods

Evaluation based on the completeness of the test, the severity of the errors made and the mastery of the course contents.

### Contents

- Introduction: electrical quantities (charge, current, voltage, work, power), measuring instruments, conventions, circuit model, electric gate, two-terminal elements, N-terminal elements and M-port elements, energy behavior of two-terminal elements. - Electrical components: constitutive equation, I–V curve, zero-th order ideal two-terminal elements (resistor, short circuit, open circuit, switch, diverter, diode, voltage and current sources), dynamic two-terminal elements (capacitor, inductor). - Topology of electrical networks: graph theory and Kirchhoff's laws, power conservation, series and parallel of two-terminal elements. - DC circuits: resistive circuits, independent equations of linear circuits, methods of analysis (superposition of effects, mesh and nodal analysis), theorems (substitution, Thévenin, Norton), zero-order two-port linear elements, ideal transformer, controlled sources, hints on analysis of linear networks with N-terminal elements. - AC circuits: sinusoidal electrical quantities, phasors, impedances and admittances, powers, series and parallel resonance, symbolic circuit, analysis methods (superposition of effects), theorems (substitution, Thévenin, Norton). - Time-varying networks: critical instant and initial conditions, variables at the critical instant, independent linear network equations, analysis examples of simple circuits (RC, RL, RLC).
