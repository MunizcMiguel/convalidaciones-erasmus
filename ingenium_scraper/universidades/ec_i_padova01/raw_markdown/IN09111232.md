## Learning objectives

The course provides a fundamental understanding of the physical principles underlying the operation of the main semiconductor devices: p-n junction diodes, bipolar transistors and field-effect transistors. Furthermore, it aims to familiarise students with the most common analogue electronic circuits, including those built using operational amplifiers (e.g. filters, summing amplifiers, integrators, differentiators), transistors (single-stage and multi-stage amplifiers) and diodes (rectifiers, limiters, non-linear transfer function circuits).

By the end of the course, students should be able to analyse simple circuits using operational amplifiers, diodes, bipolar transistors and field-effect transistors. In particular, they must be able to determine the operating point of an amplifier and calculate its fundamental parameters for small-signal, centre-band conditions, including voltage gain, current gain, input and output resistances, and plot the frequency response (Bode plots) of linear circuits.

## Examination methods

The examination consists of two distinct stages: a written paper and an oral examination. Admission to the second stage is conditional upon passing the first.

The written paper assesses whether the student has acquired the fundamental skills required to analyse simple circuits and solve problems relating to the physical behaviour of semiconductor devices. It consists of solving a number of exercises, including equations and numerical values.

The oral examination assesses whether the student has developed the ability to present topics covered in lectures in a structured manner and can handle simple analytical or design problems. It consists of a discussion of the topics covered in the theoretical lectures, the completion of exercises, or the analysis of one or more circuits from those presented in lectures or covered in practical sessions.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of students’ preparation is based on evaluating their level of knowledge of the course’s theoretical topics and their ability to apply the analytical techniques covered in lectures to specific problems independently. As this is a technology and engineering course, passing the exam requires the student to have acquired a sufficient ability to synthesise theoretical knowledge, thereby being able to translate it into procedures for the analysis and/or design of the fundamental electronic circuits covered during the course. The assessment considers not only the technical correctness of the procedures, but also the clarity and structure of the oral presentation and the performance of calculations.

## Contents

Fundamental electronic device physics: diodes and transistors (bipolar and field effect ones). Diode applications (rectifilers, non linear networks). Single transistor amplifiers: bias networks and small signal analysis. Multiple stage amplifiers, differential amplifiers. Operational amplifiers: inverting and non-inverting configurations, diferential and instrumentation amplifiers, Miller integrator, basic filter structures. Non ideal characteristics of the operational amplifiers: input and output impedances, offset voltage, offset and bias currents, slew-rate limitations, limited bandwidth. Bode diagrams. Application examples of the operational amplifiers.

## Teaching methods

The course is delivered through lectures and exercises. In the lectures, the theoretical skills are provided and the methodologies for approaching the analysis and design problems that are the subject of the examination are illustrated. The exercises, on the other hand, provide examples of these problems and allow the student to check his or her degree of confidence in the topics presented. In order to train students to solve problems related to the topics covered in the course, exercises (homework) are provided weekly via the course's moodle site, the solutions to which are, however, published the following week. In addition to contacting the course instructor, students with disabilities, Specific Learning Disorders (SLD), Special Educational Needs (SEN), and other health conditions can reach out to the Student Services Office - Inclusion Unit to receive more information about opportunities to access teaching with specific support and tools. Finally, the use of generative artificial intelligence tools (e.g. ChatGPT) is permitted as long as it is accompanied by a critical evaluation of the information obtained with such tools.
