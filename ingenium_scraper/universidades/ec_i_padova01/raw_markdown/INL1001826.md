## Prerequisites

There are no mandatory prerequisite courses. However, students are strongly encouraged to have acquired the fundamental knowledge provided by the courses **Fundamentals of Computer Science** and **Fundamentals of Electronics** (the latter may be attended concurrently).

Students are also encouraged to have completed an introductory course on digital systems, such as **Digital Circuits** or **Computer Architecture**, which provides the basic concepts of digital system organization and hardware description languages.

In particular, students are expected to be able to:

- apply the principles of Boolean algebra to the synthesis and analysis of simple logic circuits;
- describe the general architecture of a digital system, including registers, buses, memory, arithmetic logic units (ALUs), and interconnections;
- use a hardware description language (preferably VHDL) to describe simple combinational and sequential circuits;
- explain the operation of the MOS transistor as a digital switch and the fundamental principles of CMOS logic;
- analyze simple digital circuits from a functional perspective, with familiarity with the basic concepts of signal timing.

## Learning objectives

The course provides the theoretical and practical knowledge required to understand, design, and verify digital systems based on CMOS technology and programmable logic devices (FPGAs), integrating circuit-level, architectural, and methodological aspects of digital design.

**1. Knowledge and understanding**

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- understand the operation of CMOS digital circuits, with particular emphasis on the static and dynamic characteristics of logic gates;
- understand the main methodologies for the design of combinational and sequential logic circuits;
- understand the characteristics of the main logic families and digital architectures;
- understand the principles of hardware description using VHDL and the FPGA design flow;
- understand the fundamentals of CMOS integrated circuit fabrication and their implications for digital circuit design.

**2. Applying knowledge and understanding**

Students will be able to:

- design combinational and sequential logic circuits of moderate complexity;
- analyze propagation delay, power consumption, and silicon area in common digital architectures;
- describe digital systems using VHDL and verify their correct operation through simulation;
- implement and validate simple digital systems on FPGA devices;
- compare alternative design solutions and select the most appropriate one according to given performance, area, and power requirements.

**3. Making judgements**

Students will be able to:

- critically evaluate different circuit- and architecture-level design solutions by considering performance, silicon area, and power consumption;
- identify and assess the main design trade-offs involved in the implementation of digital systems.

**4. Communication skills**

Students will be able to:

- use the technical terminology of digital system design appropriately;
- describe and document digital circuits using schematics, timing diagrams, and VHDL code;
- collaborate effectively in the design and verification activities carried out during the laboratory sessions.

**5. Learning skills**

Students will be able to:

- independently approach the study of more advanced digital architectures;
- use technical documentation, datasheets, and CAD tool manuals to further develop their knowledge of digital design methodologies and technologies;
- continuously update their skills in a field characterized by rapid technological evolution.

## Examination methods

The final assessment consists of a **single individual written examination** lasting **2 hours and 30 minutes**, designed to assess the achievement of the intended learning outcomes of the course.

The examination is divided into two main sections.

**1. Theoretical section**

This section includes multiple-choice and/or open-ended questions aimed at assessing students' understanding of the theoretical concepts covered during the course, including:

- operation of CMOS circuits and the main logic families;
- static and dynamic characteristics of logic gates, propagation delays, power consumption, and technology scaling;
- digital system architecture, including memories, registers, datapaths, and arithmetic logic units (ALUs);
- principles of hardware description using VHDL;
- topics introduced during the laboratory sessions, including FPGA devices, the digital design flow, simulation, synthesis, and basic timing constraints.

**2. Design section**

This section consists of design-oriented problems intended to assess the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to the design and analysis of digital systems. The exercises may include:

- synthesis and analysis of combinational and sequential logic circuits;
- transistor-level design of CMOS logic gates;
- analysis and comparison of alternative digital design solutions.

Successful completion of the examination requires adequate preparation in both the theoretical and the design-oriented aspects of the course.

### Formative Assessment

Throughout the course, formative assessment activities are provided to support learning and help students monitor their progress. These activities do not contribute directly to the final grade and include:

- exercises solved and discussed during lectures and laboratory sessions;
- sample examination questions with commented solutions made available through the Moodle platform;
- classroom discussion of common mistakes and recommended design and problem-solving strategies.

### Use of Artificial Intelligence

The use of **generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools is not permitted during the written examination**.

During laboratory activities, AI tools may be used to support the design process, for example to generate VHDL code, develop testbenches, or explore alternative design solutions. Students are required to explicitly disclose the use of AI, indicating which parts of their work were AI-generated and which were independently developed or modified. In all cases, assessment will be based on the student's ability to understand, justify, and critically adapt the proposed solutions.

## Assessment methods

The written examination is designed to assess the achievement of the intended learning outcomes of the course. The evaluation is based on the following criteria:

- **Knowledge and understanding of the theoretical concepts**, including CMOS circuits, digital system architectures, memories, arithmetic logic units (ALUs), programmable logic devices, and the fundamental principles of digital design.
- **Ability to apply the acquired knowledge** through the synthesis and analysis of combinational and sequential logic circuits, as well as the solution of digital design problems.
- **Circuit-level analysis and design skills**, assessed through exercises involving transistor-level CMOS logic design and the evaluation of key design metrics such as propagation delay, power consumption, and silicon area.
- **Understanding of hardware description languages**, with particular emphasis on reading, interpreting, and analyzing VHDL descriptions.
- **Knowledge of FPGA-based design methodologies**, including the fundamental concepts of synthesis, simulation, and timing verification.
- **Clarity of presentation**, appropriate use of technical terminology, and completeness and correctness of the explanations and design solutions provided.

The maximum possible score is **30/30**, and the examination is passed with a minimum grade of **18/30**.

**Honours (30 cum laude)** may be awarded to students who, in addition to achieving the maximum score, demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the course topics, rigorous technical reasoning, the ability to establish connections among different concepts covered in the course, and exceptional clarity in presenting their solutions.

## Contents

The course is organized into the following thematic modules.

**1. Fundamentals of Digital Systems (4 hours)**

- Digital signals and their representation.
- Noise margins and robustness of digital systems.
- Representation of numerical data and complex information.

**2. CMOS Digital Circuits (18 hours)**

- The MOS transistor as the fundamental building block of digital electronics.
- The CMOS inverter: static and dynamic characteristics.
- Propagation delay and power consumption.
- CMOS logic gates based on static logic.
- Introduction to dynamic logic and pass-transistor logic.

**3. Memory Elements (6 hours)**

- Latches and flip-flops.
- Registers and shift registers.
- SRAM, DRAM, and non-volatile memory technologies.

**4. Fundamental Building Blocks of Digital Systems (8 hours)**

- Basic arithmetic blocks of an Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU).
- Shifters.
- Adders.
- Comparators.
- Multipliers.

**5. Programmable Digital Systems (12 hours)**

- Programmable logic devices.
- FPGA architecture.
- VHDL description of combinational logic.
- VHDL description of sequential logic.
- Timing constraints.
- Synthesis, implementation, and verification of digital systems on FPGA devices.

The order in which the topics are presented may be adjusted to ensure effective coordination between lectures and laboratory activities.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of **72 hours** of teaching activities, organized as follows:

- **Lectures (48 hours)** covering the theoretical foundations of digital systems electronics, including CMOS technology, the design of combinational and sequential circuits, memory elements, digital architectures, and the fundamental building blocks of digital systems.
- **Applied lectures (14 hours)** devoted to digital design using hardware description languages, the FPGA-based digital design flow, and the engineering analysis of a modern FPGA development board through the study of its technical documentation and hardware architecture.
- **Laboratory sessions (10 hours)** dedicated to the description of digital systems in VHDL, functional simulation, synthesis, implementation, and experimental verification on FPGA development boards.

Lectures are complemented by guided exercises and classroom discussions aimed at the design and analysis of combinational and sequential digital circuits.

The course adopts teaching methodologies that promote a progressive and application-oriented learning process. In particular, students will engage in:

- guided problem-solving activities in digital system design;
- analysis and comparison of alternative design solutions;
- simulation and verification of VHDL-based digital circuits;
- development and validation of simple digital systems on FPGA devices.

### Use of Artificial Intelligence

During lectures and individual exercises, students are expected to develop their design skills independently.

During laboratory activities, the use of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools is permitted as a design aid, for example to generate VHDL templates, develop testbenches, or obtain implementation suggestions. Students are required to explicitly disclose the use of AI, clearly distinguishing AI-generated content from the parts that have been independently developed or modified. Assessment will always be based on the student's ability to understand, justify, and critically adapt the proposed solutions.
