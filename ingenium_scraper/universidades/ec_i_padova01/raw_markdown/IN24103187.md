## Learning objectives

Knowledge, skills, and competencies to be acquired: By the end of the course, the student must acquire basic notions of electromagnetism and optics based on the experimental scientific method; must be able to tackle problems related to the topics covered, correctly setting up and solving a physical situation proposed in the form of an exercise, through the application of appropriate physical laws, demonstrating the ability to solve algebraically and numerically proposed problems; must also be able to provide a critical description of the physical phenomena considered, formulating the laws in a mathematically correct manner.

## Examination methods

Summative assessment (i.e., obtained entirely during the exam) on a 30-point scale. The exam is structured as follows: - 100% laboratory attendance (an absence in one of the lab-sessions will be recovered in a recovery-session) - one ongoing written test (1 written problem in electrostatics) - written exams during the official sessions (3 exercises/problems to do) - oral exam There will be no formative assessment (i.e., students will not be evaluated during the learning process, except for the ongoing tests). However, exercises and questions will be continuously provided to allow students to test themselves. The use of tablets, smartphones, smartwatches, IA, etc. is prohibited during exams. The COVID-19 emergency has forced the use of telematic tools for remote assessment. Following the University's guidelines and government decrees, in case of recurring emergency situations, these tools may also be used.

All communication and interconnection devices (telephones, smartphones, smartwatches, smart glasses, tablets, notebooks, PCs, etc.) are strictly prohibited during exams.

All AI tools are strictly prohibited during exams.

Electronic calculators (even programmable ones, but not connected to the internet) are allowed during exams.

## Assessment methods

Ability to complete exercises correctly and accurately, using the methodologies learned during the course. Completeness of exposition during the oral exam (typically 2 fairly open questions on one of the course topics). The student must state the physical principles, demonstrate the theses with mathematical steps, provide graphs and examples.

## Contents

Electric charge. Coulomb's law. Electrostatic field and potential. Superposition principle. Charge density. Work of electric force. Electrostatic energy and electrostatic potential. Gradient of electrostatic potential. Electric dipole. Electrostatic field and potential of continuous charge distributions. Elementary charge and its quantization. Flux of electrostatic field. Gauss's theorem. Circulation, curl, and divergence. Applications of Gauss's law. Electrostatic equilibrium. Electrostatic induction. Hollow conductors. Capacity. Capacitor. Capacity calculation. Series and parallel connections of capacitors. Electrostatic energy. Electrostatic pressure. Polarization of a dielectric. Polarization charge and its surface density. Relationship between polarization and electric field: electric susceptibility. Dielectrics in capacitors. The dielectric induction vector. Gauss's law in dielectrics. Electric conduction in solids. Electromotive force. Electric current and electric current density. Charge conservation. Ohm's laws (first and second). Resistance. Resistivity and conductivity, Ohm's law of conduction. Classical theory of conduction. Joule effect. Ohmic conductors. Resistors in series and parallel. EMF generators and internal resistance. Stationary currents. Kirchhoff's laws. Electromotive field. Ammeter, voltmeter, ohmmeter. Charging and discharging of a capacitor. Definition of magnetic field. Lorentz force. Magnetic force on a current-carrying conductor: II elementary law of Laplace. Motion of charges in magnetic field. Hall effect. Mechanical moment on a current-carrying loop. Magnetic moment. Magnetic energy. Moving coil galvanometer. Magnetic field produced by a current. I elementary law of Laplace. Applications of the first law of Laplace. Interaction between current-carrying wires. Magnetic field flux and Gauss's law. Flux linked with a closed line. Ampère's theorem. Field of an infinite straight solenoid and a toroidal solenoid. Brief overview of the origins of the magnetic dipole. Polarization of the magnetic dipole. Amperian currents. Magnetization density vector. The magnetizing field H. Magnetic susceptibility. Relationship between B, H, and M. Paramagnetic, diamagnetic, and ferromagnetic substances. Hysteresis cycle. Curie's law and critical temperature. Time-varying magnetic fields. Electromagnetic induction. Faraday's and Lenz's laws. Alternator. Self-induction. Inductance. RL circuits. Magnetic energy. Mutual induction. Alternating electromotive forces. Impedance. Rotating vector method and generalized Ohm's law. Ideal oscillator. Electric and magnetic energy of the oscillator. Series RCL circuit: damped and forced oscillations. Resonance. Transformer. Time-varying electric fields. Displacement current. Ampère-Maxwell law. Maxwell's equations. Waves and vibrations in matter. Wave equation. Harmonic waves. Wave number, frequency, and wavelength. Electromagnetic waves. Derivation of electromagnetic waves from the solution of Maxwell's equations in vacuum. Plane waves. Polarization. Refractive index. Energy and intensity of electromagnetic wave. Radiation pressure. Huygens' principle. Reflection. Refraction. Fresnel coefficients. Polarization by reflection. (Brief overview of anisotropic materials, dichroism, and polarizers). Scattering. Interference of two point sources. Interference of N sources. Standing waves. Fraunhofer diffraction from a slit. Diffraction grating.
