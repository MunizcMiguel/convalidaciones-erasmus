### Prerequisites

For understanding the course, it is essential to be familiar with differential and integral calculus in one variable and with linear algebra (matrices, determinants, vector spaces).

### Learning objectives

COGNITIVE SKILLS Students should be able to: • Recall, recognize, and contextualize the mathematical tools developed during the course • Present definitions and statements, using mathematical language consciously and correctly • Argue the proofs indicated in the detailed course syllabus • Solve problems of similar type and difficulty to those covered in class and proposed for practice during the course, possibly with some new elements for higher grades • Explain the solution of problems of similar type and difficulty to those covered in class and proposed for practice during the course, using appropriate terminology and making precise reference to theorems from the course • Recognize the usefulness of applying some of the course tools in other disciplines, such as physics PRACTICAL AND TRANSFERABLE SKILLS The following will be developed: • Critical and analytical thinking, including the ability to engage with artificial intelligence • Problem-solving skills, or the ability to explore and present possible strategies • The ability to compute relevant quantities of the mathematical objects introduced • Optionally, evaluation skills and the ability to collaborate effectively in a team

### Examination methods

The exam can be taken in two different ways:

1. **“Follower” mode**: The ongoing activities, carried out mainly on online platforms from home, make it possible to earn a bonus. This bonus may be used to increase the final grade and/or to supplement the final in-class test by reducing its scope, exclusively in the winter exam session (in both exam sittings, also for those who submit but do not pass the first sitting). We limit the validity of the bonus to the two sittings of the first session in order to encourage daily study and promote timely completion of the exam, without procrastination.
2. **“Traditional” mode**: This consists of taking the final in-class test in one of the official exam sittings, without any addition or reduction deriving from the bonus earned through the ongoing activities, as well as completing a peer review on first integrals before the test.

The final in-class test consists of a one-time oral test that grants access to the possibility of taking any written test, and of an in-class written test, possibly at a computer terminal, with submission of a written paper justifying the answers. At the discretion of the examination board, an additional test may be required. An additional oral examination may be requested by the examination board.

Unless otherwise specified, the final test includes from 3 to 6 multiple-choice or numerical-answer quizzes and from 0 to 3 theoretical questions, either multiple-choice or open-ended, to be answered on paper.

Generative AI tools are not permitted during exams in order to ensure academic integrity. Violation of this rule constitutes academic misconduct.

### Assessment methods

The examination consists of an **oral examination** and a **written examination**.

The **oral examination** consists of two theoretical questions and requires knowledge of the definitions and theorems covered in the course. The oral examination is passed if the student answers at least one of the two questions completely correctly. Once passed, the oral examination remains valid as an admission requirement for all subsequent written examinations within the same academic year.

The **written examination** consists of solving exercises in the form of quizzes on Moodle Esami, with written justifications for all answers. Correct answers without an adequate justification, as well as answers supported by incorrect or incomplete reasoning or reasoning that does not lead to the correct conclusion, will not be awarded any credit.

The examination may be taken in one of the following two ways.

#### 1) "Follower" Mode

The online and in-class activities carried out during the course contribute to a formative assessment, which is combined with the grade obtained in an examination during the first examination session (oral and written examinations taken during the first session). This option may lead to a higher final grade but requires continuous engagement throughout the semester and regular participation in the scheduled activities. The formative assessment is taken into account only for examinations held during the first examination session.

The written examination consists of **4 problems** and **2 short conceptual exercises**.

#### 2) Standard Mode

This is the traditional examination format, consisting of a computer-based written examination together with the submission of a handwritten paper containing the justifications for the answers. In exceptional cases, the examination committee may require an additional oral examination. The award of cum laude requires achieving the maximum possible score in the written examination.

The written examination consists of **6 problems** and **2 short conceptual exercises**.

### Assessment methods

We evaluate both the knowledge and the skills acquired on the topics in the program. The final grade will be calculated as the average of the grade obtained in the skills test (solving exercises) and in the knowledge test (stating or directly applying fundamental theorems and definitions in the program, with proof where required). This grade may subsequently be modified by the additional test.

In each test, the following is evaluated:

- The correctness of the answers
- The completeness of the answers
- The proper use of technical mathematical language

The optional ongoing activities resize the final exam, reducing its scope and/or increasing the grade in proportion to the consistency between the expected and received grade.

For honors, an oral validation is required.

The bonus can only be used in the winter session.
