## Contents

[0.5 cfu] Elementary algebraic structures. Groups, rings, fields, algebras. [1 cfu] Matrices. Operations on matrices. Determinant of a square matrix; properties of the determinant and methods of calculations of the determinant. Inverse matrix. [1.5 cfu] Vector spaces. Linear combinations; systems of generators; bases and dimension. Linear transformations and their properties. Matrices associated to a linear transformation. Rank of a matrix; algorithms for the computation of the rank. Parametric and Cartesian representation of vector subspaces. [1 cfu] Linear systems Discussion of a linear system; structure of the space of solutions of a compatible linear system. Cramer systems. Algorithms for the solution of compatible linear systems. [1 cfu] Canonical forms of matrices Similarity of matrices; diagonalization problems. Eigenvalues, eigenvectors and eigenspaces. Algebraic and geometric multiplicity. Spectral theorem. [1 cfu] Euclidean vector spaces Scalar product; norm of a vector; angle between vectors and orthogonality. Orthogonal bases and orthonormal bases; Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization method. Orthogonal complement of a subspace of a Euclidean space. [1.5 cfu] Euclidean geometry Coordinate system; subspaces; relative positions. Euclidean plane: representations of straight lines; parallelism and othogonality; distances; areas; plane isometries. Three-dimensional Euclidean space: representations of straight lines and planes, parallelism and othogonality; distances; areas and volumes. Projective extension of an Euclidean space. [1.5 cfu] Theory of conics Homogeneous and non-homogeneous equations; related matrices. Degenerate and non-degenerate conics. Classification of non degenerate conics. Polarity. Centre of a conic and its coordinates. Diameters. Asintots. Axes and vertices. Euclidean canonical equations of conics. Characterization of equilateral hyperboles, of void ellipses, of circunferences. Focus and directrix of a non-degenerate conics. Some elements about sheaves of conics. The scan of the contents by CFU is to be understood as indicative. In fact, it can undergo changes during the course of teaching in the light of the feedback and the participation of the students.

## Learning objectives

Introduction to the basic concepts and structures of linear algebra in relation to their use in other sectors.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge in mathematics, and set theory language.

## Teaching methods

The course consists of lectures (in presence, in italian language, also carried out via multimedia tools), engaged with exercise activity, which allows the immediate application of the concepts and theoretical methods learned. The teaching also consists of an EXERCISES activity, carried out by a co-teacher, dedicated above all to the applicative aspects of the subject. Teaching is then accompanied by a teaching support activity, carried out by qualified personnel outside the official hours of lessons, which provides students with a further opportunity for the assimilation of the theoretical contents and methods for their application. Attendance at lessons is not compulsory, but it is strongly recommended. The course is delivered face-to-face, in Italian. All technical and organizational information on teaching, as well as teaching material, will be uploaded to the Dolly platform. The student is invited to register and consult this platform regularly.

## Assessment methods

The exam - which takes place at the end of the teaching, in at least six rounds per year - consists of a written test and an oral test, both carried out without the aid of texts and/or notes. The written test consists of a multiple-choice test: 11 questions, some (usually 4) theoretical and others (usually 7) in the form of exercises, each with four possible answers, only one of which is correct. The score is obtained by calculating 3 points for each correct answer, -1 points for each incorrect answer and 0 points for each question that has not been answered. The results of the written test are made known to candidates within three days via email from ESSE3, with publication under password on Moodle. During the lesson period it is possible to obtain exemption from the written test - and therefore direct access to the oral test - by passing two intermediate tests, of the same type as the written tests: one is conducted in the middle of the course, in the week of suspension of lessons at the beginning of November, and the other immediately after the end of the lessons. To obtain the written exemption - valid for the entire academic year - both grades must be greater than or equal to 12, with an arithmetic mean greater than or equal to 15. The results of the intermediate tests are made known to candidates within ten days via email from ESSE3, with publication under password on Moodle. Access to the oral exam through the written exam is granted - always within the academic year. - if the grade is greater than or equal to 15. In both cases (both after passing the intermediate written tests and after passing an exam written), if you take an oral test with a negative result, the validity of the written test remains limited to a single further oral test within the a.a.: after two negative oral tests, the written test loses its validity. The oral exam is aimed at verifying the theoretical knowledge of the topics in the program and the ability to argue about them, with a correct use of mathematical formalism and deductive hypothetical thinking (through the demonstration of some important results of linear algebra and Euclidean geometry). The duration of the oral exam is 20-30 minutes. The final grade is established by the examiner on the basis of the oral test, also taking into account the written grade; however, there is no predetermined constraint between the two scores. For the attribution of the vote, the following are considered: - basic knowledge of the topics and partial ability to apply knowledge: minimum grade (18/30) - full knowledge of the topics and excellent ability to apply knowledge: maximum grade (30/30 cum laude) - graduation of the intermediate grades based on the achievement of the expected learning outcomes, including the transversal ones demonstrated during the exam.
