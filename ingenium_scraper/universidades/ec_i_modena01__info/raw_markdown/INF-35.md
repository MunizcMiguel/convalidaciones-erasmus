## Contents

1) Introduction - Digital data and signals; information according to Shannon; binary encoding - Hamming redundant coding: error detection and correction - Functional description of the computer; the Von Neumann architecture - The components of the computer: memory, CPU, I/O and buses - History and evolution of computer and processor architecture 2) Logic networks - Combinational logic networks: Boolean algebra, truth tables and Karnaugh maps; analysis of logic networks; canonical synthesis; synthesis with the Quine-McCluskey method - RTL level: register-transfer description and programmable logic devices - Synchronous sequential logic networks: finite state machines; memory elements, D latches and flip-flops; synthesis examples; RAM memory 3) Instruction Set Architecture and assembly programming - Instructions and the fetch-decode-execute cycle - Instruction formats, instruction types and addressing modes - Introduction to the RISC-V architecture: programming model and register file - Elementary assembly constructs: arithmetic and logic operations, conditional branches, loops - Stack management, calling conventions and the C-assembly interface 4) Computer architecture - Performance: computers of the 21st century; performance computation and measurement; Amdahl's law; Moore's law; RISC and CISC architectures - The CPU: computer architecture and processor microarchitecture; buses and communication with peripherals; interrupts and interrupt service routines - Memories: the memory hierarchy; RAM, ROM and cache; technologies for main memory; memory access and performance computation - I/O peripherals: the hard disk, characterization and performance; disk organization and RAID controllers 5) Parallelism and modern architectures - Pipelining and hazard handling; branch prediction - Flynn's taxonomy - Multicore architectures and embedded systems - Introduction to GPUs: the SIMT execution model, memory organization, CPU-GPU comparison and an outline of heterogeneous computing

## Learning objectives

The Computer Architecture course is intended for students of the Bachelor's Degree in Computer Engineering at the "Enzo Ferrari" Department of Engineering, University of Modena and Reggio Emilia. The course aims to provide the fundamental knowledge of the architecture of electronic computers, with emphasis on the hardware structure, on the logic networks employed as components of the computer, on the models of the Instruction Set Architecture and of the processor microarchitecture, on the memory hierarchy, and up to input and output peripherals. Processors are covered, from simple processors to multicore architectures, with specific reference to the RISC-V architecture. Lab sessions are planned on both assembly programming and the design of logic networks and digital circuits for computers. As a foundational course, it is predominantly theoretical in nature, presenting architectural models, the basics of performance evaluation, and the organization and structure of the fundamental components of every computer, independently of specific technological implementations. For this reason, references to commercial systems will be occasional and purely illustrative. References to current technologies will mostly be oriented towards the personal computer world, although embedded, graphics, and mobile systems will also be presented. Computer Architecture is a foundational discipline; its prerequisites are the notions taught in the Computer Science Fundamentals courses, and it requires no prior knowledge of digital electronics.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: knowing and understanding the fundamentals of electronic computers, with particular reference to the RISC-V architecture and to contemporary parallel and heterogeneous architectures, from multicore systems to graphics processing units. Applying knowledge and understanding: being able to analyse the main computer architectures and to quantitatively evaluate their performance, to design simple combinational and sequential logic networks, and to write simple functions in RISC-V assembly language. Making judgements: acquiring the methodological and operational tools needed to address, with independent and objective judgement, the typical problems of the design and development of software applications and of digital information processing, being able to assess the impact of architectural choices on the efficiency of the adopted solutions. Communication skills: the communication skills required of a future Computer Engineer concern in particular the ability to communicate information, ideas, problems and solutions to specialist and non-specialist audiences, in written and oral form, in Italian and in English, using the technical terminology of computer architecture appropriately. Learning skills: these skills are assessed by asking students to independently deepen the knowledge acquired in specific areas of interest, also through the consultation of technical documentation and reference literature.

## Assessment methods

Written exam, with multiple-choice questions. Oral examination with questions on computer architecture theory.
