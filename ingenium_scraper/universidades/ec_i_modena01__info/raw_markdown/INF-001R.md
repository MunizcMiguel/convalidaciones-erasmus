## Contents

The course is held in the first semester of the first year (9 CFU). The program of the course can be summarized in the following main topics, and for each topic you can find approximate information about the related number of CFU. Real Numbers (about 1 CFU): order relation, infimum and supremum, maximum and minimum, topology. Sequences and series of real numbers (about 1.5 CFU). One variable functions (about 3 CFU): fundametal functions; monotone functions; composite and inverse functions; limit, continuity, derivative and tangent line, higher order derivatives, convexity and concavity. Taylor formulas and Taylor series (about 1.5 CFU). Integral Calculus of one variable functions (about 2 CFU): definite and indefinite integral, fundametal theorem of calculus, integration methods; improper integrals.

## Reference texts

## Learning objectives

The course provides basic notions of mathematical analysis for one-real-variable functions, differential and integral calculus. At the end of the course, students should be able to understand, to describe and to solve simple problems of mathematical analysis for one-real-variable functions.

## Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of elementary algebra, equations and inequalities, elementary functions including trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions.

## Teaching methods

The lectures are planned in presence. Attendance in this course is not mandatory but strongly recommended. The method of learning is based on theoretical frontal lectures and exercises. Complete and updated information about the organization of the course and class materials will be available on Moodle, and students are asked to check it regularly.

## Other information

1) Through lectures and individual study, knowledge and understanding of the basic notions of the analysis of one variable functions, computation of limits, derivatives and integrals, qualitative study of functions, study of sequences and series of real numbers. 2) Through exercises and individual study, ability to tackle problems with logical accurancy and find the most suitable techniques for solving them. 3) Independent judgement: frame problems (even new problems) in the most suitable framework, identify the characteristic features of problems and the connections with theoretical results. 4) Communication skills: articulate a given topic in a consistent way arguing with a precise mathematical language. 5) Learning skills: develop a methodological approach to improve learning capablities.

## Assessment methods

The final exam is splitted into two parts: a written and an oral exam. More specifically, the assessment of learning is carried out through a final exam, which verifies the acquisition of the expected knowledge and skills through the completion of a written exam and an oral exam without the aid of books. Only after passing the written exam the student can access the oral exam. The written exam, in general, consists of 6 open questions. Open questions always require to provide detailed information about the arguments applied to answer. The duration of a written exam is 2 hours. The results of the written exam will be communicated within two weeks of the exam and, in any case, in time to participate in the subsequent oral exam. The written exam is passed with a minimum score of 18/30. In case of a passing grade in the written exam, the student is admitted to the oral exam, and the oral exam consists of open questions about definitions, relevant examples, theorems and proofs listed in the final program published at the end of the course. The duration of an oral exams is approximately 30 minutes. Students will pass the exam if they demonstrate the knowledge of the key concepts presented in the course, and the correct use of mathematical language. A higher score will be awarded to students who demonstrate a good understanding and the ability to use all the course content, explaining it precise mathematical language and solving even complex problems. Failure to pass the exam may be due to insufficient knowledge of key concepts or lack of appropriate use of mathematical language. The final grade corresponds to the arithmetic mean of the grades obtained in the written and oral exams.
