## Contents

1. Basic Concepts (1 CFU) Systems and mathematical models. Block schemes. Signal-flow graphs. Mason's formula. Feed-forward control and feedback control. Dynamic models of some physical systems. 2. Analysis of linear dynamical systems (1 CFU) Differential equations. Laplace transform and its properties. Partial-fraction expansion. Impulse response. Analysis of the first and second order systems. Systems with dominant poles. 3. Frequency-domain analysis (1 CFU) The frequency response. Bode plots. Frequency response of systems with dominant poles. Bode's formula. Nyquist plots. Rules for the qualitative drawing of the Bode and Nyquist plots. Nichols plots. 4. Stability of the closed-loop systems (1 CFU) Stability of linear dynamical systems. Routh criterion. General properties of the closed-loop systems. Sensitivity to disturbances and to parameters variations. Steady-state errors. Nyquist criterion. Amplitude and phase margins. Systems with time delays. Resonant frequency, resonant peak and bandwidth. 5. Root-Locus technique (1 CFU) Definition and properties of the Root-Locus. Rules for the graphical drawing of the Root-Locus. Contour-Locus. 6. Design of lead and lag compensators (1 CFU) Design specifications. Lead, Lag and Lead-Lag compensators. Inversion formulas. Design of a lead/lag compensator on the Nyquist and Nichols planes. PID standard regulators. 7. Digital control (2 CFU) Discrete systems. Difference equations. Z-transform. Inverse Z-transform. S-plane and Z-plane correspondence. Compensator design via discretization. 8. Recap exercises about the course topics. (1 CFU)

## Learning objectives

Provide the following basic tools: 1) describe and analyze the feedback dynamic systems both in time and in frequency; 2) design continuous and discrete regulators useful to improve the dynamical behavior of the controlled systems.

## Prerequisites

Complex numbers. Differential equations (basic elements).

## Teaching methods

Lectures with slides and exercises done on the blackboard.

## Other information

Knowledge and understanding: through classroom lessons the students learn the main methods of time and frequency analysis of linear dynamic systems and the synthesis of simple lead and lag networks. Applying knowledge and understanding: the student is able to analyze the time and frequency dynamics of linear physical systems described by transfer functions. Autonomy of judgment: the methods of analysis and the control techniques studied provide students with the skill of analyzing and controlling linear physical systems. Communication skills: the theoretical lessons provide students with the ability to express the learned concepts with an appropriate language and to properly discuss the topics of the course. Learning skills: the activities described allow the student to acquire the methodological tools to continue their studies and to be able to arrange their own update.

## Assessment methods

Two partial written exams (Controls A and Controls B). The first partial written exam (Controls A) will be in april in correspondence of the didactic break. The second partial written exam (Controls B) will be in the first half of june. There will also be partial written exams (Controls A and Controls B) in the following periods: a) second half of june (Controls A) and first half of july (Controls B); b) first week of september (Controls A) and second week of september (Controls B). The partial written exams are structured as follows: a) duration: approximately 2 hours; b) 13-14 multiple-choice questions or immediate-answer questions (30 minutes, 11 points); c) 6-7 numerical exercises or qualitative analyses (90 minutes, 21 points); d) no didactic material is allowed during the exam; e) the use of the calculator is allowed during the exam; f) the maximum mark of the written exams is 32 points. The final mark is given by the sum of two contributions: a) the average of the two best positive-rated marks obtained by the student in the two partial written exams (Controls A and Controls B); b) an additional point if the student took the Matlab OnRamp course. This additional point will be assigned only if the student attaches the certificate of successful completion of the course when asking, by email, for the online mark registration. Matlab OnRamp certificates sent with times and methods that differ from those specified above will not be accepted. The maximum mark is 33 points. Laude is granted if more than 31.5 points are gained. The students who do not wish to take the partial written exams may ask to take an oral exam. The oral exam (consisting in theoretical questions, numerical exercises and qualitative analyses) will be when there are no written exams available, and will last approximately 30 minutes for Controls A and 30 minutes for Controls B.
