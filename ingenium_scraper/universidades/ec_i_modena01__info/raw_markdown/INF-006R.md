## Contents

Ordinary Differential Equations. First order differential equations in normal form and notion of solution. Cauchy problem. Linear differential equations. Separable equations. Maximal existence interval. First order linear equations and associated Cauchy problem: existence, uniqueness. Bernoulli and Riccati equations. Peano's existence theorem. Local and global existence and uniqueness theorem. Application to a Cauchy problem associated with Bernoulli-type equations. Linear equations of order k with variable coefficients. General solution of a non-homogeneous equation. Equations with constant coefficients and characteristic polynomial. Particular solution of an equation with a quasi-trigonometric forcing term. Elements of topology in RN. Limit and continuity. Bolzano-Weierstrass and Weierstrass theorems. Directional derivative, partial derivatives, gradient, proof of Fermat's theorem, Schwarz's theorem, study of the image of a continuous function on a compact connected set. Differential and gradient, and their geometric interpretation. Chain rule, characterization of functions with zero gradient, Hessian matrix. Study of local extrema. Second order Taylor formula. Classification theorem of points with zero gradient using the Hessian matrix. Kinematic interpretation of curves, tangent vector as velocity, Jacobian matrix, tangent space, unit vector normal to the graph, equation of the plane tangent to the graph at a point of a C^1 function. Implicit function theorem. Lagrange's multiplier theorem and applications. Sequences of functions. Point-wise convergence. Uniform convergence vs. integrals and derivatives. Series of functions and their convergence. Weierstrass M-test. Periodic functions. Fourier coefficients. Point-wise and uniform convergence of the Fourier series. Complex Fourier series. Geometric interpretations. Proof of the global existence and uniqueness theorem for first-order differential equations in normal form.

## Learning objectives

This taught module gives a basic knowledge in differential calculus for functions of several real variables, on sequences and series of functions and on ordinary differential equations. This knowledge not only is required to progress within any university programs in engineering, but contributes to developing analysis and problem solving skills which are essential in most of the jobs, especially for engineers.

## Prerequisites

Calculus: continuity, differentiability and integrability for functions of one real variable. Sequences and number series. Moreover, some basic knowledge of the linear functions in finite dimension vector space is desirable.

## Teaching methods

The course is essentially based on lectures in which the results of the theory are presented with examples that clarify their meaning and application in solving mathematical problems. The lecture format encourages participation with questions from students. A few moment at the end of important topics will be devoted to solving problems in the classroom, with feedback from the teacher. Support sessions like example classes are also scheduled throughout the semester.

## Other information

Knowledge and analytical skills: Through lecture, notes and teaching activities carried out during the course, the student will acquire basic knowledge on sequences and series of functions, on differential equations and on differential calculus for functions of multiple real variables . Ability to apply the knowledge acquired: Through example classes, support activities and individual work, the student will develop the ability to solve mathematical problems using the techniques of mathematical analysis. Independence in the study of related topics: At the end of the course the student will be able to independently study topics related to those in the syllabus. Communication skills: Thanks to constructive interaction with teachers and colleagues, the student will be able to communicate with mathematical language on the course topics.

## Assessment methods

The exam is made up of a written + an oral tests. To access the oral test it is essential to pass the written test. The written test is made up of three problems, possibly divided into sub-questions. Each of the three problems reflects one of the three main parts of the syllabus. The oral exam focuses on a topic to be studied in depth by the candidate, to which additional questions on the syllabus and the written text are added. The opportunity for one or more exam simulations will be considered based on student input.
