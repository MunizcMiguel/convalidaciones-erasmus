## Learning outcomes

At the end of the course, students will be able to:

**Knowledge and understanding**

- understand the fundamental principles of human biomechanics and the basic methodologies for analyzing stresses acting on body segments during daily activities.

**Applying knowledge and understanding**

- apply mechanical and biomechanical principles to analyze loading conditions and stresses in the main segments of the human body.

**Making judgements**

- critically evaluate the results of biomechanical analyses, interpreting the effects of loading conditions on the behavior of body segments.

**Communication skills**

- describe the adopted methodologies and obtained results in biomechanical analyses using appropriate technical terminology.

**Learning skills**

- independently develop the ability to explore methodologies and tools for human biomechanical analysis through technical texts and scientific documentation.

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide students with the basic tools for the analysis of stresses acting on human body segments during activities of daily living.

The course introduces the fundamental principles of human body biomechanical analysis, providing the knowledge required to understand the distribution of loads and stresses in the main body segments through an engineering approach.

## Prerequisites

To understand the course contents, students are required to have basic knowledge of Physics, Mathematical Analysis, Geometry, and Technical Drawing.

## Contents

**1. Biomechanical properties of biological tissues**

- Bone tissue: structural characteristics and mechanical behavior.
- Articular cartilage: functional properties and role in joint biomechanics.
- Tendons and ligaments: mechanical characteristics and function within the musculoskeletal system.
- Muscles: biomechanical properties and role in motion generation.

**2. Biomechanical analysis of human movement**

- Principles of human body mobility.
- Analysis of human motion and interactions between body segments.

**3. Biomechanics of major joints**

- Biomechanical analysis of the knee.
- Biomechanical analysis of the hip.
- Biomechanical analysis of the ankle.
- Biomechanical analysis of the lumbar spine.
- Biomechanical analysis of the shoulder.
- Biomechanical analysis of the elbow.

**4. Engineering approach to posture**

- Principles of biomechanical posture analysis.
- Evaluation of static and dynamic human body conditions through engineering models and methodologies.

## Teaching methods

The course includes lectures and classroom exercises aimed at achieving the educational objectives and expected learning outcomes.

In particular:

- lectures are provided for the presentation and discussion of theoretical topics related to human biomechanics and the analysis of stresses acting on body segments;
- classroom exercises are carried out under the supervision of the instructor to apply theoretical concepts through the analysis and solution of biomechanical problems.

## Assessment methods

The assessment of learning is carried out through a written and/or oral examination aimed at verifying the achievement of the expected learning outcomes.

The examination consists of questions concerning the theoretical topics and methodologies covered during the course. Students must demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the fundamental principles of human biomechanics and of the methodologies used for analyzing stresses acting on body segments.

The evaluation considers the student's ability to apply theoretical concepts to the solution of biomechanical problems, the correctness of the acquired knowledge, the ability to interpret results, and the clarity of the presentation using appropriate technical terminology.

## Reference texts

Basic biomechanics of the musculoskeletal system. Margeret Nordin, Victor H. Frankel

## 2030 Agenda Sustainable Development Goals

The course contributes to the achievement of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by providing students with skills in biomechanics and in the engineering analysis of the human body.

In particular, the course contributes to SDG 3 – Good Health and Well-being, by providing knowledge and tools for understanding the biomechanical behavior of tissues, joints, and body segments, supporting the analysis of human motion, posture, and loading conditions of the musculoskeletal system.

The acquired skills can contribute to the development of engineering methodologies aimed at the functional assessment of the human body and at improving health, safety, and quality of life.

## Other information

During the course, software tools may be used to support the analysis and simulation of biomechanical systems through an engineering approach. In particular, Working Model software may be employed for the modeling and simulation of the kinematic and dynamic behavior of equivalent mechanical systems, allowing students to further explore theoretical concepts related to motion analysis and loading conditions.

The use of computational tools enables students to integrate theoretical knowledge acquired during lectures with practical activities, improving the understanding of biomechanical phenomena through numerical models and simulations.
