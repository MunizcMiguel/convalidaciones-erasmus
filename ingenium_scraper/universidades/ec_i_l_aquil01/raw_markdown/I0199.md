## Partition: GENERAL PHYSICS I (Cognomi H-Z)

### Language

Italian

### Learning outcomes

At the end of the course, students are expected to:

- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the fundamental principles of classical mechanics and thermodynamics, including the laws governing the motion of particles, systems of particles, and rigid bodies, as well as the principles governing energy transfer and thermodynamic processes; understand the physical meaning of the main physical quantities, conservation laws, and the models used in the analysis of mechanical and thermodynamic systems;

- apply and explain the laws of kinematics, dynamics, and thermodynamics to solve quantitative problems by formulating appropriate physical models, using mathematical methods, and determining unknown physical quantities through systems of algebraic and differential equations; analyze collision phenomena, rigid-body systems, and thermodynamic processes of engineering interest;

- critically evaluate the results obtained by assessing their physical plausibility, dimensional consistency, and the validity limits of the adopted models; identify the simplifying assumptions used in the analysis of physical problems and evaluate their effects on the proposed solutions;

- communicate effectively using the scientific language and notation of physics to describe mechanical and thermodynamic phenomena, present problem-solving procedures, interpret results, and explain solutions in a clear, rigorous, and logically consistent manner;

- develop independent learning skills by adopting a study approach based on the quantitative analysis of physical phenomena and the scientific method, thereby acquiring the competencies required to successfully undertake subsequent courses in physics, engineering, and related scientific and technological disciplines.

### Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the fundamental knowledge of classical mechanics and thermodynamics, with particular emphasis on the principles governing the motion of particles, systems of particles, and rigid bodies, as well as the fundamental laws of thermodynamics.

Starting from the study of kinematics and dynamics of a particle, the course introduces Newton's three laws of motion and develops the main theorems and conservation laws of energy, linear momentum, and angular momentum. The course also covers the dynamics and statics of rigid bodies, collision phenomena, and the principles of thermodynamics, with particular emphasis on the first and second laws of thermodynamics, reversible and irreversible thermodynamic processes, the concept of entropy, and the operation of heat engines and refrigeration systems.

Particular emphasis is placed on the development of problem-solving skills through the analysis and solution of exercises and practical problems. Students learn to apply physical models and mathematical methods to the quantitative analysis of mechanical and thermodynamic systems relevant to industrial engineering.

The main topics will be introduced in the course include:

1. Kinematics of a material point

2. Dynamics of a material point

3. Dynamics of systems of material points

4. The conservation laws of energy, momentum, and angular momentum

5. Dynamics and statics of the rigid body

6. Impact phenomena between material points and between material points and rigid bodies

7. First law of thermodynamics

8. Second law of thermodynamics

### Prerequisites

The student must have passed the exam of Mathematical Analysis I. The student must have a deep knowledge of (i) the fundamental mathematical tools such as trigonometry, vector calculus, and solution of systems of algebraic equations; (ii) of the differential analysis for what concerns the calculus of derivatives and integrals and the solution of the first order differential equations.

### Contents

The motion of a material point along one spatial dimension: definition of speed and acceleration. Uniform and uniformly accelerated motion. Motion along two spatial dimensions: tangential and radial speed; tangential and centripetal acceleration. Uniform and uniformly circular motion: angular speed and acceleration. Parabolic motion. Harmonic oscillator.

The principles of dynamics. The notion of strength. Definition of momentum and impulse theorem. Supporting reactions. Friction forces. Inclined plane. Elastic force. Simple pendulum. Work of a force. Kinetic energy theorem. Conservative forces. Potential energy. Mechanical energy. Non-conservative forces. The conservation law of mechanical energy.

Non-inertial reference systems. Theorems of the relative speed and acceleration.

Dynamics of a system of material points. Definition of internal and external forces. Centre of mass and its determination. The theorem of the center of mass. Angular moment and moment of a force (torque). The theorem of the angular momentum. The conservation laws of the angular momentum and energy: the two Konig’s theorems.

Definition of rigid bodies. Definition of density. Definition and determination of the moment of inertia. Huygens-Steiner theorem. The general equations describe the motion of a rigid body.

Shock phenomena. Impulsive forces.

Thermodynamic systems. Thermodynamic environment and universe. Definition of state variables and their use to describe the thermodynamic processes. The zero principle of thermodynamics. Definition of ideal gases. The first principles of thermodynamics. Principles and use of calorimetry. The work of an ideal gas. Definition of the state function internal energy and determination of its variation. Thermal and cooling engines. Engines based on the Carnot cycle. The statements defining the second principle of thermodynamics. Carnot’s theorem. Clausius’s theorem. Definition of the state function entropy. Determination of the variation of entropy for the thermodynamic universe in the absence and the presence of irreversible transformations. The principle of increase of entropy.

### Teaching methods

Lectures and exercises held in class and discussed with the teacher. During explaining the theoretical concepts and their use for the solution of exercises, the teacher stimulates the discussion with the students to bring out the main doubts and the encountered difficulties.

### Assessment methods

A formal assessment of the required prerequisites is not planned; however, this evaluation will be carried out during the assessment of the learning process. The examination consists of a written test and an oral test. The oral test can only be taken if the student has obtained a mark greater than or equal to 15/30 in the written test.

The written test aims to verify whether the student has successfully achieved Learning Objectives 1–6 described previously and accounts for 40% of the final grade. The written test consists of solving three original problems on:  
(i) the dynamics of material points (maximum score: 7 points);  
(ii) the dynamics of rigid bodies (maximum score: 15 points);  
(iii) thermodynamic processes involving ideal gases (maximum score: 8 points).

A minimum score of 3.5/7 on the first exercise is required to pass the written test. During the written test, students are only allowed to use numerical calculators.

During the course, optional partial written tests are scheduled on separate dates. The final grade is the sum of the grades obtained in the partial tests. To be exempted from the written test, the total score must be greater than or equal to 15/30.

The oral test is aimed at verifying the achievement of the Learning Objectives and accounts for 60% of the final grade.

If the instructor determines that the student has not successfully achieved all Learning Objectives 1–6, the examination is not passed and the written test is considered invalid.
