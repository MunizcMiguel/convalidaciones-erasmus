## Learning outcomes

At the end of the course, the student will possess the fundamental knowledge required to analyze a chemical process and perform material balances involving chemical reactions. Furthermore, the student will have knowledges on classical thermodynamics and the principles for evaluating the thermodynamic properties of pure substances, with particular emphasis on gaseous and liquid compounds. Finally, the student will acquire fundamental knowledge regarding the thermodynamics of mixtures, as well as the tools to evaluate their properties and the equilibria of phases and reacting systems.

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide to the student the basic knowledge to deal an analysis of a chemical process and to carry out material balances in the presence of chemical reactions. Further goal is to provide the knowledge on the elements of thermodynamics, to provide the fundamentals for the evaluation of the thermodynamic properties of pure substances, with particular reference to gaseous and liquid compounds. Provide the fundamentals of the thermodynamics of mixtures, and the tools for the evaluation of their properties, phase equilibria and chemical equilibria. On successful completion of this module, the student will be able to select and apply the main equations of state to different fluids, will be able to determine the work and efficiency of different thermodynamic cycles, will be able to apply the main relationships to solve liquid-liquid and vapor-liquid equilibrium of multicomponent mixtures, will be able to solve reagent systems.

## Prerequisites

The course requires a deep knowledge of the concepts learned during the three-year degree course, with particular reference to the knowledge acquired in the course of General Physics I, Chemistry, Applied Physics. These knowledges, in fact, are non-mandatory but highly preparatory prerequisites for a full understanding of the topics covered in this course.

## Contents

1. Chemical engineering: history, evolution and perspectives, specificity in the field of engineering. 2. Preliminary concepts and basic tools for the analysis of physical phenomena, the mathematical simulation of real systems and the validation of results 3. Fundamental equations in terms of macroscopic balances with particular reference to the material balances with chemical reactions. 4. First and second principles of thermodynamics. Thermal machines and heat pumps. Combination of the first and second principles. 5. Ideal gases. 6. Volumetric behavior of pure fluids, state diagrams and tables, Equations of State (EoS). 7. Thermodynamics of pure fluids: thermodynamic functions, Maxwell relations, residual functions, Joule - Thomson coefficient. 8. Thermodynamics of phase equilibrium (Clausius-Clapeyron, Antoine). Fugacity and equilibrium conditions, calculation of fugacity with the equations of state and by indirect method for condensed phases. 9. Properties of mixtures, partial molar properties, Gibbs - Duhem equation. Perfect gas mixtures, real fluids-residual functions from EoS. (Coefficient of) Fugacity for a compound in a mixture and relationship with the residual Gibbs free energy. 10. Condensed phases. Ideal mixtures, non-ideal mixtures (excess functions). Activity coefficient models (Margules, van Laar, Wilson), NRTL, UNIQUAC and UNIFAC models. 11. Phases equilibrium in mixture. Gibbs phase rule. Equations for calculating equilibrium conditions. Vapor Liquid Equilibrium (Raoult's law), supercritical compounds, direct methods for high pressures. Liquid-liquid equilibrium (solubility diagrams), calculation at medium and low pressure. Vapor-Liquid-Liquid Equilibrium. Solid-liquid equilibrium. Dilute systems: freezing point depression, Boiling-point elevation, solubilities of a solute in two liquids, solubility of gases in liquids (Henry's law) 12. Systems with chemical reactions. Closed systems and open systems at stady-state, extent of reaction, equilibrium conditions, standard reference states. Influence of temperature on the equilibrium constant K. Heterogeneous systems with chemical reacions. Francis diagram. Non-stoichiometric method: the Gibbs free energy minimization method. Energy balance in multicomponent systems with chemical reactions. 13. Introduction to Aspen Plus, determination of the thermodynamic parameters of the various components of the software library, Plot of VLE diagrams, Equilibrium Reactor and Gibbs Reactor for the calculation of equilibrium compositions in reagent systems.

## Teaching methods

The teaching method will include face to face lectures with the use of the classic blackboard and when useful Power Point slides. At the beginning of each lesson the concepts explained in the previous lesson will be recalled and questions will be asked to the classroom to verify the degree of understanding and learning. Periodically exercises will be carried out on the concepts learned, one lesson per week will be scheduled.

## Assessment methods

Formative assessment: Informal discussions are used to stimulate a deeper student understanding. The exercises and applications are the best environment for start a Q&A short sessions about theoretical issues. Conjectures and techniques conceived by the students are discussed in groups for achieving the correct answer together. Summative assessment: The course includes a written test and an oral test during the exam sessions. Written test, Oral exam (50%: 50%) In particular, the written test consists in the resolution of 3 exercises similar to those carried out in class during the exercises. . The oral test consists of a discussion of the written exam with 2-3 additional questions, in order to verify the knowledge of the thermodynamics concepts learned at lesson and the ability to apply the rules studied. The excellence is reached if the student is able to solve unusual problems or problems not expressly dealt during the lessons.
