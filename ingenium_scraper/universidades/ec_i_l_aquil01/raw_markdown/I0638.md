## Learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of this module, the student should: - have profound knowledge of conventional technologies in manufacturing processes, - know and understand arguments of metallic materials manufacturing and machining, - understand and explain the meaning of conventional manufacturing processes applied to traditional materials, - understand the fundamental concepts concerning the choice of the best technology to manufacture a product and be aware of potential applications in other fields, - demonstrate skilled reasoning in traditional manufacturing processes and the ability to conceive principles of conventional machining of products. - be able to read and understand other texts on related topics.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with fundamental knowledge for selecting, managing, and controlling manufacturing processes in industrial engineering. These goals help develop knowledge of the technical foundations of industrial engineering, adequate competencies in identifying, analysing, and solving problems in manufacturing technologies using consolidated methods, and the ability to apply acquired knowledge and competencies to analyze and improve manufacturing processes. Students should also be able to identify, from both technical and economic perspectives, the most suitable technological process for manufacturing a component. The course includes a 9 CFU section that serves as a unifying factor across two specialisations: Management and Mechanics. This section fosters a shared understanding and knowledge base among these specialisations. Additionally, another section from 3 CFU is specific to the Management specialisation only.

## Prerequisites

The student must know the basic notions of General Physics I, Technical Drawing and Materials Science and Technology.

## Contents

The topics of the Module (9 CFU), which are common to both Management and Mechanics, include: Properties of materials relevant to different technological processes. Fundamentals of Metal Casting: cast structures of solidified metals, basic types of cast structures, segregation, shrinkage of casting, shrinkage stresses, common casting defects, general design rules for casting, materials and processes classification. Welding technology: analysis and classification of the essential processes. Forging of metals: characteristics of forging, cold and hot plastic deformation. Lamination. Extrusion of metals: direct-extrusion, products made from extrusions, types of extrusion, process variables in extrusion, extrusion force, impact extrusion. Drawing of metals. Sheet-metal forming processes. Fundamentals of machining: tool geometry, cutting process analysis, tool wear, tool materials, surface integrity of workpiece. Turning operation. Machining processes used to produce various shapes: milling, drilling, broaching, abrasive machining and finishing operations. The topics of the module (3 CFU) for the Management specialisation include non-destructive testing (visual inspection, radiography, gamma-ray, ultrasonic testing, penetrating liquids, magnetoscopy, eddy current testing, thermography, and acoustic emission).

## Teaching methods

The teaching activities used in the course and the related learning activities required of the student are:

- lectures to acquire knowledge and understanding, using slides in PowerPoint format that are made available at the beginning of the course;

- interaction with students through the proposition of problems and decision-making to develop the ability to understand and identify appropriate solutions;

- participation in guided exercises, both in the classroom and in the laboratory, to develop the ability to communicate and support theses.

## Assessment methods

Preliminary verification of the class's skills: No formal verification of the required prerequisites. During the first lesson, the teacher highlights essential knowledge deemed indispensable and whose eventual deficiencies must be addressed for the course to be used correctly.

Ongoing assessment of the learning process: the active participation of students is stimulated throughout the course through the teaching methods described above. This active participation allows the teacher to monitor the learning process.

Assessment of the learning process (final exam): The exam consists of answering several questions on manufacturing processes in approximately an hour. The final grade will be assigned as follows: foundry processes (30%), plastic deformation processes (30%), machining by chip removal (30%), and welding processes (10%).

The evaluation criteria will be the level of knowledge of the course topics, the ability to apply them, the scientific language used, and the clarity and completeness of the explanations.

The mere passing of the examination is subject to the demonstration by the student of the following minimum knowledge and skills:

a) the essential characteristics of the foundry process;

b) identification of the main plastic deformation processes;

c) identification of the main machining operations by chip removal;

d) the essential characteristics of the welding process.

Grades will reflect basically four levels of preparation:

sufficient: basic ability in applying methodologies and tools to analyse the manufacturing processes;

adequate: basic skills in designing the manufacturing processes;

good: critical evaluation of manufacturing processes;

Very good: advanced ability in evaluating the different manufacturing processes.
