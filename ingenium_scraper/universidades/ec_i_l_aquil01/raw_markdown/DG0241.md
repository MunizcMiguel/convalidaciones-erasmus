### Learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

- LO1 - Knowledge and understanding: describe the principles of geometric representation methods, technical drawing standards, dimensioning, dimensional and geometrical tolerancing, surface texture, and the main design solutions used in mechanical and industrial systems.
- LO2 - Knowledge and understanding: describe the operating principles, resolution, main applications, and limitations of biomedical imaging technologies based on ionising and non-ionising radiation, as well as the fundamentals of image enhancement, quality assessment, segmentation, and visualisation algorithms.
- LO3 - Applying knowledge and understanding: interpret an assembly drawing and produce correct technical representations of components using views, sections, dimensions, tolerances, and surface texture indications, with reference to technical standards and component catalogues.
- LO4 - Applying knowledge and understanding: apply fundamental biomedical image processing and segmentation algorithms and organise a workflow for visualisation and three-dimensional reconstruction from imaging data.
- LO5 - Making judgements: select and justify representation methods, dimensioning and tolerancing criteria, and design solutions appropriate to the problem; compare imaging technologies and algorithms considering image quality, resolution, intended application, and limitations.
- LO6 - Communication skills: clearly communicate the geometrical and functional characteristics of components and systems through standardised graphical language and technical terminology, including English terminology, and clearly present the choices made in biomedical image processing.
- LO7 - Learning skills: independently consult standards, handbooks, catalogues, technical documentation, and scientific literature and update their skills in using methods and tools for technical representation and biomedical imaging.

### Learning outcomes

Knowledge and understanding: students learn the principles of parametric, associative solid modeling (constrained sketching, feature-based modeling, the feature/construction tree) and standards-compliant drawing generation.

Applying knowledge and understanding: students are able to independently build 3D models of mechanical components and to derive the associated 2D technical drawings.

Making judgements: students can select the modeling strategy (feature sequence and type) that is most efficient and robust with respect to later design changes.

Communication skills: students can document a CAD model, justifying the representation choices made.

Learning skills: students acquire a parametric, associative, feature-based way of working that can be transferred independently across different CAD environments.
