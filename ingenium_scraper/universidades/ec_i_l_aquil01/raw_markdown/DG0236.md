## Learning outcomes

The course aims to provide students with a thorough understanding of the principles of energy conservation, applied thermodynamics, and the analysis of the main thermal power systems, enabling them to apply these concepts in a wide range of operational scenarios. These tools are essential for the characterization and preliminary design of machines and engineering systems. Furthermore, learning outcomes are expected to encompass the distinctive focus of the course, namely the study of machines and systems in biomedical engineering contexts. The primary expected outcome is the ability to apply the aforementioned knowledge to biomedical environments, which require a fundamental understanding of physiological and anatomical principles.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to contribute to the understanding of the phenomena characterizing energy systems, with particular attention to fluid machines operating in the biomedical field. To achieve this goal, the course seeks to provide a solid theoretical foundation based on the principles of conservation of mass, momentum, and stationary energy applied to dynamic and volumetric fluid machines. Equally important will be the understanding of the interaction between these machines and human anatomy and physiology with regard to their specific functions. Therefore, methods of mathematical modeling will be studied to represent physiological functions such as the physiology of respiration and the heart, lungs, and kidneys. This theoretical foundation will enable the analysis, characterization, and selection of the main applications used in the biomedical field.

## Contents

The principles of conservation of mass, momentum, and energy applied to closed and open systems in steady-state form will be studied. The main thermodynamic transformations will then be analyzed: isothermal, adiabatic isentropic, and polytropic processes, compression and expansion, and the exchange and work mechanisms in fluid machines. Both dynamic and volumetric machines will be studied in this regard. The properties of fluids and mixtures will also be a subject of study. After acquiring knowledge of the fundamentals of thermodynamic and fluid dynamic analysis, the study will proceed to applications in the biomedical field. In particular, anesthesia machines and pulmonary ventilators, fundamentals of anesthetic gases, the study of pneumatic circuits, cylinders, hydraulic accumulators and pressure reducers, valves, flowmeters, mixers, and vaporizers will be examined. Mechanical ventilators and mathematical models for pulmonary mechanics, anesthesia ventilators, gas humidifiers, and monitoring systems in anesthesia, pulmonary ventilators, and ventilation techniques will then be studied. Hyperbaric chambers and hyperbaric oxygen therapy will also be subjects of study. Starting from the physiology of respiration, the principles of hyperbaric oxygen therapy and its applications will be explored, with particular attention to hyperbaric chambers, focusing on pressurization circuits and ambient air supply, as well as compressors used in oxygen therapy. Specifically, piston compressors, diaphragm compressors, vane compressors, screw compressors, and centrifugal compressors will be studied. Special attention will be given to vacuum machines, vacuum gauges, vacuum creation, and various types of vacuum pumps (vane pumps, oscillating piston pumps, gear pumps, fluid motor pumps, and liquid ring pumps). An important topic of the course will be the study of machines for extracorporeal circulation and dialysis. Starting from the physiology of the heart, lungs, and cardiovascular system, the heart-lung machine and its components will be studied, including centrifugal and roller pumps, heat exchangers, and filters. Machines used in dialysis will then be examined. Beginning with the analysis of the anatomy and physiology of the kidney, the principles and functioning of dialysis will be studied, including the urea model, the hemodialysis machine, and pumps used in dialysis such as peristaltic pumps (rotary and linear), piston pumps, syringe pumps, the dialyzer, water purification, and dialysate production. Finally, infusion pumps, drop-controlled infusion pumps, rotary, linear, and piston peristaltic pumps, and pumps for infusing liquids containing drugs will be studied. Dental machines will also be analyzed, focusing on the types and sizing criteria of dental turbines. In conclusion, techniques for the creation of sterile areas in industrial and hospital settings will be studied.
