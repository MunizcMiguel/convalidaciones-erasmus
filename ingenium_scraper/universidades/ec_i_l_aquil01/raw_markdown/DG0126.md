## Learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

-Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the general organization of the human body, including the main organs, systems, and their functional relationships.

-Understand the fundamental principles of the major diagnostic imaging techniques and their applications in diagnostic pathways.

-Relate anatomical knowledge to the representations obtained through different medical imaging modalities.

-Recognize the potential, limitations, and fields of application of the principal diagnostic methodologies.

-Use appropriate anatomical, technical, and scientific terminology.

-Critically interpret scientific and technical information related to the topics covered during the course.

- Demonstrate the basic knowledge required for subsequent courses in biomedical sciences and biomedical engineering.

## Learning objectives

The course contributes to the Biomedical Engineering curriculum by providing fundamental knowledge of human body organization and of the principles underlying the main diagnostic imaging techniques. In line with the educational objectives of the degree program, the course promotes the acquisition of interdisciplinary competencies required to understand the interactions between biological systems, diagnostic technologies, and engineering applications in healthcare settings.

The course introduces the principles of human anatomy and the structural and functional organization of organs and body systems, providing the foundations required for the understanding of subsequent biomedical disciplines. In parallel, the physical, technological, and clinical principles of the main medical imaging techniques are presented, highlighting their role in diagnostic processes and clinical practice.

The integration of anatomical and diagnostic knowledge enables students to develop a comprehensive understanding of the relationships between body structure, function, and their instrumental representation, providing an essential foundation for future biomedical engineering applications.

## Contents

DIAGNOSTIC IMAGING AND RADIOTHERAPY MODULE (3 ECTS)

1.Notions of physics of ionizing radiations;

2. Biological effects of radiation;

3. Sources of ionizing radiation;

4. Overview of radiographic image formation mechanisms

5. Plain film;

6. Computed tomography;

7. Magnetic Resonance Imaging;

8. Nuclear medicine

9. Principles of diagnostics applied to the various organs and systems

--------------------------------------------------

HUMAN ANATOMY MODULE (3 ECTS)

1) Fundamentals of Biology and Histology

2) Anatomical terminology; classification of body systems, organs, tissues and membranes; regions and cavities of the human body

3) Functional anatomy of the human body systems: musculoskeletal, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive, endocrine, integumentary and nervous systems, and sensory organs.

## Teaching methods

The course is mainly delivered through lectures. Additional learning activities may include the use of anatomical models, diagnostic images, multimedia resources, and discussion of practical examples aimed at facilitating the understanding of the relationships between anatomical structure, physiological function, and diagnostic imaging techniques.

The teaching activities are designed to provide students with the knowledge required to understand the organization of the human body and the principles of the main diagnostic technologies used in healthcare settings, while promoting the integration of biological, anatomical, and engineering perspectives.

Lectures may be delivered online, in accordance with approved course regulations.

Teaching methods may be adapted in accordance with the guidelines of the University Disability Commission.

## Assessment methods

DIAGNOSTIC IMAGING AND RADIOTHERAPY MODULE

The exam consists of an oral part with 2/3 of the questions focused on the main topics covered in class. The final grade is given by the oral exam (50%) and the homework/thesis papers (50%)

HUMAN ANATOMY MODULE

Assessment of learning outcomes is based on a written in-course examination and, for students who do not take or do not pass this assessment, an oral examination during the official examination sessions.

In-course assessment

The assessment includes a written examination administered at the end of the course. The test consists of 31 multiple-choice questions.

Each correct answer is awarded 1 point; wrong or unanswered questions receive 0 points.

The total score is expressed in thirtieths.

Students who pass the written examination are exempt from the oral examination and may directly register the grade obtained.

Oral examination

Students who do not take, do not pass, or choose to reject the grade obtained in the written examination may sit the oral examination. The oral examination consists of an interview based on three or four questions on module topics.

--------------------------------------------------

The evaluation of the Integrated Course will take place simultaneously by sharing the marks reported in each module (Human anatomy Module -50% , Diagnostic Imaging Module -50%)

The assessment methods are consistent with the intended learning outcomes and allow a comprehensive evaluation of students' knowledge, application skills and appropriate use of discipline-specific terminology. _________________________________
