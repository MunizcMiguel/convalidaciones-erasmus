## Partition: 37143-1

### Language

Italian

### Learning outcomes

At the end of the course, the student should

- **know**the fundamentals of electrical circuit theory;
- **understand**and **be able to explain** the electric, magnetic, and electromagnetic phenomena;
- **be able to**solve problems related to time-constant or time-harmonic electric circuits;
- **be able to** read and understand other mathematical texts on related topics.

### Learning objectives

The objectives of this course are to provide the fundamentals of electrical engineering as related to biomedical systems. Such objectives contribute to the learning goals of the entire course of studies.

### Prerequisites

Physics II - Electromagnetism

### Contents

1. COURSE INTRODUCTION: Introduction to electrical- and bio-engineering. Practical notes on power systems: generation, transport and distribution, usage of electricity.  
  
2. CONDUCTIVE PHENOMENA: Fundamental laws. Conductivity properties of media. Ohm's Law. Joule's Law. Skin effect and cable design of a power line.  
  
3. CIRCUITS AND NETWORKS IN DIRECT CURRENT: Basic electric quantities. Resistance and conductance. Series and shunt connections, star and triangle connections. Transformation triangle-star. Ideal and real voltage and current souces. Transformation between voltage and current sources. Kirchhoff's Laws. Theorems of Thevenin and Norton. Theorem of Tellegen. Principle of superposition effects.  
  
4. DIELECTRIC PHENOMENA: Fundamental laws. Dielectric properties of media. Dielectric strength. Capacity definition and connections. Capacity of coaxial cables and parallel cylindrical conductors. Auto- and mutual-capacitance of a bi-wire or tri-wire line.  
  
5. MAGNETIC PHENOMENA: Fundamental laws. Magnetic properties of media. Circuitation Theorem. Magnetic field generated by an infinite rectilinear conductor. Faraday-Neumann-Lenz Law. Electromotive force induced in a conductor embedded in a time-varying magnetic field. Magnetic circuits. Hopkinson's law. Auto and mutual inductance of magnetic circuits. Perfect and not-perfect coupling. Coupling factor.  
  
6. CIRCUITS AND NETWORKS IN ALTERNATE CURRENT: Periodic alternate and sinusoidal quantities: definitions and terminology. Representation of isofrequencial sinusoidal functions by means of phasors and complex numbers. Instantaneous, active, reactive and apparent power in the sinusoidal regime. Circuit theory in alternate current: Method of nodes and meshes. Voltage drop and Power factor correction. Series and parallel resonances.  
  
7. THREE-PHASE SYSTEMS: Three-phase generation of electricity. Starry and chained voltages. Instantaneous, active, reactive and apparent power in symmetric and equilibrated three-phase systems. Voltage drop and Power factor correction of a three-phase system.  
  
8. ELECTRIC MACHINES. 8.1 TRANSFORMERS: General aspects and construction principles. Theory of single-phase transformer. Without load, with load and short-circuit functioning. Equivalent electric circuit of the single-phase transformer. Energetic performance. Basic concepts on three-phase transformers.  
8.2. ROTATING ELECTRIC MACHINES: General constructive aspects.  
Rotating magnetic field. Main electrical rotating machines and converters.  
  
9. BIOELECTROMAGNETIC INTERACTIONS: Human body model (dielectric properties of biologic tissues). Biological effects of electromagnetic fields at low and high frequencies.  
  
10. ELECTRIC SAFETY: Induced and contact currents. Earth connection of electric systems.  
Current dispersers and earth resistance. Safety protection devices

### Assessment methods

The written test consists of one exercise related to electrical problems similar to the ones already solved in class. A large number of previous written tests and corresponding solutions are available on the Module website. The oral examination will take place within the same or next exam sessions of the successful written test. It will cover the field of the written answers that need clarification, and additional subjects (belonging to the whole program of the course). Criteria of evaluation will be: the level of knowledge of the basic physical laws presented in the course, as well as the ability to apply them; the proper use of the scientific language; the clarity, logic and completeness of explanations. Based on the above mentioned evaluation criteria, the grades will reflect four preparation levels: - sufficient: basic understanding of theoric definitions and exercises; - adequate: being able to explain and solve some theoretical and practical problems; - good: being able to explain and solve most of theoretical and practical problems; - very good: mastering the main topics developing the skill to tackle new problems and theories autonomously.
