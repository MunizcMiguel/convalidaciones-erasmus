## Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module, the student should - have profound knowledge of basic principles of heat transfer and engineering thermodynamics; - have knowledge and understanding of numerical manipulations and orders of magnitude of various parameters; - understand and explain the physical mechanisms; - understand new situations and problems by developing his own equations; - demonstrate skill in the use of empirical and practical correlations and ability to calculate heat and work exchanged in thermal machine components; - demonstrate capacity for reading and understand other texts on related topics.

## Learning objectives

To give a basic understanding of the principles of heat transfer and engineering thermodynamics. Also, to require students the application of these basic principles to new situations and technical problems, and the development of their own equations. In addition, to familiarize the student with the numerical manipulations, units and orders of magnitude of various parameters which occur in the subject of heat transfer and engineering thermodynamics.

## Prerequisites

Knowledge of Mathematics II, Physics I and Chemistry is required. They are mandatory according to the degree program.

## Contents

PART I: HEAT TRANSFER. Heat diffusion: rectangular and cylindrical bodies, homogeneous and multi-layers, steady state and lumped transient models. Heat convection: boundary layer on a flat plate (laminar and turbulent), thermal boundary layer, empirical and practical correlations for forced and natural convection. Heat radiation: electromagnetic spectrum, thermal radiation, emissive power, radiation properties, Kirchhoff law, black and gray bodies, shape factors, radiation network. Radiation/convection heat transfer coefficient.

PART II: ENGINEERING THERMODYNAMICS. Elements of thermodynamics terminology, ideal and real gases, equation of state (EoS), the first law for closed and open systems, the second law for closed and open systems, gas Carnot thermal cycle, single- and multi-phase systems, state diagrams, gas turbine, steam-turbine power plant (Carnot thermal cycle).

## Teaching methods

Lectures, active learning discussion sessions and active problem solving classes. The aim is to understand the theoretical content of the course and enhance the problem-solving skills, as indicated in the section "Learning outcomes."

## Assessment methods

The exam is only written and consists of 3 questions concerning the main topics discussed in the course to be solved within 3 hours. In detail, 2 questions are focused to ascertain the knowledge of the heat transfer topic and 1 question the part related to the engineering thermodynamics. The questions always include a practical test in order to assess the problem-solving skills of the student. The optional possibility to split into two parts the written test is also provided which is meant to help the students to split the workload. If a student passes the first part of the written exam, for example the one related to heat transfer, he/she will take a final-term written test concerned with the second part of the course content only, in such a case engineering thermodynamics, but not before of two weeks. The final score is determined giving the same weight to the three questions.

Criteria of evaluation will be: the level of knowledge of the topics presented in the course, as well as the ability to apply them; the property of use of the scientific language; the clarity and completeness of explanations.
