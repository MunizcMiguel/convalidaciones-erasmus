## Learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

- demonstrate knowledge of the fundamental principles of rigid body mechanics, continuum mechanics, and structural mechanics;
- apply the methods and models of Structural Mechanics to the analysis of structural systems, determining equilibrium conditions, internal forces, stresses, strains, and displacements;
- identify the most appropriate analytical model and critically evaluate the obtained results;
- use the technical terminology of Structural Mechanics correctly when describing and discussing structural problems;
- independently deepen their knowledge of the subject and apply the acquired competences in subsequent courses in structural engineering.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the fundamental concepts of Structural Mechanics and the theoretical tools required for the modelling and analysis of the mechanical behaviour of rigid bodies, deformable continua, and elastic structures. The course contributes to the educational objectives of the Degree Programme by providing the foundations for structural analysis and for subsequent courses in structural and construction engineering.

## Prerequisites

The student must know the basics of Calculus, Linear Algebra and Physics, contained in the exams of Calculus I, II, Geometry and Physics.

## Contents

**Rigid Bodies:**

- Kinematics of rigid body systems.
- Statics of rigid body systems.
- Duality principle and the Principle of Virtual Work.

**Structural Theory:**

- Internal force resultants in beams.
- Formulation of the elastic problem for bars and beams.
- Principle of Virtual Work and displacement methods.
- Analysis of beam structures.

**Mechanics of Deformable Continua:**

- Kinematics and statics of the Cauchy continuum.
- Stress and strain states; equilibrium and compatibility equations.
- Linear elastic constitutive law and formulation of the elastic problem.

**Saint-Venant Problem:**

- Problem formulation and fundamental assumptions.
- Cross-sectional geometry.
- Solutions for axial loading, bending, shear, and torsion.
- Strength verification.

## Teaching methods

Lectures on theoretical topics, with discussion and proof of the outcomes; numerical exercises, with physical interpretation of the outcomes.

## Assessment methods

• Written exam, three hours long. Student must obtain the score of 18/30 and the weight of the written exam is 50% of the final evaluation. Written exam is made of exercises on: i) structures constituted by beams, ii) evaluation of the stress field and of the safety of a cross section of a De Saint Venant beam.  
• Oral exam, about thirty minutes long, on the theoretical part of the course and applications. The weight of the oral exam is 50% of the final evaluation.
