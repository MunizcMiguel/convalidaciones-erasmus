## Learning outcomes

Upon completion of the course, students will acquire basic and advanced knowledge of - differential and integral calculus for functions of several variables and optimization - differential and integral calculus for vector-valued functions - series of functions. Students will understand the fundamental definitions, the statements of the main theorems, and the assumptions required for their application. Students will be able to apply these tools to solve mathematical analysis problems. They will also be able to independently select the most appropriate method for tackling a problem, critically evaluate logical steps, and explain theoretical content, procedures, and results using rigorous and appropriate language, developing autonomy in learning more advanced mathematical content.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students should 1. know the basic techniques of the theory of series of functions, differential and integral calculus, functions of several variables, and vector-valued functions; 2. know and understand logical and analytical arguments; 3. understand and be able to explain the meaning of complex statements using mathematical notation and language; 4. understand the fundamental concepts of analysis and be aware of their potential applications in other fields of study and research; 5. be skilled in mathematical reasoning and calculation and be able to understand and apply a theorem; 6. be able to read and understand other scientific texts on related topics; 7. be able to solve a problem by applying the theorems studied.

## Prerequisites

The topics of the Mathematical Analysis 1 course, which is preparatory, and those of the Geometry course, which is strongly recommended.

## Contents

The course covers the following topics: - Series of functions: general theory, power series, and Fourier series. - Differential calculus for curves. - Differential calculus for real scalar functions of several variables: limits in several variables, partial derivatives, and directional differentiability. Free and constrained optimization. - Integral calculus in several variables. - Differential and integral calculus for vector-valued functions: integral calculus on curves and surfaces.Vector fields. Green's Theorem, Stokes' Theorem, and the Divergence Theorem. Most of the theorems required for the course topics will not be proved. The theorems will be presented with emphasis on the hypotheses and the thesis.Understanding the theorem statements will be necessary for the solution of the proposed exercises.

## Teaching methods

Face-to-face lessons. All lessons will be taught in person, using a tablet connected to the screen so everyone can read.Notes will then be uploaded to the Moodle platform. Students will be encouraged to read the topics covered in the textbook beforehand so as to be active during the lesson. One of the group exercises will consist of introducing and/or summarizing a topic from the syllabus to encourage dialogue and discussion in class. Exercises. The instructor assigns an exercise and gives students a few minutes to independently think about the solution and the questions to ask. The instructor then suggests a solution. The instructor assigns the exercises to the various groups, and the exercises are marked and graded. Students can contact the instructor for individual and/or group discussions.

## Assessment methods

Written exam: This consists of a 120-minute written test with six exercises, each with multiple questions, and a section testing understanding of the theorems and definitions presented during the lecture. Examples of the exercises included in the written exam, along with solution plans, are available on e-learning. To pass the written exam, students must have a score of 18/30 or higher. The instructor may request an oral interview for clarification on the written exam. Oral exam: Optional. The first question is chosen by the student. Group exercises: During the course, the instructor will propose group exercises for students. Groups will be formed automatically on Moodle. The exercises will be corrected and graded.The evaluation will contribute to the final grade.
