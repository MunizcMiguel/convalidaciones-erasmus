## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- apply basic computational and numerical knowledge;
- select the numerical method most appropriate for solving a given problem, taking its specific characteristics into account;
- implement numerical methods using the reference programming language, MATLAB;
- process, interpret, and present the results obtained, including in graphical form;
- assess numerical errors and analyze the convergence and stability properties of the methods employed;
- identify the main issues associated with the use of computers in the numerical solution of mathematical problems.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide students with the basic computational and numerical knowledge and skills needed to solve simple mathematical problems on a computer. In particular, the course introduces fundamental methods of Numerical Analysis for scientific and technological applications, with an emphasis on error, discretization, data and function approximation, convergence, stability, and the numerical integration of differential equations.

The course also aims to develop awareness of the potential and critical issues associated with using computers to solve mathematical problems.

## Prerequisites

Nobody. The course does not require prerequisites. It is advisable to have basic knowledge of Mathematical analysis, Linear Algebra, and Geometry.

## Contents

- Computer Arithmetic The numbers. Basis. Floating-point numbers and arithmetic. Errors in computation. Stability of algorithms. Condition number. - Nonlinear Equations Iterative methods. Convergent sequences. Existence and unicity theorems. Bisection algorithm. Fixed point iteration. Newton's methods. Stopping criteria. - Numerical linear algebra Linear systems; computational cost; errors and conditioning; estimate errors. Direct Methods for linear systems: Gauss and matrix factorizations. Iterative Methods for linear systems: Jacobi e Gauss-Seidel. Convergence theorems. Stopping criteria. - Polynomial Approximation Interpolation (Lagrange, Newton). Interpolation error. Least squares approximation. - Ordinary differential equations: Initial Value Problems. Explicit one-step methods (Euler, Heun e Runge Kutta). Upon successful completion of this module, the student should have; - Knowledge of the various methods described from the theoretical and algorithmic point of view; - Ability to apply the methods learned during the course to simple application exercises; - Properties of the mathematical terminology and correctness of the exact resolution of the calculations; - Ability and familiarity with the use and writing of simple programs in the Matlab environment; - Capacity for reading and understanding other scientific texts on related subjects.

## Teaching methods

The course is divided into lectures and practical lessons. The practical lessons will take place with the help of the PCs in the IT classrooms. The classes involve close interaction between the teacher and the students. The student will gradually use many of the primary methods of numerical computing illustrated during the lessons, which will be progressively used in the laboratory to demonstrate their practical use and potential. Students will be offered some laboratory exercises to integrate their learning at home.

## Assessment methods

Formative assessment: Informal discussions are used to stimulate a deeper student understanding. The lab exercises are the best environment to start short Q&A sessions about theoretical issues by proving the correctness of the answers through experiments. Conjectures and techniques conceived by the students are discussed in working groups to achieve the correct answer. Summative assessment: Written and oral exam (40%:60%). The written test consists of a Laboratory test in the computer didactic laboratory. The test consists of the resolution of simple numerical calculation problems using Matlab language with the development of script, function, and possible production of graphs. The oral test, lasting about half an hour, aims to ascertain the ability to expose the statements and proofs of theorems, definitions, examples/counterexamples, and the calculation techniques introduced (60%). Particular attention will be paid to the student's ability to connect the various topics.
