## Partition: MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS I (Cognomi A-G)

### Language

Italian

### Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should:  
1. understand the basics of limit theory, derivatives, optimization problems, integrals, and differential equations;  
2. understand and be able to explain theses and proofs in mathematical analysis;  
3. have strengthened their logical argumentation and mathematical calculation skills;  
4. be able to read and understand other scientific texts on related topics.

### Learning objectives

Knowledge and the ability to work with derivatives and integrals, essential tools for further studies of mathematical subjects, as well as for applications of mathematical techniques in Industrial Engineering. The Course will focus on understanding calculus concepts, analytical reasoning and developing crucial skills in order to calculate, analyse, interpret and communicate the results clearly.

### Prerequisites

A solid background in secondary school algebra, geometry and trigonometry, as contained in  
Mathematics Precourse Program  
  
http://www.ing.univaq.it/noniscritti/precorsi.php

### Contents

Complex numbers: introduction. Sequences. Functions. Limits. Continuity. Asymptotes. Derivatives. Linear approximation. Maximization. Mean value theorem. Second derivative. Convexity. L’Hospital’s theorem. Optimization problems. Series. Definite and Indefinite Integrals. Techniques of integration. The fundamental theorem of integral calculus. Differential equations.

### Teaching methods

All lectures are taught on the board or using images projected in classrooms. Lecture notes will be made available on the Moodle platform for students to subscribe to.  
Much of the work in this course will require students to work in cooperative learning groups. For mathematical problem solving, group sizes of around three students seem to work best. Students will be encouraged to work in groups on in-class or lab activities, as well as on homework problems. While some students enjoy group work more than others, working well in a group is an important skill for life beyond the mathematics classroom. Many of our graduates tell us that the skills they develop while working in cooperative learning groups in our classes have been very useful in the workplace. Problem solving is a social activity. A major goal of any mathematics course is to help students learn to think about problems mathematically and to solve problems independently. Working in small groups, doing lab work, and talking about problems with other students are all strategies to help the student achieve these goals.  
In the case of particular external events, lessons will be taught online and appropriate writing programs or the graphic board will be used. Group work will still be encouraged, if necessary in online mode.

### Assessment methods

The final exam consists of a preliminary written test of a practical nature with an evaluation, which must be passed in order to access eventually to the theoretical oral exam with an evaluation of thirty.

### Reference texts
