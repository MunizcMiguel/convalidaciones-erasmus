## Language

The course is held in Italian language.

## Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this course, the students should be able to:  
- assess the technical-economic viability of an industrial investment;  
- develop the sizing of a production plant;  
- use the main project management techniques to manage the main phases of plant construction;  
- define the best equipment layout with respect to the kind of product manufactured and production volumes forecast;  
- identify the correct holding techniques in specific manufacturing environments;  
- use the main techniques for time and work study;  
- decide on the use of the best maintenance techniques with respect to company equipment;  
- carry out the sizing of the plant main auxiliary systems (heating system, electrical system, combined heat and power generation; industrial water supply and distribution; fire-fighting systems; production and distribution of compressed air).

## Learning objectives

The aim of the course is to provide the student with knowledge of the fundamentals of manufacturing and plants in which it is carried out.

## Prerequisites

Mathematics

## Contents

Introduction to industrial manufacturing: structure of manufacturing systems and classification criteria for industrial enterprises; the manufacturing system in the industrial enterprise. Development of an industrial initiative: market analysis; production capacity sizing; plant location. Feasibility Study: technical - economic - financial assessments; profitability indexes (PBP, NPV, DCFRR); investment risk analysis. Manufacturing system design: plant layout; continuous and batch production; Economic Production Quantity; sizing of the means of production; queuing theory. Project planning: program evaluation techniques and their use in the design, construction and operation of industrial plants (PERT and CPM). Work measurement and organization: Taylor and the principles of scientific management; Methods and Time Measurement; stopwatch time study; predetermined times methods (Work Factor, MTM); work sampling; manpower sizing; Design for Manufacturing and Assembly. Inventory management: raw materials and finished goods storage; Economic Order Quantity; quantity discounts; techniques of inventory management; safety stock. Reliability: fundamentals; components and systems reliability; Fault Tree Analysis. Plant maintenance: maintenance policies (fault maintenance, preventive maintenance, predictive maintenance); Total Productive Maintenance. Auxiliary systems: piping; Hardy Cross method; industrial water supply and distribution; fire-fighting system; heating system; electrical system; Combined Heat and Power generation; production and distribution of compressed air; lighting systems; dust and gaseous effluent collection and purification systems.

## Teaching methods

Teaching activities used in the course (and, respectively, student learning activities) are:  
- lectures (theoretical understanding);  
- problem solving classes (problem solving skills);  
- participation in tutorials, team work (communication skills);  
- prepare written reports (synthesis and application skills).

## Assessment methods

Pre-assessment; there is no formal pre-assessment, however, during first lecture the lecturer communicates to students required basic knowledge and invites them to fill any gap. Formative assessment: the active learning is stimulated and supported by participation in tutorials and team work supervised by the teacher. Summative assessment: oral examination consisting of: 1. the discussion of team works developed during the course, personally revised by each student in form of written report which must be submitted at least one week before the date of examination; the discussion is aimed at assessing student’s understanding and solving skills with respect to the problems concerning the topics covered during lectures and his/her synthesis and application skills; the ranking of written report contributes 40% to examination total mark. 2. an oral test, consisting of two questions concerning the main topics of the course, aimed at assessing student’s knowledge and comprehension skills regarding theoretical principles of the subject; the ranking of oral test contributes 60% to examination total mark. In order to pass the exam, the student must demonstrate the following minimum knowledge and skills: - knowledge of the basic terminology of production engineering; - know how to assess the technical-economic viability of an industrial investment; - know how to develop the sizing of a production plant; - know how to apply PERT and CPM; - know how to design equipment and plant layout; - know how to apply the main holding techniques; - know how to use the main techniques for time and work measurement; - know how to size the plant main auxiliary systems. Grades will reflect four levels of preparation: - sufficient: basic knowledge topics; - adequate: basic skills in problem solving; - good: skills in rigorous thinking and communication; - very good: ability in drawing connections with subjects of other industrial engineering courses.
