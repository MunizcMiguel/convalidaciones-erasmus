## Learning outcomes

Upon completion of the course, students will be able to:

- master the basic knowledge needed to address and solve problems encountered in professional chemical engineering practice;  
- be able to identify, describe, and model basic transfer phenomena between phases and the main separation operations in the chemical industry;  
- independently assess, based on the supported classroom activities, the relative importance of the transport phenomena involved in order to effectively simplify solution models;  
- improve their professional communication skills through group work recommended by the instructors;  
- adequately master further study, both institutional and independent, thanks to the discussions and cultural input provided in the classroom.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to provide the student with the basic knowledge necessary to face and solve the problems encountered in the chemical engineering practice, particularly for transfer phenomena occurring in the separation operations.  
  
After passing the exam, the student will be able to identify and govern the phenomena that occur during separations, to choose the best-operating methods, and to obtain useful information for the actual design of the equipment that he will meet later of studies.  
  
The topics covered in the module include:  
  
macroscopic and microscopic balance equations;  
equilibrium and transfer rate between phases;  
basic transfer equations: Newton, Fourier, and Fick;  
momentum, heat, and mass transfer fundamentals;  
graphical techniques for the resolution of mass balances; retrieval of thermodynamic information;  
  
selection of the most appropriate unit operation; single-stage calculations and multi-stage arrangements; design and verification problems; mechanical separations.  
  
At the end of the lessons, after passing the exam, the student will be able to:  
  
identify the nature, typology, time of contact, and transfer rate between the phases; know the physical phenomena that govern the unit operations;  
  
identify the most appropriate unit operation for a given separation problem;  
  
identify the operating parameters that influence separation performance; formulate and resolve mass balances both in analytical and graphic form; calculate the number of ideal stages necessary for a given separation; predict the effect of varying the operating parameters of separation equipment;  
  
know and apply the fundamental laws at the base of mechanical separations.

## Contents

Introductory framework of the subjects; concept of unit operation; description of the transfer phenomena between the phases in the primary unit operations; driving force, thermodynamic limits and rate of transfer phenomena between phases.  
Principles of conservation and conservative entities: momentum, energy, matter. Transfer rate. Generalities on microscopic and macroscopic balances. Constitutive equations. Fundamental laws of the transfer: Newton, Fourier, Fick. Simple one-dimensional examples of transfer rate calculation. Fundamentals of fluid dynamics: laminar and turbulent motion, concept of head loss and coefficient of friction. Tools and methods for calculating the pressure drop in simple equipment. Bases of energy transport: conduction in solids, forced and natural convection in fluids. Tools and methods for calculating temperature profiles in simplified models. Transfer coefficients between phases and practical use of correlations and diagrams for their calculation. Fundamentals of matter transfer: binary diffusion in solids and fluids. Concept of analogy with energy transport with simple applications. Dimensionless groups and semi-empirical correlations. Main flow patterns. Reading and use of phase distribution diagrams for the main equilibrium cases, their construction starting from experimental data. Lever-arm rule, graphic techniques for calculating currents. Equilibrium stage concept with single stage calculation examples. Continuous and discontinuous equipments; general information on the flow diagrams in equi- and countercurrent; physical and mathematical models of transfer equipment; contact time; Double film model. Material balances around separation equipment; graphic techniques for the resolution of balance equations, operating lines; efficiency of the effective stage; calculation of the equivalent number of theoretical stages; concept of HTU and NTU. Examples of mechanical separations: filtration, ultrafiltration, centrifugation, cyclone separator.  
Case studies for practical application to the various unit operations on simple mixtures: absorption; flash vaporization; batch distillation; fractional distillation; liquid-liquid extraction, adsorption, leaching; mechanical separations.

## Assessment methods

Formative assessment: Informal discussions are used to stimulate the student's understanding. During lectures and classroom exercises, the teacher actively involves students with questions on the topics covered and encourages them to come to the solution of problems consciously.  
  
Assessment of learning outcomes: written and oral exam. The final assessment takes into account the student's overall performance.  
The written test (three hours) is conducted leaving the candidate free to use any source except the internet, and consists of three exercises, at least two of which must be addressed. The oral exam focuses on the discussion of the written paper and on practical cases involving the application of the topics analyzed during the lectures. The average duration ranges from about twenty minutes (in the case of answers given quickly and correctly), up to an hour if the candidate hesitates in answering and requires time to think. The level of sufficiency corresponds to the demonstration of the ability to identify the methodological tools to be used, to correctly set up the model equations and to indicate viable ways of solving the problem. The level of excellence is achieved when the student proves able, directed by the teacher, to effectively deal with aspects of the problems not explicitly covered in class. The assessment depends on the level of exposure and the degree of confidence shown with the teaching topics and with the methodological tools whose use has been described in the course.
