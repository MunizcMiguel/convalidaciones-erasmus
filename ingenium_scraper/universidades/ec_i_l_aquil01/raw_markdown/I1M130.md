## Learning outcomes

Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to:

Knowledge and understanding

- Explain the fundamental techniques used in the analysis of energy systems.
- Explain the operating principles of the main fluid machines and heat exchange components.
- Interpret the thermodynamic meaning of different plant configurations and their effects on energy performance.

Applying knowledge and understanding

- Apply the criteria for the selection and use of machines and heat exchange components.
- Analyse the behaviour of individual machines and components and evaluate their performance within integrated energy systems.

Making judgements

- Compare different plant configurations and critically evaluate their advantages, limitations and energy implications.

Communication skills

- Correctly use the technical and scientific terminology of fluid machinery and energy systems to describe phenomena, components and plants.

Learning skills

- Independently deepen knowledge of related topics through the consultation of university textbooks, technical documentation and specialist literature.

## Learning objectives

The course aims to introduce students to the study of fluid machinery and energy conversion systems from natural sources. After some reminders and expansions of the main concepts of applied thermodynamics, it addresses the thermodynamic analysis of thermal power plants fed by fossil fuels. Then, it deals with the study of operating principles and application criteria of fluid turbomachinery, both driving and driven units, volumetric machines, thermal power plants and their components.

The course contributes to the educational objectives of the Degree Programme by providing the knowledge and methodological tools required for the analysis and performance assessment of machines, thermal power plants and energy systems.

## Prerequisites

The complete understanding of the topics covered in the course requires knowledge and basic skills gained in the course Heat Transfer and Engineering Thermodynamics, with particular reference to the principles of thermodynamics, energy balances, and the thermodynamic cycles of thermal power plants.

As specified in the section “Propedeuticità” of the Teaching Regulations of the CdS, it is advisable to take the exam of Machines and Thermal Power Plants after having acquired the contents of Heat Transfer and Engineering Thermodynamics.

## Contents

Topics of the module include:

THERMODYNAMICS APPLIED TO MACHINES AND THERMAL POWER PLANTS. Classification of machines and engines, conventional and non-conventional energy conversion systems, energy conservation principle, compression and expansion and their efficiencies, typical efficiencies of thermal power plants, estimation of the cost of electricity.

TURBOMACHINES. Generalities, axial and radial machines, impulse and reaction stages; nozzles and diffusers; the Euler work equation for driving and driven machines; velocity triangles, stages of axial and radial machines, impulse and reaction stages of turbines; degree of reaction; blade and stage efficiencies; turbine losses, recovery factor, moisture in steam turbines; comparison between impulse and reaction stages.

STEAM POWER PLANTS. Generalities, methods to improve the efficiency of a steam power plant, thermodynamic regeneration, deaerator and surface heat exchangers, combustion processes, elements on the operation of steam generators.

GAS TURBINE POWER PLANTS. Generalities, study of gas turbine cycles, thermodynamic regeneration, gas turbines in single and double shaft schemes, gas-steam combined cycles, gas turbines for aircraft propulsion.

## Teaching methods

Frontal lessons and classroom exercises.

During the lectures, all the topics of the course will be developed gradually by the teacher who will use chalkboards extensively to present and illustrate energy balances, thermodynamic cycles, operating principles, power plant schemes and main equations that characterize their behavior and performance.

During the exercises, by means of oral questions addressed to the whole class or to groups of students, a fruitful interaction between the teacher and the students will be promoted so that they appreciate the close connection between the practical part and the theoretical part.

## Assessment methods

FORMATIVE ASSESSMENT. The assessment of the learning process is carried out through interaction between the teacher and the students during lectures and classroom exercises. Students are involved in discussions and in proposing strategies for solving exercises through oral questions addressed to the whole class or to groups of students.

SUMMATIVE ASSESSMENT. Written and oral examination (30%/70%). The examination includes an oral discussion preceded by the solution of exercises on specific topics. These exercises may be waived upon successful completion of partial assessments carried out during the course. It is important that students view the exercise component and the oral component as closely interconnected rather than as separate stages of preparation and assessment.

The written examination is aimed at verifying the ability to apply the acquired knowledge to the analysis of machines, components and energy systems, as well as the ability to formulate and solve engineering thermodynamics and power plant problems. The oral examination is aimed at verifying knowledge and understanding of the operating principles of the machines and power plants covered in the course, the ability to interpret their behaviour, and the ability to critically discuss alternative plant configurations and technical solutions.

The final grade will reflect the level of knowledge and understanding of the fundamental topics covered in the course, the ability to apply the acquired concepts to the analysis of machines and energy systems, the autonomy of judgement in the evaluation of different technical solutions, the correct use of scientific terminology, and the clarity and completeness of the discussion.
