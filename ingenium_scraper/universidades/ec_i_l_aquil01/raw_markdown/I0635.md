## Learning outcomes

Upon completion of the course, students should:  
- have a thorough understanding of basic definitions and concepts in measurement;  
- have a thorough understanding of basic concepts in measurement theory;  
- have a knowledge and understanding of the operating principles of basic electronic instruments, such as voltmeters, ammeters, meters, digital oscilloscopes, spectrum analyzers, and wattmeters;  
- demonstrate the ability to define and select the best problem-solving strategies using electrical measurement results as a starting point;  
- be able to set up a measurement, choosing the appropriate methods, tools, and data processing techniques, and determine the uncertainty of the final result, including in relation to applicable regulations;  
- demonstrate skill and ability in using the main instruments for measuring voltage and current and associated quantities, such as power, energy, frequency, time, and impedance;  
- understand and be able to explain the validity and reliability of measurements performed;  
- demonstrate the ability to communicate results clearly and coherently.

## Learning objectives

The goal of this course is to provide the measurement and working principles of various instruments and devices used to measure electrical and electronic parameters. On successful completion of this module, the student should understand the fundamental concepts of measurement theory and should be aware of potential applications of basic electronic instrumentation in the different engineering fields.

## Prerequisites

There is no formal pre-assessment, but passing an Electrotechnical exam and an Electronics exam are the prerequisites for taking the final exam.

## Contents

Topics covered in the module include: fundamentals of measurement theory; quality of measurements; standards; measurement of electrical resistance and impedance; operating principles of digital electronic uinstruments; electronic counters; main sensors and transducers; data acquisition systems; digital oscilloscopes; spectrum analyzers; electronic wattmeters; protection from electrical risk in the instrumentation. On successful completion of this module, the student should: - have profound knowledge of basic concepts and definitions in measurement; - have profound knowledge of basic measurement theory; - have knowledge and understanding of working principle of basic electronic instrumentation, such as voltmeters, ammeters, counters, digital scopes, spectrum analyzers, wattmeters; - demonstrate skill in main instruments and ability to measure voltage and current signals and their associated quantities, such as power, energy, frequency, time and impedance; - understand and explain the validity and the reliability of measures taken from a given system attached to a signals source; - demonstrate capacity for defining and selecting appropriate strategies to solve problems using electrical measurements as the subject; - demonstrate capacity to communicate the results of technical works in a clear and coherent way, with the generation of systematic and meaningful documentation for any designed and assembled system.

## Teaching methods

Lectures and laboratory activities.

## Assessment methods

The formative assessment of the learning process is carried out through a continuous interaction between the teacher and the students, during the lessons and the laboratory activities. Summative assessment: verification of the student's ability to understand and know how to apply knowledge in the field of electrical measurements through an oral examination, during which any three topics from the course, proposed by the teacher, will be discussed. The evaluation criteria will be: the level of knowledge of measurement theory and of the main tools and measurement techniques presented in the course, the ability to apply knowledge, the communication skills and the scientific language used, the clarity and completeness of explanations, the critical sense.
