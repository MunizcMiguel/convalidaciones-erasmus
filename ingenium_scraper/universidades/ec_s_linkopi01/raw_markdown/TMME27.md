# Mekanik I

- **Credits**: 10 hp (ECTS)

## Intended learning outcomes

The course familiarises students with the laws and methods of classical mechanics and practice in applying these. After the course the student is expected to:

- know and understand central concepts, models and methods of statics, particle dynamics and rigid body dynamics.
- have acquired a systematic approach to the solution of engineering problems based on the chain idealization - mathematical formulation - interpretation of results.
- be able to critically assess results of calculations, both with respect to the suitability of the model and its correct application.

## Course content

- First part. Statics: force systems in two and three dimensions, couple, moment vector, reduction of force systems, support conditions, free body diagrams, force equilibrium, moment equilibrium, Coulomb’s law of friction. Particle dynamics: Newton’s laws of motion, gravitation, velocity and acceleration in cartesian, polar and natural coordinates, work and energy, power, impulse and momentum, impact, linear oscillations, initial conditions.
- Second part. Rigid body dynamics in the plane: velocity and accleration for absolute and relative motion, instantaneous center of zero velocity, Euler’s equations of motion, work and energy, impulse and momentum, angular impulse and moment of momentum. Rigid body dynamics in three dimensions: time differentiation of a vector in rotating coordinates, moment of momentum, the inertia matrix, Euler’s equations of motion in 3D, work and energy, forces of imbalance, gyroscopic motion.

## Teaching and working methods

The course is given as lectures and classes. The course runs over the entire autumn semester.

## Examination

| TEN2 | Written examination | 5 credits | U, 3, 4, 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| TEN1 | Written examination | 5 credits | U, 3, 4, 5 |

An examination is held after each subcourse to assess the student’s knowledge. The examinations are designed with a maximum score of 15 points. 6 points are passing.

For grade 3 the student must show basal understanding for the theoretical concepts included in the course and ability to apply the course content on engineering problems.

For grade 5 the student must show very good understanding for the theoretical concepts included in the course and very good ability to apply the course content on engineering problems.

The criteria for grade 4 are essentially the same as for grade 5, but some deficiencies can be accepted.
