## Partition: MANUFACTURING I (Iniziali cognome A-K)

### Contents

1. Foundry Techniques (1.7 ECTS) Study of solidification and cooling of castings; cooling modulus; shrinkage phenomena; residual stresses. Design of the pattern, gating system, and feeding system. Techniques for the production of expendable moulds (sand moulding). Main characteristics of permanent mould casting. Typical casting defects. 2. Plastic Deformation Processes (1.3 ECTS) Mechanisms of hot and cold plastic deformation. Bulk deformation processes (Forging, Rolling, Extrusion, Drawing). Calculation of forces and power requirements. Introduction to sheet-metal forming processes. 3. Machining Processes (2.5 ECTS) Fundamentals of cutting processes: orthogonal cutting; mechanics of chip formation and shear-plane models; cutting forces. Cutting tools and tool geometry; rake and clearance angles in turning tools. Tool wear mechanisms; optimization of machining operations. Process parameters and main characteristics of the conventional machining techniques (Turning, Milling, Drilling) and related machine tools. Process planning and machining cycles. 4. Overview of Main Conventional Welding Techniques (0.5 ECTS) Characteristics and applications of the principal conventional welding processes.

### Reference texts

### Learning objectives

The course aims to give the students the knowledge of the principal manufacturing techniques, focusing on the main conventional material-processing methods (Foundry, Machining, Plastic Deformation, and Welding). The objective is to enable students to develop a basic understanding of the technical challenges inherent to production processes and to identify and structure the technological procedures most suitable for manufacturing a given component. The course introduces simplified yet effective methodological approaches for performing, selecting, and determining the data required for the design of Foundry, Machining, and Plastic Deformation processes. Students will also develop the ability to: (i) use tables and charts for selecting tools and process parameters; (ii) determine the appropriate machine tool dimensions for Machining and Plastic Deformation operations; and (iii) prepare and interpret a process sheet. Upon completion of the course, students will be able to: - critically compare different technological solutions and identify the most appropriate one; - acquire and employ the terminology specific to manufacturing processes; - integrate diverse learning resources (textbooks, including those in English, online videos, and technical catalogues).

### Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of algebra, geometry, and mathematical analysis (including trigonometry, algebraic equations, function analysis, and introductory concepts of differential and integral calculus).

### Teaching methods

In‑class lectures combined with theoretical and applied practice sessions.

### Assessment methods

The assessment is designed to evaluate both the ability to solve numerical exercises (Practical Test) and the ability to explain theoretical concepts (Theoretical Test). Practical Test (duration: 2 hours) The practical test requires students to solve exercises related to: - foundry processes, - machining processes, - plastic deformation processes. Each exercise is assigned a score, expressed in thirtieths, as indicated on the test sheet. Theoretical Test Students are admitted to the theoretical test only after passing the practical test with a minimum score of 16/30. The theoretical test may be administered either as a written exam or as an oral examination (20–30 minutes). It assesses knowledge of: - main issues related to foundry processes (sand casting and permanent mould casting) and the most common techniques for mould production; - characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages of bulk plastic deformation processes (Forging, Rolling, Extrusion, Drawing); - mechanics of chip formation; tool angles and their effect on machining; tool wear mechanisms; main characteristics of turning, milling, and drilling operations; process planning; - characteristics and execution methods of the main conventional welding techniques. Both tests are managed through “partial examinations” on ESSE3 and are based on the types of exercises solved in class as well as on the topics covered during lectures. The final grade is calculated as the average of the two tests (rounded up). Minimum Learning Requirements Practical Test: For each proposed exercise, students must demonstrate at least the ability to set up a conceptually correct solution procedure. It is not permitted to leave any of the exercises completely unanswered. Theoretical Test: For each question, students must show adequate command of the subject matter and appropriate technical vocabulary, as well as a sufficient ability to independently elaborate the required concepts, including through the use of schemes and diagrams where appropriate. It is not permitted to lack knowledge of any of the proposed topics.

### Learning objectives

The student identifies and describes the main manufacturing processes used in the production of industrial components, along with their related physical principles, machinery, tools, technological parameters, fields of application, advantages, and limitations. The student analyzes a component and selects the appropriate manufacturing processes; he designs the manufacturing cycle, identifying operational sequences, tools, equipment, machines, and process parameters consistent with the geometric and functional requirements. The student can critically compare alternative technological solutions, evaluating achievable quality, productivity, costs, process limitations, defects, and the sustainability of production choices in accordance with the technical specifications provided The student explains how the processes work using correct technical and scientific language, justifies technological choices, clearly discusses parameters, equipment, tools, and expected results, and develops manufacturing cycles suitable for production. The student uses independent study methods to gain a deeper understanding of traditional and advanced manufacturing processes, consulting technical texts, handouts, and industrial documentation.

### Teaching methods

Teaching activities are organized into lectures, guided practical exercises, and discussions of application-oriented case studies related to industrial components and manufacturing processes. Instruction is delivered through traditional blackboard lectures, PowerPoint presentations, and educational videos. Lectures introduce the fundamental concepts and physical principles underlying manufacturing processes, machine tools, cutting tools, process parameters, and their fields of application. Practical exercises develop students' ability to design manufacturing processes by selecting appropriate processes and cutting tools, defining production sequences, and calculating machining parameters, cutting forces, and power requirements. Attendance is not compulsory but is strongly recommended. Course materials are made available through the Microsoft Teams platform and are regularly updated throughout the semester. Working students, students with disabilities, specific learning disorders, or other specific needs are encouraged to contact the instructor to discuss appropriate support measures. Total CFU: 6. Total student workload: 150 hours. Face-to-face teaching hours (including practical exercises): 60 hours. Online teaching hours: 0. Individual study hours: 90.

### Assessment methods

The assessment is designed to evaluate both the ability to solve numerical problems (PRACTICAL test) and the ability to explain theoretical concepts (THEORETICAL test). The PRACTICAL test (2 hours) requires students to solve numerical problems related to casting processes, machining processes, bulk metal forming processes. The score assigned to each problem is indicated in the examination paper and expressed on a 30-point scale. The THEORETICAL test, which students may take only after passing the PRACTICAL test with a minimum score of 16/30, may be administered either as a written examination or as an oral examination (lasting approximately 20–30 minutes). It is intended to assess students' knowledge of the main issues associated with casting processes (sand casting and permanent mould casting) and the most widely used mould-making techniques; the characteristics, advantages, and limitations of the main bulk metal forming processes (forging, rolling, extrusion, and drawing); chip formation mechanics; cutting tool geometry and its influence on machining; cutting tool wear; the main characteristics of turning, milling, and drilling operations; and machining process planning; the characteristics and operating principles of the main conventional welding processes. Both assessments are managed through the ESSE3 examination platform and are based on the types of problems solved during practical sessions and on the topics covered in lectures. The final grade is calculated as the arithmetic mean of the PRACTICAL and THEORETICAL test scores, rounded up to the nearest whole number. Minimum learning requirements for the PRACTICAL test: for each proposed problem, students must demonstrate at least the ability to formulate a conceptually correct solution procedure. Leaving any problem completely unanswered is not permitted. Minimum learning requirements for the THEORETICAL test: for each question, students must demonstrate an adequate command of the subject matter and appropriate technical terminology, together with the ability to independently organize and explain the concepts, including through the use of diagrams and sketches where appropriate. A complete lack of knowledge of any of the required topics is not acceptable. The final grade is expressed on a 30-point scale. Grading criteria 18–20: Basic knowledge of the topics, with a minimum ability to describe the main manufacturing processes and their fundamental parameters. 21–23: Fair knowledge of the topics and satisfactory ability to describe the main manufacturing processes and their fundamental parameters. 24–26: Good knowledge of the course contents and correct application of the main concepts. 27–29: Thorough knowledge of the subjects, ability to establish connections among different topics, and autonomy in selecting appropriate technological solutions. 30–30 cum laude: Comprehensive, critical, and integrated knowledge of the course contents, with excellent analytical, synthesis, reasoning, and argumentation skills. Students with disabilities or Specific Learning Disorders (SLD) will be provided with the reasonable accommodations and compensatory/support measures identified according to their individual needs and in compliance with the applicable legislation.
