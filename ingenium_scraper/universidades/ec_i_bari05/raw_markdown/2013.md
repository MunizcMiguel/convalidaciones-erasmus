Eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Topic 2 Strain. 1,2 CFU (lesson 0,8 CFU = 8 hours + practice 0,4 CFU = 4 hours) Deformation of continuum bodies. Strain measures. Linear theory. Principal stretches and principal direction of strain. Examples of elementary deformations. Representation formula of infinitesimal strain. Compatibility equations. Topic 3 Stress. 1,2 CFU (lesson 0,8 CFU = 8 hours + practice 0,4 CFU = 4 hours) Force system. Axioms of Euler. Stress tensor. Equilibrium equations. Principal stresses and principal directions of stress. Mohr’s circle. Examples of elementary states of stress. General theorems: work done and virtual work. Topic 4 Constitutive equations and the elastic problem. 1,0 CFU (lesson 0,8 CFU= 8 hours + practice 0,2 CFU = 2 hours) General aspects. Linear elastic and hyperelastic materials. Statement of the elastic equilibrium problem. Energy theorems. Unicity theorem of Kirchhoff. Elastic isotropy. General aspects. Representation formula. Technical moduli and restrictions on their values. Topic 5 Saint-Venant’s problem. 1,5 CFU (lesson 1,0 CFU= 10 hours + practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) General formulation. Normal force. Bending. Torsion. Shear. Combined normal force and bending. Topic 6 Strength criteria. 0,8 CFU (lesson 0,8 CFU = 8 hours) General aspects. Maximum and minimum normal stress criterion. Maximum shear stress criterion. Maximum and minimum strain criterion. Energy criterion. Safety evaluation. Topic 7 Statically determinate beams and frames – Kinematics. 1,5 CFU (lesson 1,0 CFU= 10 hours + practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) Preliminary concepts of kinematics of rigid bodies. Plane case. Linear kinematics of rigid bodies. Definition of beam. Plane beam. Kinematic characterization of external and internal constraints. Kinematic analysis. Theorem of kinematical chains. Topic 8 Statically determinate beams and frames – Statics. 1,5 CFU (lesson 1,0 CFU= 10 hours + practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) Force systems for beams. Axioms of equilibrium. Definition of internal forces. Equilibrium theorems for plane beams. Static characterization of external and internal constraints. Static analysis of beams and frames. Solution of problems on statically determinate structures. Principle of virtual works. Topic 9 Elastic beams. 1,5 CFU (lesson 1,0 CFU= 10 hours + practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) Elastic beams: Elastica for normal force, shear, bending and torsion; thermal deformations. Virtual work equation. Evaluation of displacements and rotations. Topic 10 Statically indeterminate beams and frames. 1,0 CFU (lesson 0,5 CFU= 5 hours + practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) Solution of statically indeterminate beams and frames. Force method and Moment method. Topic 11 Design and safety evaluation of structures. 0,5 CFU (practice 0,5 CFU = 5 hours) Solution of simple problems on design and safety evaluation of structures.

### Learning objectives

1. Knowledge of the basic tools for the description of the mechanical behavior of one-dimensional elastic structures and of elastic bodies. In particular, the student must understand and operate with the fundamental issues of deformation, stress states and constitutive equations with particular attention to the case of linearly elastic structures and solids. 2. Knowledge of the fundamental concepts of the strenght of materials both from a theoretical and experimental point of view.

### Prerequisites

Vector algebra and calculus,fundamental operations on force systems, kinematics and statics of rigid bodies

### Teaching methods

Lectures in the classroom, guided exercises in the classroom

### Assessment methods

The exam consists of an oral examination preceded by a written test, and concerns both modules I and II. The written test is a partial test with a typical duration of 3 hours. It concerns problems aimed at assessing the student's knowledge in the analysis of statically determinate and indeterminate structures, in the determination and analysis of stress and strain, in the design and in the assessment of structures. The minimum requirements for passing the written test, corresponding to the achievement of the mark of 18/30, are to demonstrate sufficient knowledge of the methodologies for the solution of the proposed exercises. To current students is offered the exemption from the written test by passing two partial tests scheduled in the suitable periods of the academic calendar, concerning the same topics listed above about the written test. Passing the written test gives access to the final oral examination. The latter is aimed at assessing the ability to set up applicative problems and the theoretical knowledge of: strain analysis, stress analysis, constitutive equations, elastic problem and Saint-Venant problem, strength criteria. The minimum requirements for passing the oral examination, corresponding to the achievement of the mark of 18/30, are to demonstrate sufficient competence and property of language on the topics of the interview, as well as a sufficient ability to re-elaborate independently the concepts, also through diagrams and graphics. Not fully knowing one of the required topics is not allowed. The final grade of the exam represents an overall assessment of the student's knowledge, as emerged from both the written test and the oral examination. For students who have passed the exemption tests, the oral exam concerns only theoretical topics. In accordance with the University regulations, on explicit demand by a student, it is allowed to take the exam only through an interview. In this case, the oral examination is divided into a first part about the solution of problems of the same type as those listed above for the written test; the same criteria of the written test are employed for the evaluation. If the student passes this first part, then he is admitted to the second part of the examination. For the latter, the same procedure and the same evaluation criteria exposed above for the oral examination are adopted.
