### Contents

The course is worth 9 CFU, for a total of 90 hours of in-person instruction, divided into Part I — Technical Drawing and Part II — 3D CAD and Technical Documentation. Part I — Technical Representation Topic 1 — Introduction to Industrial Technical Drawing (7.5 HOURS – 0.75 CFU) The role of drawing in engineering. Course objectives, syllabus, and instructional materials. Definition of technical drawing, standards, product life cycle, classification of drawings, and drawing tools. History and evolution of industrial technical drawing. Topic 2 — Projection Methods (12.5 HOURS – 1.25 CFU) Classification of projection methods and an introduction to descriptive geometry. Theory of projections, representation methods, practical projection methods, special cases of representation, and standards for views. Topic 3 — Standards for Industrial Drawing (15 HOURS – 1.5 CFU) Standards for technical representation: formats, title blocks, scales, lines, text, views, cuts, and sections. Representation of sectioned surfaces, specific conventions, criteria for indicating dimensions, types of dimensioning, errors to avoid, and exercises. Topic 4 — Introduction to Using AutoCAD (5 HOURS – 0.5 CFU) Introduction to the model space, drawing and editing commands, snaps, layers, annotations, paper space, blocks, and printing. Topic 5 — Manufacturing Errors (10 HOURS – 1 CFU) Manufacturing errors, dimensional tolerances, types of fits, and calculation of ISO tolerances. Tolerances in industrial practice. Geometric tolerances: classification, notation on drawings, tolerances of form, orientation, and position. Definition and notation of surface roughness. Topic 6 — Machine Elements and Assembly Drawings (10 HOURS – 1 CFU) Functionality, use, and realistic, conventional, simplified, and symbolic representation of machine elements: threads, threaded connections, shaft-hub elements, transmission components, supports, seals, and gaskets. Reading and interpreting assembly drawings. Total Part I: 60 hours – 6 CFU Topic 6 — Machine Components and Assembly Drawings (10 HOURS – 1 CFU) Functionality, use, and realistic, conventional, simplified, and symbolic representation of machine components: threads, threaded connections, shaft-hub assemblies, transmission components, supports, seals, and gaskets. Reading and interpreting assembly drawings. Total Part I: 60 hours – 6 CFU Part II — 3D CAD and Technical Documentation Topic 7 — Introduction to 3D CAD Modeling (5 HOURS – 0.5 CFU) Introduction to three-dimensional modeling, the CAD environment, parametric logic, and the relationship between 3D models and technical documentation. Topic 8 — Constrained Sketching and Part Modeling (7.5 HOURS – 0.75 CFU) Technical sketching, constrained sketching, geometric and dimensional constraints, part modeling, main modeling functions, and lab exercises. Topic 9 — Modeling, Model Modification, and Feature Management (7.5 HOURS – 0.75 CFU) Development and modification of CAD models, feature editing, parameterization management, modeling exercises, and model modification. Topic 10 — Drafting and Technical Documentation from CAD (7.5 HOURS – 0.75 CFU) Generating technical drawings from 3D models, views, sections, dimensions, annotations, and technical documentation. Drafting lab. Topic 11 — Interoperability (2.5 HOURS – 0.25 CFU) Interoperability between Autodesk Inventor and AutoCAD. Total Part II: 30 hours – 3 CFU

### Learning objectives

-Knowledge and Understanding • Understand the principles of technical representation and the main projection methods used in industrial drawing. • Know the UNI/CEN/ISO standards governing product technical documentation. • Understand the fundamental principles of 3D CAD modeling and the generation of technical documentation from digital models. -Applying Knowledge and Understanding • Correctly represent mechanical components through technical sketches and drawings. • Apply the rules for the representation of tolerances and surface roughness specifications. • Create 3D CAD part models and produce the corresponding technical drawings. -Making Judgements • Critically analyze technical drawings to extract geometric, functional, and manufacturing information. • Evaluate the correctness and completeness of a technical representation with respect to standards and component functionality. • Select appropriate methods for representation, sectioning, dimensioning, and documentation according to the product being described. -Communication Skills • Communicate technical information through drawings, sketches, CAD models, and technical documentation compliant with industrial drawing conventions. • Correctly use the graphical language and technical terminology of mechanical drawing. • Present and justify representation choices adopted for mechanical components and assemblies. -Learning Skills • Independently use standards, learning materials, examples, and practical exercises to improve the quality of technical representation. • Progressively develop autonomy in the use of 2D/3D CAD tools and in the preparation of technical documentation. • Transfer the acquired knowledge and skills to subsequent courses and design activities within the Mechanical Engineering curriculum.

### Teaching methods

The course consists of 9 ECTS credits, corresponding to a total student workload of 225 hours: 90 hours of classroom instruction and 135 hours of individual study. Teaching activities are carried out in parallel across the two parts of the course: - Part I – Technical Drawing and Representation (6 ECTS, 60 hours) - Part II – 3D CAD and Technical Documentation (3 ECTS, 30 hours) Part I includes lectures, graphical exercises, and drawing assignments to be completed independently. Part II includes lectures and CAD laboratory activities using Autodesk Inventor, with introductory concepts of interoperability with AutoCAD. The course includes 15 hours of laboratory/practical activities, of which 12.5 hours are dedicated to the CAD component. Practical sessions are recorded and made available to students, while theoretical lectures are delivered in person. Attendance is not mandatory but is strongly recommended, particularly for exercises, CAD activities, and assignments. Students who do not have access to a suitable personal computer may use the laboratory workstations equipped with Autodesk Inventor, according to the arrangements communicated by the instructor. Teaching materials, announcements, assignment specifications, and exercise sheets are made available through Microsoft Teams.

### Assessment methods

The assessment is divided into two parts, consistent with the structure of the course: Part I – Technical Drawing and Representation and Part II – 3D CAD. The final grade is calculated as follows: Final Grade = 67% Part I + 33% Part II + assignment bonus/malus Part I – Technical Drawing and Representation (67%) The assessment for Part I is conducted in person and consists of: • Multiple-choice theoretical test: 15 questions; • Open-ended theoretical questions: 2 groups of 3 questions; • Practical freehand drawing test: manual representation of a mechanical component according to technical drawing standards. Assignments 1, 2, and 3 of Part I consist of graphical exercises to be submitted through Microsoft Teams. The overall evaluation of these assignments affects the final grade with a score ranging from –1.4 to +1.4 points. Submission within the intermediate deadlines (generally about 10 days for each assignment) grants a 0.3-point bonus per assignment, which may be used up to and including the October examination session. Students whose submitted work is deemed seriously inadequate will not be admitted to the examination. Part II – 3D CAD (33%) The CAD examination is held in the computer laboratory and consists of a 90-minute practical test using Autodesk Inventor, focusing on part modeling, parameterization, and technical drawing production. Assignment 4 is a prerequisite for the CAD examination and concerns part modeling, parameterization, and technical drawing production. It does not contribute to the final grade but must be assessed as satisfactory. Failure to submit the assignment, late submission, or an unsatisfactory evaluation will prevent admission to Part II. Assignment 5 is optional and concerns the modification, using AutoCAD, of an assembly drawing originally created in Inventor. If submitted within the specified deadline, generally 2–3 weeks after the end of the course, it awards a bonus ranging from 0 to +1.2 points. The two parts belong to a single examination session and must be taken within the same session, even if they are scheduled on different days for organizational reasons. Grading Criteria • 18–21/30: The student is able to produce drawings that meet the minimum requirements but contain significant errors in geometric representation and in the interpretation of views and sections. • 22–25/30: The drawings are substantially correct in terms of representation but contain significant errors or omissions in dimensioning. • 26–28/30: The drawings are correct in terms of representation and dimensioning but may contain errors in the specification of dimensional and geometrical tolerances and surface roughness indications. • 29–30/30 cum laude: The drawings are complete, correct, and fully compliant with applicable technical standards. Students who achieve the maximum grade of 30/30 and demonstrate an excellent level of performance in both the practical and theoretical assessments, particularly in the open-ended questions, may be awarded cum laude. Minimum Requirements Students must be able to: -apply, with sufficient competence, the methods of technical representation and, in particular, correctly produce standardized dimensioned views and/or section views of an industrial product; -correctly represent symbols and specifications related to surface roughness, dimensional tolerances, and geometrical tolerances; -identify, with sufficient competence, the main mechanical elements represented in an assembly drawing. For students with disabilities or specific learning disorders (SLD), appropriate compensatory tools and accommodation measures will be provided according to individual needs and in compliance with current regulations.
