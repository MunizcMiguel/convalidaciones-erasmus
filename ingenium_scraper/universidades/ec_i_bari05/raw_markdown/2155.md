### Contents

Plant layout (1.5 CFU) Classification of industrial plants and production. Study of plant layout. Typological classification of layout. Production yields. Calculation of the number of layout operating units by departments. Quantitative methods in the study of layout by departments. Balancing of production lines. Industrial buildings (1.5 CFU) Classification of industrial buildings. Construction types. Constituent parts. Interior lighting. Calculation of Primary Energy Requirement (FEP). Material handling systems (1.5 CFU) Classification of internal transportation means. Loading units. Belt conveyors. Pneumatic conveyors. Hoists and winches. Augers. Bucket elevators. Trolleys. Piping (1.5 CFU) Characteristic elements. Basic material selection and process design. Technical specification for component procurement. Assembly and protection of piping. Inspection and testing. Control system. Criteria for selecting a regulating valve. Operating characteristic of a regulating valve. Systems reliability and maintenance (1.5 CFU) Non-repairable components. Time to failure, reliability, failure rates. Reliability evaluation. Repairable components. MTBF, MTTR and Availability. Functional and reliability diagrams. Series and parallel systems. Redundancy. Maintenance policies. Fundamentals of work safety (1.5 CFU) Occupational safety legislation and guiding principles. Main regulatory references and figures of responsibility. Concepts of risk, harm, prevention, protection. Organization of corporate prevention. Occupational injury and occupational disease. Classification and statistics of accidents. Definition and process of risk assessment (ISO 14121). Risk analysis methodologies. DVR and DUVRI

### Learning objectives

EXPECTED LEARNING RESULTS A) Knowledge and understanding skills The course aims to provide knowledge of manufacturing plants through the fundamentals of design and maintenance of mechanical industrial plants and issues for managing the safety and health of operators employed in industrial settings. B) Ability to apply knowledge and understanding The course, through the analysis of case studies referring to real plant systems and current technical and legislative regulations on occupational safety and health, will provide students with multiple evaluation tools that will enable them to properly apply the acquired knowledge in the design and maintenance of major non-complex mechanical industrial plants and the main tools for dealing with industrial safety management issues. C) Further learning outcomes, with reference to - Judgment skills: through the study of alternative scenarios and decisive factors of choice, with reference to possible plant and organizational choices, the student will be able to refine technical knowledge and judgment in order to identify the best plant solutions referring to specific project scenarios, evaluate their inherent reliability and the associated risks involved. - Communication skills: the description of plant factors and risk analysis, with reference to integrated design techniques, will be carried out in a way that allows the acquisition of appropriate technical terminology. The development of oral and written communication skills will also be stimulated through classroom discussion of case studies and through the support of multimedia tools. - Learning skills: it will be stimulated through classroom discussions followed by an interactive student analytical process aimed at stimulating high levels of understanding of the topics covered. Learning skills will also be stimulated by various supplementary teaching aids, such as: design documents, specialized manuals, numerical exercises and industrial case studies, aimed at enhancing students' application skills.

### Prerequisites

Fundamentals of mathematical analysis (differential and integral calculus, function study), physics (thermodynamics, heat transfer phenomena), economics and business organization (basics of investment analysis), construction science (statics of beams).

### Teaching methods

Classroom lectures and exercises supported by power point slides, tutoring in the form of individual assistance.

### Assessment methods

LEARNING VERIFICATION METHODS The examination comprises a written test for admission to a subsequent oral test. The two tests will be held on different days. The oral test must necessarily be held in the same roll call as the written test. The written test is oriented towards assessing both the knowledge acquired during the course (free-response questions) and the student&amp;#39;s ability to apply the tools and solution approaches learnt during the course in an organic and exhaustive manner. A mark of at least 18/30 in the written test is required for admission to the oral test. The oral test is aimed at assessing the student&amp;#39;s knowledge in relation to the ability to synthesise and integrate themes and design models covered in the course by verifying the level of understanding of the subject, expressive skills and use of appropriate terminology. A minimum score of 18/30 is required to pass the oral test. Failure to participate in or pass the oral test invalidates the written test taken. MINIMUM LEARNING REQUIREMENTS Both tests require the following minimum requirements: knowledge of different types of layouts, industrial buildings, internal material handling systems, basic concepts of reliability theory, the main regulatory references, and the contents of a DVR and DUVRI; ability to expound the acquired knowledge in appropriate technical language; ability to apply the main techniques of sizing a layout and an internal material handling system, and ability to correctly assess the reliability of a system. GRADING CRITERIA FOR THE FINAL GRADE The final exam grade represents an overall assessment of the student&#39;s preparation, as revealed by both the written test and the oral exam, based on the following criteria: knowledge of the topics in the course syllabus, ability to set and solve problems in different contexts, ability to make independent judgments, ability to express concepts using technical language. The assignment of the minimum grade (18/30) will take place if the minimum learning requirements defined above are verified. The assignment of the maximum grade (30/30) will take place if an excellent knowledge of the topics covered and a complete and thorough understanding of the concepts, the ability to solve very complex problems and to apply the knowledge learned in innovative contexts, an excellent ability to formulate autonomous, critical and original judgments together with an excellent ability to express concepts using high-level technical language and in a clear, coherent and persuasive manner are verified. Honors will be awarded if exceptional and comprehensive knowledge of the topics covered is verified, with ability to connect concepts in innovative ways. Ability to solve highly complex problems and apply knowledge in highly innovative and interdisciplinary contexts. Excellent ability to make autonomous, critical and original judgments with a high degree of insight. Outstanding ability to express concepts using high-level technical language clearly, coherently, persuasively and with a high level of accuracy.
