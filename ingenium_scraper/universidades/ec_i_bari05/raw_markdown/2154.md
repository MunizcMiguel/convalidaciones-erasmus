### Learning objectives

+ Knowledge and understanding skills The course intends to transfer the ability to correctly interpret the measurement results in the different areas of professional interest of the mechanical engineer. The fundamental theoretical concepts, the main criteria for analyzing the performance of measurement systems, the technical procedures for the correct use of the instrumentation will be provided. + Ability to apply knowledge and understanding At the end of the course, students will be able to choose the most appropriate components to configure a measurement chain for mechanical and thermal quantities in static and dynamic conditions and to analyze the main causes of measurement uncertainty. This knowledge is crucial for building decision-making skills based on real data, such as those of the main applications in the field on which the student will be called to work, being aware of the risks as a consequence of the level of confidence in the information available. + Any further expected learning outcomes, relating to: • Autonomy of judgment: At the end of the course the student will develop critical analysis skills for choosing the most suitable tools for the intended use. • Communication skills: The study will allow him to acquire the correct terminology to communicate and discuss technical topics with colleagues, both in textual and graphic form. • Learning skills: The use of theoretical and practical exercises, as well as classroom discussions will allow you to speed up the learning skills thus stimulated by the practical applications of the proposed concepts.

### Prerequisites

Knowledge from the courses of Mathematics, Physics, Mathematical Physics, Applied Mechanics to Machinery, Principles of Electrical Engineering.

### Teaching methods

Frontal lectures and numerical practise with the aid of a projector and / or digital board, with direct intervention of the students during the practise. Laboratory practise with instrumentation brought to the classroom (given the high number of students and the impossibility to manage groups)

### Assessment methods

The assessment of learning is based on the interaction between instructor and student, which takes place during lectures and exercises. Engagement occurs by asking participants oral questions during these sessions to solve exercises or stimulate discussion, primarily aimed at refining the connections between the topics covered. The final exam consists of a written test (weight: 30%) and an oral exam (weight: 70%). The written test (lasting about 50 minutes) includes 4-5 questions, both open-ended and numerical exercises, and is complementary to a mandatory oral exam (lasting about 20 minutes) on the topics covered during the course. Minimum requirements: Written test: the ability to establish correct resolution procedures on the main theoretical and practical aspects of measurement, such as: combined type uncertainty, expanded uncertainty, conformity assessment, static and dynamic characteristics of instruments, and acquisition of time-varying signals. Oral exam: the student must demonstrate acquired knowledge and the use of appropriate terminology by answering 3-4 questions related to the course topics. The student should show sufficient command and language proficiency concerning the requested topic, as well as sufficient ability to independently rework concepts using diagrams and charts. Criteria for the evaluation of the final grade: - 18-20 (Sufficient): Basic understanding of the subject, with minimal competence in applying the proposed concepts. - 21-23 (Fair): Adequate knowledge of key topics, though not in-depth; moderate ability to apply principles and techniques learned during the course. - 24-26 (Good): Well-structured understanding of the subject, with a good capacity for analysis and application of the concepts learned during the course. - 27-29 (Very Good): Complete and in-depth understanding, with strong analytical skills and the ability to apply studied concepts to engineering practice. - 30 (Excellent): Exceptional mastery of the subject, with outstanding analytical skills and practical application abilities. A personal elaboration is generally required, based on the knowledge acquired during the third and final year of the degree program, applying studied concepts in a critical and innovative manner. - 30 cum laude (Excellent cum laude): Reserved for outstanding performances that exceed standard evaluation criteria, often requiring correct answers to additional challenging questions during the oral examination. It also demonstrates an excellent and comprehensive understanding of the subject, with autonomous and original critical thinking skills.
