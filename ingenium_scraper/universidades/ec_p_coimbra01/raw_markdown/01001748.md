## Recommended Prerequisites

Mathematical Analysis I ,II.

## Teaching Methods

The teaching in this course assumes two formats: theoretical and example classes. During a theoretical class teaching is mostly expository. During an example class teaching consists of problem solving by the students under the guidance of the lecturer. A strong interaction between notions and their practical application is emphasized. In this task, the visualization and the analysis of concrete examples takes on a central role and prepares the way for the abstract definitions. Tutorial support is available to students to help them on the tasks assigned by the lecturers.

## Learning Outcomes

The student who successfully completes this course will be able to:

1. Compute the probability of an event;

2. Model nondeterministic characteristics using random variables and their distributions;

3. Analyse samples and interpret the results;

4. Compute estimates for the mean and variance of a population and for a proportion;

5. Obtain the confidence intervals for the mean and variance of a population and for a proportion;

6. Design hypothesis tests for the mean and variance of a population and for a proportion;

7. Assess the compatibility of a sample with a statistical model using adjustment tests;

8. Use the notion of p-value in statistical software.

## Work Placement(s)

No

## Syllabus

1. Probabilities: Kolmogorov probability definition and its consequences. Conditional probability. Independence of events.

2. Random variables and distributions: continuous and discrete real random variables. Discrete and continuous probabilistic models. Multidimensional distributions. Central Limit Theorem and applications.

3. Point and interval estimation of parameters.

4. Hypothesis tests: Parametric tests. Adjustment tests. Independence tests.
