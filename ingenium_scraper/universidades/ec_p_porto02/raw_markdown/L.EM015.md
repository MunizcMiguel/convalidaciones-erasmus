# Análise Numérica

- Teaching language: Portuguese
- Edition: Instance: 2025/2026 - 1S

## Objectives

General: The students will be able to choose the most efficient methods for the solution of eachtypical Numerical Analysis problem. The students are expected to understand the theorems and convergence conditions of each of the methods described, to be able to program them, to test them effectively on a computer and discuss the results obtained. Specific: For each chapter in the program the students will be able to: - list the applicability conditions of the numerical methods and state the corresponding theorems of convergence ; - apply the methods, formula and algorithms taught to real problems; - describe the behavior of the methods, translate them into algorithms and ‘Matlab Functions’ as well as test them on examples comparing and analyzing the results; - explain the proofs of the theorems given and apply the proof techniques involved to other related situations; - solve new problems with the numerical tools here taught and compare the performance of the various numerical methods in terms of speed and accuracy.

## Learning outcomes and competences

Upon completing the Numerical Analysis course, students should have developed the following competencies and achieved the following learning outcomes: 1. Mastery of Numerical Methods and Convergence Theorems: Understand and apply the main numerical methods covered in the course, identifying the conditions for their applicability and the respective convergence theorems. Demonstrate the ability to critically analyze the efficiency and robustness of the methods for different types of numerical problems. 2. Programming Skills and Computational Implementation: Translate mathematical methods and algorithms into computational code, particularly in Matlab, by implementing functions that accurately solve numerical problems. Perform numerical simulations and interpret the results, adjusting parameters and refining solutions to achieve optimal accuracy. 3. Practical Application and Problem-Solving: Apply the numerical methods learned to practical situations, such as solving nonlinear equations, numerical integration and differentiation, linear systems, and eigenvalue analysis. Analyze and solve new problems using the numerical tools studied, comparing the performance of different methods in terms of speed, precision, and robustness. 4. Theoretical Analysis and Proof Skills: Explain theorem proofs and apply theoretical concepts to new contexts, demonstrating a deep understanding of the underlying mathematical principles. Show the ability to derive new results or methods based on the concepts studied, applying logical and mathematical reasoning. 5. Critical Thinking and Comparative Evaluation: Critically assess the results obtained from simulations, discussing the accuracy, efficiency, and limitations of the numerical methods applied. Compare different numerical approaches, selecting the most appropriate one for each problem based on criteria such as convergence speed, ease of implementation, and robustness. 6. Communication Skills and Teamwork: Develop the ability to clearly and objectively communicate technical concepts and results. Collaborate effectively in teams, sharing ideas, discussing solutions, and contributing to the collective development of numerical projects.

## Pre-requirements (prior knowledge) and co-requirements (common knowledge)

The students are supposed to know the subjects taught in Linear Algebra and Mathematical Analysis as well as in Computer programming.

## Program

Chapter 1 Number systems and errors; number systems on computers; representation of integers and floating point arithmetic; round-off error; absolute error and relative error, significant digits, Taylor's formula and error estimation; error analysis. Chapter 2 Linear systems of equations: Gaussian elimination. Round off errors and possible instability of the numerical methods, pivoting strategies. Solution of triangular systems. Tridiagonal systems. LU factorization; application to the computation of determinants and to the inversion of matrices. Iterative methods: Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel; convergence theorems and algorithms.Definition of truncation error, estimation of truncation errors for the previous methods. Chapter 3 Non linear equations: general conditions for the solution, stopping criteria for iterative methods; some iterative methods: successive bisection, fixed point iteration, Newton's method, secant method. Convergence theorems, estimation and upper bound of truncation errors, order of convergence. Chapter 4 Least squares approximation. Orthogonal polynomials. Curve fitting. Over-determined systems of equations. Chapter 5 Polynomial interpolation: methods of Newton and Lagrange; error of the interpolating polynomial. Hermite interpolation and Splines. Numerical differentiation anddivided differences. Chapter 6 Numerical integration: Newton-Cotes formulae. Composite rules. Numerical integration errors. Gaussian quadrature. Chapter 7 Ordinary differential equations: Euler's method for ODE of order 1. Taylor methods. Order of a method for ODE of order 1. Runge-Kutta methods of order 2 and 4.

## Teaching methods and learning activities

Theoretical expositions are primarily delivered on the board and through PowerPoint presentations, with a strong focus on Analysis and Algebra concepts. These sessions are accompanied, whenever possible, by motivating practical examples. Additional examples are suggested for students to test on the computer, allowing them to observe practical behavior, which is subsequently explained through the learned theory. In the laboratory sessions, students complete and present small projects proposed during the theoretical-practical classes. These projects are prepared outside of class hours in computer-equipped rooms with the necessary software.

## Calculation formula of final grade

The assessment of this curricular unit (u.c.) will be carried out as follows: 1st test, T1, mandatory, on a date to be communicated to students; this test will have a weight of 50% in the final grade; a minimum grade of 7 out of 20 is required; 2nd test, T2, exclusively for students who scored 7 or higher in T1, and will only cover the topics after T1; this test will have a weight of 50% in the final grade and also requires a minimum grade of 7 out of 20; final exam, EF, TO BE TAKEN SIMULTANEOUSLY WITH THE 2nd TEST, which must be taken MANDATORILY by students who scored less than 7 in T1; however, any student may choose to take this exam instead of the 2nd test; the EF covers all the topics; retake exam, ER, will be taken by students who did not obtain the minimum grade in either T1 or T2, or who did not achieve an average of 10 in the two tests or in the EF exam, and also for grade improvement. This exam also covers all the topics. The final grade, CF, will thus be obtained by: CF = 0.5 * T1 grade + 0.5 * T2 grade, provided that T1 >= 7 and T2 >= 7, or CF = EF grade, or CF = ER grade. Important notes: A CF grade of 18 or higher (out of 20) is subject to an oral exam; Grades from assessment components from previous years are not considered and are not carried over to subsequent years; Grade improvement is done through an exam, according to the specific assessment regulations of FEUP. This exam will only be taken in the following term(s) after the final CF grade is determined.
