# Mecânica dos Fluidos II

- Teaching language: Portuguese
- Edition: Instance: 2025/2026 - 1S

## Objectives

Analyse, understand and characterise the behaviour of fluids in internal flows, the energy requirements of a flow and the operation of pumps and centrifugal fans, the measurement of fundamental quantities in fluid flows, the interaction between a moving fluid and an object immersed in it, and some basic aspects of open-channel and compressible flows. Solve and understand fluid mechanics problems.

## Learning outcomes and competences

It is expected that, at the end of the semester, students will be able to/know: 1. The fundamental differences between flows with negligible fluid density variation and compressible flows; 2. Apply the mass conservation and energy equations to viscous flows in pipes, calculate pressure drop, energy needs and available flux, and dimensioning simple pipes and ducts; 3. The fundamental principles of operation and the characteristic curves of centrifugal fans and pumps, and selecting and analysing the operation of such equipment; 4. The principles of operation and the domains of use of the most common types of speed meters, flow meters and pressure transducers; 5. The general characteristics of open-channel flow and the use of a specific energy diagram. 6. Understand the essential aspects of flows around immersed bodies and characterise the forces resulting from the interaction flow-body.

Mathematics, Mechanics I and II

## Teaching methods and learning activities

The unit is organised into two weekly sessions (one theoretical class and one theoretical-practical class) lasting 2 hours each. The three laboratory sessions (each lasting 2 hours) are compulsory for first-time students. Students who took the laboratory sessions in the 2023-2024 academic year may choose not to attend them and keep the grade they have already obtained.

## Calculation formula of final grade

During the semester: two theoretical quizzes on the Moodle platform. Quiz 1: between November 3 and 7, on a day and time to be announced. Quiz 2: between December 15 and 19, on a day and time to be announced. No consultation, 30 minutes. It is graded from 0 to 20, weighing 30% (15% each) in the final grade. During the semester: laboratory sessions. Three laboratory sessions (each lasting 2 hours) to carry out experimental work. A questionnaire is answered and evaluated in each session, constituting the laboratory grade. During the evaluation period, a practical written test. Exam (9 January 2026, time to be announced) or Appeal (27 January 2026, time to be announced) Limited consultation (two-page A4 form), 90 minutes duration. Access to this test requires a minimum of 7/20 in the theory test (Moodle) and laboratory tests. Obtaining a minimum mark of 7 in the Moodle test and laboratory sessions is a condition for taking part in the written portion of the Exam or Appeal. The appeal period or grade improvement includes, as a whole, assessment in both the theoretical and practical components. The weights of the theoretical and practical components in the final classification will be the same as in the exam period. The theoretical components also require a minimum mark of 7 in the appeal period. The mark obtained in the Moodle test at the end of the semester is an integral part of the exam. The Appeal exam will have its Moodle exam hours before the written component. The final grade will be determined with weights of 30 per cent and 10 per cent from the Moodle and laboratory tests, and 60 per cent from the written test. Final grade = 0.6 x (Exam or Supplementary) + 0.3 x Moodle + 0.1 x Laboratory In all these components (Exam or Appeal, Moodle and Laboratory), a minimum mark of 7 (seven) is required. The final mark may be adjusted by plus or minus 1/20 depending on attendance criteria, class participation, etc. Students with a mark of more than 16 may be given an oral exam.

## Eligibility for exams

Attendance of, at least, 70% of theoretical-practical and lab sessions. Participation in the 3 laboratory sessions (each lasting 2 hours) is compulsory for first-time students. Students who have held laboratory sessions in the 2024-2025 academic year may choose not to attend these sessions, maintaining the previous year's classification.
