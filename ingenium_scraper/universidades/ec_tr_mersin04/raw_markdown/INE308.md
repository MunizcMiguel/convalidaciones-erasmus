# INE308 — FORECASTING METHODS

ECTS: 4 · Year 3 · Semester 6 · optativa · content year id 48

## Prerequisites

It is recommended that students acquire the fundamental learning outcomes for Probability and Random Variables and Engineering Statistics.

## Objectives of the course

The aim of this course is to provide engineering students with the fundamental concepts of statistical estimation and forecasting methods and to develop their ability to apply these methods to real data sets. Within the scope of the course, students are expected to quantitatively assess uncertainty through confidence intervals and hypothesis testing, perform forecasting using regression and time series models, and select appropriate models by comparing different forecasting methods. In addition, the course aims to enhance students’ skills in statistical thinking, model building, and the critical interpretation of results.

## Course content

In this course, an introduction to forecasting and statistical preliminaries are presented, with an emphasis on the relationship between forecasting and data, forecasting methods, the evaluation of forecasting errors, and the selection of appropriate forecasting techniques. Confidence intervals for large and small samples are examined in detail, including confidence intervals for means, proportions, and differences, as well as the relationships among prediction intervals, confidence intervals, and tolerance intervals.

Within the scope of hypothesis testing, one-sample and two-sample tests, tests for proportions, and the interpretation of hypothesis test results are discussed. In the section on time series forecasting theory, the differences between time series and cross-sectional studies, components of time series, model selection, and basic smoothing methods are examined; moving averages, weighted moving averages, and exponential smoothing methods are covered in detail.

Subsequently, AR, MA, ARMA, and ARIMA models are introduced, and model identification and evaluation processes are explained using autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation functions. Under the topics of correlation and simple linear regression, covariance and correlation concepts, the least squares method, model assumptions, and the analysis of uncertainties are addressed. In the multiple linear regression section, model construction, variable selection, confounding, and multicollinearity problems are discussed.

Throughout the course, applications are carried out on real data sets, and examples are presented in the R programming environment to enable students to transform theoretical knowledge into practice.

## Learning outcomes

1. Defines forecasting problems and selects appropriate forecasting methods based on the type of data, the objective of the study, and error measures.
2. Computes confidence intervals, prediction intervals, and tolerance intervals, and quantitatively interprets uncertainty.
3. Applies hypothesis tests (one-sample and two-sample tests for proportions and means) and draws statistical inferences from test results.
4. Defines and applies time series forecasting models (moving average, exponential smoothing, AR, MA, ARMA, and ARIMA) and identifies the most appropriate model by comparing alternative models.
5. Constructs correlation and simple linear regression models, evaluates model assumptions, and analyzes forecasting uncertainties.
6. Constructs multiple linear regression models and evaluates issues related to variable selection, confounding, and multicollinearity.
