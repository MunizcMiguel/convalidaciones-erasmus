# MAT203 — PROBABILITY AND RANDOM VARIABLES

ECTS: 4 · Year 2 · Semester 3 · obligatoria · content year id 48

## Prerequisites

It is recommended that students acquire the fundamental learning outcomes for Mathematics I and II.

## Objectives of the course

This course introduces the fundamentals of probability theory and random processes. Designed for students seeking to understand the principles and applications of probability, it focuses on developing intuition, model-building skills, and formal reasoning. Through a mathematical foundation, students will gain a solid understanding of fundamental probabilistic concepts and learn to approach real-world problems under uncertainty. The course emphasizes structuring problems, building models, and analyzing them to derive meaningful insights.

## Course content

Combinatorial analysis. Permutations and combinations. Axioms of probability. Sample space and events. Equally likely outcomes. Conditional probability. Bayes formula. Independent events and trials. Discrete random variables. Probability mass functions. Expected value, variance. Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, geometric, negative binomial random variables. Continuous random variables, density functions. Expected value and variance. Uniform, normal, exponential, Gamma random variables. Independent random variables and the distribution of their sums. Conditional expectations and distributions (discrete case). Law of large numbers. Central limit theorem.

## Learning outcomes

1. Understanding Random Events: Students will be able to define relevant random events for a given random experiment and compute probabilities of simple and composite events.
2. Independence and Bayes' Theorem: Students will be able to check the independence of events, calculate conditional probabilities, and apply Bayes’ Theorem.
3. Random Variables: Students will compute probabilities, expected values, and variances of random variables using probability mass functions (PMF), probability density functions (PDF), and cumulative distribution functions (CDF).
4. Probability Distributions: Students will understand and utilize properties of well-known discrete and continuous probability distributions.
5. Joint Distributions: Students will analyze joint distributions to compute probabilities for events involving multiple random variables, derive marginal distributions, and determine the distributions of functions of two random variables.
6. Random Samples and Sampling Distributions: Students will understand properties of random samples and the distributions of sample means and variances.
