## Module: DENTAL MATERIALS (TO0018)

### Language of instruction

Italian

### Contents

Basic Concepts;Property of Dental Materials;Biocompatibility Of Dental Materials;Materials in Restorative Dentistry;Materials In Endodontics;Materials in Prosthetic dentistry;Materials In Implantology;Materials In Orthodontics;Materials In Periodontology;Materials For Hygiene And Prevention;

New Technologies And Eco-Sustainable Materials In Dentistry; Biomaterials and nanotechnologies

### Reference texts

-"Science of dental materials" Vol 1 andVol 2 (ed.2018) SIMIONATO-pubblished by PICCIN-"Materials for restaurative dentistry" (ed. 1998), Robert G. CRAIG.pubblished by Elsevier - Masson. Additional teaching materials will also be available on the University’s e-learning platform (https://elearning.unich.it/).

### Learning objectives

The training activity aims to provide the student with basic knowledge of dental materials in chemical, physical and biological terms and above all in clinical application. The fundamental knowledge of the manufacturing procedures, the indications for use, the components constituting the individual dental materials, their use in dentistry will be presented, in order to guide the student in the choice of materials in relation to optimal clinical use.The main objective of the training will be the acquisition of guiding concepts useful for quickly orienting oneself in the choice of materials and biomaterials among the numerous products and technologies available in the dental field in order to choose the most performing materials for clinical use.The course will also provide the foundations for each student to develop their research skills on the main electronic databases in the dental materials sector. Cooperation in groups will be encouraged and students will be encouraged to actively interact with the teacher through participation in open lectures, seminars, the discussion of scientific articles, and the critical evaluation of specific scientific literature.

### Prerequisites

Knowledge of basic subjects: physics, chemistry, anatomy and histology

### Teaching methods

The training activity includes practical exercises involving: viewing and handling certain dental materials. Simulation of clinical applications of the main materials used in dentistry.

### Assessment methods

Learning outcomes will be assessed through a mid-term examination and a final written examination. Students will be required to demonstrate that they have acquired fundamental knowledge of dental materials, as well as the ability to apply this knowledge in the selection and use of different dental materials. The final grade will be awarded on a scale of thirty points (out of 30), in accordance with the assessment criteria established by the integrated course.

### Extended programme

Evaluation of the properties and clinical application of dental materials for dental use. Specifically: impression materials, plasters, waxes, adhesive systems, whitening systems, composites, CAD-CAM materials, ceramics, implantology materials, biomaterials. Guide to the study of scientific literature.

Dental ceramics: introduction, historical background, definition, and classifications. Chemical-physical structure of glass ceramics, reinforced glass ceramics, and crystalline ceramics.

- Production methods of glass ceramics, reinforced glass ceramics, and crystalline ceramics, and their clinical indications. Ceramics in orthodontics.

- Chemical-physical properties of dental ceramics (chemical, thermal, electrical, optical properties, hardness, mechanical strength, modulus of rupture, fracture toughness) and their behavior (fatigue, wear, scratching, chipping). Insights from the scientific literature.

- Surface determinants (surface energy, wettability, roughness), biological properties (biocompatibility, immunogenicity, interactions with epithelial, connective, periodontal, and peri-implant tissues and with oral microorganisms) and biological behavior (epithelial adhesion, fibroblast response and adhesion, microbial adhesion and biofilm formation) of dental ceramics. Insights from the scientific literature.
