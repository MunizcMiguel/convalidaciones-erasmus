## Contents

The course introduces the fundamental principles of electromagnetism and optics. Particular attention will be devoted to the study of the electrostatic field, the magnetic field, the relationship between electric and magnetic fields, and electromagnetic radiation, with reference to the fundamental laws governing these phenomena.  
The analysis of these topics will enable students to develop an integrated understanding of the physical principles underlying many biological and physiological processes. This approach will promote a systemic view of electromagnetic phenomena and their applications, providing the theoretical foundations necessary for the use and development of technologies in the field of engineering.

## Learning objectives

The course is designed to provide knowledge of the fundamental concepts of electromagnetism and optics. It also aims to enable students to solve physics problems related to these topics. Through the solution of numerical examples, students will further develop their ability to critically analyze results and to apply theoretical principles to the solution of concrete problems.  
In particular, the course is intended to develop:  
  
Knowledge and understanding: Acquisition of the fundamental concepts of electromagnetism and optics. Understanding of the theoretical principles and physical laws governing electromagnetic and optical phenomena.  
Applying knowledge and understanding: Application of concepts and physical laws to the analysis and solution of numerical problems and practical exercises. Ability to connect theory with experimental practice and applied contexts.  
Making judgements: Development of critical skills in the interpretation of numerical and theoretical results. Evaluation of the appropriateness of physical models in relation to the problems addressed.  
Communication skills: Ability to present theoretical concepts and solution procedures clearly and rigorously, using appropriate scientific language. Ability to argue methodological choices in a logical and coherent manner.  
  
Learning skills: Development of the ability to independently approach the study of more advanced topics in physics. Strengthening of learning capacity through both guided and autonomous problem-solving.

## Prerequisites

Students are required to know the basics of trigonometry, algebra and calculus: vectors and vector operations, derivatives, integrals, differential equations, coordinate systems. It is also required that the students know the basics of physics acquired in the first module (Fisica 1): forces, vector fields, work, energy, power.

## Assessment methods

The final examination consists of a written test followed by an oral test, which together contribute to an integrated assessment of the competencies acquired.  
The written test is intended to verify mastery of theoretical content and the ability to solve quantitative problems, demonstrating adequate knowledge and understanding of the fundamental principles, as well as the student’s ability to apply methods and concepts to specific contexts and problems.  
The minimum duration of the written exam is 120 minutes.  
The results of the written exam will be posted on the course e-learning page, together with the schedule for the oral exam.  
The oral test, accessible with a score of 18/30 or higher in the written part, evaluates the ability to critically analyze results, present theoretical constructs, establish connections between different topics, and formulate reasoned evaluations. The oral test also serves to assess clarity of presentation, proper use of scientific language, and the ability to argue coherently.  
The examination is considered passed if the weighted average of the two components (written and oral) is equal to or greater than 18/30. The oral test normally takes place one or two days after the written test. Since the two components constitute a single integrated assessment, it is not possible to retain the result of the written test for a subsequent examination session.  
The criteria for the awarding of the final grade are as follows:  
18/30 (minimum pass): The student demonstrates a basic understanding of the main concepts of electromagnetism and optics. They are able to solve simple standard numerical exercises, though with limited ability. The interpretation of results is elementary and critical capacity is weak. Explanations are essential but comprehensible, while further study can only be pursued with external support.  
19–22/30 (satisfactory): The student demonstrates adequate, though not complete, knowledge of the main topics. They can correctly apply methods to standard problems, albeit with some uncertainty. They show some ability to evaluate common results, but with limited critical autonomy. Explanations are coherent though not very detailed, and study is carried out with fair autonomy, especially when guided.  
23–25/30 (good): The student shows solid mastery of the fundamental concepts. They are able to solve problems that are not entirely straightforward, demonstrating good application of acquired knowledge. They can interpret and analyze results with a reasonable level of critical insight. Explanations are clear and sufficiently rigorous. Study and re-elaboration of content are undertaken with good autonomy.  
26–28/30 (very good): The student demonstrates in-depth and well-structured knowledge of the topics covered. They are able to solve complex problems with confidence in the methods used. They can establish links between different topics and evaluate alternative models. Explanations are clear, rigorous, and well-argued. Study is conducted with strong autonomy and a high capacity for further exploration.  
29–30/30 (excellent): The student possesses complete and detailed knowledge, without significant gaps. They are able to apply concepts flexibly even to new or complex contexts. Their analysis of results is mature, showing strong synthesis skills and independent evaluation. Explanations are precise, rigorous, and fluent, with appropriate use of scientific language. Study is approached with strong autonomy and the ability to transfer competencies to different contexts.  
30/30 with honors (outstanding): The student achieves a level of excellence beyond the required standards, demonstrating impeccable and thorough mastery of the subject. They can apply concepts creatively and originally, even in unconventional contexts. They show full critical independence, brilliance in analysis, and originality in evaluation. Explanations are excellent, clear, rigorous, and engaging. Study is pursued with extraordinary autonomy and the ability to independently extend knowledge.

## Extended programme

Electromagnetism  
Electrostatics: Coulomb’s law. Electric field of discrete and continuous charge distributions. Electric flux and Gauss’s law with applications to symmetric charge distributions. Electric potential: definition, relation to the electric field, equipotential surfaces. Electrostatic potential energy and work of electric forces.  
Conductors, capacitance, and dielectrics: Conductors in electrostatic equilibrium. Capacitance and capacitors (in series and in parallel). Energy stored in an electric field. Dielectrics: dielectric constant, polarization, capacitance with dielectrics.  
Current and resistance: Electric current and current density. Resistivity and conductivity; Ohm’s laws. Direct current circuits: resistors in series and parallel, Kirchhoff’s rules. Electric power.  
Magnetic fields: Magnetic forces on moving charges and current-carrying conductors. Motion of a charge in a uniform magnetic field (cyclotron motion). Biot–Savart law. Ampère’s law and applications (solenoid, toroid). Magnetic moment and magnetic dipoles.  
Electromagnetic induction: Faraday’s law and magnetic flux. Lenz’s law. Inductance and inductors; energy stored in a magnetic field. RL circuits, LC oscillations, and RLC circuits (analogy with mechanical oscillations).  
Maxwell’s equations: Integral and differential form. Displacement current. Electromagnetic waves: wave equations, propagation speed, electromagnetic spectrum, energy, and the Poynting vector.  
  
Optics  
Geometrical optics: Fermat’s principle and Snell’s law of refraction. Reflection and refraction. Thin lenses: conjugate point formulas, magnification. Spherical mirrors. Simple optical instruments: microscope, telescope.  
Physical optics: Interference: superposition principle, Young’s fringes, thin films. Diffraction: single slit, double slit, diffraction grating.
