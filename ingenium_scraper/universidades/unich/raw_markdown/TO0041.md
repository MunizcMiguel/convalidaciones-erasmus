## Learning objectives

The **Structural Mechanics** course aims to provide students with the fundamental theoretical models and operational tools for the study of biomechanical structural systems composed of continuous bodies, with particular reference to structural elements that can be modeled as beams. The conditions of equilibrium, compatibility, constitutive behavior, strength, and stability are analyzed within the framework of solid mechanics.

The course builds upon the knowledge of Mathematics and Physics acquired in previous years and aims to provide a rigorous formulation of the mechanical models used in structural analysis in the field of biomechanics.

The teaching methods and course contents are designed to foster a critical approach to modeling and solving concrete problems, with particular attention to the consistency of the adopted assumptions and to the range of validity of the models employed.

## Extended programme

Ecco la versione in **inglese tecnico per syllabus**, con terminologia coerente in ambito Mechanics / Biomechanics:

### INTRODUCTORY CONCEPTS

Modeling of structural response in bioengineering. The nine fundamental principles of biomechanics. Anatomical and functional concepts. The musculoskeletal system. Mechanical properties of biological tissues.

### KINEMATICS OF RIGID BODIES

General formulation of infinitesimal rigid displacement. Planar rigid body motions. Kinematic characterization of external and internal constraints. The kinematic problem. Exercises.

### STATICS OF RIGID BODY SYSTEMS

Forces and couples. Resultant force and resultant moment of a system of applied forces. Equivalent force systems; elementary operations of equivalence. Equilibrium of a rigid body and of rigid body systems. Fundamental equations of statics. Coplanar force systems. Distributed forces over a volume (body forces, e.g., gravity), over a surface, and along a line; concentrated forces and moments. Reaction forces for smooth, fixed, and bilateral constraints; static characterization of external and internal planar constraints. Determination of admissible reaction states. Statically determinate, statically indeterminate, unstable, and degenerate structures. Internal force resultants in beam systems: axial force, shear force, bending moment, torsional moment; differential equilibrium equations for straight beams.

### GEOMETRIC PROPERTIES OF PLANE AREAS

Area; first moment of area; centroid; second moment of area; radius of gyration; product moment of area; parallel-axis theorem (Huygens–Steiner). Principal axes of inertia; central ellipse of inertia.

### INTRODUCTION TO THE THEORY OF ELASTIC STRUCTURES

Limitations of the rigid-body model. Elementary deformable model: axial bar; linear elastic constitutive relation. Equilibrium, compatibility, and constitutive equations for a straight bar. The linear elastic problem; solution methods: force method and displacement method for axially loaded members.

### ELASTIC BEAM THEORY AND STATICALLY INDETERMINATE BEAM SYSTEMS

Differential relations between transverse displacement of the centroidal axis, cross-section rotation, and bending curvature; curvature induced by thermal strains or bending moments; integration of the elastic curve equation. Deformation measures (bending curvature, torsional curvature, axial strain, shear strain); elastic relations between internal force resultants and deformation measures. Principle of Virtual Work for deformable beams; application to displacement and rotation evaluation in statically determinate structures. Analysis of statically indeterminate structures through compatibility equations (Müller–Breslau equations).

### CAUCHY CONTINUUM – STRESS ANALYSIS

Cauchy stress tensor. Cauchy’s lemma. Decomposition of the Cauchy stress vector; Cauchy’s formula. Differential equilibrium equations and boundary conditions. Principal stresses and principal directions. Triaxial, cylindrical, and spherical stress states. Stress deviator; octahedral stress. Principal reference system. Mohr’s circles. Plane stress, pure shear, and uniaxial stress states. Mohr’s circles for stress analysis at a point in a De Saint-Venant beam. Stress trajectories (isostatic lines).

### STRAIN ANALYSIS

Rigid body motion. Local decomposition of the displacement gradient: infinitesimal strain tensor and infinitesimal rotation tensor. Mechanical interpretation of strain components: normal strains and engineering shear strains. Decomposition of the strain process. Volumetric strain. Cauchy strain relations. Principal strains and principal directions. Triaxial, cylindrical, and spherical strain states. Principal reference system.

### HOOKE’S LAW

Hooke’s law under uniaxial stress; linear elastic behavior and plastic response. Tensile and torsion tests. Constitutive law under multiaxial stress: generalized Hooke’s law. Elastic constants: Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, bulk modulus. Anelastic strains.

### THE ELASTICITY PROBLEM

Existence and uniqueness of the solution to the linear elastic equilibrium problem; Navier and Beltrami equations. Solution of the De Saint-Venant problem for combined axial force and bending (stress-based approach). Neumann problem for torsion (displacement-based approach).

### ENGINEERING BEAM THEORY – THE DE SAINT-VENANT SOLID

Determination of stress and strain fields in homogeneous linear elastic beams from internal force resultants (engineering formulation): hypothesis of plane sections remaining plane; centric axial load; pure bending; combined axial force and bending (symmetric and unsymmetric bending).  
Torsion of thin-walled closed sections (Bredt formula). Torsion of elongated rectangular and open thin-walled sections (C- and L-sections). Torsion of solid circular sections. Approximate shear stress distribution (Jourawski formula). Combined loading: unsymmetric bending; eccentric axial load; relation between load application point and neutral axis; core of inertia; shear and torsion; shear center.

### FAILURE CRITERIA

Overview of strength criteria under uniaxial and multiaxial stress states.

### CONSTITUTIVE MODELS IN BIOENGINEERING AND BIOMECHANICS

Biomechanical model of bone. Biomechanical modeling of skeletal muscles. Constitutive models for fiber-reinforced soft tissues.
